Is Muse available in Italy?

No. As of November 13, 2026, the Muse launch list has five countries and Italy is not one of them: the US, where it launched on September 8, 2026, Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. Neither is any other EU country. Meta has published no country list and no timeline for any further country, and it has announced no date for Italy.

The October picture is consistent across every check. A country by country review updated October 31, 2026 lists the whole European Economic Area, including Italy, with no launch announced. An earlier check on October 7 had found the app in the US and Canada stores only, absent from Italy and the other European stores checked. The Italian App Store shows the same today: search it for Muse from Meta and the listing is not there, because Apple shows the app only where Meta has released it. Muse.ai behaves the same way from Italy. The pages load, the sign in button works, and then a waitlist screen says Muse is not available in your country or region yet. That screen is the country gate, not a fault in the site, and it is identical across the EU. It will not change with a different browser or a different device. Only an Italian launch removes it, and Meta has given no sign of when that comes. No Meta page names Italy, and no Meta executive has mentioned an Italian date in any statement checked for this guide.

That is the whole official position: Meta has said it is working on more countries, takes care with each one, and has no timing to share. Treat any date you see for Italy in a social post or a video as a guess, not a fact.

Italy has stopped an AI chatbot before

Italy is the one country in Europe that has already stopped a mainstream AI chatbot, and that history is the most Italy specific fact in this guide. It does not predict what happens with Muse. It shows what Italy's regulator is willing to do, and how fast it can act.

On March 30, 2023, Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, ordered OpenAI to stop processing the personal data of people in Italy for ChatGPT, and announced the order on March 31. The order cited no legal basis for the mass collection and processing of personal data for training, no information given to users, and no age checks. It followed a data breach reported on March 20 that had exposed some users' conversations and subscribers' payment information. OpenAI geoblocked ChatGPT in Italy, and the service went offline in the country. TechCrunch reported the block on April 1, 2023, noting it was widely seen as the first block of the service by a Western regulator.

The block was temporary. On April 12, 2023, the Garante said it would lift the limitation if OpenAI met a set of measures by April 30, including an information notice, a legal basis for training, tools to object and to erase data, and an age gate. OpenAI published a notice, added an opt out form for training and an age check at sign up, and the Garante reinstated access for Italian users on April 28, 2023. The story continued: on December 20, 2024, the Garante fined OpenAI 15 million euros, finding it had not notified the March 2023 breach and had trained on users' data without an appropriate legal basis. In March 2026, a court in Rome overturned the fine. A governance institute timeline lays out the full sequence from primary sources.

What does this mean for Muse? It means Meta knows, from direct precedent, that an Italian launch can be stopped after it ships, not just delayed before it ships. An agent that reads email, checks calendars, and pays on your behalf raises every question the Garante asked about ChatGPT, plus new ones about acting inside other people's accounts. Meta has not said the Garante is the reason for the Italian delay, and this guide does not claim it is. But any Italian launch has to survive contact with a regulator that has already shown it will act first and negotiate second. That is a real part of the Italian picture, and it is a reason to expect a careful launch rather than a fast one.

The EU rules behind the wait

The honest starting point is that Meta has explained nothing. No statement names a rule, a regulator, or a date for Europe. The rules below are the backdrop, and for Italy they include a law that has already been used against an AI product. None of this is presented as Meta's reason, because Meta has given none.

The newer law is the EU AI Act, and its timetable is already running. General purpose AI models have faced its core obligations since August 2, 2025, except models already on the EU market, which have until August 2, 2027. Article 50 adds transparency duties from August 2, 2026, so that people know they are interacting with AI from the first interaction. Generative systems must also carry machine readable marking, with systems already on the market given until December 2, 2026. None of this prohibits an agent like Muse. It sets the conditions Meta must satisfy in Italy as in every EU country.

In Italy, the GDPR is not an abstraction. It is the law the Garante used against OpenAI in 2023, and it has governed personal data across the EU since 2018. For an agent, its rules decide what may be collected from your email and calendar, how long it may be kept, and what it may be trained on. The industry pattern fits: a June 2026 study by the Centre for the Governance of AI, comparing US and European launch dates for 375 models, found about one in nine reached the EU late or not at all, with Meta delaying around a quarter of its EU releases, the highest rate among the four companies studied. The researchers named data protection rules as the most common regulatory obstacle.

Italy has already seen this pattern play out once, from the regulator's side. When Meta AI's European debut stalled in 2024 over training on EU users' public posts, Meta paused AI training on EU user data in June 2024, and the product reached 41 European countries only in March 2025, about 18 months after its US launch, as a text only assistant not trained on EU user data. That history does not set a date for Muse in Italy. It shows that Meta will delay a European launch for a year or more over data questions, and will ship a reduced product when it finally arrives.

Will Muse speak Italian?

Italian is what daily life runs on in Italy: government, schools, business, and home. A launch that spoke only English would reach only part of the country. Meta has said nothing about Italian language support for Muse, and no announcement covers Italian for any country. But the launch pattern so far suggests language follows country, not the other way round.

Japan launched with Japanese support on October 23, 2026, and Mexico launched in Spanish on November 5, with local connectors and a Spanish language experience. Meta localizes per country. An Italian launch would almost certainly speak Italian, because Meta already operates in Italian across Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. None of that is an Italian Muse announcement, and this guide does not present it as one. It is an inference from how Meta has launched elsewhere.

Muse runs on the Muse Spark model family, and large models generally handle Italian well, so an English workaround account would probably understand Italian requests. Probably is doing a lot of work in that sentence: Meta has published no statement about Muse's Italian ability, and no Italian user has been able to test it officially. Do not assume Italian works perfectly until Italian users can actually try it. The connector question is separate: an Italian launch would need deals for Italian banks, Italian retailers, and Italian services, just as Japan's launch needed Japanese integrations, and none of those deals has been announced.

Italian tech press covers Muse already, and Italian readers are asking the same country questions as readers everywhere else. That coverage is worth reading for what Muse does. But it is not a sign that a launch is near. The sign to watch is a Meta announcement in Italian, naming Italy.

WhatsApp is Italy's everyday app

WhatsApp is Italy's most used messaging app, and it is not close. One analysis put WhatsApp penetration in Italy at 75 percent of phone users, ahead of every other messaging app. The Reuters Institute's 2026 survey found 89 percent of online adults in Italy used WhatsApp in the past week, the second highest share in the surveyed markets, level with Spain. The figures measure different things, but they agree on the point: WhatsApp runs daily life in Italy, from family groups to school chats to small business orders.

Open WhatsApp in Italy today and the assistant you find is Meta AI, the older assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger. It answers questions in chat. It does not log into your accounts, book travel, or pay on your behalf. Muse is the product that does those things, and Muse inside WhatsApp follows the same country rule as the Muse app: it is available only where Muse itself has launched. Mexico's launch on November 5 included WhatsApp alongside iOS, Android, and the web, which shows WhatsApp is part of the launch package, not a back door around it. Muse has not launched in Italy in any form.

If you are unsure which assistant you are talking to, Meta has answered that question directly. The official @meta.ai account has explained that Meta AI is the assistant built into Threads, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp, while Muse is the standalone personal agent app. They share a company and a chat window, so the mix up is natural. An AI assistant in WhatsApp in Italy today is Meta AI. WhatsApp is a likely part of an Italian launch, given its dominance here and the Mexico pattern, but Meta has confirmed nothing about Italian WhatsApp onboarding.

The waiting advice for Italy is to keep WhatsApp in its current role. Do not look for Muse inside it before a launch is announced, and do not mistake Meta AI for the agent. On launch day, WhatsApp onboarding may be the simplest way in, because the app and the account are already on your phone. Until then, treat WhatsApp as Meta AI's home. Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Italy?

The workaround tutorials are easy to find, in several languages, and they tell a consistent story: a VPN, a fresh email address, and a new account. Many claim the method works. This guide makes no such promise and does not recommend it. What follows is the honest picture, including an Italy specific footnote at the end.

The reports from users tell the story first. Logins have exposed the user's actual country in spite of the VPN, and waitlist screens have appeared even with a US server connected. The reason is where the gate sits: Muse checks location at the account layer, and a VPN changes only your IP address. The store region, the payment method, the device settings, and the account history all carry location signals as well. Success stories and waitlist stories both circulate, and neither proves the method works. Meta offers no supported route from Italy.

A workaround account can also be taken away. Meta may review an account, spot the location mismatch, and restrict or close it. That risk appears in coverage as a caution, not as a verified Meta enforcement policy, and Meta has not published a rule saying VPN signups are banned. The safe response is to treat a workaround account as disposable: no primary email address, no payment method you need, no work material. Everything in a closed account goes with it.

The first setting to change is data sharing. Under Settings, open Data controls and turn off "Help improve our AI models", tapping "Turn off" to confirm. That setting decides whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off means your chats are not used to train future models. It does not conceal your country, and the account risk stays exactly as it was. Change it anyway, on any account you ever open.

There is an Italy specific footnote to the VPN question. When OpenAI geoblocked ChatGPT in Italy in 2023, reporting at the time noted that a VPN offered a simple workaround for the block. That was a different product, a different block, and a different year. It does not mean a VPN reliably works for Muse today. The account layer gate Muse uses is harder to pass than a simple IP geoblock, and the account caution above still applies.

What will Muse cost in Italy?

What Muse costs in Italy today is nothing, because there is nothing to buy. No Italian price has been announced, and no euro figure exists in any source checked for this guide. A euro price in a post or a video should not be trusted. The pricing that exists is the reported global pricing: a free tier with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy usage capacity, not features, so the free tier gets the same agent with a smaller weekly allowance.

The numbers behind the tiers are reported, not published. Zuckerberg's launch statement put the free tier at 100 million tokens a week, and the same reporting puts Power at 500 million a week and Maximum at 3 billion a week. Meta's help pages do not publish those figures, so treat them as reported, not confirmed, even though they appear consistently across sources. What will launch day look like in Italy?

Without a date, the five launches so far are the template. This section applies their shape to Italy, with the caveat that Italy may differ.

The listing appears first. The App Store listing shows up in Italy, and the Google Play listing follows the same day. The app itself costs nothing to download. What signing up needs is a Meta account, an age check, and an age of 18 or over. If a Meta account from Facebook or Instagram already exists, it can be linked. One warning: use a single sign in method and do not switch. Switching sign in methods has left some users staring at an empty account, because the method used at sign in differed from the first one.

Launch day brings the code window with it. Redeem a code in Settings within 48 hours of account creation, and both sides get 1 billion tokens. One code per account, and codes are case sensitive. No cap figure is confirmed: September sources put it at 20 uses per code, November creators say 30, and Meta publishes no figure. Waitlist members receive their code on launch day. Everyone else can take one from the community pool on the homepage, and should redeem it the same day the account is created.

Start small on launch day. One app, one small job, one careful check: that is the safe start. The connections are what make Muse useful: Instagram, Gmail, Google Calendar, and dozens of other services, with Muse acting inside them once connected. Which connectors an Italian launch brings is unknown, but Japan and Mexico both launched with local connectors alongside the global ones. And before you connect anything at all, turn off the "Help improve our AI models" setting.

Expect a staged rollout, and expect Italian scrutiny. Features do not always appear on every account on day one. In the US, Voice took until November 5, two months after launch, to reach all users. Spaces launched November 6. Muse Mail launched November 10, for paid subscribers in the US and Canada only. An Italian launch would bring the core agent first, and newer features would follow on their own schedule. Given the Garante's history, also expect the launch to come with prominent data controls and a clear statement about training. Read them before you connect anything.
