Is Muse available in Sweden?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is available in exactly five countries. It launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5, 2026, its fifth market and its first in Latin America. Sweden is not on that list, and Meta has announced no date for Sweden or for any other EU country.

Sweden is named explicitly in the availability trackers. One country by country tracker answers the Sweden question directly: no, not as of November 2026, with Sweden in the same European position as the rest of the EU, meaning no availability and no announced launch date. A separate check of 22 App Stores on October 8, 2026, listed Sweden with the other EU countries as not listed, meaning Apple's App Store data had no Muse from Meta listing in the Swedish store. A Sweden focused analysis published September 28, 2026, put it plainly: Muse cannot be installed in Sweden.

Meta itself publishes no country list and no expansion timeline. Its Help Center describes Muse subscriptions as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, and it requires users to be located in a country where Muse operates. That wording is the closest thing to an official position on Sweden: not available, no date, no further detail. This guide is current as of November 13, 2026, and the status will change only when Meta says so.

What happens when you look for Muse in Sweden?

Open the App Store on an iPhone set to Sweden and search for Muse from Meta, and you will not find it. The listing exists in the US store, and it appeared in each new launch country as Meta added them, but the Swedish storefront has never carried it. The October 8 check of 22 storefronts confirmed the absence, and no source has reported a Swedish listing since. The same applies on Android: no source has reported a Google Play listing for Sweden either.

The web tells the same story one step later. The Muse marketing site loads fine from Sweden, and muse.ai opens normally. The block appears at the account step. Sign in from a Swedish account and you get the message Meta shows everywhere Muse is not live yet: Muse is not available in your country or region yet. We will let you know when it is. In an October 8 test from outside the launch countries, the sign in page showed a Join the waitlist for Muse prompt with that same message.

That sequence matters because it shows where the gate sits. The gate is at the account layer, not the web layer. Meta checks the country attached to your account when you sign in, not just the page you loaded. That is why the site looks open and the product stays closed, and why changing a browser setting or a network setting does not change the answer.

Why is Sweden still waiting? The EU regulation question

Meta has given no Sweden specific reason for the delay, and it has given no EU specific reason for Muse either. What exists is a pattern, and Sweden sits squarely inside it. Meta has repeatedly shipped AI products to the EU late, and the reasons it has named in the past are regulatory: data protection rules, and the uncertainty around them.

A technical report from the Centre for the Governance of AI, published in June 2026, measured the pattern across 375 model releases from Meta, Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic between June 2018 and May 2026. It found that Meta had the highest rate of delays and non releases of any company in the dataset, with about 26 percent of its releases delayed or withheld in the EU, against about 15 percent in the UK. Regulatory factors were the primary cause in 56 of the 68 delay cases, and data protection regulation, especially around training on personal data, was the main barrier. The same report found no strong evidence that the EU AI Act itself had caused delays so far, while noting that its rules for general purpose AI providers only became enforceable in August 2026.

The Meta AI precedent is the one Swedish readers will recognize. In July 2024, Meta said it would release its multimodal Llama model outside the EU but not inside it, citing uncertainty over European regulation. That decision came a month after the company paused plans to train its models on EU users public Facebook and Instagram posts, following concerns raised by Ireland's Data Protection Commission. Meta AI itself launched in the EU in March 2025, almost a year after that pause, and Meta only resumed training on EU public content in April 2025 after a European Data Protection Board opinion in December 2024.

Meta has said the quiet part out loud in Stockholm itself. Speaking at the Techarena conference in Stockholm on February 20, 2025, Meta public policy chief Chris Yiu said European regulation meant products get delayed or watered down, and that European consumers lose. He held up Meta's AI powered smart glasses as the example, saying the glasses took months longer to reach Europe than other regions because of regulatory hurdles. Sweden is not just inside the EU pattern. It is where Meta chose to complain about it.

The rules in play are the GDPR, which has governed personal data in the EU since 2018, and the EU AI Act, whose transparency obligations for chatbots and voice assistants took effect on August 2, 2026. Under Article 50, providers must make sure people know they are interacting with AI, no later than the first interaction. Muse is a harder case than a chatbot: it reads email, touches payments, and launched with health connectors, which means it has to satisfy data protection and AI rules before it can act for a user in Sweden. Meta has published no plan for either, and given how long Meta AI took in Europe, planning a project around a 2026 EU launch would be unwise.

None of this is Meta's stated reason for Muse in Sweden. It is the context that explains why Sweden waits while Mexico, Japan, and Singapore go live. The honest summary is the short one: no date, no statement, and a regulatory backdrop that has slowed every Meta AI product before this one.

What can you use in Sweden today?

Meta AI, the chat assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, is available in Sweden. It launched in the EU in March 2025, and it is the product most Swedes mean when they say they already use Meta's AI. It answers questions, drafts text, and generates images. It is not Muse. Muse is the standalone personal agent that carries out tasks, connects to your apps, and acts on your behalf. The two share a parent company and some technology, and they are different products with different availability.

For developers and technical users, more of the Muse family is reachable. Muse Spark, the model behind Muse, is available through the Meta Model API, and Muse Code is the coding tool built on the same family. A Sweden focused analysis from September 2026 concluded that the API route was the one piece usable in the EU on terms a Swedish data protection officer would sign, because it carries business data terms rather than consumer terms. Muse Glimmer, the open weights model in the same family, is available worldwide for anyone who wants to run it. These are developer tools, not the consumer app, and they do not give you the Muse agent, its connectors, or its token rewards.

For everyone else, the realistic today option is to learn the product from the outside: read the launch coverage, follow the feature announcements, and be ready with an account and a code when Sweden opens. The rest of this guide covers exactly that preparation.

Will Muse speak Swedish?

Meta has said nothing in its launch announcements about Swedish language support for Muse. No announcement names Swedish, no help page lists supported languages for the agent, and no Swedish launch exists to test against. Anyone telling you Muse will work in Swedish on day one is guessing.

What is known is that Muse is not English only. The Mexico launch on November 5, 2026, was a Spanish language launch, the first in Latin America, and Japan's launch was announced through Meta's Japanese newsroom. Meta localizes each market as it opens it. Whether a Swedish launch would include Swedish language support, or start in English, is unconfirmed, and Meta has given no signal either way.

The practical consequence is small. The demand from Sweden is already visible in English: the trackers, the analyses, and the questions Swedes ask about Muse are overwhelmingly in English, and the product's own interface in the launch countries is English first. If you can read this guide, you can use Muse in English when it arrives. Swedish support would be welcome, and it is not something to count on.

Can a VPN get you Muse in Sweden?

This is the most asked question from every unlaunched country, and the honest answer is that no method is promised to work, and every method carries risk. The region check happens at the account level when you sign in. A VPN changes the IP address a site sees. It does not change the country attached to your Meta account, your App Store region, or your payment card. Multiple guides report that Muse looks at account region and App Store country too, so a VPN often gets people to the sign in screen and stops there.

Meta's own account has warned about this directly. Replying to a user in Malaysia on Threads, Meta's official @meta.ai account wrote that you might get past the signup with a US VPN, but you would be bypassing the geo restriction, risking your account. That reply is the clearest official word on VPN use anywhere, and it applies to Sweden exactly as it applies to Malaysia. Treat account trouble as a real caution, not a verified certainty: Meta has said the risk exists, and it has not published a list of accounts it has acted against.

Getting the app itself is a second hurdle. The App Store listing appears only in launch countries, so a Swedish Apple account does not see it even with a VPN running. Downloading it from another country's store generally means an account in that country, with a local payment method and address. None of this is a recommendation. It is the reason VPN guides that promise easy access should be read with skepticism, and why waiting for the official launch is the only route that keeps your Meta account, the same account that holds your Facebook and Instagram, fully safe.

The privacy setting to turn off first

When you do get into Muse, do this before anything else. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off the Help improve our AI models setting. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off keeps your conversations out of training data, and it is the single most important privacy step for any new Muse user, in Sweden or anywhere else.

Swedish users have an extra reason to care. The GDPR gives EU residents rights over their personal data, and the whole history of Meta AI in Europe is a fight about exactly this: whether Meta may train on user content, and on what legal basis. The European Data Protection Board opinion of December 2024 affirmed Meta's approach for its existing products, but Muse is a new product with deeper access to your life than a chatbot: email, calendar, shopping, and health data if you connect those apps. The data sharing setting is your own control, separate from any regulator, and it takes seconds to switch off.

One caution, stated plainly. If you reach Muse from Sweden before it launches here, and Meta ever reviews your account and sees the mismatch between your location and your account, the account can get flagged or banned. That is a caution, not a verified fact about enforcement. Turning the data sharing setting off does not hide your location. It only keeps your chats out of training data.

What will Muse cost in Sweden?

Meta has announced no Swedish price for Muse, because Muse has not launched in Sweden. The prices that exist are the US ones, reported consistently across launch coverage: Free at $0 with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy usage capacity, not features, which means the free tier gets the same agent with a smaller weekly allowance.

The free tier's size comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, which put the free allowance at 100 million tokens a week. That figure is attributed to Zuckerberg, not to Meta's help pages, and Meta's help pages describe the free tier only as a weekly usage limit. Whether a Swedish launch would price in kronor, and at what level, is unconfirmed. The only local pricing reported anywhere is India, where The Hindu reported Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month on November 9, 2026, and that report is unconfirmed by Meta. Sweden has no equivalent report at all.

Do not pay anyone who offers to sell you Muse access for Sweden. Meta's own account has described offers selling early access as not official Meta offers. The real product, when it arrives, will be free to start, from Meta, through the App Store or muse.ai.

What launch day in Sweden would look like

Based on the five launches so far, a Swedish launch would follow a recognizable pattern. The App Store and Google Play listings would appear in Sweden, muse.ai/join would open for Swedish accounts, and Meta would announce it through its newsroom or through Alexandr Wang's account, as it did for Mexico. Each launch has also brought local connectors: Singapore launched with eight local partners including Shopee, Grab, and Singapore Airlines, and Mexico launched with local connectors of its own. No Swedish partner has been named, and no Swedish connector list exists, so what a Swedish launch would connect to is unknown.

Two newer features would also be in question. Meta says Spaces, launched November 6, 2026, is available everywhere Muse is available, so a Swedish launch should include Spaces from the start. Voice, which rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, is US only to start, and Meta has given no date for other countries. A Swedish launch day would answer the Spaces question in the affirmative and leave the Voice question open.

Until that day, the status is what it has been since September: no launch, no date, no statement. Check the App Store, check the newsroom, and ignore anyone selling certainty. When Sweden opens, this guide will be updated the same day.
