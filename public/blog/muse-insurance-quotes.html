How to compare insurance quotes with Muse

1. The short answer

Yes, and also no, and the two answers belong in the same sentence. As of October 25, 2026, Muse can compare insurance quotes, find identical coverage for less, buy the cheaper policy, and cancel the old one. Entrepreneur Joe Devoy posted on X on September 10, 2026 that he uploaded his auto policy, asked for identical coverage at a lower cost, and in about five minutes Muse found a policy saving him $3,500 a year, bought it, and cancelled his old one. That post was widely reported, including by Fast Company, Winbuzzer, and DNYUZ, and it is the best known insurance win Muse has produced. Muse can also refuse the job entirely. Ahmad Alkabra posted on X on October 16, 2026 that he tried an insurance prompt and Muse would not pose as a licensed insurance broker, giving safety and scope warnings instead of a quote. No quote, no policy change, no saving. Both posts are real. This guide shows you how to get the Devoy result and where the Alkabra wall stands.

The difference between the two cases is the ask. Devoy asked Muse to match an existing policy and find the same coverage cheaper, which is comparison shopping with a document in hand. Alkabra asked for something that reads as licensed broker work, and Muse declined to play that role. The practical rule is simple: bring your current policy, ask for identical coverage, and let Muse compare. Ask it to act as your broker, and expect a refusal. The rest of this guide covers the method that works, the wall that stops the other method, and the checks that keep a cheaper policy from being a worse policy.

Muse is available in the United States, Canada, and Japan, where it launched on October 23, 2026. Insurance is regulated state by state in the United States, so the quotes and the rules in this guide are American. Readers elsewhere can use the comparison method, but the licensing wall and the buying steps will follow local rules.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • Your current policy, as a file. Upload the declarations page at minimum: the page that lists your coverage limits, deductibles, and premium. The full policy is better. Without the document, Muse is guessing at what identical means.
  • Your driver and vehicle details. Drivers on the policy, dates of birth, driving history, the vehicle identification number, and where the car is parked overnight. Quotes change on every one of these, so have them ready.
  • Your address and garaging details. Rates are priced by location. A quote built on the wrong address is a quote that will not survive underwriting.
  • Approvals set to ask me. A policy purchase and a cancellation are money and legal decisions, so set approvals before you start. Our permissions guide is the prerequisite for everything on this page: read it first, set ask me, then ask for quotes.
  • A licensed human for the final call. Muse can compare and draft. It cannot act as your broker, and it cannot tell you whether a cheaper policy is right for your situation. Keep a real agent or the insurer direct line in the loop for the decision itself.

3. The method, step by step

Run the steps in order, and do not cancel the old policy until the new one is active. A gap in coverage is the one mistake that costs more than any saving.

  1. Upload the declarations page. Give Muse your current policy and point it at the declarations page: limits, deductibles, premium, and named drivers. Ask it to read the numbers back to you before it shops, so a misread does not become a wrong comparison.
  2. Ask for identical coverage, in words. Say the words identical coverage and list the limits and deductibles yourself. A quote that is cheaper because the liability limit dropped from 250,000 to 50,000 is not a saving. It is less insurance.
  3. Collect at least three quotes. Ask Muse to gather quotes from three or more insurers for the same coverage, and to show them in one table: insurer, premium, limits, deductibles, and exclusions. One quote proves nothing. Three quotes show the market.
  4. Compare more than the premium. Ask Muse to flag every difference between the quotes beyond price: deductible, liability limit, collision and comprehensive terms, rental coverage, roadside help, and exclusions. The cheapest quote with a $1,000 deductible is not cheaper than a pricier quote with a $250 deductible if you claim once.
  5. Check the insurer is licensed in your state. Ask Muse to confirm each insurer is licensed where you live, and check the state regulator site yourself for the winner. An unlicensed seller is a dealbreaker, whatever the price.
  6. Read the new policy before you buy. Ask Muse to summarize the new policy terms and flag anything that differs from your current one. Read the summary against the actual policy document. Approve the purchase yourself, and only after you have read it.
  7. Buy the new policy first. Confirm the new policy is active and you have proof of insurance in hand. Then, and only then, cancel the old one. Overlap of a day is cheap. A gap of a day can be expensive.
  8. Cancel the old policy and confirm the refund. Ask Muse to handle the cancellation and to confirm the effective date and any refund of unused premium. Save the cancellation confirmation and check the refund arrives. Keep both policies on file until the refund lands.

4. Where is the licensing wall

The wall is real, and Ahmad Alkabra hit it on October 16, 2026. He tried an insurance prompt copied from a use case account and received safety and scope warnings, including that Muse would not pose as a licensed insurance broker. No quote, no policy change, no saving. The use case catalog that records his post is careful about what it establishes: the refusal is reported, and nothing else is claimed. The lesson is not that Muse cannot help with insurance. It is that the help stops where licensed advice begins.

Insurance broking is a licensed activity in every United States state. A broker advises on coverage, recommends a policy, and acts for the buyer, and the license is what makes that advice accountable. Muse has no license, and Meta has built the refusal in rather than letting the agent improvise around it. That is why the Devoy style prompt works and the broker style prompt does not: comparison shopping with your own policy in hand is research, and research is allowed. Presenting as your broker is a regulated role, and the agent declines it.

The wall shows up in smaller ways too. Adebowale Ojo posted on X on October 22, 2026 that Muse called his insurer about a quote with deductible changes, and when the representative asked to speak to the account owner after recognizing an AI agent, Muse transferred the call to him. No quote or policy change was confirmed. The handoff is the pattern: Muse can do the legwork and the call, but the moment a human asks for the account owner, the agent steps aside. Mike Doan reported the opposite failure mode on October 15, 2026: Muse proactively supplied a local umbrella insurance rate comparison he had not requested. Unsolicited insurance advice is the thing the wall exists to prevent, and the fact that it leaked through once does not mean you should rely on it.

Work with the wall, not against it. Ask Muse to compare, explain terms in plain words, fill in forms, draft emails to agents, and make the calls that do not need a license. Ask a licensed agent or the insurer itself for the recommendation, the binding quote, and the final decision. If Muse ever offers broker style advice, treat it as a draft to check with a human, not as advice to act on. 6. What to watch for

Identical coverage is the phrase that decides whether a saving is real. Two policies with the same premium can differ in limits, deductibles, exclusions, and rental coverage, and a policy that is cheaper because the liability limit halved is not a bargain. Read the table in step three of the method, not the headline premium. If Muse reports a saving, ask it to list what changed besides the price, and check the list against the actual policy documents yourself.

Never let a gap open between policies. Buy the new policy, confirm it is active, hold proof of insurance, and only then cancel the old one. A single day without coverage can void a claim, break a loan or lease requirement, and raise your next quote, because insurers price continuous coverage. Overlap of a day costs almost nothing. A gap costs far more than the saving.

Watch for teaser rates and cancellation fees. A quote is not a renewal price, and the cheap first year can rise at renewal. Ask Muse to check whether the quoted premium is fixed for the term or an introductory rate. On the old policy, ask for the cancellation terms before you cancel: some policies refund unused premium in full, some charge a short rate fee, and some keep an earned premium minimum. The refund math belongs in the saving, not after it.

Check the insurer, not just the quote. Confirm the insurer is licensed in your state on the state regulator site, and check its claim service record before you switch. The cheapest insurer with a reputation for slow claims is a bad trade, and no agent can check that for you. Lowpass made the underlying point in its October 22, 2026 piece on AI agents and subscriptions: nobody wants to lose home or health insurance because a buggy agent decided you would save money without it. The same applies to a cheaper policy that leaves you exposed. The agent finds the price. You judge the protection.

Finally, remember the licensing wall from section four. If Muse declines a prompt, do not reword it until it complies. The refusal is the safety feature working. Take the comparison Muse will do, and take the advice from a licensed human. If the wall keeps stopping you, the broker you call is the answer, not a cleverer prompt.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muse really buy an insurance policy for me?
Joe Devoy reports that it did exactly that for his auto policy in about five minutes. The purchase still needs your approval, and you should read the new policy before you approve it. No purchase happens without your tap if approvals are set to ask me.
What does identical coverage actually mean?
The same limits, the same deductibles, the same drivers, and the same exclusions. A cheaper quote with a lower limit or a higher deductible is less insurance, not a saving. Compare the full table, not the headline premium.
Why did Muse refuse my insurance prompt?
Acting as a licensed insurance broker is a regulated role, and Muse will not pose as one. Ask it to compare quotes for identical coverage instead, and take the recommendation from a licensed agent or the insurer itself.
Will there be a gap in my coverage when I switch?
Not if you buy first and cancel second. Confirm the new policy is active and you hold proof of insurance before you cancel the old one. Overlap of a day is cheap, and a gap can be expensive.
How do I check an insurer is licensed?
Check the state regulator site for your state, which lists every licensed insurer. Ask Muse to confirm the license too, but treat the regulator site as the source of truth.
Can Muse call my insurer for me?
It can make the call and handle the phone menu, but the moment a representative asks for the account owner, it hands the call to you. Adebowale Ojo reported exactly that handoff on October 22, 2026. Stay near your phone when it calls.
Does a cheaper quote stay cheap at renewal?
Not always. A quote can be an introductory rate that rises at renewal. Ask Muse to check whether the quoted premium is fixed for the term, and compare renewal terms, not just the first year price.
What if the saving comes with a higher deductible?
Then it is a trade, not a pure saving. A $1,000 deductible instead of $250 saves premium every year but costs you $750 more on the one claim you make. Run the claim math before you accept it.
