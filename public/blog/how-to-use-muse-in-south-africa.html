How to Use Muse in South Africa

Muse has not launched in South Africa, and Meta has announced no date for it. South African tech press has covered the agent closely since launch, and Meta smart glasses are officially coming to the country, but the agent itself is still closed to South African accounts. This guide covers what happens when you try, what the glasses arrival does and does not mean, and what South African businesses can do today.

Is Muse available in South Africa?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. South Africa is not on that list, and Meta has published no South Africa launch date, no timeline for Africa, and no country list that names South Africa as coming next. The full list of countries where Muse is live tracks every launch, and no African country has appeared on it.

South African readers got the answer from local press within weeks of launch. On October 2, 2026, Dannywired reported that Meta listed Muse as available in the United States and Canada, and warned South African buyers not to expect Muse to arrive with the glasses. TechCentral reported the same day that Muse was available in the US and Canada, with the glasses announcement saying Muse on glasses would be available in the US only at first. The country count in those reports is now out of date, since Japan, Singapore, and Mexico have launched since, but the South Africa finding in them is unchanged: no launch, no date. You can read them in the Dannywired report and the TechCentral report.

The storefront record backs the press reports. An independent check of Apple's App Store in 22 countries on October 8, 2026 found "Muse from Meta" listed in the US and Canada stores only, with South Africa among the stores where it did not appear. The check is documented in the October 8 availability check. A tracker updated on October 31, 2026 then grouped Nigeria, South Africa, Morocco, and Egypt under a single line: no launch announced. Two more countries launched between those two checks, and neither was in Africa.

Why are South Africans asking about Muse?

South Africa has followed Muse more closely than most unlaunched countries, and the local coverage tells you why. Within days of each Muse event, a South African outlet had written up what it meant locally: the launch itself, the Shopify checkout news, and the glasses announcement that named South Africa. Readers in Johannesburg, Cape Town, and Durban are reading about an agent that books travel, answers mail, and shops on request, and they want to know when they can use it.

Much of the interest is commercial. South Africa has a large community of online sellers, freelancers, and small business owners who already run their work through WhatsApp, Instagram, and Shopify. Those are the exact surfaces Muse is built around. When Shopify switched on agentic checkout for Muse in September 2026, a South African agency wrote up the local meaning within days. When the glasses announcement named South Africa, three local outlets covered it the same week. This is not idle curiosity. It is business owners asking whether a new tool will reach them.

Interest does not move the schedule. Meta has not said that demand in a country speeds up its launch, and it has not named South Africa in any launch communication. The rest of this guide keeps the two things apart: what is confirmed, and what is only hoped for.

What would Muse do for a South African user?

Think of Muse as a worker you instruct in plain words, not as a search box. You tell it the outcome you want, a flight booked, a supplier chased, an inbox cleared, a price tracked, and it goes and does the work across the apps you connect. Before anything that matters, sending a message, publishing a post, spending money, it stops and asks you. That approval rule is the core of the product: the agent acts, but you stay in charge of every consequential step.

For a seller, the picture is concrete. Muse for Small Business, announced on September 29, 2026, connects the agent to the tools a small business already runs: Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Canva, Notion, Slack, and the rest of the connector list, plus Facebook Pages, Instagram analytics, and Meta ad accounts. The agent drafts campaigns, reads sales history, and flags odd expenses, and nothing publishes or spends without approval. None of that is open to South African businesses yet, because the business offer follows the same country map as the consumer agent. The connector list is tracked in the Muse connectors list.

For everyone else, the entry point is simpler. Muse costs nothing to start, with a weekly usage limit that refreshes. The two paid tiers, Power at $20 a month and Maximum at $100 a month, raise the weekly allowance. They do not unlock new features, and they do not unlock new countries. If you are new to the product, the full explainer on what Muse is covers the agent, the tokens, and the invite codes in one place. No South African price exists, because there is no South African launch to price.

What happens when you try from South Africa?

The clearest record of the South African experience is a sign in test. On October 8, 2026, testers signing in from outside the US and Canada were shown a waitlist screen carrying the message "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet." That screen is what a South African account meets today: not an error, not a crash, but a deliberate stop at the account step, with a promise of notice when the country opens. The test is documented in the October 8 availability check.

Before you even reach sign in, the app itself is missing. The South African App Store does not list "Muse from Meta," and searching for Muse returns lookalike apps that borrow the name. The check that matters is the seller name: Meta's listing names Meta Platforms, Inc. as the seller. Any listing with a different seller is a different app, and installing it will not open a path into Muse. The same applies on Google Play: if the listing is absent from the South African storefront, the product has not launched here.

Every surface enforces the same rule. The iPhone app, the Android app, the Mac app, the web at muse.ai, and the Muse chat inside WhatsApp all answer to the country on the account. Changing phones, changing browsers, or opening a different app does not change the answer, because the answer comes from the account, not the device in your hand.

One nuance is worth knowing for launch day. The surfaces have shipped on slightly different schedules in launched countries, so a block on one surface has not always meant a block on all of them. From South Africa today that nuance changes nothing, since all surfaces are closed. But when launch day comes, check more than one surface before concluding anything: the app, the web, and WhatsApp may not all open in the same hour.

Meta smart glasses are coming. Does that mean Muse is coming?

No, and this is the most important distinction in this guide. Meta's smart glasses and Meta's Muse agent are two separate products on two separate schedules. South Africa is named for the glasses. South Africa is not named for the agent.

The glasses news is real. At Meta Connect on September 23, 2026, Meta announced its biggest expansion of smart glasses styles to date, built with its eyewear partner EssilorLuxottica. A footnote to Meta's announcement listed 11 markets where its smart glasses lines would be available later in 2026, and South Africa was on that list, alongside Turkey, Poland, Portugal, Israel, Indonesia, Thailand, Hong Kong, New Zealand, Malaysia, and the Philippines. EssilorLuxottica confirmed the same expansion the next day, saying its smart glasses would expand into South Africa as part of a wider retail rollout.

What the announcement left out matters as much as what it included. Meta confirmed no exact South African launch date, no local price, and no list of retailers. TechCentral asked Meta for exact dates, local pricing, retail partners, and whether all features would be available in South Africa, and reported that it would update the story when Meta responded. South African retailer Evetech already lists the glasses from about R10,745, but those are grey imports with warranty support handled through the retailer, not an official Meta retail footprint. Do not treat an Evetech listing as a launch.

The Muse part of the glasses story points the other way. The joint announcement said Muse on the glasses would initially be available in the US only. Dannywired put it plainly: South African buyers should not expect Muse to arrive with the glasses immediately. TechCentral said the same: one feature local buyers should not expect immediately is Muse. So even when the glasses reach South African shelves, the Muse experience on them is not promised on day one.

Two more limits complete the picture. The cheaper Meta Glasses collection is launching first in Singapore, South Korea, Mexico, and the UAE, with Brazil to follow, and South Africa was not named for that collection. And the audio only glasses, the VR glasses, and the Muse Charm companion device were announced with no African rollout plan at all. If you are tracking the glasses as a sign of a Muse launch, you are tracking the wrong product. For the agent on glasses generally, see the guide to Muse on smart glasses.

What about South African businesses and Shopify stores?

South African businesses cannot use Muse for Small Business yet. Meta announced that offer on September 29, 2026, and it follows the same map as the consumer agent: the US and Canada at announcement, with Japan, Singapore, and Mexico added to the agent since. No African country is on the map, and Meta has named no date for one.

The Shopify story is closer, but it still stops short. On September 21, 2026, Shopify switched on agentic checkout with Shop Pay for Muse across every Shopify store, a day after Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on its site. A South African agency covered it the same month and answered the local question directly: South African Shopify stores cannot sell through Muse yet, because Muse is only available to shoppers in launched countries. Shopify's position, as quoted in that coverage, is that when its AI partners expand to more regions, its merchants will be ready by default. You can read it in the Subliminal report on Muse and Shopify.

One part already reaches South African stores. Any Shopify store that sells to buyers in the US can have its products shown in ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot, wherever the store is based, through Shopify's Agentic Storefronts. For a South African brand that ships abroad, AI agents are already a live sales channel, just not through Muse yet. The practical step for a store owner is to keep product data clean and complete, so that when Muse reaches South African shoppers, or when US shoppers browse through Muse, the store is readable by the agent.

Do not buy a tool or a course that promises to put your South African store inside Muse today. No such path exists. The store side is ready by default through Shopify when the shopper side arrives, and the shopper side has no date.

Has Meta ever put South Africa first in Africa?

Yes, for hardware. South Africa was the only African country in the new wave of smart glasses markets Meta named at Connect on September 23, 2026. No other African market received any of Meta's other new hardware in that announcement: the audio only glasses, the VR headset, and the Muse Charm companion device were announced with no African rollout plan at all. That makes South Africa Meta's first African market for camera equipped smart glasses, and the clearest signal of where the company sees its eyewear business heading on the continent.

That signal is about glasses, not about the agent, and the two must not be merged. Meta AI, the chat assistant inside WhatsApp, reached South Africa as part of an expansion to seven African countries south of the Sahara within roughly a year of its US launch, and that history is sometimes cited as a reason Muse will follow. It is background, not a commitment. Muse is a separate product with its own app, its own account gate, and its own rollout, and Meta has announced no Africa date for it. The Meta AI history is described in a report on Muse and WhatsApp in Africa published on October 13, 2026.

Read the two signals together and the honest summary is this: Meta knows the South African market well enough to name it first for hardware, and well enough to have brought Meta AI here early. Neither fact is a Muse launch date. Watch for an announcement, not for a pattern.

Why is there no date for South Africa, or for Africa at all?

Start with what Meta has actually said about Africa and Muse: nothing. No African country appears in any Muse launch announcement, no African country is named on any Muse availability page, and no Meta executive has named an African market in any statement about expansion. The silence is continent wide, not South Africa specific. Every launch so far has been announced on its own, with no published queue behind it, and Africa has not been queued in public at all.

The contrast with hardware is instructive. For glasses, Meta published a footnote naming 11 markets, including South Africa, months ahead of availability. For the agent, Meta has published no equivalent list, no footnote, and no forward naming of any kind. The company communicates the two rollouts differently, and the agent rollout is the closed one. That is why glasses news cannot be read as agent news.

The nearest thing to an explanation for the pace came from Mark Zuckerberg on Meta's earnings call on October 21, 2026. He described Muse as expensive in compute to run, and said expansion waits on two things: running the agent more efficiently, and adapting it country by country, connectors included. That is a general account of a slow rollout, not a South Africa reason, and it names no next country. It does explain why launches arrive one at a time instead of in a wave.

Ignore any page that gives you a South Africa date. If the date is not on a Meta page, it is a guess dressed as news. This guide will update the day Meta announces something real.

Why a VPN does not settle the South Africa question

Three separate signals decide whether Muse lets an account in, and a VPN touches only one of them. The first is the region on your app store account, which is set in your Apple ID or Google account, not by your connection. The second is the country on your Meta account, which is a setting plus account history. The third is the IP address you appear from at sign in. A proxy or VPN changes the third signal only. It leaves the store region and the account country exactly where they were. That three signal picture is set out in the proxycove breakdown of how Meta decides your country, published in November 2026.

That is why the familiar advice fails here. Changing your network does not change your store, and changing your store does not change your Meta account. An account created in South Africa, with a South African store region, stays a South African account behind any VPN. The same breakdown notes the one case where a proxy does help: an account created in a launched country, used while traveling somewhere Muse is not available, where the proxy resolves a conflict between the IP and the other signals. That is a traveler's fix, not a launch workaround, and it does not apply to an account created in South Africa.

Then there is the risk to the account itself. The terms say you must be in a country where Muse is available. An account whose three signals point in different directions is exactly the kind of account those terms describe, and Meta could flag or ban it. No published case involves a South African account, so this stays a caution, not a proven pattern. The asymmetry is what matters: you keep the account only as long as nobody looks closely, and everything inside it goes if they do.

Two complete guides on this site walk through the workaround attempts in detail, including the error screens and the fixes people try. Your first job on launch day: one privacy switch

Before the first task, before the first connection, set the privacy switch. In the Muse app, open Settings, then Data controls, and find the switch labeled "Help improve our AI models." Toggle it off and confirm "Turn off" when asked. From that point on, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models. Your chats stay out of training data.

Pair that switch with the approval habit and you have the two controls that matter most in the app. Approvals decide what the agent may do in the world. The data switch decides what Meta may do with your words. Both are set by you, both cost nothing, and both are worth setting before the agent has any history to learn from.

Do not confuse the switch with a way in. It changes nothing about availability, nothing about the country on your account, and nothing about what the agent can do for you. It is purely a choice about your chats, and it is the one to make first because it governs everything the agent hears from the start.

How you will know Muse has launched in South Africa

Ignore the false signal first. Glasses on shelves, glasses in local advertising, and glasses reviews in South African press are all hardware news. None of them means the agent has launched. The glasses announcement itself said Muse on glasses starts in the US only, so even a full glasses launch leaves the agent question open.

A real launch shows up as a set, not a single clue. Meta or the Muse account posts the country's name. The store listing goes live under Meta Platforms, Inc. And sign in starts working for local accounts on muse.ai. The five launches so far each showed that shape. One element alone, a rumor, a screenshot, a listing without sign in, proves nothing. The set proves the launch.

You can run the store check yourself in under a minute. Apple's lookup pages are public: take the US listing address for "Muse from Meta" and replace the us part with za, the South Africa country code. A page that says the app is not found means the launch has not started. A page that loads with the listing means it likely has, though sign in can lag behind the listing because the account check is separate.

Do not expect a date in advance. Meta has not announced any launch date ahead of time so far, and there is no reason to expect South Africa to be the exception. The announcement and the opening have arrived together, or days apart. The waitlist on this page is how you hear about it the same day it happens.
