How to Use Muse in Hong Kong

Is Muse available in Hong Kong?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is available in exactly five countries. It launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5, 2026, its fifth market and its first in Latin America. Hong Kong is not on that list, and Meta has announced no Hong Kong launch date.

Hong Kong reviewers verified the absence directly. A Hong Kong review verified on October 24, 2026, reported that Muse is open only in the US, Canada, and Japan, that it is not on the Hong Kong App Store, and that Meta has announced no date. The same review noted that there is no Hong Kong listing and therefore no Hong Kong rating for the app. A second Hong Kong focused review, also verified October 24, 2026, reached the identical conclusion: no Muse on the Hong Kong App Store, Meta's help centre says Muse is not available everywhere yet, and there is no word on Hong Kong. The availability trackers agree, grouping Malaysia, Hong Kong, and Taiwan together as no launch announced as of October 31, 2026.

Meta itself publishes no country list and no expansion timeline. Its Help Center describes Muse subscriptions as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, and it requires users to be located in a country where Muse operates. Hong Kong has received no official statement of its own. What it has, uniquely, is a policy barrier documented in Meta's own terms, covered in the section below.

What Hong Kong reviewers found

The Hong Kong coverage of Muse is unusually thorough, because Hong Kong's tech press checked the facts instead of repeating the launch headlines. The Perk review, verified October 24, 2026, walked through the position market by market: the US launch on September 8, the Canadian App Store listing, and the Japan launch on October 23, described by Meta as its first market outside North America. It then stated the Hong Kong position plainly: as of October 24, 2026, the Muse app is not available on the Hong Kong App Store, Meta's help centre states that Muse is not yet offered in all locations, and no Hong Kong launch date has been announced.

The review also answered the question Hong Kong readers ask first: does the Japan launch help? Its answer was no. The Japanese launch is of no practical help to Hong Kong residents, because the app is listed only in the American, Canadian, and Japanese stores, and Meta's subscription guidance requires users to be in a country where Muse is offered. The same logic now extends to Singapore and Mexico: each new launch helps its own market, and none of them extends access to Hong Kong.

A second Hong Kong review, from a deals focused site, added the referral code angle that Hong Kong searchers keep hitting. It published a Muse invite code and told readers that on the day Muse opens in Hong Kong, they should join and redeem the code within 48 hours. The advice is correct about the mechanics and honest about the timing: the code is useless until Hong Kong opens, because there is no account to redeem it on. Hong Kong residents, the review concluded, can neither register nor claim the referral reward at present. Any code offer that promises otherwise is selling something that cannot be used yet.

The restriction that goes deeper: Meta's Geographic Use Policy

Hong Kong faces a barrier that Sweden, Malaysia, Bangladesh, Vietnam, and Taiwan do not. Meta's Geographic Use Policy, last updated October 12, 2026, lists restricted territories where its AI services are not available, and that list includes China, including Hong Kong and Macau. The policy is documented in an analysis of Meta's Muse Wonder API setup, which quotes the restricted territories list in full: Afghanistan, Belarus, Cambodia, China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Cuba, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Haiti, Iran, Iraq, Libya, Myanmar, Nicaragua, North Korea, Pakistan, Russia, Somalia, Syria, the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions, Venezuela, and Western Sahara. The same analysis notes that Meta's help center says that if you travel to one of these territories, you will not be able to log in.

Precision matters here, because two different restrictions are in play and they must not be confused. The Geographic Use Policy governs the Meta Model API and Muse Wonder, the developer facing products, and it is the policy that names Hong Kong explicitly. The consumer Muse app has its own availability, governed by its launch list and its Help Center wording, and the consumer app has simply not launched in Hong Kong. Hong Kong is therefore blocked twice over: once by the absence of a consumer launch, and once by a written policy that names the territory for the API products. A second analysis from the same source makes the distinction explicit: a reader in the UK or Spain cannot subscribe to the Muse app yet and is not on the restricted list, while Hong Kong is on the list.

What this means for a Hong Kong reader is sobering but important. For most unlaunched countries, the question is when Meta will get around to launching. For Hong Kong, there is an additional written policy to be reversed or worked around before any launch could happen, and Meta has said nothing about reversing it. This guide does not predict that Hong Kong will never get Muse. It states the documented position: no launch, no date, and a policy that names Hong Kong as restricted. That is a harder position than any other country in this wave, and Hong Kong readers deserve to know it plainly.

English, Cantonese, and the language question

Hong Kong is a bilingual city, and that shapes what a launch here would need. English is an official language, widely used in business, law, and tech, and Cantonese is the everyday spoken language of most residents, with written Chinese used across formal and informal life. A product that works in English works for a large share of Hong Kong users on day one, and the Hong Kong coverage of Muse, from the Perk review to the deals sites, is written in English.

What Meta has actually said about language support for Muse is limited. The Mexico launch on November 5, 2026, was a Spanish language launch, the first in Latin America, which proves Muse is not English only. Japan's launch was announced through Meta's Japanese newsroom. No announcement names Cantonese or written Chinese support for Muse, and no announcement names any language roadmap at all. Whether a Hong Kong launch, if one ever comes, would include Cantonese or Chinese language support is unconfirmed.

The honest position is that language is not the blocker for Hong Kong. The blockers are availability and policy, both documented above. When and if Meta opens Hong Kong, English will work, and Cantonese and Chinese support will be questions for launch day. Until then, any claim about Muse speaking Cantonese is speculation, and this guide will not present speculation as fact.

What Hong Kong businesses are doing instead

Hong Kong's business press asked the practical question early: can a Hong Kong company use Muse for Small Business? The answer, from a Hong Kong business guide published in early October 2026, was not officially. As of early October 2026, Muse was available only to adults aged 18 and over in the United States and Canada, Meta said it would add more countries in a measured way but had given no dates, and a Hong Kong company could not sign up through normal channels. Muse for Small Business, launched September 29, 2026, with connectors for Shopify, QuickBooks, Slack, Stripe, and Meta's own Facebook and Instagram business tools, uses the same availability as the consumer app, and Hong Kong is outside it.

The same guide compared the alternatives actually available in Hong Kong. Google Gemini began rolling out natively to Hong Kong users on March 16, 2026, the first major US assistant to do so, according to the Digital in Asia access tracker. Microsoft Copilot is sold to Hong Kong businesses through Microsoft 365. ChatGPT, by contrast, is not on OpenAI's list of supported countries for Hong Kong, according to the same tracker. The guide's conclusion is worth quoting in substance: the restriction comes from the providers, not from the Hong Kong government. Hong Kong has not banned these products. The companies have chosen not to offer them here.

For a Hong Kong business owner, the practical advice is to use what is available today rather than wait for Muse. Gemini for offices that live in Gmail, Copilot for offices on Outlook and Excel, and local Chinese AI chat apps for simple drafting are all working options in Hong Kong right now. Muse for Small Business remains, in the guide's words, a strong idea that Hong Kong cannot buy yet. When that changes, this guide will say so.

Can a VPN get you Muse in Hong Kong?

No method is promised to work, and every method carries risk, with an extra Hong Kong specific warning attached. The region check happens at the account level when you sign in. A VPN changes the IP address a site sees. It does not change the country attached to your Meta account, your App Store region, or your payment card. Guides that have studied the signup report that Muse looks at account region and App Store country too, so a VPN often gets people to the sign in screen and stops there.

Meta's own account has warned about this directly. Replying to a user in Malaysia on Threads, Meta's official @meta.ai account wrote that you might get past the signup with a US VPN, but you would be bypassing the geo restriction, risking your account. That reply was written for Malaysia, and it applies to Hong Kong exactly as it applies everywhere else. On top of that, Hong Kong's position on the Geographic Use Policy restricted list means the policy barrier is written down, not just implied by the launch list, and Meta's help center says travelers to restricted territories cannot log in.

Treat account trouble as a caution, not a verified certainty. Meta has said bypassing the restriction risks your account, and it has not published a list of accounts it has acted against. What is certain is narrower: no method is promised to work, methods that work today may stop working at any time, and the App Store listing appears only in launch countries. For Hong Kong, where the policy barrier is explicit, waiting is not just the safe route. It is the only route that does not fight a written policy.

The privacy setting to turn off first

When you do get into Muse, do this before anything else. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off the Help improve our AI models setting. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off keeps your conversations out of training data, and it is the single most important privacy step for any new Muse user, in Hong Kong or anywhere else.

The setting matters more for Muse than for a chatbot, because Muse sees more of your life. Connect Gmail and it reads your email. Connect a calendar and it knows your schedule. Connect shopping and it knows what you buy. For Hong Kong users, who may connect business tools and cross border accounts, the exposure is larger still. The data sharing switch is your own control over whether those conversations feed future models, and it takes seconds to switch off. Do it on day one, before your first real task, not after a month of chats.

One caution, stated plainly. If you reach Muse from Hong Kong before it launches here, and Meta ever reviews your account and sees the mismatch between your location and your account, the account can get flagged or banned. That is a caution, not a verified fact about enforcement. Turning the data sharing setting off does not hide your location. It only keeps your chats out of training data.

What will Muse cost in Hong Kong?

Meta has announced no Hong Kong price for Muse, because Muse has not launched in Hong Kong. The prices that exist are the US ones, reported consistently across launch coverage: Free at $0 with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy usage capacity, not features, which means the free tier gets the same agent with a smaller weekly allowance. The Hong Kong business guide cited above uses the same US prices for its comparison, converting at HK$7.8 to US$1 for illustration.

The free tier's size comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, which put the free allowance at 100 million tokens a week. That figure is attributed to Zuckerberg, not to Meta's help pages, and Meta's help pages describe the free tier only as a weekly usage limit. Whether a Hong Kong launch would price in Hong Kong dollars, and at what level, is unconfirmed. The only local pricing reported anywhere is India, where The Hindu reported Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month on November 9, 2026, and that report is unconfirmed by Meta. Hong Kong has no equivalent report.

Do not pay anyone who offers to sell you Muse access for Hong Kong. Meta's own account has described offers selling early access as not official Meta offers, and the Hong Kong reviews are explicit that residents can neither register nor claim rewards at present. The real product, if it ever arrives, will be free to start, from Meta, through the App Store, Google Play, or muse.ai.
