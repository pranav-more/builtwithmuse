How to Use the Philips Hue Connector in Muse

What is the short answer?

As of October 25, 2026, Philips Hue is a live connector in Muse. It is one of the 54 connectors counted in the web app in the October 15 in app check, with about 58 to 60 live in total, counting phone only connectors, and 18 announced but not live. The October 7 update brought the iPad app and an expanded connector list, and Muse launched in Japan on October 23, so Muse is now available in the United States, Canada, and Japan.

Here is the quotable answer. Connect Philips Hue and your lights answer plain words: a wind down scene at night, bright light for focus, and everything off when you leave. Philips Hue is a system of smart bulbs, lamps, and fixtures run from the Hue app, with a Bridge at the heart of the system or Bluetooth for a smaller setup, and the app already handles on and off, scenes, timers, automations, and syncing lights to TV and music. The connector is supposed to bring those lights into Muse so you can run the house by talking instead of opening the app.

Here is the honest version, and it matters. Meta has not published a detailed list of what the Philips Hue connector can read or change inside Muse, so the uses in this guide are the reasonable ones to try first, not promises. We could not verify in October whether Muse can see your rooms and scenes, change brightness and colour, run automations, or only turn lights on and off. Test every use below with one room first, check the result with your own eyes, and trust only what you have seen work with your own account. What do you need?

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • A Philips Hue setup that already works. The connector talks to Hue, so your lights must already work in the Hue app, with rooms named and scenes saved, before Muse can do anything useful with them. Fix the Hue app first, then connect Muse.
  • Clear room and light names. Name each room and each light so a sentence can point at it: living room, bedroom, desk, kitchen. A light called Light 3 is a light Muse cannot find by name.
  • Your scenes in plain words. Know the scenes you actually use: wind down, focus, movie night, bright morning. One clear name per scene beats ten scenes you never open.
  • Approvals set to ask me. Before you connect anything, set approvals so nothing is changed without your tap. This is the single most important step on this page.

How do you connect it, step by step?

The connection itself takes a few minutes once Hue already works. The naming work before it matters more, because a connector cannot find a light you never named, so run the steps in order and test with one room first.

  1. Set up Hue in the Hue app first. Add your Bridge, add your lights, put them in rooms, and save the scenes you use. Test each room from the app and fix any light that does not answer before you bring Muse into it.
  2. Name every room and light in plain words. Living room, bedroom, desk, kitchen, hallway. Rename anything called Light 3 or Bulb 2, because a sentence cannot point at a number it does not know. Do this in the Hue app, not in Muse.
  3. Open Muse and go to Settings. The connectors list lives there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  4. Find Philips Hue in the connectors list and open it. If Philips Hue is not listed, your account has not received it yet. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Check again in a few days.
  5. Sign in with your Hue account. Use the account where your Hue home lives. If your household shares one Hue home, sign in with that home, because Muse should see the lights everyone uses.
  6. Grant the access Muse asks for. Read the permission screen before you accept it. If a scope surprises you, stop and check what it covers before continuing.
  7. Test with one room and your own eyes. Ask Muse to turn off the living room lights, then look at the living room. If the lights answer, the connector can reach your home. If they do not, check the Hue app first, because the fault is usually in Hue, not in Muse.

What does it actually do?

The connector earns its place with five uses. Everything else is a variation on one of them. Each use below starts with a test, because the connector details are not published, and a use you have tested beats a use you assumed. Our complete connectors list tracks which connectors are live now and which are only announced, so check there if a connector you want is missing from your settings.

How does one sentence set the wind down scene?

Ask Muse for wind down in the living room, and let the lights dim warm and low without you opening the app. This is the highest value use, because the end of the day is when you are least willing to fiddle with controls, and a house that settles itself is a house that helps you sleep. Name the scene, name the room, and stop there: one sentence, one scene, no list of options to choose from. If the connector cannot see your scenes yet, the request will fail in a way you can see at once, and you should run the scene from the Hue app and tell Muse the scene name again. A scene that works from the app but not from Muse is a naming problem, not a broken home, and renaming the scene in plain words usually fixes it.

How does bright light help you focus?

Ask Muse for bright light at your desk when work starts, and dim warm light when work ends. Light is the cheapest focus tool in the house: bright cool light says work, warm dim light says rest, and the shift between them tells your body what the hour is for. Ask for the change at the start of the work block, not in the middle of it, because a light change mid task is a distraction, not a help. If Muse cannot set brightness or colour through the connector, ask it to turn the desk light on and set the scene in the Hue app, which takes ten seconds and works every time. The pairing matters more than the path: the light should change when the work changes, however the change is made.

What happens to the lights when you leave?

Ask Muse to turn everything off when you leave the house. This is the quiet use that saves power and ends the argument about who left the kitchen light on. One sentence at the door beats a walk through every room, and a house that is dark when it should be dark is a house that is not wasting power all day. Tell Muse which lights stay on if any do: the porch light, the hallway light, the one that keeps the house looking lived in. If Muse cannot reach a light, the Hue app will show it as unreachable, and the fault is usually a bulb that lost power or a Bridge that lost the network, not a Muse problem. Check the app, fix the bulb, and try again.

How does movie night work with guests?

Ask Muse for movie night in the living room when guests sit down, and let the lights drop low and warm while the TV takes over. This is the use that shows off the system, because a room that changes with one sentence feels like the future, and a host who fiddles with an app for two minutes while guests wait feels like the past. Set the scene before guests arrive, test it once, and then trust it for the evening. Hue can also sync lights to TV and music through its Sync apps, and that is a Hue feature to set up in the Hue app, not a thing to ask Muse to invent. If the connector cannot run scenes, run the scene from the Hue app and enjoy the evening anyway: the scene is the point, not the path to it.

How should mornings start with light?

Ask Muse to bring the bedroom up gently in the morning, or to brighten the kitchen while coffee brews. Mornings go better when light arrives before the phone does, and a house that wakes up with you is a gentler start than an alarm and a screen. Keep the morning scene soft and slow, because bright light at full power in a dark room is a shock, not a wake up. Pair it with your calendar, and our Google Calendar connector guide covers that half, so the light follows the day: early on meeting days, slower on weekends. If Muse cannot run timed scenes through the connector, set the timer in the Hue app, where timers and automations already live, and let Muse handle the requests you make in the moment.

What can it not do?

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector. The first limit is the one this whole guide keeps repeating, because it shapes everything else.

  • It cannot promise what Meta has not published. We could not verify in October whether Muse can see your rooms and scenes, change brightness and colour, run automations, or only turn lights on and off. Treat every use above as a test, and trust only what you have seen work with your own account.
  • It cannot fix a light that Hue cannot reach. A bulb with no power, a Bridge off the network, or a light in a dead spot is unreachable for Muse too. Check the Hue app first, because the fault is almost always in the home, not in the connector.
  • It cannot guess a name you never gave. A light called Light 3 is a light Muse cannot find by name, and a scene with no name is a scene Muse cannot run. Name things in plain words in the Hue app, and the connector gets dramatically more useful.
  • It cannot keep the house safe on its own. Lights are not locks, cameras, or alarms, and a lighting scene is not a security system. Do not ask Muse to make the house look occupied while you travel and then trust the lights alone.
  • It cannot change lights without your approval when approvals are set right. That is a feature, not a bug. If you turn approvals off, you lose the safety net, so keep them on for anything that changes more than one room at a time.

What should you watch for?

As of October 25, 2026, Philips Hue is a settled connector in the list: one of the 54 connectors in the web app in the October 15 in app check. There is no October change to how it works, so the habits around it still matter. Test every scene with your own eyes for the first week, especially the ones that change more than one room. A wrong room acted on fast is worse than a slow read you verified, and a bedroom lit bright at midnight is a night lost.

Watch for the household that did not agree. Lights are shared, and a scene that one person loves is a scene another person hates, especially when it changes a room someone is already using. Agree the scene names and the night time rules together, and keep a simple way for anyone to turn a room back to normal without opening a chat: a wall switch, a Hue dimmer, or the app. The best smart home is one a guest can use without instructions, and a house that only works through Muse is a house that fails the moment Muse is not there.

Check your data controls. In Muse settings, open Data controls and look at the switch for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to run your home. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models.

Finally, remember the rollout is staged. Your account may see a different connector list from a friend in the same city on the same day, and a use that works for them may not work for you yet. That is normal for a launch this young, and it is not a thing you broke. Check your own settings, test with one room first, and build the habit on what your account can actually do today.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muse turn my Philips Hue lights on and off?
That is the basic use, but we could not verify the details in October. Test with one room first, and look at the room with your own eyes before you trust it.
Can Muse run my Hue scenes?
We could not verify this in October. Meta has not published whether the connector can see scenes or only single lights. Name your scenes in plain words in the Hue app and test one scene first.
Do I need a Hue Bridge for the connector?
Not always. Hue works with a Bridge at the heart of the system, or over Bluetooth for a smaller setup. Make the lights work in the Hue app first, and the connector builds on that setup.
Can Muse set brightness and colour?
We could not verify this in October. If Muse cannot set brightness or colour through the connector, set the scene in the Hue app, where brightness and colour already live. The scene is the point, not the path to it.
Can I use the Philips Hue connector outside the United States, Canada, and Japan?
Not yet. Muse itself has launched in the United States, Canada, and Japan only. Join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day in your country.
What happens if I disconnect Philips Hue?
Muse loses access to your lights. Your rooms, scenes, and automations stay in the Hue app, and nothing Muse set is changed after you disconnect. You can reconnect the same account later.
