Is Muse available in Thailand?

No. Muse launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. Thailand is not among them. Meta has published no country list, no timeline, and no statement naming Thailand.

Thai coverage has said so from the start. A Thai marketer's report on the September 8 launch noted that Thailand was not included, and a follow up on the September 29 Small Business announcement said Thai businesses could not yet use it. The launch report names the US only, and the Small Business report is explicit that Thailand is not yet included. Both are Thai sources, and both agree with Meta's own silence.

Thailand's only forward looking signal is the Bangkok Post report of November 12, 2026, covered in the next section. It is a trade press expectation from an ecommerce executive, not a Meta announcement. Until Meta speaks, Thailand sits in the same group as every other unlaunched country: no date, no timeline, no promise.

The launches that are confirmed, each with its date and source, are listed on the Muse available countries page. Thailand is not among them, and that page, not a tutorial, is where a Thailand launch would first be recorded here.

What does the 'early next year' report actually say?

On November 12, 2026, the Bangkok Post published "AI digital assistants set to reshape online shopping," a story about shopping agents and the coming wave of AI driven commerce. In it, Thanawat Malabuppha, the chief executive of Priceza, said: "Muse is already available in Singapore and should come to Thailand early next year." The article is open on the Bangkok Post site, and its photo caption repeats the expectation: "The Muse AI assistant from Meta is already available in Singapore and is expected to be available in Thailand early next year."

Read that carefully. The expectation belongs to Thanawat Malabuppha, an ecommerce executive speaking in a trade story about shopping agents. It is not a Meta announcement, not a Meta date, and not a commitment of any kind. The article quotes no Meta source on Thailand timing, and Meta's own pages name no Thailand date. Treating "early next year" as a launch date would be reading more into the story than it says.

The context of the story matters too. It is about a trend, not a launch: Google's Universal Commerce Protocol, expected early in 2026, shopping agents from several companies, and a 700,000 baht prize for an AI shopping challenge in Thailand. Muse appears as one example among several, in a story about where ecommerce is heading. That is useful context for Thai readers. It is not a launch report.

What should a Thai reader do with this? Treat "early next year" as a watch window, not a promise. If Meta confirms a Thailand launch, it will appear as an App Store listing, a Meta newsroom post, or a post from Meta's Muse account. Until one of those appears, the Bangkok Post report is the most hopeful signal Thailand has, and it is still only an expectation.

What happens when you try from Thailand?

Muse gives a Thai visitor two different answers, and both are true. The public site welcomes you: muse.ai loads, the demos play, the pricing page shows the tiers. The account gives the other answer: sign in, and the block message appears, "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet," with a Join the waitlist button. The first answer is marketing. The second is the product.

Your App Store gives the second answer too. The Thai storefront carries no "Muse from Meta" listing, and that has been true since the September 2026 storefront checks, which found the listing only in the US across fifteen other storefronts checked. No Thailand specific check since has found it. An empty search result is the expected result, not a problem with your phone, and the guide to Muse not showing in your App Store explains the checks.

Both answers come from the same design. Muse checks the country on your Meta account at sign in, not the device or the connection, so a page can load from anywhere while the product stays shut to an unlaunched account. An independent check of the block describes that account layer gate and found the same wall from every unlaunched country it tested.

The gate is the same on the iPhone app, the Android app, the web, the Mac app, and the Muse chat inside WhatsApp. There is no surface with a softer rule, and switching between them changes nothing. If Thailand opens, the change will show up on all of them at once.

Why does LINE matter more than WhatsApp in Thailand?

Thailand runs on LINE. LY Corporation reports that LINE has about 54 million users in Thailand, roughly 80 percent of the population, and describes the app as deeply embedded in daily life: chat, payments, shopping, and official accounts for businesses. LY Corporation's own account of LINE in Thailand notes that the app is opened dozens of times a day by its users. WhatsApp, by contrast, reaches about 12 million users in Thailand, against LINE's roughly 47 million in the same comparison, based on 2023 figures. That comparison is set out in a Thailand messaging app overview.

That gap matters for Muse, because Muse's chat surface is WhatsApp. The guide to using Muse in WhatsApp describes a chat you message like a contact, after connecting your account once. In Thailand, that habit would need building from scratch, because WhatsApp is not where Thais message each other. Meta has announced no LINE version of Muse, and no LINE connector.

For now, the realistic expectation is the standalone app and the web, if and when Thailand opens. A launch that meets Thais where they are would need a LINE answer, and none has been announced. The Bangkok Post story's shopping angle hints at the same point: Thailand's ecommerce runs through channels Muse has not yet connected, and connecting them is part of what takes time.

Does Muse speak Thai?

There is no table to check. Meta has published no list of the languages the Muse agent supports, no help page that names Thai, and no launch material in Thai. The question of Thai support therefore has no published answer, in either direction, and this guide will not fill the gap with a guess.

Mexico shows what localization looks like when it happens: Muse launched there in Spanish on November 5, 2026, with local services connected from the start. That is proof that Meta localizes. It is not proof about Thai, and no source has reported Thai testing, a Thai interface, or Thai connectors.

The confusion to avoid is Meta AI. Meta AI is the assistant built into Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and WhatsApp, and it is a different product from Muse, the standalone agent. A language working in Meta AI proves nothing about Muse, and the two products' availability and language support are separate questions. The Muse vs Meta AI explainer sets out the difference.

For a Thai reader, the practical point is patience: Thai support, if it comes, will be announced with a launch, not discovered in a tutorial. Until a launch names Thai, treat it as unproven.

How does Thailand's data law affect a launch?

Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act, the PDPA, has been fully enforceable since June 1, 2022. It is modeled on Europe's GDPR, it is enforced by the Personal Data Protection Committee, and it carries fines of up to THB 5 million for violations. A PDPA summary for businesses sets out the scope and the penalties. Muse is an agent that reads inboxes, calendars, messages, and shopping history, and does things on a user's behalf. That is exactly the kind of processing a data protection law governs.

This guide does not claim the PDPA blocks Muse. It claims something narrower: a product that touches this much personal data takes longer to launch in a market with a serious data law, because the launch has to be built right for that law. Meta itself has named the same shape of problem in general terms: on the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg described Muse as compute intensive and said per country tailoring, including connectors for local services, takes time. That account of the call is set out in an analysis of the global rollout.

That is context for why expansion is slow everywhere, not a stated reason for Thailand. No Meta source has named the PDPA as a factor in Thailand timing. But a Thai reader who knows the PDPA will understand why a launch here is not a flipped switch: the data handling, the connectors, and the language all have to be built for Thailand, and none of that work has been announced.

Can a VPN get you Muse in Thailand?

The "early next year" report makes the wait feel short, and a short wait makes shortcuts tempting. The shortcut on offer is usually a VPN, and the promise is usually that changing your address opens the product. The promise fails on the mechanism: Muse does not check your address. It checks your account.

A VPN changes the address the internet sees, and that is all it changes. The country on your Meta account stays what it was, and your store region stays what it was. The account check runs at sign in, which is why the marketing pages can load from anywhere while the product stays shut.

Reports from other unlaunched countries describe what follows a VPN signup: the waitlist screen, or an account that stops working later. Some of those reports warn that using a VPN to sign up may break Muse's terms and that Meta can limit or close accounts. Those warnings are user beliefs and cautionary reporting, not verified Meta enforcement. This guide states them as caution, not fact.

The honest close is that no shortcut in this guide exists, because no shortcut has been shown to work. Paying for a "Thailand Muse method" buys a promise, not access, and bought accounts have been reported as disconnected without warning in other markets. The guide to buying codes explains why paying for access is a bad deal in every market.

What privacy setting should you turn off first?

Your first job inside the app is a setting, not a prompt. Open Settings, then Data controls, and turn off "Help improve our AI models." The dialog will ask you to confirm; tap "Turn off." From that point, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models.

Keep the switch and the law separate in your mind. The switch is a product setting inside Muse. The PDPA is Thai law, and your rights under it stand apart from any switch. The switch controls what Meta does with your Muse chats under its own terms. The law controls what any company may do with your personal data in Thailand. Both matter, and neither replaces the other.

Unless you change it, the switch starts on: multiple reports on Muse's defaults say training is enabled until the user switches it off. It applies from the moment you set it, so earlier chats fall under the earlier rule, and setting it first is the only way to cover everything from the start. It takes seconds, and it is worth doing before your first real chat.

What will Muse cost in Thailand?

No baht price exists, because no Thailand launch exists. Meta has announced no local currency, no local billing, and no local price, and there is no figure to quote. A Thailand price will exist the day a Thailand checkout exists, and not before. The Hindu's reported India pricing in rupees, dated November 9, 2026, is unconfirmed by Meta and silent on Thailand.

Only one set of prices circulates, and it is the US set: Power costs $20 a month, Maximum costs $100 a month, and a free tier carries a weekly usage limit. Subscriptions buy more usage, not more features. The weekly limit on the free tier has no published Meta number; the up to 100 million tokens a week figure comes from the launch statement by Mark Zuckerberg. The full tier breakdown is in the guide to whether Muse is free.

Age verification is the cost that hides. Muse is for adults 18 and over, and verification is reported to require either a linked Instagram or Facebook account, or a payment card carrying a $1 temporary authorization, refunded in 5 to 7 business days, though Meta has not confirmed the card requirement in the sources checked. The step is small for a reader with a card, and a wall for a reader without one. The age verification problems guide covers the failure modes and fixes.

But it launched for businesses in the US and Canada only. Thai businesses cannot use it yet, and cannot connect their Pages or ad accounts to Muse. The Thai coverage of the announcement is explicit on that point.

The preparation a Thai business can do today is the same preparation that helps in any market: a clean product catalog, connected social accounts with real details, and an understanding of what an agent can and cannot do on a business's behalf. The Muse for Small Business setup guide describes the onboarding for readers in launched countries, and it is worth reading now so the day Thailand opens is a setup day, not a learning day.

Do not pay anyone who promises to connect a Thai business to Muse today. There is no Thai business onboarding, no Thai connector set, and no date. The Bangkok Post story's shopping angle is about where ecommerce is heading, not about a tool Thai sellers can use this week.
