Meta Muse Charm: Price, Release Date, and What It Is

Meta's keychain sized Muse device is the breakout gadget of September 2026. No price announced yet, release targeted for December. Here is what it is, what it does, and what is still unknown.

1. The short answer

Meta Charm is a keychain sized device for talking to Muse, Meta's personal AI assistant. It is about the size of an AirPods case, with a small touchscreen, built in 5G, a fingerprint sensor, and a camera. Tap the sensor and you can start talking to Muse without unlocking your phone or opening an app.

Meta has not announced a price. Any number you see quoted online is speculation. The release target is December 2026, in time for the holidays, but treat that as a target, not a promise. Meta has said the hardware is still being finalized.

The rest of this guide covers what the Charm is, what it does all day, the honest state of pricing, and the questions everyone is asking: is it a Tamagotchi, is it another Humane AI Pin, and do you still need your phone.

2. What the Charm actually is

Meta unveiled the Charm at its Connect event on September 24, 2026, alongside new smart glasses and VR glasses. It is a pendant like device meant to live on a keychain, a lanyard, or in a pocket. Here is what Meta has shown so far.

  • Size: about the size of an Apple AirPods case, small enough to fit on a keychain.
  • Screen: a roughly 2 inch touchscreen that shows an animated Muse avatar.
  • Connectivity: built in 5G, so it can reach Muse without a phone nearby.
  • Talk button: a fingerprint sensor in the corner. Tap it and you start talking. No unlock, no app to open.
  • Camera: a camera for visual context, so you can show Muse what is going on around you. Demos show a front facing camera.
  • Also inside: speakers, microphones, and an action button.

Our What is Meta Muse guide explains the assistant itself, if you are new to it.

3. What it actually does all day

Zuckerberg's pitch was simple: the whole Muse experience, including the live voice and avatar stack, packed into something that fits on a keychain and is always available to talk to. For people not wearing smart glasses, he called it by far the fastest way to talk to your Muse and to show it what is going on around you.

In practice that means voice first interaction. You tap the fingerprint sensor and speak. You do not unlock anything, find an app, or type. The camera lets Muse see what you see, so you can ask about things in front of you instead of describing them.

One important detail: the Charm is set up through the Muse app on iPhone or Android, and the agent itself runs in the cloud. It is a window into Muse, not a computer that replaces your phone.

4. The price question, answered honestly

Meta has not announced a price for the Charm. That is the complete official position, and it has not changed since the unveiling.

This matters because price guesses are already circulating. Treat every number you see quoted online as speculation until Meta itself announces it. We are deliberately not repeating rumored prices here, because repeating a guess enough times turns it into a fact in search results, and then everyone quotes the echo.

The missing price is itself worth noticing. It tells you Meta has not locked how it wants to position this device: as an affordable companion that spreads Muse everywhere, or as a premium standalone gadget. Which way it goes will say a lot about who the Charm is really for.

If you see a price quoted without a link to an official Meta source, treat it as unverified. We will update this guide the day Meta announces pricing.

5. Release date and where you can get it

Meta is targeting December 2026 and has said the Charm should be ready to ship in time for the holidays. Read that as a target, not a commitment. Meta has also said the hardware is still in development and components are being finalized, which is normal this far out but means dates can move.

Meta has not announced which countries get it first, how ordering will work, or whether there will be a preorder window. If you are outside the US, assume nothing about local availability yet. We will update this guide when dates and countries firm up.

6. Why everyone calls it a Tamagotchi

One of the fastest rising searches around the Charm is people comparing it to a Tamagotchi, the digital pet from the nineties. The resemblance is visual and immediate: a tiny screen, a small animated character living on it, something dangling off your bag.

The comparison is affectionate more than technical. A Tamagotchi was a pet you kept alive by feeding it. The Charm is a terminal for a full AI agent that can see, hear, remember, and act. What the meme gets right is the feeling of the form factor: small, personal, always with you, a little bit charming. That is presumably where the name came from.

7. Is this another Humane AI Pin

It is a fair question, because the last company that tried a standalone AI gadget became a cautionary tale. The Humane AI Pin launched in 2024 at $699 plus a monthly subscription, then failed on slow responses, heat, and weak sales, and was discontinued.

The Charm differs in three visible ways. It has a real screen instead of a projected display. Meta has not announced any subscription. And Meta has billions of existing users across its apps to sell it to, which Humane never had.

None of that guarantees success. The category is unproven, the price is unknown, and the honest answer is that nobody knows how this lands until it ships and people live with it for a month. If you are deciding whether to be excited or skeptical, both reactions are reasonable right now.

8. Do you still need your phone

The most useful skeptical question: my phone already does all of this, so why carry another gadget. The short answer is that the Charm is not a phone replacement and is not trying to be one. It is set up through the Muse app, and the heavy work happens in the cloud.

Meta's argument is speed and friction. Unlock phone, find app, type or hold to talk, versus tap a sensor on your keychain and speak. For quick questions, reminders, and showing Muse something in front of you, the Charm removes every step between the thought and the answer.

Whether that is worth carrying a second device is the question every buyer will answer for themselves in December. The phone does everything the Charm does. The Charm just does the talking part faster.

The short version Meta Charm is a keychain sized Muse device with a small touchscreen, 5G, and a tap to talk sensor. No price announced yet. Release targeted for December 2026. It does not replace your phone. It is the fastest way to talk to Muse without one.
