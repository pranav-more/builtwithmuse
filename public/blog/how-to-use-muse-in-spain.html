Is Muse available in Spain?

No. Spain is not among the five countries where Muse is live as of November 13, 2026. The list is the US, where it launched on September 8, 2026, Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. Neither is any other EU country. Meta has published no country list and no timeline for any further country, and it has announced no date for Spain.

Start with the store. Search the Spanish App Store for Muse from Meta and you will not find it, because the listing appears only in storefronts where Meta has released the app. Independent storefront checks have found the same result since launch: a check on September 10, 2026 queried Apple's lookup endpoint for the Muse listing and found it in the US store and in none of fifteen other storefronts, including Spain. A country by country review updated October 31, 2026 lists the whole European Economic Area, including Spain, with no launch announced. See the full list in our Muse available countries guide.

The sign in screen is where the website stops you. Load muse.ai from Spain and the pages work. The moment you sign in, a waitlist screen says Muse is not available in your country or region yet. It is the same screen readers see across the EU, and it is not an error: it is the country gate doing its job. No setting on the site changes it, and no Meta page names Spain or gives a date. Only a Spanish launch removes the screen.

Spanish readers are asking more than most. Tutorials about reaching Muse circulate in Spanish, alongside tutorials in Bengali, Tagalog, Hindi, Korean, Chinese, and Vietnamese, and Spain appears in the country questions people ask most. That demand is real, but it has not produced a date. Demand does not set Meta's timetable. Only a Meta announcement does, and there is none for Spain.

Spain was the exception once already

Spain has a history with Meta AI launches that makes the Muse wait feel familiar, and it is the most Spain specific fact in this guide. When Meta AI finally reached Europe in March 2025, after a delay of almost a year over data protection concerns, the rollout covered 41 European countries. Spain was not one of them, at first.

Reporting at the time said the rollout did not include Spain, as regulatory challenges had reportedly delayed its availability in the country, and that Spain's data protection authorities had imposed additional scrutiny, making Spain one of the few major European markets where Meta AI remained unavailable. That reporting is second hand, and this guide presents it as reported, not as a confirmed Meta statement. But the pattern it describes is consistent with what followed: Meta AI did reach Spain eventually, as part of the European rollout, but Spain was the exception inside the exception, the country that waited longest even among the countries that waited.

What does that mean for Muse? It means Spanish readers should be careful with any claim that Europe launches as one bloc. Meta's European history shows country by country timing even inside the EU, with individual countries held back over regulatory questions. A European Muse launch, if and when it comes, may not include Spain on day one. It may also include Spain first. Meta has said nothing either way, and this guide does not predict which. The point is narrower: do not assume a European date is a Spanish date. Wait for a statement that names Spain.

The broader Meta AI precedent still applies. Meta AI launched in the US in 2023, and its European debut stalled when Ireland's Data Protection Commission raised concerns about training on EU users' public posts. Meta paused AI training on EU user data in June 2024, and after almost a year of intensive engagement with regulators, Meta AI reached 41 European countries in March 2025, about 18 months after its US launch, as a text only assistant not trained on EU user data. That history is the backdrop for every European Muse question, and Spain's extra wait inside it is the reason Spanish readers should watch for Spain by name.

WhatsApp is Spain's default, and Muse is not in it yet

No country in this wave uses WhatsApp like Spain does. According to Spain's telecom regulator, the CNMC, WhatsApp was used by 95.3 percent of internet users in Spain in the second quarter of 2026, in the regulator's household panel reported in October 2026. A separate analysis put WhatsApp penetration in Spain at 85 percent of phone users, and the Reuters Institute's 2026 survey found 89 percent of online adults in Spain used WhatsApp in the past week. The figures differ because they measure different things, but they agree on the point: WhatsApp is Spain's default for messaging, calls, groups, and daily life.

Mexico shows how WhatsApp fits in. Its launch on November 5 included WhatsApp alongside iOS, Android, and the web: WhatsApp arrives as part of a launch, not as a back door around one. The same country rule therefore applies inside WhatsApp as in the standalone app, and Muse is available there only where it has launched. In Spain, the assistant inside WhatsApp is Meta AI, the older assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger. It answers questions in chat. It does not log into your accounts, book travel, or pay on your behalf. That is Muse's job, and Muse has not launched in Spain in any form.

The distinction comes from Meta itself. On social media, the official @meta.ai account has explained that Meta AI is the assistant inside Threads, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp, while Muse is the standalone personal agent app. Two products, one company, one chat window: the confusion is natural. An AI assistant in your WhatsApp in Spain today is Meta AI. When Muse launches in Spain, WhatsApp will probably be part of it, given how dominant WhatsApp is here and given the Mexico pattern. That is a guess based on the pattern, not a confirmation: Meta has said nothing about Spanish WhatsApp onboarding.

For now, treat WhatsApp as Meta AI territory. Do not expect Muse to appear inside it before a Spanish launch is announced, and do not mistake the assistant already there for the agent. The EU rules behind the wait

No official reason exists for the European wait. Meta has not pointed to a law, a regulator, or a technical problem, and it has not offered a date. What exists is the rulebook Meta has to satisfy before it can launch in Spain, and that rulebook is worth understanding on its own terms, without treating it as the confirmed cause of the delay.

For an agent, the AI Act's conditions bite in three places. First, the transparency duties in Article 50, which apply from August 2, 2026: people must know they are interacting with AI, no later than the first interaction. Second, machine readable marking of generative systems, with a grace period until December 2, 2026 for systems already on the market. Third, the core obligations for general purpose AI models, which have applied since August 2, 2025, with models already on the EU market before that date given until August 2, 2027 to fall in line. The Act does not ban agentic assistants. It sets conditions, and Meta has to meet them before offering Muse in Spain.

The older law is the GDPR, in force since 2018, and for an agent it governs the most sensitive questions: what personal data may be collected, how long it may be kept, and whether it may be used to train models. An agent that reads email, checks calendars, and pays on your behalf touches all three. The pattern across the industry supports the idea that these rules slow launches: the Centre for the Governance of AI compared US and European launch dates for 375 models in a study published in June 2026, and found about one in nine reached the EU late or not at all. Meta's EU delay rate was around a quarter, the highest of the four companies studied, and data protection rules were the obstacle the researchers named most often.

Spain's own history fits inside that picture. As the earlier section covers, Spain was the exception inside the Meta AI rollout, waiting longest even among the countries that waited. That does not prove a specific rule is delaying Muse in Spain. Meta has named no reason. But it shows that European timing is country by country, not bloc by bloc, and that Spain has been held back before over regulatory questions.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Spain?

Spanish language tutorials describe reaching Muse from unlaunched countries with a VPN, a fresh email address, and a new account. They are easy to find, and many claim the method works. The temptation is stronger in Spain than in most countries, because Muse already works in Spanish: Mexico proves it. But a shared language does not change the country gate. This guide does not promise the method works, and it does not recommend it. Here is the honest picture.

Meta publishes no supported path for using Muse from Spain, and the reason is the gate. It sits at the account layer, not the network layer, so a VPN only changes one signal out of several. Users in unlaunched countries report accounts that displayed their true location even with a VPN running, and waitlist screens that appeared even with a US server connected. The region of your App Store account, the country on your payment method, your device settings, and your account history all carry location information too. No method works reliably for everyone.

The account itself is at stake too. Using Muse from a country where it has not launched means Meta may review the account, see the location mismatch, and flag or shut it down. Coverage describes that as a caution, not as a verified enforcement policy, and Meta has published no rule banning VPN signups. Even so, the sensible rule is to keep a workaround account empty of anything that matters: no main email, no payment card you use, nothing work related. If Meta closes the account, everything in it is lost with it.

Turn off data sharing first, before anything else. The path is Settings, then Data controls, then the "Help improve our AI models" toggle: switch it off and confirm with "Turn off". The toggle controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Switching it off excludes your chats from training data, and that is all it does. It changes nothing about location, and it offers no safety to a workaround account. Switch it off anyway, whether the account is real or a workaround.

What will Muse cost in Spain?

There is no Spanish price to quote. No euro figure exists in any source checked for this guide, and a euro price in a post or a video is not to be trusted. The pricing that is reported covers the launched countries: a free tier capped weekly, a $20 a month Power tier, and a $100 a month Maximum tier. The paid tiers buy capacity, not features, so free users get the same agent with less weekly room.

Weekly limits sit behind the tiers, and none of them is published by Meta. The free tier is reported at 100 million tokens a week, a number that comes from Zuckerberg's launch statement, not from Meta's help pages. Power is reported at 500 million a week and Maximum at 3 billion a week. The numbers are consistent across sources, but since Meta's help pages do not publish them, they stay reported, not confirmed. Local pricing is the open question, and Mexico is a useful warning against assuming it. Mexico launched in Spanish on November 5, but no confirmed peso price has been published for it either, which shows that a local language launch does not guarantee local currency pricing. The only local pricing reported anywhere is India: The Hindu reported on November 9, 2026 that India pricing would be Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month, but Meta has not confirmed those figures, and India has not launched. A Spanish launch could price in euros or in dollars. Meta has said nothing, and this guide will not guess.

One cost is certain before any price is: the age check. The age floor is 18. Signing up involves an age check that can ask for a payment card, with a $1 temporary authorization, refunded within 5 to 7 business days. That is money held, not money taken. The check applies to the free tier as well, as reported. Spanish cards should work for the check itself, but it is only reachable after launch.

What will launch day look like in Spain?

The only guide to a Spanish launch day is what happened elsewhere. This section follows the five launches so far and translates them to Spain.

First comes the listing: the App Store listing appears in Spain, and the Google Play listing follows the same day. The download is free. You need a Meta account and you need to be 18 or over, with the age check described above as part of signing up. A Meta account from Facebook or Instagram can be linked, but stick to one sign in method: mixing methods has produced empty accounts for some users, when the sign in method differs from the first one.

The code window opens on launch day. If you joined the waitlist, your code arrives that day. A code redeemed in Settings within 48 hours of account creation gives both sides 1 billion tokens. One code per account, and codes are case sensitive. How many times a code can be used is unsettled: September sources said 20, November creators say 30, and Meta publishes no figure, so no cap is stated here as fact. Without a waitlist code, grab one from the community pool on the homepage and redeem it the same day you create your account.

What makes Muse useful on day one? Connections. Muse works by connecting to Gmail, Google Calendar, Instagram, and dozens of other services, then acting inside them. The connector mix for a Spanish launch is unknown, but the Japan and Mexico pattern is local connectors alongside the global ones. Before any connection, turn off the "Help improve our AI models" setting. Try one app and one small job first, and inspect what Muse does before trusting it with more.

Launch day is a beginning, not a completion. Features arrive in stages: Voice went to all US users on November 5, two months after the US launch, Spaces launched November 6, and Muse Mail followed on November 10 for paid US and Canadian subscribers. A Spanish launch would bring the core agent first, in Spanish, following the Mexico pattern. Newer features would follow later. Do not assume the full Muse experience is available on day one in Spain.
