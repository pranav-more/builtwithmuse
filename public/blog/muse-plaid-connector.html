How to Use the Plaid Connector in Muse

Finances via Plaid connects your bank accounts to Muse, so Muse can read your spending, audit your subscriptions, and build a money plan from your real numbers. Here is how to connect it, and the money safety rules to set before you ask anything.

1. The short answer

As of October 25, 2026, Plaid is a live connector in Muse, listed in the app as Finances via Plaid. Connect your bank accounts and Muse can read your spending, track subscriptions, flag duplicate charges, and build a money plan around your real numbers. That is the whole idea: Muse stops guessing about your money and starts reasoning from your actual bank data.

Plaid was live at launch on September 8, 2026, named by Alexandr Wang that day among the launch connectors. It stayed live through October, and Meta singled Plaid out on the October 21, 2026 earnings call, with Mark Zuckerberg noting that Plaid has seen unprecedented levels of activity with its affiliate program in the app. More than 10 million people in the United States and Canada had set up Muse since the September launch, according to the same earnings call. This is the connector Meta is proudest of right now, and also the one that deserves the most care, because it touches your money.

Here is the honest version. The Plaid connector is the most useful money tool Muse has, and the most dangerous one to set up carelessly. It can find the subscriptions you forgot, the bills you overpay, and the savings hiding in your own spending. It can also move money if you let it, which is why the approvals setup on this page is not optional. The rest of this guide covers how to connect it, the money safety rules to set first, the uses that are actually worth your time, and the limits to know before you lean on it.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • A bank account Plaid supports. Plaid connects to thousands of banks. If your bank is not supported, the connector cannot see it, and there is no workaround inside Muse.
  • Your bank sign in. You connect through Plaid, the same way other money apps connect to banks. Keep your bank password to yourself and sign in only on the Plaid screen Muse shows you.
  • Approvals set to ask me, before anything else. Before you connect Plaid, set approvals so nothing is spent, moved, or bought without your tap. This is the single most important step on this page, because this connector touches your money.
  • A money goal in one sentence. Find 1,000 dollars of savings. Cut my subscriptions. See where my money actually goes. One clear goal beats a vague request to look at my finances.

3. How to connect it, step by step

The connection itself takes a few minutes. The setup around it matters more, so run the steps in order and do not skip the approvals step at the start.

  1. Set approvals to ask me first. Do this before you connect Plaid, not after. In Muse settings, set approvals so that spending, buying, and moving money always waits for you. Meta says Muse asks for approval before spending, and this setting is what makes that true on your account.
  2. Open Muse and go to Settings. The connectors list lives there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  3. Find Finances via Plaid in the connectors list and open it. The connector is listed under that name, not just Plaid. If it is not listed, your account has not received it yet. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Check again in a few days.
  4. Connect through Plaid. Choose your bank from the Plaid screen and sign in with your bank sign in. Read the permission screen before you accept it. If a scope surprises you, stop and check what it covers before continuing.
  5. Check what connected. Ask Muse which accounts it can now see, and read the answer against your own records. If an account is missing, it may be a bank Plaid does not support, or a second sign in you still need to do.
  6. Test with a read only request. Ask Muse to summarize your spending from last month by category. Read the summary against your bank statement. If it matches, the connection is good. Do not ask it to move money or buy anything on the first day.
  7. Use it read only for a week. Summaries, subscription checks, and duplicate charge hunts only. Once you trust what it reads, start approving actions one at a time, and never approve a spend you have not read in full.

4. What it actually does

The connector earns its place with four uses. Everything else is a variation on one of them.

See where your money actually goes

Ask Muse to summarize your spending from the last 30 days by category, and you get the truth about your money instead of the story you tell yourself about it. Food, transport, subscriptions, the small daily spends that add up to a rent payment. This is the single highest value use, and it is the one to build a habit around. A good summary tells you the three categories that matter and the number for each, and it takes two minutes to read. Run it on the first of the month, every month, and the picture gets sharper each time.

Audit your subscriptions

Ask Muse to list every subscription it can find, what each costs, and when it last charged. This is the use behind the challenge Alexandr Wang promoted at Connect: let the agent find 1,000 dollars of savings. The savings are usually not in one big bill. They are in five small subscriptions you forgot, a free trial that rolled into a paid plan, and a service you pay for twice under two names. Muse reads the charges and tells you the list. You decide what to cancel. Never let Muse cancel anything without you reading the exact subscription first, because a wrong cancel can kill a service you actually use.

Flag what looks wrong

Ask Muse to find duplicate charges, unexpected price rises, and charges from names you do not recognize. This is different from checking your statement yourself, because Muse reads the pattern across months: the charge that doubled, the subscription that billed twice in one week, the merchant name that changed. Check anything it flags against your statement before you act on it, and call your bank directly for anything that looks like fraud. Muse can spot the pattern. Your bank is the one that can stop the charge.

Build a money plan from real numbers

Give Muse a goal and your real spending, and it can build a plan around the numbers instead of around generic advice. Save a fixed amount each month. Cut one category by a fifth. Find the money for a trip by a date. Meta describes the agent as one that, once you share a goal, helps you develop a personalized plan and coordinate your time and resources, then advances the work on its own. That last part is where the approvals rule matters most: the plan can advance on its own only as far as you let it. Keep every spend and every move behind your approval, and the plan stays yours. 5. Money safety: the rules to set first

This connector touches your money, so the safety rules come before the uses, not after them. Read this section twice.

  • Approvals set to ask me, always. Meta says Muse asks for approval before spending, and where no card is stored it uses single use card numbers. That protection only works if your approvals are set to ask. Check the setting today, and check it again after every app update, because updates can reset settings.
  • Never approve a spend you have not read in full. Read the amount, the merchant, and the date before you tap yes. A spend you did not read is a spend you did not make. If a request is vague about any of those three, say no and ask Muse to spell it out.
  • Never share bank passwords or card numbers in chat. You connect through the Plaid screen, not by typing credentials into a conversation. Meta says it stores passwords and payment methods so they are hidden even from the agent. Keep it that way: if Muse ever asks for a password or a card number in chat, stop and do not paste it.
  • Check every number against your statement. A summary can misread a charge, and a plan built on a wrong number is a wrong plan. Check anything that touches money against your bank statement before you act on it. This is doubly true for totals, dates, and subscription prices.
  • Know the limits of the safety net. Meta itself says Muse is not immune to attack and will sometimes make mistakes. The Identity Theft Resource Center does not recommend the money features yet, saying the technology is too new. That is not a reason to never use them. It is a reason to use them read only first, approve everything yourself, and keep your bank alerts turned on so you hear about every charge the moment it happens.

6. What it cannot do

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector.

  • It cannot see banks Plaid does not support. If your bank is not in Plaid, the connector cannot see it. There is no workaround inside Muse for this. Check Plaid support before you plan a money review around an account.
  • It cannot spend without your approval when approvals are set right. That is a feature, not a bug. If you turn approvals off, you lose the safety net, so keep them on for anything that moves money.
  • It cannot guarantee it read every charge right. A summary can miss a subscription, and a duplicate charge check can miss the duplicate. Check anything that touches money against your statement before you act on it.
  • It cannot negotiate on your behalf without risk. Meta says Muse can negotiate bills, including by calling providers. A negotiation is a commitment: a new contract, a new term, a new charge. Never let Muse agree to anything with a provider until you have read the exact terms it agreed to.
  • It cannot replace your bank. Fraud, disputes, and chargebacks are bank jobs. If Muse flags something that looks like fraud, call your bank directly and do not wait for Muse to handle it.

7. What to watch for

As of October 25, 2026, Plaid is the most talked about connector Muse has: live since September 8, singled out by Meta on the October 21 earnings call, and reviewed in the press on October 22, with more than 10 million people having set up Muse since launch. The attention is the thing to watch, because attention brings both real improvements and real scams. Only connect Plaid through the Muse app itself, on the screen Muse shows you. Never connect a bank through a link in an email, a message, or a post, even if it looks official.

Watch your bank alerts like a hawk for the first month. Turn on alerts for every charge, every login, and every new payee at your bank, and read them the moment they arrive. If Muse ever spends or moves money you did not approve, your bank alert is how you find out fast enough to stop it. Check the summary against your statement for the first week, especially any number or date it quotes. A wrong number acted on fast is worse than a slow read you verified.

Check your data controls. In Muse settings, open Data controls and look at the switch for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to read your bank data. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Money data is the most sensitive data you will ever hand an agent, so think twice before you connect an account you would not want read aloud.

Finally, remember the goal rule. Give Muse one money goal at a time, and judge it on that goal. A connector that finds you 1,000 dollars of savings and never moves a cent without your tap is doing its job. A connector you let run loose on your money is not an assistant. It is a risk with a friendly voice.
