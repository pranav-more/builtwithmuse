Is Muse available in WhatsApp?

Yes. WhatsApp was a launch surface for Muse on September 8, 2026, alongside the iOS and Android apps and the web at muse.ai. Meta's launch post says you can message Muse in its app or directly in WhatsApp, and the WhatsApp chat has been part of the product from day one. A setup guide for Muse on WhatsApp confirms the launch day status and the setup flow.

Availability follows your Muse account, not WhatsApp itself. You need a Muse account, you need to be 18 or older, and you need to be in a country where Muse operates. Meta checks region at the account level when you sign in, so a VPN does not get you in from elsewhere.

The confirmed scope needs care. Older guides say US and Canada only, because they were written before the later launches. Mexico's launch on November 5, 2026 included WhatsApp: Muse works in Spanish on iOS, Android, muse.ai, and WhatsApp there, with local connectors rolling out gradually. For Japan and Singapore, Meta's launch announcements list iOS, Android, and muse.ai, and one availability tracker notes Meta has not announced the WhatsApp version there. So the honest position is: WhatsApp is confirmed in the US, Canada, and Mexico, and unconfirmed in Japan and Singapore. If you are in Japan or Singapore and do not see the WhatsApp option, that may be why.

No other country has a date. Meta has announced no launch date for the UK, any EU country, or any other country, and it has published no country list and no expansion timeline. Our availability list tracks the five live markets and the honest no date position for everywhere else.

How is Muse in WhatsApp different from Meta AI?

This is the mix up that trips up the most people. Meta AI is the assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger, and it is available in many countries, including places where Muse has not launched. Muse is a different product: a standalone personal agent with its own app, its own memory, and its own computer in Meta's cloud, which does things across your connected apps instead of only answering questions.

In WhatsApp, the difference is what sits behind the chat. Meta AI answers from inside WhatsApp. Muse in WhatsApp is a window onto your agent: the same agent that lives in the Muse app, with the same memory, the same connectors, and the same permission rules. A task you start in WhatsApp can continue in the app or on the web, because the agent and its memory follow your Meta account.

If you are outside the five launch countries, you will see Meta AI in WhatsApp but not Muse. That is expected. Meta AI inside WhatsApp is the closest available experience on Meta's own platforms in unlaunched countries, but it is the assistant, not the agent. Our guide for unlaunched countries covers what you can actually do today, including the waitlist.

The names make this worse. Both are Meta AI products, both live in chat, and both answer questions. The test is simple: if it can book, buy, email, and act across your apps with your approval, it is Muse. If it only chats, it is Meta AI. How do you set up Muse in WhatsApp?

Meta has not published a standalone WhatsApp help article with screenshots, and menu labels can move while Muse is in limited testing. The steps below follow the setup flow as reported by guides that have walked it, and the settings layout shown in the Muse app. If a step does not match what you see, start from the Muse app's settings.

  1. Create your Muse account first. Install the Muse app on iOS or Android, or sign in at muse.ai with your Meta account. You need a Muse account behind the WhatsApp chat. There is no WhatsApp only sign up.
  2. Connect your Meta apps during onboarding. During setup, Muse offers to link Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Threads. Facebook, Instagram, and Threads connect automatically when they share an Accounts Center with your Meta account.
  3. Turn on WhatsApp messaging. In the Muse app, open Settings, then Messaging channels, and connect WhatsApp. This is the switch that creates the WhatsApp conversation with your agent. A walkthrough of the Muse settings panes shows Messaging channels in the settings list, with Connect WhatsApp as the option.
  4. Follow the entry link if one is offered. Some versions of the flow offer an entry link from the Muse site or app that opens WhatsApp and starts the conversation. Follow the prompts to link your account the first time.
  5. Send a first message. Start small, with a clear finish line: ask Muse to summarize your unread email, or to remind you about something tomorrow. The first message confirms the channel works before you hand it anything important.

Optionally, Meta lets you send a prompt in WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, or Threads so Muse can pick up what those apps already know about you. This is the memory import step, and it is optional. You can skip it and let Muse learn from your conversations instead.

Update WhatsApp first if you cannot find the entry point. New features arrive through app updates, and an older version of WhatsApp may not show the Muse option at all. Update from the App Store or Google Play, then look again.

What can you do with Muse in WhatsApp?

Almost everything you can do in the app, through text. The WhatsApp chat is a window onto the same agent running in its Muse Secure VM in Meta's cloud, so it has the same memory, the same connectors, and the same approval rules. Ask it to plan a trip, triage your email, compare prices, draft a message, or remind you about a bill, and it works the same way it would in the app.

Reminders are the feature that fits WhatsApp best. Reminders you set in WhatsApp arrive in WhatsApp, not in the Muse app's main chat. That makes the channel genuinely useful for the kind of person who lives in WhatsApp: the nudge shows up where you already look.

Approvals work in chat too. Muse asks before sensitive actions like sending an email or making a purchase, and it comes back to the same conversation to confirm. You approve or decline in the chat, the same way you would in the app.

Conversations carry across surfaces. Start a thread in WhatsApp, continue it in the app or on the web later, because the agent, its memory, and its permissions follow your Meta account. The channel is a door, not a separate agent.

What can Muse not do in WhatsApp?

The channel has real limits, and Meta has been quiet about several of them. Here is what is confirmed or reported:

  • It does not read your other WhatsApp chats. The channel is a door to Muse. It will not read your other chats, send messages as you, or join groups. Your personal conversations stay yours.
  • It does not message your contacts on your behalf. Meta has not announced that Muse can reach out to your contacts from WhatsApp, and no guide has confirmed it doing so.
  • It does not work in WhatsApp groups. Meta has not said whether you can add Muse to a group, and the channel is a one to one conversation with your agent. If your week runs on group chats, that is a gap.
  • Settings and connectors stay in the app. You manage connections, permissions, and usage in the Muse app or on the web, not inside the WhatsApp chat.
  • Voice is unclear. One guide reports Muse cannot make phone calls or hold live voice conversations in WhatsApp, while another describes voice conversations inside the Muse chat. The reports conflict, and Meta has not clarified. Treat voice in WhatsApp as unconfirmed, and use the app for voice.
  • The web view is read only. On the web you can read your WhatsApp thread with Muse, but you cannot reply there, according to one guide's walkthrough. Reply from WhatsApp itself or from the app.

None of these limits is a reason to skip the channel. They are reasons to know what it is: a fast way to brief your agent and get nudges back, with the full product living in the app.

How do approvals work in WhatsApp?

The same way they work everywhere in Muse. Muse asks for your approval before sensitive actions, such as sending an email, making a purchase, or posting something in your name. The request arrives in the WhatsApp conversation, with the details of what Muse wants to do, and you approve or decline there.

This matters more in WhatsApp than in the app, because WhatsApp is where quick replies live. A one word "yes" in a chat can authorize a real purchase, so read the approval message before you answer it. Muse's permission model requires explicit approval before actions like posting or reading messages, and that rule does not relax because the chat is casual.

You can also set boundaries in the app that apply to every channel. Manage permissions in Settings, then Permissions, and review what Muse is allowed to do without asking. A boundary you set in the app applies to the WhatsApp channel too, because it is the same agent. Our guide to Muse permissions explains the allow always option and when to use it.

If an approval request looks wrong, say no and check in the app. The Activity log, reached by tapping your assistant icon, shows a chronological record of actions Muse has taken and permissions you have given it.

Is your WhatsApp chat with Muse private?

Not in the way a WhatsApp chat with a person is. Chats between people on WhatsApp are encrypted end to end, so only the people in the chat can read them. Messages you send to Muse are instructions to an agent running in Meta's cloud, so the agent has to read them. Meta says Muse conversations and VM data stay out of its ad systems, and you can opt out of having your conversations used to train Meta's models.

The training opt out lives in Settings, under Data controls. It is the "Help improve our AI models" setting. Turn it off if you do not want your conversations used for AI improvement. Make that choice before you connect anything sensitive, and check it again after major app updates, since settings can move. Meta's privacy help page for Muse describes the toggle and the data controls around it.

A confidential VM mode that would lock Meta out of your virtual machine is planned but not live, according to one guide's summary of Meta's statements. Until it ships, assume Meta's systems can see what Muse sees, and do not hand the agent anything you would not want Meta to hold. Our safety guide goes deeper on what Muse can see and how to limit it.

The practical rule: treat the WhatsApp chat with Muse like email to a capable assistant, not like a whisper to a friend. Useful, fast, and not private in the same way.

Why can you not find Muse in WhatsApp?

The most common reason is region. If Muse is not available in your country, you will not see the Muse entry point in WhatsApp, and no update or reinstall will change that. Check the availability list first before troubleshooting your device.

The second most common reason is an old app version. Update WhatsApp to the latest version from your phone's app store. New features arrive through updates, and the Muse option may not exist in the version you have.

The third reason is a staged rollout. Meta rolls features out progressively, so the exact placement of the Muse entry point varies by app version and region, and some accounts get it before others. If you are in a launch country, your app is updated, and you still cannot find it, wait and check again.

A fourth possibility is the account check. Meta checks region at the account level when you sign in. If your Meta account region does not match a launch country, the channel will not appear, even if your phone is physically in one. Check which account you signed in with.

A fifth possibility applies in Japan and Singapore: Meta has not announced the WhatsApp version there, even though Muse itself is live in both countries. If you are in Japan or Singapore, the missing WhatsApp option may simply be unlaunched, not broken.

WhatsApp or the Muse app: which should you use?

Use WhatsApp for briefing and nudges. It is the fastest way to hand Muse a task when you are already in WhatsApp, and it is where reminders land best. A quick message from the grocery store, a reminder set while you are chatting with a friend, a question asked without switching apps: that is what the channel is for.

Use the app for everything else. Settings, connectors, permissions, usage, voice, Spaces, and the full chat history live in the app. The WhatsApp chat covers the basics, and the full product lives in the app. If you are still deciding whether Muse is worth the download, start with what Muse is and how the invite code reward works.

Most people will use both, and that is the point. The agent is the same, the memory is the same, and the conversation carries across. Start in whichever surface is already open, and finish in the one that fits the job.
