Is Muse available in Colombia?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. Colombia is not among them. The position has been consistent since launch: the app is not listed in the Colombian store, and Meta has announced no date for Colombia or for any other country in South America.

The Colombian evidence is unusually direct. Esquire Colombia, writing in October 2026, answered its own question plainly: no, Muse was not officially available in Colombia, and there was no announced date for Colombia or the rest of Latin America. A month later, nothing has changed: the November 7 storefront sweep of 32 App Stores found Colombia still absent. The sweep covered 32 storefronts and found five with the app.

Meta publishes no country list and no expansion timeline, and its help pages say only that Muse is not available in all locations yet. An availability tracker checked against Meta's own announcements lists every other country as no launch announced. For Colombian readers that means one thing: any launch date you see for Colombia comes from someone guessing, not from Meta.

What has Colombian press said?

Two Colombian outlets have covered the question directly, and both reached the same answer. Esquire Colombia's October 2026 piece said Muse was not officially available in Colombia and recommended waiting for the official launch. The advice still holds: the official launch is the only route this guide can recommend.

The second is Tuatara, a Colombian marketing publication, which noted in October 2026 that Meta had not announced a date for Colombia or the rest of Latin America, while saying it planned to reach more countries. Its advice to brands was to follow the rollout and prepare their accounts. That is still the right advice, for businesses and for individuals.

Local coverage matters because it is written for readers in the country, not translated from US news. Both pieces treat the absence as a waiting game, not a technical problem, and neither suggests a workaround. When Colombian press starts reporting a launch, that will be a stronger signal than any international rumor page.

Does the Mexico launch help Colombia?

Mexico launched on November 5, 2026, in Spanish, as the fifth market and the first in Latin America. The Mexico guide covers that launch. For Colombia, that is encouraging and uninformative at the same time. Encouraging, because it shows Meta is willing to launch in Spanish speaking Latin America. Uninformative, because Meta announced it the same day, with no hint of what follows.

The Spanish language availability guide tracking the rollout says Mexico changes nothing yet for the rest of Latin America, and names Argentina, Colombia, and Chile as having no date. There is no queue to read and no order to infer: Japan, Singapore, and Mexico arrived in that order, and nothing about the sequence predicts the next country.

One practical detail does carry over. On the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg said Muse is compute intensive to run and that the delay was partly about running it efficiently and partly about tailoring the product to each country, including connector work. A Colombia launch would need Colombian connectors, and none has been named.

What about Muse for small business?

On September 29, 2026, Meta launched Muse for Small Business, a set of business skills and connectors inside the existing Muse app: campaign drafts, sales history, expense flags, and connections to business tools. It is not a separate app or subscription. It is the same agent, pointed at business work.

The catch for Colombian businesses is the same as for everyone else in the country: the business offer only exists where Muse exists. It launched in the US and Canada, and Tuatara's October 2026 advice to Colombian brands was to follow the rollout and prepare accounts for when it arrives. No Colombian business can use it today through any official route.

What preparation actually means is modest: a Meta account in good standing, business pages and ad accounts tidy, and an understanding of what the agent will and will not do without approval. The approval rule is the heart of the business offer: nothing publishes, sends, or spends without the owner's approval. When Colombia launches, businesses that already understand that rule will move fastest.

Will Muse work in Spanish?

Spanish is not in doubt. The Muse App Store listing includes Spanish among 39 languages, and Spanish speakers in the US use the agent in Spanish today. Mexico's Spanish language launch removed any remaining doubt that Meta ships Muse in Spanish.

Colombia was in the first Spanish language wave of the older product. In July 2024, Meta AI arrived in Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Peru, and Cameroon, with Spanish support across WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, and Facebook. That history does not guarantee Muse's timing, but it does show Colombia is not an afterthought in Meta's Spanish planning.

The open question is narrower: Meta has published no language list for Muse, so the exact set of languages on a Colombia launch day is unconfirmed. Spanish is certain. Whether voice features handle Colombian Spanish idiom well is untested, and no guide can grade it before launch. Try it on a low stakes job first, and judge the output yourself.

Voice is a separate question from text. Voice chat rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, and it is unconfirmed for Colombia in any language. The voice guide covers the rollout. Spanish voice support in Colombia would need its own announcement, and none exists. Text in Spanish is the safe expectation; voice in Spanish is a hope, not a promise.

What about Muse in WhatsApp?

Muse in WhatsApp is closed in Colombia, because it follows the same country list as the app. The gate is the Meta account, not the surface you open, so switching from the app to WhatsApp changes nothing. Mexico's launch coverage said WhatsApp use would come, without a date, which suggests the WhatsApp surface can lag even after a launch. Colombia has neither route yet.

Meta AI, the assistant already inside WhatsApp in Colombia since July 2024, is a different product. It answers questions in chat. It does not connect to your inbox and calendar, complete errands, or ask for approvals, because it is not an agent. The guide comparing Muse and Meta AI draws the line between the two, and the distinction matters in Colombia more than most places, because WhatsApp is where most people first meet a Meta assistant.

Practical rule: if someone in Colombia offers you Muse over WhatsApp today, it is not Meta. The real product will arrive through Meta's own announcements and your own app store, and it will ask you to connect a Muse account, not to pay a stranger. Do not share codes, passwords, or documents with anyone claiming otherwise.

Can a VPN get you in from Colombia?

Short answer: no verified method exists, and the structure of the block explains why. Muse's pages load from anywhere; the check runs at sign in, against your Meta account. A VPN changes your apparent network location. It does not change your account country, your store region, or your payment card country, and Meta compares those signals against each other.

An availability guide that reviewed the question in November 2026 put it honestly: signing up through a US VPN can technically work in some reports, but Meta's terms prohibit misrepresenting your region, and some sources advise against it entirely. That is the right framing: possible in anecdotes, prohibited in terms, and unverified as a reliable method.

The caution is about your account, not your morals. A mismatch between signals can trip risk checks, and an enforcement action against a Meta account affects Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp at once. This guide recommends waiting for the official launch, as Esquire Colombia did in October. The wait is boring. The alternative risks something you cannot easily replace.

What would Muse cost in Colombia?

No Colombia price exists. The reported US pricing is free with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month, with paid tiers buying weekly usage capacity rather than features. None of those figures has a Colombian counterpart, and Meta has announced no peso pricing.

The free tier figure needs its attribution: 100 million tokens a week comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, not from Meta's help pages. And Meta's subscriptions page describes Muse as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, so the eventual Colombia offer may differ from the US one in price, limits, or both.

Do not convert $20 into pesos and call the result the price. Exchange rates move, app store pricing rules differ by market, and Meta has said nothing about Colombia at all. The checkout screen in your own account, on launch day, is the only price that counts. Until then, the US figures are a reference, not a promise.

The privacy switch to set on day one

Day one has one job before any other, and it is not a feature. It is a choice about your words. Inside the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, find "Help improve our AI models", and switch it off, confirming "Turn off" in the dialog. From that moment, your chats are not used to improve Meta's models.

Order matters. The setting covers chats from the moment you change it, so anything you said before the change falls under the previous rule. That is why this guide puts the switch ahead of connectors, inboxes, and first tasks: the first chat is the one most likely to contain something personal, because it is the one where you test what the agent can do.

Turning it off costs you nothing in capability. The agent still connects to your accounts, still drafts, still books, still asks for approval. The only change is what Meta may do with your words afterwards. It is a small choice with a long tail, and day one is the cheapest time to make it.
