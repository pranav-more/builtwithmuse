How to Use the Stripe Connector in Muse

Connect Stripe to Muse and your revenue questions get plain word answers: which invoices are overdue, which payment links actually converted, and where the money is coming from. Here is how to connect it, and the money safety rules to set before you ask anything.

1. The short answer

As of October 25, 2026, Stripe is a live connector in Muse. It was named in the Muse for Small Business list on September 29, 2026, and it was still listed in the October 15 in app check, which counted 54 connectors in the web app. There is no October update beyond that: announced September 29, still listed October 15. Connect it and Muse can answer revenue questions in plain words: which invoices are overdue, which payment links actually converted, and where the money is coming from.

Here is the honest version, and the distinction that matters most on this page. The Stripe connector is the merchant side of Stripe: your invoices, your payment links, your products and prices, your revenue analysis. Link by Stripe is the checkout side: the wallet customers pay with inside Muse. They are different tools with different jobs, and this guide is about the merchant side only. The rest of it covers how to connect it, what it can read, and the money safety rules to set before you ask anything.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • A Stripe merchant account. The connector reads your Stripe account, so you need one with real activity in it. A brand new account with no invoices in it has nothing to answer with.
  • Approvals set to ask me. Anything that moves money, changes an invoice, or sends a payment link must wait for your tap. Set this before you connect, not after.
  • A read only first week. Plan to ask questions and read answers for a week before you let Muse draft anything that leaves your business. Trust is earned by the loop.
  • Your Stripe dashboard open beside you. For the first week, check every number Muse quotes against Stripe itself. That check is the habit that makes the connector safe.

3. How to connect it, step by step

The connection takes a few minutes. The safety setup around it is the real work, so run the steps in order.

  1. Open Muse and go to Settings. The connectors list lives there. If Stripe is not listed, your account has not received it yet, because rollout is staged. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Check again in a few days.
  2. Find Stripe in the connectors list and open it. Sign in with your Stripe merchant account when asked and grant the access Muse needs to read your invoices, payment links, products, and revenue.
  3. Read the permission screen before you accept it. Money access deserves a slow read. If a permission surprises you, stop and check what it covers before continuing.
  4. Set approvals to ask me for anything money related. In Muse settings, set approvals so that nothing that moves money, changes an invoice, sends a payment link, or contacts a customer about payment happens without your explicit tap. Reading and analysis can run free. Moving money cannot.
  5. Test with a read only question. Ask Muse how many invoices are overdue right now. Check the answer against your Stripe dashboard. If it matches, the connection is good.
  6. Use it read only for a week. Questions and analysis only. No drafts that go to customers, no changes to anything. Once the numbers keep matching, start approving drafts one at a time.
  7. Decide who on your team may ask money questions. If other people use your Muse account, decide now who is allowed to ask about revenue and invoices. Money questions are the ones where a shared account needs a rule.

4. What it actually does

The connector earns its place with four uses. Each one is about seeing the money clearly, not about moving it.

Overdue invoices, chased politely

Ask Muse which invoices are overdue, by how much, and for how long, and it lists them with the customer, the amount, and the days overdue. Then ask it to draft a polite follow up for each one in your voice. You approve the drafts and send them. The money that used to sit in overdue invoices because nobody had time to chase it starts moving, and the chasing stops being a job you dread.

Payment links that actually converted

Ask Muse which payment links converted and which ones were opened and ignored. This is the use that tells you where the friction is: a link that gets opened but never paid usually has a price problem, a trust problem, or a confusing page behind it. Muse reads the link data and tells you which links earn and which ones stall, so you fix the right one instead of guessing.

Revenue analysis in plain words

Ask Muse where the money came from last week, last month, or last quarter, and it answers in plain words instead of a dashboard you have to interpret. Which products earned most, which customers paid most, what changed since last month. The analysis is only as good as your Stripe data, so a messy product catalog will give you messy answers. Tidy your top products first and the analysis gets dramatically better.

Finding new customers in the data

Ask Muse who paid for the first time recently, who bought twice, and who went quiet after one purchase. The first time buyers are your growth, the repeat buyers are your base, and the quiet ones are your win back list. Muse drafts the follow up for each group. You approve it. The list you would have built by hand once a quarter now updates every Monday.

The weekly money check

The habit that ties the four uses together is the Monday money check, and it takes ten minutes. Ask Muse for last week in plain words: revenue collected, invoices sent, invoices paid, invoices overdue, and payment links that converted. Then ask for the three money moves that matter most this week, in order. Read the answer, approve the follow up drafts, and move on. The check replaces the monthly scramble where you discover an overdue invoice three weeks late, because the overdue list is in front of you every Monday instead of once a quarter. Small businesses rarely lose money on one big mistake. They lose it on a hundred small invoices nobody chased, and the weekly check is what stops that slow leak.

5. Money safety: the rules to set first

Money connectors deserve stricter rules than any other connector on this site. Set these before you ask Muse anything about revenue, and do not relax them until the loop has earned it.

  • Approvals on ask me for everything money related. Nothing that moves money, changes an invoice, sends a payment link, or contacts a customer about payment happens without your explicit tap. This is the rule that makes everything else safe.
  • Read only for the first week. Questions and analysis only. No drafts that go to customers, no changes to anything. Once the numbers keep matching your Stripe dashboard, start approving drafts one at a time.
  • Check every number against Stripe. For the first weeks, open your Stripe dashboard beside Muse and check each figure it quotes. A wrong number acted on fast is worse than a slow number you verified. Over time the checks get quicker, because the numbers keep matching.
  • Keep customer payment conversations human. Muse can draft the follow up for an overdue invoice, but an unhappy customer or a large amount deserves your own words. Treat the draft as a starting point and write it yourself when the stakes are high.
  • Know the difference between the two Stripes. The Stripe connector is your merchant side: invoices, payment links, revenue. Link by Stripe is the checkout wallet your customers pay with inside Muse. If a question is about what a customer paid with, that is Link by Stripe. If it is about what your business earned, that is this connector.

6. What to watch for

As of October 25, 2026, Stripe is a settled connector for business accounts: named September 29, still listed in the October 15 in app check, with no October update beyond that. The habits around it still matter, because money is the one place where a confident wrong answer costs real cash.

Messy data is the first thing to watch. Muse reasons from your Stripe account as it is. Duplicate products, tangled prices, and invoices with vague descriptions will give you vague answers. Spend an hour tidying your top products and your active payment links before you lean on the analysis, and the answers get sharper immediately.

Staged rollout is the second thing. Stripe is a business connector, and business connectors are available where Muse has launched, in the United States, Canada, and Japan. If Stripe is not in your connectors list, your account has not received it yet. Check again in a few days. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke.

Finally, remember what the connector cannot see. It reads your Stripe account and nothing else. Money that moved outside Stripe, refunds handled elsewhere, and deals closed in person are invisible to it. If the analysis looks wrong, check what is missing from Stripe before you doubt the connector. The gap is usually in the data, not in the reading of it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the Stripe connector and Link by Stripe?
The Stripe connector is the merchant side: your invoices, payment links, products, and revenue. Link by Stripe is the checkout wallet customers pay with inside Muse. This guide covers the merchant side only.
Can Muse charge a customer or issue a refund?
Nothing that moves money happens without your approval when approvals are set to ask me. Muse reads, analyzes, and drafts. Every money action waits for your explicit tap.
Does it work with my Stripe account?
It works with the Stripe merchant account you sign in with. If your account has real invoices and payments in it, Muse can answer from them. A brand new account with no activity has nothing to answer with.
Can Muse see my revenue?
Muse sees what is in the Stripe account you connected, and nothing else. Money that moved outside Stripe is invisible to it. If the analysis looks wrong, check what is missing from Stripe first.
What if the numbers look wrong?
Check them against your Stripe dashboard, because that is the source of truth. A wrong number usually traces back to messy data: duplicate products, tangled prices, or vague invoice descriptions. Tidy the top products first.
