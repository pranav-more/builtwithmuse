Can Muse Shop on Amazon?

No, Muse cannot check out on Amazon. Amazon blocked Muse on September 20, 2026, while Meta added Walmart, Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Ulta, Wayfair, and more as shopping partners on September 23, 2026. Here is where Muse shopping actually works, how checkout and approval work, and what to do when the store you want is Amazon.

Why did Amazon block Muse?

On the night of September 20, 2026, shoppers who sent Muse to Amazon.com saw a notice that said: "Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed." GeekWire reported the block that night, with Amazon confirming it, and other outlets picked the story up the next day. The date matters because Muse had only launched on September 8, 2026, so Amazon acted within two weeks of launch, before most shoppers had formed any habit around agent shopping.

Amazon gave GeekWire three reasons. First, Meta never told Amazon that Muse would access its store. Second, the agent does not identify itself when it browses. Third, in Amazon's reading, the agent appears to capture and store customer credentials, which Amazon said could create privacy and security risks. Amazon also said it had asked Meta to remove Amazon from the Muse experience, and that third party applications buying products for customers should operate openly and respect a service provider decision about whether to take part.

Meta's position, from its launch materials, points the other way. Meta says Muse cannot see passwords or payment methods, because credentials go into secure storage that the agent can use without reading them. Meta also describes Muse browsing as appearing as the shopper's own activity. This guide does not settle that dispute, because the reporting read for it does not settle it either. What is settled is the practical result: Amazon does not want Muse shopping on its store, and it enforced that decision with a block, not with a warning.

That distinction matters for how you plan. A store that has not partnered with Muse is one thing. Amazon is different, because it actively refused Muse and asked for removal. Reporting on the block describes both browsing and buying on Amazon.com through Muse as cut off, so do not build any shopping routine that depends on Muse reaching Amazon at all. If a task starts with Amazon, that task starts in your own browser, not in Muse.

Which retailers did Muse add as shopping partners?

Three days after the Amazon block, at Meta Connect on September 23, 2026, Meta announced a slate of retail connectors for Muse. In Meta's own recap, quoted by connector trackers, the line reads: "we're adding Walmart, Best Buy, American Eagle Outfitters, DICK'S Sporting Goods, Fanatics, Gap, Michael Kors, Sephora, Ulta, and Wayfair, plus Shop Pay and PayPal for payments." The same recap added that Expedia was coming soon for travel planning and that Instacart was named for stocking the kitchen. Those two are travel and grocery names, not general goods retailers, so this guide keeps them out of the retail partner table below.

Here is the partner list, with the kind of goods each retailer is known for, so you can see at a glance where a given purchase belongs:

  • Walmart: everyday goods, groceries, and household essentials.
  • Best Buy: electronics, appliances, and tech accessories.
  • American Eagle Outfitters: clothing and casual apparel.
  • DICK'S Sporting Goods: sporting goods, fitness gear, and outdoor equipment.
  • Fanatics: sports fan gear and licensed team merchandise.
  • Gap: clothing for adults and children.
  • Michael Kors: fashion, handbags, and accessories.
  • Sephora: beauty and cosmetics.
  • Ulta: beauty, cosmetics, and salon products.
  • Wayfair: furniture and home goods.
  • All eligible Shopify stores: every product category sold by independent stores on Shopify, reached through the full Shopify catalog that Meta said Muse could access from launch.

Read that list with one caution attached. Meta's wording was "we're adding," which is an announcement, not a promise that checkout was live at every named retailer on September 23. Connector trackers checking on September 26, 2026 still listed all ten named retailers as announced rather than live, and noted that no quote from any of the ten retailers themselves had appeared in their own newsrooms by that date. So the honest way to use this list is as the direction of travel: these are the retailers Meta says are joining Muse, and each one becomes useful to you only when its connector actually appears and offers checkout in your account.

One name this guide deliberately leaves out is GameStop. Some secondary coverage mentioned GameStop alongside the Connect partners, but connector trackers noted that GameStop appears in no Meta source for this announcement. Until Meta or GameStop confirms it, GameStop stays out of the partner list here rather than in it on the strength of a wire mention.

What can you actually buy through Muse today?

Start with what was live at launch. Muse launched on September 8, 2026 as a personal agent that browses the web in its own browser, fills in forms, and keeps working after you close the app. From day one it could search stores, compare options, and prepare a purchase, and it paid through Link by Stripe. At businesses that accept Link, which Stripe counts at more than one million businesses, Muse checks out with the shopper's saved payment method. At businesses that do not accept Link, Stripe generates a single use virtual card scoped to the specific approved purchase, so the transaction can still complete without the merchant building anything new.

Shopify is the second live path, and for most shoppers it is the clearer one. Meta said from launch that Muse had access to the full Shopify catalog. Then, on September 21, 2026, Shopify enabled agent checkout through Shop Pay by default for all eligible United States merchants. By September 24, 2026, Shop Pay was available as a wallet inside Muse. In practice that means an eligible Shopify store can take a Muse order through Shop Pay, with the merchant staying merchant of record and running its own fulfillment, taxes, fraud checks, and order creation, exactly as it would for an ordinary Shop Pay order.

Ticketmaster sits in a third category worth knowing about, because it shows that Muse buying is not limited to physical goods. Connector trackers list Ticketmaster as a live reviewed connector, with purchase completing inside the Ticketmaster marketplace, for shoppers in the United States and Canada. That is a useful example of the pattern to look for: a connector where checkout stays inside a marketplace you already trust, rather than a loose promise that Muse can buy anywhere.

Outside those paths, keep discovery and checkout separate in your head. Muse can browse retail sites, scan listings that fit your request, and show you suggested listings inside the app. Clicking a listing may open the retailer website, or it may open a checkout inside Muse where one exists. A listing is not a checkout. If the listing only opens the retailer website, you are back to buying the ordinary way, with Muse having done the finding and you doing the paying. That is still useful, but it is not agent checkout, and you should not describe it as if it were.

So, in plain terms, here is what you can buy through Muse today: products from eligible Shopify stores through Shop Pay, products from businesses that accept Link by Stripe, products from other merchants through a Stripe single use virtual card where that path applies, and tickets through the Ticketmaster connector. The ten named retailers from September 23 join that picture as their connectors go live, retailer by retailer, rather than all at once. PayPal, although named as a payment partner on September 23, was still described as announced rather than available in explainers from early October 2026, so it does not belong in a today list yet.

How does checkout work when you buy through Muse?

Checkout through Muse follows the same shape every time, and the shape is the safety feature. Nothing is charged without your approval, and the approval is where you catch mistakes, so treat it as the real checkout, not as a formality. The sequence runs like this:

  1. Describe what you want in chat, with a price cap that includes shipping, and tell Muse not to buy anything until you approve.
  2. Muse searches the catalog it can reach, narrows the options, and shows you its pick with the merchant and the total.
  3. Muse prepares the checkout and shows you an approval card with the order details: the merchant, the item, taxes, shipping, the total, and the card it plans to use.
  4. You check the approval card line by line. If anything looks wrong, decline it. A declined approval ends the attempt with no charge.
  5. Once you approve, payment runs through Link by Stripe or through Shop Pay. Link by Stripe creates a one time use card for that order, and Shop Pay pays with a one time token, so your real card number is not shared with the merchant and is not visible to the agent.
  6. The merchant then fulfills the order under its own flow, and refunds and disputes run through the retailer, just as they would for an ordinary order from that store.

Before any of that works, you connect a payment wallet inside Muse: Link by Stripe or Shop Pay. With Shop Pay, you pick a card by its masked label during a one time setup, and Shop Pay then pays with a one time token at checkout rather than your card number. With Link, the same idea applies through Stripe: your saved payment method stays with Stripe, and the merchant sees a card created for that single approved purchase. If Muse asks for your email, full name, and address and you have not given it access to those details yet, it will need them before checkout can complete, so have them ready rather than improvising at the approval step.

One more detail from Meta's own description is worth keeping in mind. Meta says a monitoring layer watches what the agent does, that Muse asks before it sends an email or buys anything, and that you get an audit trail of what it touched. That is Meta's description of its own design, not an independent audit, and no independent audit had been published at the time of the reporting read for this guide. The practical advice follows from that gap: keep approvals switched on, watch your first few orders complete correctly, and only then decide how much latitude you trust Muse with.

What are the limits of shopping with Muse?

The first limit is the one this guide opens with: Amazon is blocked, and no reporting read for this guide gives a date or a plan for Amazon letting Muse back in. Amazon said it is in direct conversation with Meta, but a conversation is not a reversal, and shoppers should not plan around one. Until Amazon or Meta announces a change, Amazon stays a shop yourself store.

The second limit is rollout timing. Announced is not live, and live for one account is not live for every account. Reporting from late September 2026 is explicit that not every connector checkout was live for every account yet, and that the ten named retailers were still listed as announced days after Connect. If a partner name appears in Muse but no checkout button appears, the correct reading is that the connector has not reached you yet, not that you did something wrong.

The third limit is payment coverage. PayPal was announced on September 23 but reported as not yet available in early October 2026, so any plan that depends on PayPal inside Muse is a plan for later, not for today. Shop Pay inside Muse also pays in a fixed set of currencies reported as United States dollars, Canadian dollars, Mexican pesos, euros, Singapore dollars, and Japanese yen. If your order sits outside that set, or your store sits outside Shopify eligibility rules, the checkout path you expected may simply not appear.

The fourth limit is the agent itself. Muse shows you a total on the approval card, and that total is the number you are agreeing to, so read it rather than the chat summary above it. Totals can include taxes and shipping that a chat reply glosses over, and a product variant, size, color, or quantity can differ from what you pictured. The approval card exists for exactly this reason. It is also why a bounded first message helps so much: a cap that includes shipping, a request for three options with total cost, and a clear instruction not to buy until you approve turn a vague shopping wish into a checkable proposal.

The fifth limit is geography and scope. This guide is written for shoppers in the United States, where Muse launched first and where the September 23 partner announcements land. Muse availability, connector availability, and merchant eligibility can all differ elsewhere, and a retailer that works in one country may not work in another. If you are outside the United States, check what Muse actually offers in your account before assuming any partner or payment path in this guide applies to you.

How do you shop with Muse without getting stuck?

Use a short playbook and it will save you most of the frustration. Start with the paths known to work: an eligible Shopify store through Shop Pay, or a business that accepts Link by Stripe. Ask for the total cost up front, including shipping, and ask for options rather than a single pick, so you can compare merchant, variant, and total before anything is charged. Keep purchase approvals switched on in Settings, and never switch them off to make a purchase go faster, because the approval card is the only moment when stopping costs nothing.

Keep your payment details inside the payment provider own screens. Add your method through Link by Stripe or Shop Pay, and do not paste a raw card number into chat or into a browsed page that is not the provider screen. If a connector is listed but checkout does not appear, stop there: shop on the retailer website yourself for that order, and try Muse again later when the connector has actually rolled out to you. Working around a missing checkout with your password on a browsed page trades a small delay for a real security risk, and it is the one shortcut this guide recommends against without qualification.

For larger orders, add one more check before you approve: open the retailer own return and shipping terms in your own browser and confirm them. Muse checkout still leaves fulfillment, refunds, and disputes with the retailer, so the retailer policy is the policy that governs your order, whatever Muse said in chat. That check takes a minute, and it is the difference between an agent purchase you control and an agent purchase you merely watched happen.

What should you do if you want something from Amazon?

Buy it on Amazon yourself, in your own browser or in the Amazon app, and use Muse for the part it can still do well: narrowing down what you want before you go there. Ask Muse to compare features, sizes, and budgets across partner stores, or to help you write down exactly which variant you need, then take that shortlist to Amazon and complete the purchase yourself. That keeps Muse useful without pretending it can reach a store that blocked it.

The same logic applies in reverse. If the item you want is also sold by Walmart, by a Shopify store, or by one of the other named partners, try the Muse path there first, where checkout is welcome, and keep Amazon as the fallback you handle yourself. Over time the partner list will matter more than any single store, because the stores that welcome agent checkout are the ones where Muse can actually finish the job, while a blocked store can only ever be a place you shop the ordinary way.

Will Amazon ever let Muse shop again?

Nobody can honestly answer that yet, and this guide will not guess. No announcement from Amazon or Meta read for this guide sets a date, a condition, or a path back for Muse on Amazon.com. What exists is a block, a set of reasons Amazon gave for it, a request from Amazon that Meta remove Amazon from Muse, and reporting that the two companies are talking directly. Any of those facts could change, and if they do, the answer to the question in this guide title changes with them. Until then, the correct answer stays short: no, Muse cannot shop on Amazon, and the stores in this guide are where Muse shopping actually works.
