Is Muse available in Belgium?

The short answer is no, and the longer answer adds no date. Meta has announced no launch date for Belgium, and none for any European country, so Belgium waits in the same position as every EU member state. No Belgian statement, leak, or listing has changed that position, and no Belgian source has named a date that Meta has confirmed.

The five launch countries are the US, September 8, 2026; Canada, September 18; Japan, October 23; Singapore, October 28; and Mexico, November 5, the fifth market and the first in Latin America. Belgium is not on the list, and no European country is. Meta publishes no list of future countries and no timeline. The current list is in our Muse available countries guide.

The Belgian App Store has no Muse listing, and a Belgian account meets the waitlist screen on signup. One check found no announcement for any of the 30 EEA countries, and an earlier App Store check found Muse listed only in the US and Canada, as BreakOrbit’s availability page records. This guide was last checked on November 13, 2026.

Brussels hosts the regulators. Does that help Belgium?

Belgium has a distinction no other country in this series can claim: the regulators are here. The European Commission, the Council, and the European Parliament all sit in Brussels, and so does the EU AI Office, the body that supervises general purpose AI models under the AI Act. It is natural to ask whether that proximity helps Belgium get Muse sooner. The honest answer is no, and the reasons are worth understanding.

The EU AI Office sits within the European Commission’s Directorate General for Communications Networks, Content and Technology, DG CONNECT, and is headquartered in Brussels, as its overview page records. It was established by a Commission decision of January 24, 2024, which entered into force on February 21, 2024, and the Commission unveiled it on May 29, 2024, with a mandate covering general purpose AI models, according to a profile of the Office. Its job is supervision and enforcement at the EU level, not launch scheduling. It does not decide which countries get a product first, and it does not give Belgium priority over any other member state.

The AI Act itself is an EU regulation, which means it applies in all member states at once, on the same timetable. The obligations for general purpose AI models have applied since August 2, 2025, and Article 50 transparency duties apply from August 2, 2026, as one analysis of Muse’s first month sets out. A product that meets the Act meets it for Belgium and for every other member state at the same time. There is no Belgian fast lane in the law, and Meta has given no sign of a Belgian fast lane in its plans.

What Brussels does give Belgium is a front row seat to the argument. The debates about AI training data, transparency marking, and enforcement happen in the city where Belgian users live, and Belgian journalists and policymakers hear them first. That is interesting. It is not a launch date. Belgium waits with the rest of the EU, and the wait ends with a European announcement, not a Belgian one.

Three official languages, and no answers in any of them

Belgium has three official languages: Dutch, French, and German. Muse has announced support for none of them. Meta has said nothing about Belgian language support in any language, and no launch coverage addresses the Belgian language question at all.

The Meta AI history splits Belgium’s languages in an awkward way. French and German were among the six languages in the Meta AI European launch on March 20, 2025. Dutch was not. The six were English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, and Italian, as TechCrunch reported at the time. Dutch speakers, the majority in Flanders, got Meta AI later and in another language first. That is a fact about Meta AI, not about Muse, but it shows how easily a language can be left out of a first wave.

The position has moved since. Dutch now appears on WhatsApp’s list of device languages for Meta AI access, which covers the languages on which access is available in some countries. WhatsApp says you can chat with Meta AI in any language, so the list governs device language access rather than comprehension. Dutch being on it means Dutch devices are covered for Meta AI. It says nothing about Muse, and nothing about whether a Belgian launch would support all three official languages, two of them, or one.

Belgium’s language question is therefore three questions, and Meta has answered none of them. A launch that supports French but not Dutch would serve Wallonia and leave Flanders waiting. A launch in Dutch but not French would do the reverse. A launch in English only would serve the many Belgians who work in English and leave everyone else to cope. Do not assume any of these outcomes. The test is launch day: open Muse, write in your language, and see what happens.

WhatsApp in Belgium, and why it matters for Muse

WhatsApp is Belgium’s main messaging app by a clear margin, and the numbers leave little doubt. Infobip’s country data puts WhatsApp penetration in Belgium at 55 percent of phone users, ahead of Instagram at 40 percent, Apple Messages at 40 percent, and Messenger at 30 percent, in Infobip’s messaging survey. Sensor Tower’s Q4 2025 figures show WhatsApp with weekly active users peaking above 9 million in a country of 11.7 million people, while Messenger ended the quarter above 4.1 million, in Sensor Tower’s Belgium data.

That dominance makes WhatsApp the likely first door into Muse for most Belgian users, if Muse follows the pattern of the Mexico launch, which included WhatsApp alongside iOS, Android, and the web. Our WhatsApp guide covers what Muse does inside WhatsApp and what it cannot do. None of it is confirmed for Belgium, but the shape of the Belgian market says the WhatsApp surface matters more here than the standalone app’s first week download count.

The Meta AI button already in Belgian WhatsApp accounts is not Muse. Meta AI answers questions in a chat. Muse is a standalone agent that takes actions across apps, as our Muse vs Meta AI guide explains. The distinction matters in Belgium more than in most countries, because WhatsApp is where Belgian daily life happens, and the arrival of a real agent inside it would be a bigger change than a new app icon on a home screen.

One caution for Belgian WhatsApp users: the app’s language follows the device, and the device language question from the previous section applies here too. A Muse that arrives inside WhatsApp in French but not Dutch, or the reverse, would split the country by language inside its most used app. Meta has said nothing about this. Watch for language details in the launch announcement, not just the country name.

Belgium and the 2024 training complaint

Belgium was one of the 11 countries whose data protection authority received a complaint from noyb on June 6, 2024, over Meta’s plan to train AI models on users’ personal data without asking for consent, as DataGuidance reported. The complaints asked regulators to act urgently before the policy change took effect on June 26, arguing that Meta had no legal basis to process personal data for undefined AI purposes.

Eight days later, on June 14, 2024, Meta paused the training plan across Europe after Ireland’s Data Protection Commission intervened, according to a summary of the sequence. Belgium’s authority, the Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit, known in French as the Autorité de protection des données, was among those asked to act, though the decisive intervention came from Dublin, as Meta’s lead supervisor. That is how EU enforcement works: the complaint can start in Brussels, and the decision lands in Ireland.

The episode matters for Belgian Muse users for a practical reason. It established, in public, that AI training on European personal data is contested, that objections work, and that timing matters: objections raised before training starts are worth more than objections raised after, because trained models cannot easily be untrained, as a legal analysis of the complaints notes. The version of that lesson inside the product is the data sharing setting: when you get access to Muse, open Settings, then Data controls, and turn off “Help improve our AI models”, confirming “Turn off” when asked, before you connect anything else.

Belgium’s role in the 2024 complaint does not give Belgian users any special protection or any special risk. It gives them a reason to know the setting exists and to use it first. The complaint was about training. The setting is about training. The connection is direct, and Belgian users were part of the argument that made it visible.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Belgium?

VPN tutorials promise Muse from Belgium. This guide promises none of them works, and the Belgian case shows why the promise is empty. A VPN changes your traffic. Muse checks your account, and your account says Belgium.

The checks are the same as everywhere: App Store region, account country, payment method, and the card used for age verification. Belgian cards and a Belgian store region identify Belgium even when the IP address says otherwise, and the usual result is the waitlist screen. Our App Store guide explains the store checks in detail.

The account risk is a caution, not a verified policy, and this guide keeps it that way. Meta may suspend or close an account that shows a location mismatch, according to user reports and guides. No Meta rule has been published that confirms it, and none has proven it safe. If you experiment, use an account that holds nothing important, and expect the experiment to fail.

Belgium adds a language reason to skip the workaround. A VPN account is configured for another country, in another country’s language, with another country’s connectors. For a Belgian user, that means the wrong language in the wrong market, with the wrong services connected, on an account at risk. The real Belgian launch, in one or more Belgian languages, with Belgian services, is worth waiting for. The workaround is a worse product, sooner, maybe.

What will Muse cost in Belgium?

Belgium shares its price position with the whole of Europe: there is none. No euro amount has been announced for Belgium, and no amount in any other currency either. The numbers people cite are the reported United States numbers, and they describe the United States launch: a free tier that limits use each week, Power, reported at $20 per month, and Maximum, reported at $100 per month. Between those tiers, the agent does not change. The allowance changes, with paid subscriptions buying more usage of the identical product, and the free tier offering the same agent inside a weekly limit.

Belgium’s price, when it exists, will be a euro price, and it will probably be the same euro price as every other eurozone country. That is a guess about how Meta prices, not a fact, and it is labeled as a guess here. What is certain is that no euro figure has been announced, and converting $20 into euros does not produce one. Exchange rates move, and Meta’s local pricing decisions do not track them mechanically.

One figure in this section needs a warning attached to it. The free tier is usually described with a weekly allowance of 100 million tokens, and that description traces back to a launch statement by Mark Zuckerberg, not to any Meta help page. The help pages say something more cautious: subscriptions are in limited testing and are not available everywhere, a position one availability guide records from Meta’s own wording. Pricing follows the same local logic. The Hindu reported Indian prices of Rs 1,799 a month for Power and Rs 8,999 a month for Maximum on November 9, 2026, without Meta confirmation, which is consistent with prices being set per market. A Belgian euro price, when it appears, will be Meta’s Belgian decision, not an exchange rate calculation.

Belgian users should expect a free tier, a euro price announced with the launch, and no early information. If a site claims to know the Belgian price today, it is guessing, and this guide will not join it.

What will launch day look like in Belgium?

Launch day in Belgium will most likely be launch day in Europe. Meta AI arrived in 41 countries at once in March 2025, and a Muse European launch would plausibly follow the same wave pattern. Belgium would be in the wave, but no source has said so, and the announcement to trust is one that names Belgium or lists the launch countries with Belgium among them.

The account comes before the agent, and the account starts with proof of age. Muse is 18 and older, and the proof is a payment card or a linked Instagram or Facebook account, with the card route placing a $1 temporary authorization that returns in 5 to 7 business days. Our age verification guide explains why that step trips people up: checks tightened in October, and prepaid cards fail regularly. Once you are in, two moves follow in order. First the setting: “Help improve our AI models” off, in Settings under Data controls, confirming “Turn off” when asked, before anything is connected. Then the code, redeemed within 48 hours of creating the account, for the 1 billion token bonus shared by both sides.

Then the language test, which matters more in Belgium than almost anywhere. Open Muse and write in Dutch, French, or German, whichever you use daily. Check whether it answers well, whether it takes actions correctly, and whether it handles the language you actually write in, not just the language of the launch announcement. If it handles one Belgian language and not another, that is worth knowing on day one, not discovering a month later.

Measure launch day against a realistic feature list. Voice, which rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, remains US only at the start, as our Muse voice guide covers. Connectors are announced per market, so Belgium will get its own list over time, in the pattern Mexico showed. Spaces, which launched November 6, 2026, should be there from the start, since it is available everywhere Muse is available, as our Spaces guide explains. A Belgian launch that delivers a working agent, at least one Belgian language, and a working WhatsApp surface is a strong start, and the remaining features can arrive in the months that follow.
