What Happens to Your Muse Account If You Change Countries?

People move between countries all the time, and Muse users are no exception. Here is an honest breakdown of what is known about your account when you change countries, and what is still unclear.

1. Your account and tokens stay yours

Let us start with the reassuring part. Your Muse account is your account, and your tokens are your tokens. Tokens you receive from an invite code never expire, and nothing publicly known suggests they vanish when you cross a border. Your balance is tied to your account, not your location, as far as anyone outside Meta can tell.

Need an invite code? Click here to grab one free from the community pool. Waiting on a launch in your country? Join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day.

The key fact to hold onto is that tokens never expire. Because there is no countdown on your balance, a move does not create a use it or lose it problem. You do not need to rush to spend tokens before a flight, and you do not need to worry that travel gaps will eat into what you have earned. The tokens sit in your account until you use them, wherever you are.

Your account itself travels with you in the ordinary sense: you sign in the same way, and what is attached to the account stays attached to it. Nothing in Meta's public invite materials suggests an account gets reset, split, or recreated when the owner relocates. If you can sign in, your account and its balance come with you.

That said, keep your sign in details safe around a move. Travel is when people lose access to email accounts and phone numbers, and losing your sign in is a far more realistic risk than losing tokens to a border. Make sure you can still reach the email or sign in method on your Muse account after you change numbers or providers.

2. The app experience follows local availability

What does change with location is availability. Muse has launched in some countries and not in others, so the app experience follows the local launch status wherever you are. If you move somewhere Muse has fully launched, expect business as usual. If you land somewhere it has not launched, you are in the same boat as everyone there.

For the not launched case, these guides walk through it: how to use Muse if it has not launched in your country and how to use Muse in India.

To be precise about what is known and what is not: it is known that Muse's launch is regional, with some countries in and others still waiting. What Meta has not published is how the app behaves at the edges, for example for an account created in a launched country that is then used daily somewhere unlaunched. The honest answer is that the app follows local availability, and anything more specific than that is unconfirmed.

One practical consequence is about getting the app in the first place. App stores are regional, so if you might need to reinstall while abroad, download the app before you travel, while you are somewhere it is available. This is the single most useful preparation step, and it costs nothing.

3. Invite codes work the same everywhere

Moving does not change how codes work. A code is redeemed in Settings within 48 hours of joining, and both the referrer and the new user receive 1 billion Muse tokens each, according to Muse's current invite copy. Share your code from anywhere, redeem codes from anywhere, and the mechanics stay the same.

This is worth spelling out because it surprises people. The invite flow does not check your country before handing out the tokens. You can share your code with a friend back home while you are abroad, and you can redeem a code a friend sends you while you are traveling. The 48 hour redeem window and the 1 billion tokens on each side apply exactly as they do at home.

The one thing that does not change with location but still matters is timing. The redeem window is only 48 hours, so if you are mid move and distracted, redeem first and unpack later. It takes a minute, and the tokens never expire once they are in.

4. Short trips versus a permanent move

It helps to separate two situations, because they carry different levels of uncertainty.

Short trips

For a short trip, there is very little to think about. Your account and tokens stay yours, your codes keep working, and you return home before any of the open questions matter. Download the app before you leave in case you need to reinstall, redeem any pending codes before the 48 hour window closes, and travel as usual.

Permanent moves

A permanent move is where the unknowns live. Your tokens are still yours and they still never expire, but your day to day app experience will follow whatever the local launch status is at your destination. If Muse has launched there, expect continuity. If it has not, read the country guides linked above and join the waitlist so a fresh code reaches you on launch day.

For a permanent move, the practical checklist is short: confirm you can sign in to your account independent of any phone number you are about to give up, redeem any codes you are holding, and join the waitlist if your destination has not launched. Everything else is waiting on Meta to publish a policy.

5. If your destination has not launched Muse

If your destination has not launched Muse yet, set yourself up before you travel. Join the waitlist so a fresh code reaches you on launch day, and read the country guides so you know your options on arrival. Your existing tokens will be waiting whenever you are back in a supported region.

Start with the waitlist. It is the official way to hear about launch day, and it costs nothing. Join the waitlist before you fly, so the notification finds you instead of you having to watch for news from abroad.

Next, read the country guides for your situation: how to use Muse if it has not launched in your country and how to use Muse in India. They lay out the options people actually use, and the tradeoffs of each, so you can decide with open eyes rather than improvising after you land.

And remember the reassuring math: your tokens never expire, so there is no penalty for waiting. Whatever you have earned is still yours on launch day, whether that day comes next month or next year. Meta has not announced launch timelines publicly, so treat any date you see online as a rumor until Meta itself says otherwise.

6. What is still unclear

Here is the honest part: Meta has not published a policy on accounts moving between regions. We do not know whether long term use from an unlaunched country triggers any review, or whether anything about the account changes quietly in the background. This guide will be updated if Meta clarifies it. Until then, treat the unknown as unknown and keep your expectations modest.

To make the unknown concrete, here are the specific questions Meta has not answered publicly:

  • Whether an account used long term from an unlaunched country faces any review or restriction.
  • Whether there is a way to update an account's country, or whether accounts even have a country field.
  • Whether features, limits, or the invite program differ by region once you have an account.
  • How availability is determined for a traveler: by account origin, current location, app store region, or some mix.

Anyone who answers these questions with confidence is guessing. Guides that state regional rules as fact are inventing policy Meta has not published. The responsible version of this article is the one you are reading: what is verified, stated plainly, and what is not, marked clearly.

The short version Your account and tokens stay yours when you change countries, and invite codes work the same everywhere. What follows you is local availability: the app experience matches wherever you are. Meta has not published a regional policy, so the rest is still unclear.
