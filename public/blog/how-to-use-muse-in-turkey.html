Is Muse available in Turkey?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. Turkey is not on that list, and Meta has published no Turkey launch date, no Turkey timeline, and no country list that names Turkey as coming next. The full list of countries where Muse is live tracks every launch, and Turkey has not appeared on it.

The most direct Turkey evidence comes from a reviewer in Istanbul. Writing in October 2026, he published a source led review of Muse and opened with a plain admission: he could not use Muse hands on from Istanbul. He opened the product, help, privacy, security, and business pages, but he did not run a task, connect an inbox, attempt a purchase, or measure latency, because the product would not let him in. His conclusion on availability was careful: as of October 2026, he could not verify Turkish availability, and Meta had published no Turkey launch date in the sources he checked. You can read his account in the Istanbul review of Muse.

Meta's own pages point the same way. The reviewer found Meta's small business page describing Muse as available in the US and Canada, and Meta's subscription help page saying Muse and its subscriptions are in limited testing and are not available in all locations. Those pages were written before Japan, Singapore, and Mexico launched, so the country list on them is out of date, but the second half of the sentence still holds: Muse is not available in all locations, and Turkey is one of the locations still waiting.

Every independent availability tracker checked for this guide agrees. A tracker updated on October 31, 2026 listed the four countries launched by then and named no other country anywhere in the world. A guide updated in November 2026 lists five countries, with Mexico added on November 5, 2026, and names no date for anywhere else. None of them names Turkey.

What is Muse, in one minute?

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent. It is not a chatbot that only answers questions. You give it a job, and it carries the job out across your connected apps: booking travel, drafting mail, moving calendar entries, tracking orders, and paying at checkout, asking your approval before consequential actions like sending, publishing, or spending. It runs in its own app, on the web at muse.ai, on Mac, and inside WhatsApp in the countries where it has launched.

The agent runs on Meta's models in Meta's cloud, inside its own secure virtual machine, with a system called Sentinel watching over the connected services. That architecture is part of why the rollout is slow: every new country needs the agent tailored to it, including work on local connectors. When Muse does reach Turkey, the connectors that matter to Turkish readers, local shops, local travel, local calendars, will only be known on the day Meta announces them. This guide will not guess that list.

Muse is free to use with a weekly usage limit. Two paid plans buy more usage, not more features: Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. The free tier is the way most people will start, and paying does not unlock Muse from a country where it has not launched. If you are new to the product, the full explainer on what Muse is covers the agent, the tokens, and the invite codes in one place.

What happens when you try from Turkey?

The marketing pages load. You can read about Muse from Istanbul, Ankara, or anywhere else in Turkey. The block comes at the account step. As one guide puts it, the gate sits at the account layer, not the web layer: muse.ai loads fine from anywhere, then stops you at sign in. You can read the full breakdown in the guide to the country block.

On an iPhone, the app is the first wall. An independent check of 156 App Store storefronts in early October 2026 found "Muse from Meta" listed in only three: the US, Canada, and, oddly, Saint Kitts and Nevis. Turkey was not one of them. If you search the Turkish App Store for Muse, you will find other apps using the name, not Meta's agent. Check the seller name on any listing you find: Meta's app names Meta Platforms, Inc. as the seller. The storefront check is described in the 156 storefront check.

On the web, the wall comes at sign in. Tests from outside the launched countries in October 2026 showed a waitlist screen with the message "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet. We'll let you know when it is." The same message appears if you open muse.ai/join from Turkey: the open entry point that needs no code in launched countries shows the region message instead. The sign in test is documented in the October availability check.

That message is a hard block, not a queue with a number. There is no public waitlist page with a position, no country by country roadmap, and no date attached to the promise of a notice. Joining the waitlist means Meta says it will let you know when Muse is available to you. It does not give early access, and it does not change the block.

Why is there no Turkey launch date?

Meta has not said. The company has published no timeline for any further country, and it has not named Turkey, or any other country, as next. Every "coming soon" line you read about Turkey is someone else's guess, not a Meta statement. Treat any page that gives you a Turkey date as rumor until Meta itself says it.

The closest thing to an explanation came on October 21, 2026, when Mark Zuckerberg told analysts on Meta's earnings call that Muse is compute intensive to run, and that the delay in expanding is partly about running it efficiently and partly about tailoring it to each country, including work on connectors. That is a general explanation for the slow rollout, not a reason specific to Turkey. The earnings call account is covered in the report on why Muse has not launched globally.

One thing that does not explain Turkey is European regulation. Turkey sits outside the EU and the EEA, so the EU AI Act and GDPR debate that shapes the Europe question is not the Turkey story. Meta kept its multimodal model out of the EU in 2024, citing an unpredictable regulatory environment, and Meta AI only reached Europe in March 2025, long after the US. That precedent matters for readers in Europe. For Turkey, Meta has given no reason at all, and this guide will not invent one.

The pattern of launches so far gives no clue about order. Canada followed the US by ten days. Japan came next, then Singapore, then Mexico, the first market in Latin America. Each launch arrived with its own local announcement, and none was trailed by a published roadmap. Turkey could be next, or tenth. Nothing Meta has published lets you tell.

Does Muse speak Turkish?

The honest answer is that nobody outside Meta can confirm how well Muse handles Turkish, because nobody in Turkey can use it yet. What can be confirmed is narrower, and it is good news: the App Store listing for Muse already names Turkish among its supported languages, alongside Hindi, Bengali, Arabic, Urdu, and dozens more. That listing detail is noted in the guide to Muse in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan.

That listing is about the app, not a promise about the agent. The listing covers the languages the App Store page is available in. Meta has published no list of the languages the Muse agent itself supports in conversation. So the right way to read the Turkish listing is simple: language is not the blocker. The blocker is the country gate.

The same point holds across the launch map. The App Store listing covers English plus 38 other languages, including French, German, Spanish, Hebrew, Arabic, and Hindi, and most of those countries still cannot sign in. Language support in the listing has never predicted the next launch country. When Muse does arrive in Turkey, Turkish support in the agent is likely, given the listing, but "likely" is not "confirmed," and this guide will update when there is a real test to point to.

For now, the guides on this site are in English, and the product material Meta publishes is mostly in English too. If you read English, you can prepare in English. If you do not, the waitlist notice and the launch announcement are the two things to watch, and both will be covered here when they happen.

What about Muse in WhatsApp?

Muse runs inside WhatsApp in launched countries, alongside the iPhone app, the Android app, the Mac app, and the web at muse.ai. The Istanbul review approaches the product through exactly that lens: its title frames Muse as the personal agent inside WhatsApp. The review's framing is a reminder that Muse is not only an app. In launched countries it also lives inside WhatsApp chats, where you talk to it like a contact.

But the same country gate covers every way in. One availability guide puts it plainly: the country list applies across every way you can reach Muse, the iPhone app, the Android app, the muse.ai website, and the Muse chat inside WhatsApp. Opening WhatsApp in Turkey does not get around the gate, because the gate checks the account, not the app you open. That account wide rule is set out in the guide to Muse outside the US.

There is one distinction worth knowing. Meta AI, the assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Messenger, is a different product from Muse, and it is already available in many countries where Muse is not. If you have used Meta AI inside WhatsApp in Turkey, you have used Meta's assistant, not its agent. Muse is the standalone agent with its own app, its own account, and the ability to carry out tasks across your connected apps. The two share a parent company and a chat box. They do not share a launch map.

What can you do today from Turkey?

Four things, all honest, and none of them a workaround.

  1. Join the waitlist. Open muse.ai and sign in, or open muse.ai/join, and you will see the waitlist screen. Joining puts you on Meta's list for a notice when Muse opens in Turkey. It does not give early access.
  2. Join the Built with Muse waitlist. Join the waitlist here on Built with Muse, and you will get a fresh invite code the day Muse launches in Turkey. No setup, no workarounds, and the code arrives exactly when you can use it.
  3. Learn the product before it arrives. Read what Muse does, how tokens work, and which connectors it supports, so launch day is setup, not study. Start with how Muse tokens work and the full connectors list.
  4. Watch the right sources. Meta's Newsroom, the Muse account on X, and the App Store listing are where a launch shows up first. A launch rumor on social media is not a launch.

What not to do: do not pay anyone for a code, an account, or "early access." Invite codes are free in the community pool, and purchased accounts have been reported to get disconnected. Anyone selling Turkey access to Muse is selling something Meta has not shipped.

Also, keep your expectations about price honest. Muse is free with a weekly usage limit, and its paid plans buy more usage, not more features. Paying does not unlock Muse from Turkey. The paid tiers raise your usage limit inside a supported country. They do not buy access from outside one. The pricing explainer has the full picture.

Should you use a VPN to get in from Turkey?

This guide will not promise that a VPN works, because the evidence says the gate is not the network. The gate sits at the account layer: muse.ai loads fine from anywhere, then stops you at sign in. A VPN changes the IP address you appear from. It does not change the country on your Meta account, and it does not change the region of your App Store or Google Play account, which is set in the Apple ID or Google account, not by IP.

People have tried the full stack and still hit the wall. One poster listed everything they had tried: a US VPN, location spoofing, several email addresses, a US App Store account, and US English settings. Their verdict: "somehow I still always end up on the waitlist or get blocked because of my region." That is one person's report, not a rule, but it matches how the gate is described everywhere else.

Caution, stated plainly: if Meta reviews an account and sees a location mismatch, the account could be flagged or banned. That is a caution, not a verified fact about how Meta enforces the rule, and this guide has no confirmed case of a Turkey account being banned for VPN use to point to. Reports describe VPN use as risking account suspension. Weigh that before you try anything, and do not build anything important on an account that depends on a workaround.

If you still want the full picture, the site has two complete guides for readers outside the launched countries: one for using Muse when it is not available in your country, and one for using Muse outside the US. Read them before you try anything, and read the caution above twice.

If you get in, turn this setting off first

Do this before anything else. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off the "Help improve our AI models" setting. Toggle it off and confirm "Turn off" when asked. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to improve its models. Turning it off keeps your chats out of training data.

Turning it off does not hide your location, and it does not change the country on your account. It is a privacy choice, not a workaround. But it is the first privacy choice to make, and it takes seconds.

The same advice applies wherever you use Muse from, but it matters more from a country where Muse has not launched, because your account already sits outside the map Meta drew. Keep your chats out of training data, and keep your expectations honest about what the account can do.

What will launch day look like in Turkey?

Based on the five launches so far, a Turkey launch would show up in three places at once: an announcement from Meta or the Muse account on X, the app appearing in the Turkish App Store and Google Play, and sign in opening on muse.ai. Japan's launch came through Meta's Japanese newsroom. Singapore's came through the English newsroom's Asia Pacific section. Mexico's was announced by Alexandr Wang and the Muse account on X. The announcement pattern is tracked in the country by country availability guide.

The fastest check you can run yourself is the App Store lookup. Apple's lookup pages are public: open the US listing for "Muse from Meta" and swap the us in the address for tr, the Turkey country code. A "not found" page means it is not there yet. When the page loads, the launch has likely started.

On launch day, you will not need an invite code to use Muse. The open entry point at muse.ai/join needs no code. A code is still worth having: redeem one within 48 hours of creating your account, and both you and the person who shared it get 1 billion Muse tokens. The redeem guide covers the exact steps and the 48 hour window.

Until then, the waitlist is the only official path, and this page will be updated when Meta announces a date. Check the date at the top of this guide: it was last updated in November 2026.
