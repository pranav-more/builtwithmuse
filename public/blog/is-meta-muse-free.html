1. The short answer

Yes, Meta Muse is free right now. The app is in an invite only rollout, and the only way in is an invite code. There is no price tag on Muse itself, no subscription to buy, and no payment step anywhere in the signup flow.

When people ask whether Muse is free, they usually mean one of three things. First, is there a charge to get the app and start using it. Second, is there a free trial or a free tier with limits. Third, will it stay free once it fully launches. The answer to the first is a clear yes. Nothing in the official invite flow asks for money. The answer to the second is that there is no trial because there is no paid plan to try. The invite is not a sample of a paid product. It is the whole product, free, for now. The answer to the third is the one honest unknown in this guide, and we cover it in full below.

It helps to be precise about what free covers. Free means the app costs nothing to install, the invite code costs nothing to receive, redeeming the code costs nothing, and the tokens you receive cost nothing. There is no card on file, no billing page, and no moment where the app pauses and asks you to pay to continue. If you follow the official path, your wallet never leaves your pocket.

That is the whole answer for today. The rest of this guide covers what an invite actually gets you, what costs nothing, and the one honest unknown: what pricing looks like after the full launch.

2. What an invite code gets you

An invite code is your ticket in. Redeem it and you get access to Muse, plus a token bonus on both sides. According to Muse's current invite copy, when a code is redeemed, the referrer receives 1 billion Muse tokens and the new user receives 1 billion Muse tokens. Both sides win, which is why codes are worth grabbing and worth sharing.

Tokens are the fuel you spend when you ask Muse to do things. Every request you make draws from your balance, and the balance grows when you earn more. The good news is that tokens you receive from an invite code never expire. Redeem today and those tokens are still yours months from now, whether you spend them this week or save them for later.

One billion tokens is a serious starting balance. To put it in practical terms, it means you can actually use the app instead of rationing every request. New users often worry that a free start means a tiny allowance that runs out in a day. That is not what happens here. The bonus is designed to let you explore the app properly, try real work, and learn what Muse is good at before you ever think about where the next tokens come from.

The bonus also keeps working after you join. Once you have your own code, every person who redeems it earns you the referrer bonus again. Each redemption adds another 1 billion tokens to your balance and 1 billion to theirs. Sharing is the simplest way to keep your balance growing, and it costs the people you invite nothing. Our tokens guide explains what the bonus buys you in practice.

Think of your code as a small engine. The more people who join through it, the more fuel you collect, and the more they collect too. There is no trick here and no catch hidden in the fine print. The invite copy states the bonus plainly, both sides get it, and the tokens never expire.

3. What costs nothing right now

Getting a code costs nothing. Joining costs nothing. Redeeming the code in Settings costs nothing. The tokens cost nothing. At no point does the official flow ask for a card or a payment.

Walk through the official path slowly and notice where money never appears. You pick up a code from the community pool, free. You install the app from the official store listing, free. You open Settings, enter the code, and redeem it within 48 hours, free. The token bonus lands in your account, free. You start asking Muse to do things, and the requests draw from a balance you never paid for. There is no checkout screen, no billing page, and no premium tier waiting behind a paywall. The word free here means the complete absence of a payment step, not a limited sample.

This is also your scam filter, and it is worth taking seriously. Because codes have real value, people try to sell them. If anyone asks you to pay for a code, that is not the official flow. If a site asks for your card to unlock a code, that is not the official flow. If someone in a chat group offers guaranteed working codes for a fee, that is not the official flow. The official flow has exactly one price, and that price is nothing. Read why you should never pay for a code before you spend a cent.

Keep this rule of thumb: if money enters the picture at any point, stop. The real flow is code, app, Settings, done. Anything with a price tag attached is someone else's business model, not Meta's.

One more practical note. Because the flow is free, there is no refund to chase and no charge to dispute if something goes wrong. If a code does not work, the fix is to grab a fresh one and redeem it right away, not to pay someone for a better one. Free means you can afford to try again.

4. What is still unknown

Here is the honest unknown: Meta has not announced any pricing for Muse after the full launch. Right now there is no paid plan at all, so there is nothing to compare against. We will not guess at a price or a timeline.

It is worth saying plainly what has not been published. Meta has not published a price, a subscription tier, a launch date for paid plans, or any statement that Muse will always be free. The absence of news is not a promise in either direction. The app is free today because the rollout is invite only and there is simply no payment system to charge you through, not because Meta has committed to free forever.

What we can say with confidence: everything in the invite flow today is free, and the tokens you earn now never expire. Anything you collect today keeps its value no matter what Meta announces later. A token balance earned during the free period does not get wiped out by a pricing announcement. It stays in your account.

Treat pricing rumors with distance. If you see a quoted price for Muse on social media or in a video, ask where it came from. Unless the source is Meta itself, treat it as speculation. Screenshots can be faked, and old mockups get recycled as news every few weeks. Real pricing news, when it arrives, will come through official channels and will be loud enough that you will not need a rumor to hear it.

If pricing news lands, we will update this guide the same day. Check back before you believe any price you see quoted elsewhere.

6. How the free invite compares to a free trial

Many apps offer a free trial, which is a limited taste of a paid product. Seven days free, then the card gets charged. Some questions about Muse sound like they expect this model, so it is worth drawing the line clearly.

Muse has no free trial because there is no paid plan to trial. The invite is not a sample with an expiry date. It is full access to the app as it exists today, and the token bonus that comes with it never expires. Nobody is going to charge your card in seven days because there is no card on file and no charge scheduled.

This matters because it changes how you should use the app. With a typical trial, the smart move is to test fast before the clock runs out. With Muse, the clock is not running. Your tokens do not expire, your access does not downgrade after a week, and there is no billing date circled on your calendar. You can learn the app at your own pace.

The one clock that does run is the 48 hour redemption window on the code itself. That window belongs to the invite, not to your account. Once the code is redeemed, the pressure is off.

7. What to watch for if pricing ever arrives

Someday Meta may announce pricing, or it may not. Either way, here is how to stay ahead of the news instead of reacting to rumors.

Watch official channels first. A real pricing announcement will come from Meta, not from a stranger in a comment section. If you see a price quoted without a link to an official source, treat it as unverified. Screenshots can be faked, and old mockups get recycled as news every few weeks.

Second, remember what you already own. Tokens earned through invites never expire. A pricing announcement does not erase your balance. Whatever you have collected stays yours, which takes the urgency out of every rumor.

Third, keep your code sharing habit. Each redemption earns both sides 1 billion tokens, so a healthy balance built now is the best hedge against any future cost. The users who shared early will be the ones with the deepest reserves if the economics ever change.

We will update this guide the day real pricing news appears. Until then, the honest answer stays the same: free today, unknown tomorrow, and your tokens are safe either way.
