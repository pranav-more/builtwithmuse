How to Use Muse on Apple Watch and Wear OS

Muse reached the wrist on November 9, 2026, with a companion app for Apple Watch and Wear OS. This guide covers what the watch app can do, what you need to run it, how to set it up, and the honest limits, including what Meta has not confirmed about Wear OS.

Muse is now on Apple Watch and Wear OS, as a wrist companion to the phone app. You can talk to Muse through voice calls, text messages, or dictation from your wrist. Meta announced it on November 9, 2026, and the watch app needs a paired phone and a Muse account.

What is Muse on a smartwatch?

Muse on a smartwatch is a companion app for your wrist. It puts the Muse agent one tap away, so you can start a request, read an update, or approve an action without pulling out your phone. It is not a standalone watch app. The phone still runs the main agent, and the watch acts as its remote. That boundary matters, because it decides what the watch can do on its own, which is less than the announcement headline suggests.

Meta announced the launch on November 9, 2026, through its @Muse account on X, with the line: "Works around the clock, and now on your wrist. Muse is now on Apple Watch and Wear OS." TechCrunch reported the launch on November 10, confirming that users can interact with Muse through voice calls, text messages, or dictation directly on the smartwatch, with a personalized avatar. The launch came about a month after the dedicated iPad app, and it was the third Muse expansion in a week, after Voice on November 5 and Spaces on November 6.

On Apple Watch, the companion app arrived inside version 15.0 of the Muse iPhone app, released November 9. The App Store release notes put it in one line: "Add your Muse to your wrist with the new Apple Watch Companion App!" On Wear OS, Meta confirmed a version for Android watches the same day, though the details of which watches can install it are thinner, as covered below.

What can you do with Muse on your wrist?

Three kinds of input work on the watch: voice calls, text messages, and dictation. You can call Muse and talk, type a short message, or dictate a request and let the watch turn speech into text. The personalized avatar carries over, so the Muse character you customized on the phone, as a person, an animal, or another character, shows up on the watch too, according to TechCrunch's report.

On Apple Watch, there is also a watch face complication, reported by MacRumors, which puts Muse one tap away from the watch face. A complication is a small widget on the watch face, and adding Muse there means you can open it without hunting through the app list. That one tap access is the main reason to install the watch app at all. A voice assistant buried three taps deep is barely faster than the phone in your pocket.

Beyond input, reporting describes the watch as a place to start tasks, read updates, and approve actions. One writeup of the launch puts it plainly: the Apple Watch and Wear OS apps start tasks, read updates, and approve actions, while the agent itself runs inside the cloud computer Meta calls the Muse Secure VM. A second report, from The Gadgeteer, lists notifications, shortcuts, and a watch face, plus tasks and connectors from the wrist. Those wider claims come from secondary reporting, not from a Meta page, and no Meta page checked in the days after launch lists which tasks or connectors actually work from the wrist. Treat the task and connector list as reported, not confirmed, until Meta publishes its own list.

The honest summary is that the watch is best for short interactions. Ask a question, dictate a reminder, check what Muse is doing, approve a step. Long work, like reading a full draft or reviewing a document, still belongs on the phone or a bigger screen. The watch earns its place by removing the phone from the small jobs, not by replacing the phone for the big ones.

What do you need for Apple Watch?

Three things, all confirmed through the App Store listing. You need version 15.0 or later of the Muse iPhone app, which is the version that added the companion app. Your iPhone needs iOS 18.0 or later. Your watch needs watchOS 9.0 or later. The App Store listing, published by Meta Platforms, Inc., lists iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch together, with those minimum versions.

You also need a paired phone and a Muse account. TechCrunch states that boundary directly: the watch app requires a paired phone and a Muse account. There is no sign in flow on the watch itself that replaces the phone, and there is no way to use the watch app without a Muse account in one of the countries where Muse is live. If you do not have a Muse account yet, set one up on the phone first.

Version 15.0 landed on the App Store on November 9, 2026, the same day as the announcement. If your iPhone app shows an older version, update it from the App Store before you look for the watch app. Companion apps install through the Watch app on your iPhone, and the usual path is to open the Watch app, find Muse in the available apps, and install it to the watch. If it does not appear, check that the iPhone app updated first, because the watch app ships inside the phone app update.

Once installed, add the complication to your watch face for one tap access. Press and hold the watch face, tap Edit or Customize, and add the Muse complication to a slot that supports it. Which slots support complications depends on the watch face you use, and Meta has not published a list of supported faces, so if Muse does not appear as a complication option on your face, try a face with more complication slots.

What about Wear OS?

Meta has confirmed Wear OS support. The November 9 announcement names Wear OS alongside Apple Watch, and TechCrunch's report covers both platforms with the same voice, text, and dictation inputs. A round watch running Muse appears in Meta's own announcement imagery, sitting next to Google's Gemini on compatible models. That is the confirmed part, and it ends there.

What Meta has not published is a compatibility list. No Meta page names supported watch models or a minimum Wear OS version. Reports put the Android side at Android 10 and up, attributed to The Gadgeteer's coverage, but that is a phone OS floor reported second hand, not a Wear OS version requirement from Meta. If you run an Android phone on an older version, check the Play Store listing on your watch before assuming it will work.

Whether the Wear OS app was actually installable on announcement day is also unsettled. The Gadgeteer's wording described the Wear OS app as waiting in the Google Play Store, which suggests availability may lag the announcement. A separate check on November 10 searched Google Play for "Muse from Meta" and found no Muse app from Meta Platforms and no Wear OS support, concluding that the Wear OS claim rests on the X post and the TechCrunch report. Other coverage states the Wear OS version is live. The reports disagree, and no Meta page settles it.

The practical move is to check the Play Store on your watch or your phone. If "Muse from Meta" appears with a Wear OS install option, you have it. If it does not appear, the app has not reached your watch yet, and no reinstall or setting will force it. Meta has also not confirmed a Wear OS equivalent of the Apple Watch complication, so do not expect a one tap watch face widget on Android until Meta or a hands on review confirms one.

How do you set it up?

Meta has published no step by step setup guide for the watch app, so the steps below follow the standard companion app path plus the requirements Meta and the App Store listing confirm. On Apple Watch, the flow is:

  1. Update the Muse app on your iPhone to version 15.0 or later from the App Store. Your iPhone needs iOS 18.0 or later, and your watch needs watchOS 9.0 or later.
  2. Open the Watch app on your iPhone, find Muse, and install the companion app to your watch. If it installed automatically with the phone update, it will already be on the watch.
  3. Open Muse on the watch. It uses the Muse account signed in on your paired phone, so there is no separate sign in to complete on the wrist.
  4. Add the Muse complication to your watch face for one tap access.
  5. Try a short request first: dictate a reminder or ask a question, and confirm the answer comes back on the watch.

On Wear OS, the flow is shorter and less certain. Open the Play Store on your watch, or the watch section of the Play Store on your phone, and look for "Muse from Meta." If it is listed, install it and open it from the watch. It should connect to the Muse account on your paired Android phone. If it is not listed, the app has not reached your watch, and the only move is to check again later. Do not sideload an APK from a third party site claiming to be Muse. Meta publishes the app through the Play Store, and anything else is not Meta's app.

One setup question has no confirmed answer: whether the watch works on cellular when the phone is away. No Meta page and no launch report confirms standalone cellular operation, and the paired phone requirement points the other way. Until Meta says otherwise, assume the phone needs to be in reach. The same applies to plan and token treatment for watch use. No source confirms whether watch requests count against your weekly usage the same way phone requests do, though the natural reading is that they do, since the same account and the same agent are doing the work.

Where does the watch app work?

Only where Muse works. The watch announcement did not launch Muse in any new country, and it did not expand the service to a new market. Muse is live in five countries: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. If you are outside those five, the watch app will not help you, because the account behind it cannot be created there. Availability varies by country, and Meta has announced no date for any further country.

Some early coverage of the watch launch still said four countries, because it was written from the position before Mexico launched on November 5. The correct count is five. If a guide you read says the watch app works in four countries, it is out of date, not describing a watch specific limit. The watch follows the same country list as the phone app.

The age rule follows the phone app too. Muse is for people 18 and over, and the watch app inherits that. There is no separate watch age check described anywhere, but the account behind the watch is the same 18 and over account, so the rule applies the same way.

If you are in a country where Muse has not launched, the watch app changes nothing about your options. The waitlist, the not available guide, and the honest no date position are the same as for the phone. Do not buy a watch expecting it to unlock Muse. It will not.

Does the watch app work without your phone nearby?

No, as far as anything confirmed goes. TechCrunch states that the watch app requires a paired phone and a Muse account. The phone runs the main agent, and the watch acts as a remote for requests, updates, and approvals. No standalone smartwatch operation has been confirmed by Meta, and no launch report describes the watch working on its own.

That is normal for a first companion app. The watch is a small screen with a small battery, and the agent's work happens in the cloud, reached through the phone's connection and account. The watch sends your request and shows the answer. It does not run the agent itself, and it does not hold your connected accounts on its own.

The open question is cellular. A cellular Apple Watch can reach the internet without the phone nearby, and some companion apps work over that connection. Meta has not said whether Muse on the watch works that way, and no hands on review has tested it. Until someone confirms it, the safe assumption is that the phone needs to be in Bluetooth range or on the same network. If you test it yourself and it works over cellular, that is a bonus, not a promise.

The same caution applies to leaving the phone at home for a run or a swim. If your routine depends on Muse being reachable from the wrist alone, verify it on your own watch before you rely on it. Ask Muse something with the phone in another room, then with the phone off, and see what comes back. That two minute test tells you more than any launch post.

What is different from using Muse on your phone?

The input is shorter and the output is shorter. On the phone you can type a long brief, read a long answer, and review a document. On the watch you dictate a sentence, read a glance, and approve a step. The watch is built for the jobs that fit in a sentence: a reminder, a question, a status check, a yes or no.

Muse Voice is the closest comparison on the phone. Voice, which rolled out to all users in the US on November 5, 2026, lets you talk to your Muse agent with a real time voice and an expressive avatar. The watch voice call is the same idea shrunk to the wrist: talk, listen, done. The difference is that Voice on the phone shows you the avatar full screen and lets you keep talking, while the watch gives you the answer and gets out of the way.

Approvals are where the watch earns its keep. Muse asks for approval before sensitive actions, and a watch notification that you can approve with a tap is faster than unlocking the phone, opening the app, and finding the request. If you use Muse for bookings, purchases, or messages that need approval, the watch turns approval from a chore into a glance. That is the use case to try first.

What does not change is the account, the usage, and the limits. The watch uses the same Muse account, the same connected apps, and the same weekly usage allowance as the phone. There is no separate watch plan, no watch specific price, and no confirmed difference in how tokens are counted. If you hit your weekly limit on the phone, the watch will hit the same wall.

How does this compare with the Muse Charm?

The Charm is Meta's own wearable: a pocket pendant with its own screen, cameras, and a cellular connection, announced at Connect in September 2026 and planned before the end of 2026, with no price announced. The watch app is the opposite approach. It runs on hardware you may already own, costs nothing extra, and is available now in the five launch countries.

The two will overlap. Both put Muse on your body, both use voice, and both aim at the moments when the phone is in your pocket. The Charm adds cameras and a dedicated device, which a watch cannot match, and it is built for a world where the agent sees what you see. The watch adds a screen you already wear, a complication you can tap, and no new hardware to buy or charge.

If you already own an Apple Watch or a Wear OS watch, the watch app is the thing to try today. If you are waiting for the Charm, the watch app is a preview of the same idea on hardware you have. Neither replaces the phone. Both are ways to reach the same agent from somewhere closer than your pocket.

Smart glasses are the third surface in the same family. Meta has said Muse is coming to its AI glasses, and the glasses, the watch, and the Charm all point the same way: the agent follows you off the phone. The watch is the first of those surfaces to ship, and the one with the clearest setup path today.

What if Muse does not show up on your watch?

Work through the causes in order, from the most common to the least. First, check the iPhone app version. If it is below 15.0, the companion app is not there to install. Update from the App Store, then check the Watch app again. Second, check the OS versions: iOS 18.0 or later on the phone, watchOS 9.0 or later on the watch. An older watch cannot run the companion app, and no setting overrides that.

Third, check the country. If your Muse account is not in one of the five launch countries, the watch app will not appear, because the service behind it is not available there. Fourth, check the paired phone and the account. The watch app needs the paired phone and a signed in Muse account on that phone. If you signed out on the phone, or paired the watch to a different phone, the watch app has nothing to connect to.

On Wear OS, the check is simpler and less satisfying: open the Play Store on the watch and search for "Muse from Meta." If it is not listed, the app has not reached your watch. Reports differ on whether the Wear OS app was live in the Play Store on announcement day, and a check on November 10 found no listing. Check again after a few days, and check the phone Play Store's watch section too, since listings sometimes appear there first.

If everything checks out and the watch app still does not appear, restart both the phone and the watch, then check the Watch app or Play Store again. That is generic advice, not Muse specific, but it resolves the stale listing problem that affects companion apps generally. If it still does not appear, wait. Companion rollouts can lag the phone update by days, and Meta has published no schedule for the watch rollout beyond the announcement itself.
