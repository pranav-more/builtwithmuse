What Muse for Small Business actually is

Muse for Small Business was announced by Meta on September 29, 2026. Despite the name, it is not a separate app, not a separate subscription, and not a separate product you sign up for somewhere new. It is a set of business skills and connectors that live inside the existing Muse app, switched on from the same settings screen as everything else, at the same prices as consumer Muse. If you already have Muse, you are most of the way there. If you do not, the setup below starts from zero.

The offer is available in the United States and Canada, for users aged 18 and over, matching Muse itself. It arrived less than a month after the consumer launch, and the demand was already visible: according to Vishal Shah, Meta's VP of AI Products, speaking to the Wall Street Journal, one third of Muse users had connected a business account, an Instagram business profile or a Facebook Page, before the business offer even existed. Meta also points out that more than 200 million small businesses already use its apps. This guide covers what you get, what it costs, how the approval system keeps you in control, the order to set things up in, the first jobs worth running, and the limits owners are finding.

What you can connect

The announcement named fifteen third party connectors, plus the Meta properties a small business already lives on. The four headline connectors show the shape of the offer:

  • Intuit QuickBooks. Cash flow, invoicing, and business intelligence, in Intuit's own description. Ask about money in plain language instead of opening reports.
  • Stripe. Find new customers, send invoices and payment links, manage products and prices, and analyse revenue.
  • Asana. Ask what is at risk across your projects, and the next steps land back in Asana as tasks.
  • Canva. Bring your brand templates into the conversation and go from an idea to an editable design.

The full named fifteen are Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom. In plain terms, that is project tracking, file storage, design, meeting notes, a customer platform popular with agencies, email and text message marketing, documents and wikis, your online store, team chat, payments, and video meetings. On top of those sit the Meta properties: your Facebook Page, your Instagram professional account and its analytics, and your Meta ad accounts. Meta says dozens more tools that businesses already run on are available beyond the named fifteen, with the full list visible inside the app settings.

One warning, because it saves real confusion later: an announcement is not the same as a live connector in your account. Rollout is staged, lists published by different outlets disagree with each other, and some announced partners were still not live weeks after being named. Our Muse connectors list keeps a dated record of what is live, what is announced but not yet live, and how to check your own account.

What it costs, honestly

The business offer uses the ordinary Muse plans, as CNBC confirmed at launch. There is no business tier and no per seat pricing.

  • Free. A weekly usage limit that refreshes. Meta does not publish the free limit as a number. Mark Zuckerberg has said the free tier runs up to 100 million tokens a week.
  • Power, $20 a month. 500 million Muse tokens a week.
  • Maximum, $100 a month. 3 billion Muse tokens a week.

Three things owners should know before those numbers impress them. First, the paid tiers buy usage volume only. Meta lists no feature differences between them, so Power and Maximum are about how much work you can hand over, not about unlocking business tools. Second, Meta publishes no conversion between tokens and tasks, so nobody can honestly tell you how many invoices or email audits 500 million tokens buys. Anyone who gives you a precise tasks per plan number is guessing. Third, as TechCrunch reported, a payment card is required even to start on the free tier. Our Muse tokens explained guide goes deeper on how the token system works.

The control model: nothing moves without your approval

The sentence Meta repeats in every business context is this: nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval. That is the architecture, not a slogan. A separate agent called Sentinel gates outbound actions, so the Muse that drafts is not the Muse that releases. Your credentials for connected tools sit in secure storage that the agent itself never sees, and there is a full audit trail of what was done, so you can review a week of activity the way you would review a staff member's work.

Two settings deserve attention on day one. The training opt out exists, and Meta says your Muse conversations and the data on its cloud computer are not shared with Meta's ad systems. Both are worth confirming in your own settings rather than taking from an article, including this one. The control model is also the honest answer to the most common owner question in the launch discussions: does it post as me without asking? No. Drafts, yes. Publishing, sending, and spending wait for you.

Setup, step by step

Screens are still rolling out and labels may differ slightly between accounts, so treat this as the order that matters rather than a pixel perfect map. The order is the part worth getting right, because connecting everything at once is how owners end up approving things they have not read.

  1. Get Muse itself. The business offer lives inside the regular app on iPhone, Android, Mac, iPad, and the web. Access runs on invite codes in the United States and Canada. If you do not have an account yet, get a code from the community pool or join the waitlist for your country, then come back here.
  2. Open Settings and turn on the business skills. Same app, same settings screen. This is the switch that makes the business offer visible.
  3. Connect your Meta properties first. Your Facebook Page, Instagram professional account, and ad account connect through your existing Meta accounts and unlock the fastest wins, performance reviews and drafted posts in your own voice.
  4. Add one money tool. QuickBooks or Stripe, whichever holds the truth about your cash. Money questions are where owners feel the value first.
  5. Add one work tool. Asana, Notion, Slack, or whichever tool your week actually runs on. One at a time, always.
  6. Run a read only job first. Ask for a review or a summary, not an action. A monthly money review or a Page performance report lets you judge the quality of Muse's work with nothing at stake.
  7. Approve actions one by one. Let the approval habit form before the volume grows. The audit trail is there for your weekly review.

First jobs worth running

Start with jobs that are easy to judge and hard to damage. These four come straight from what Meta demonstrated and what independent testers have run:

  • A Page and Instagram performance review. Ask what worked, what flopped, and what to post next week. An independent hands on test by AI Agents Library in October 2026 ran a full Facebook workflow on the free plan: the Page analytics Muse produced matched Meta Business Suite figures exactly, captions sounded like the owner because they were drawn from her past posts, and a scheduled post went live. Across four tests the workflow averaged 7.9 out of 10, and the verdict was a good supervised assistant, not an unsupervised publisher.
  • An email automation audit. If customers get your messages from more than one tool, ask Muse to find overlaps before your customers find them for you. This is the job from the Ohio story below.
  • A QuickBooks monthly review. What came in, what went out, and which expenses look odd. It is one of Meta's own example tasks, and it is read only by nature.
  • Stripe invoice follow up. Ask who owes you, since when, and for drafts of the follow up messages with payment links. You approve every send.

Once those feel routine, the bigger delegation is a growth plan. Meta's own example is to hand Muse a goal, such as finding new customers, and let it build the plan from your sales, campaigns, and social data. Goals run in the background and check in for approvals, which is where the approval model earns its keep.

What it did for three real businesses

Meta named three early testers at launch, and their stories are worth more than any feature list, with one honest label: these are Meta's chosen examples, quoted by Meta and CNBC, not independent audits.

The Iowa grocer. Tom Mulholland of Mulholland Grocery photographed his employee time cards and had Muse total them, cutting at least 15 minutes from payroll. No integration, no project. A phone camera and a weekly chore.

The Ohio shoe boutique. April Polk of JaeLuxe Shoetique let Muse audit her email automations. It found duplicate flows that were emailing customers twice, rebuilt the templates, and turned on tracking. This is the audit job above, with the receipts.

The Delaware orchard. Henry Bennett of Bennett Orchard uses Muse for frost protection data and food safety compliance paperwork, the seasonal, document heavy work that small farms drown in.

Notice what the three have in common. None of them is a growth hack. All three are recurring chores with a clear right answer, handed to an agent that asks before it acts. That is the shape of work Muse for Small Business is actually good at today.

The honest limits

One account per email provider. The Gmail and Outlook connectors each hold a single account, and no shared mailbox or helpdesk connector has been named. If your business runs on a shared inbox your whole team answers, Muse cannot sit in it today. This is the limit rival products attack most directly, and it is real.

You are the bottleneck, by design. Approval per action means throughput is capped by how often you check your phone. Work that must start the moment a client writes in does not fit this model. Muse for Small Business is a diligent assistant with a supervisor, and the supervisor is you.

Rollout is uneven. Announced and live are different lists, as the PayPal delay showed, and your account may simply not have a connector yet. Check the dated connectors list before planning around any single tool.

Performance drops away from home. Review roundups found Muse strongest inside Meta's own properties and weaker outside them, and platforms can push back entirely: Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on September 20, 2026. Connect the tools your week depends on with that in mind, keep payments last on your connection list, and build the audit habit before you build volume. One question from the launch discussions, should you connect bank or payment accounts on day one, answers itself once you have read this section: no. Earn trust with read only work first.
