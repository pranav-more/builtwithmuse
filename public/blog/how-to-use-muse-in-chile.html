Is Muse available in Chile?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse works in five markets: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. An availability guide verified on November 10, 2026 lists the same five and nothing else. Chile is not one of them, and Meta has announced no date for Chile, no date for any other South American country, and no order in which countries will follow.

If you search for Muse in the Chilean App Store or Google Play store, you will not find it. The listing is published only in launched markets, and the web route ends the same way: muse.ai loads, and sign in stops you with a message that Muse is not available in your country or region yet. That is the expected behavior outside the five markets, not a problem with your device.

Meta publishes no country list and no expansion timeline. Its help pages say only that Muse subscriptions are in limited testing and are not available in all locations yet. A country list checked against Meta's own pages repeats the same position. Treat any Chile date you see as invented unless Meta itself announces it.

What did the November store check find?

On November 7, 2026, a Spanish language guide ran the same storefront check for the third time in ten days, this time across 32 App Stores. Muse appeared in five: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico, where it had just arrived under the name Muse de Meta. It did not appear in the other 27, and Chile was on the absent list.

The repetition is what makes the check trustworthy. The same guide had checked 13 stores on October 29 and 24 stores on October 31, and Chile was absent both times. A single check can catch a store mid update; three checks across ten days, all showing the same absence, is a pattern.

You can run the same test yourself in seconds, and you should, because your own store is the authority for your own phone. When Chile launches, the listing will appear in the Chilean store the way Mexico's did on November 5. Until that day, the store is telling you the truth and the rumor pages are not.

Why do launches arrive without warning?

Every Muse launch so far was announced the day it happened. Japan arrived on October 23 through Meta's Japanese newsroom, Singapore followed on October 28, and Mexico appeared on November 5. New countries have arrived through regional announcements first, while Meta's older global pages still lag behind: the muse.ai business page said the US and Canada long after Japan launched.

That pattern is why this guide gives no Chile date and no Chile window. There is no pipeline to read, no beta list to join, and no regional announcement to watch for Chile specifically. The next country will most likely arrive the same way: a same day announcement, a store listing that appears, and coverage catching up afterwards.

The reason for the pace came from the top. Asked on the October 21, 2026 earnings call why the rollout was slow, Mark Zuckerberg pointed to two things: the cost of running a compute intensive service, and the work of tailoring the product to each country, connectors included. Chile needs both problems solved, and neither has a public timetable.

What does Spanish support look like?

Spanish is the one question with a confident answer. The Muse App Store listing includes Spanish among 39 languages, and Spanish speakers in the US already use the agent in Spanish. Mexico then launched in Spanish, which settled the question of whether Meta ships a Spanish language Muse: it does.

The longer precedent is Meta AI. In July 2024, Meta brought Meta AI to seven new countries, including Chile, and added Spanish support across WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, and Facebook. That expansion covered Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Peru, and Cameroon. It was a different product, but it shows the shape of Meta's Spanish rollout: Chile was in the first Spanish language wave.

What remains unconfirmed is the exact language set on a Chile launch day, because Meta has published no language list for Muse. Spanish is a near certainty given the store listing and the Mexico launch. Any other language, including Chilean Spanish idiom in voice features, is untested and unconfirmed, and this guide will not grade what it cannot test.

Can you reach Muse through WhatsApp in Chile?

No. The Muse chat inside WhatsApp checks the same country list as the app, so it is closed in Chile too. The confusion is understandable: WhatsApp is where most Chileans already meet a Meta assistant, because Meta AI has lived inside WhatsApp in Chile since July 2024. But Meta AI is a chat assistant, and Muse is a personal agent that completes tasks across your accounts. They share a parent company, not a rollout. The comparison guide explains the difference.

Mexico shows how the WhatsApp route arrives: the launch coverage said Muse would be usable from WhatsApp, without giving a date for that part. So even in a launched market, the WhatsApp surface can lag the app. In Chile, both are still to come, and the app will almost certainly arrive first.

If a WhatsApp number or contact in Chile claims to be Muse today, it is not Meta's product. The real route will ask you to connect a Muse account through Meta's own entry points, and it will not ask for payment or personal documents over chat. Until Meta announces Chile, treat every such contact as a scam risk.

What about a VPN?

The honest answer is that no method is verified, and the mechanism explains why. Three signals decide the check, and all three are server side: the country on your Meta account, which is set in your account settings and does not move when you change phones; your apparent network location, which Meta compares against the account country; and your store region. A VPN touches only the second of the three.

A mismatch between those signals can read as unavailable, or as try again later, or can trip risk checks on the account. That is why VPN tutorials disagree with each other: the same steps produce different results depending on the account, the store, and the payment method attached to it. No tutorial can control all three.

This guide recommends waiting, not because waiting is pleasant, but because the alternative risks the Meta account that holds your Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. Workarounds that misrepresent your region sit against Meta's terms, and Meta has published no procedure for getting an account back after an enforcement action. The wait ends with Meta's announcement, and nothing you install will bring it forward.

What would Muse cost in Chile?

The figures people quote for Chile are borrowed from the US, because Chile has none of its own: Maximum at $100 a month, Power at $20 a month, and a free tier with a weekly usage limit. Paying buys weekly usage capacity, not features, so the agent is the same on every tier. None of this is a Chile price, and Meta has announced no Chile price.

Two details need care. First, the free tier figure of 100 million tokens a week comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, not from Meta's help pages, so it is reported, not published. Second, Meta's subscriptions page describes Muse as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, which means the offer you eventually see in Chile may differ from the US offer.

Do not convert the dollar figures into pesos and present the result as the Chile price. No peso price exists, no Chile specific price has been announced, and the only figure that will bind you is the one on the checkout screen inside your own account. If you plan a budget, plan in dollars and treat $20 a month as the reference point, not a promise.

Which setting should you change before your first chat?

There is one choice to make before the agent hears anything from you, and it is easy to forget in the excitement of launch day. Open Settings, then Data controls, and turn off "Help improve our AI models". Confirm "Turn off" in the dialog. From then on, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models.

The timing matters more than the tap. The setting applies from the moment you change it, so chats from before the change fall under the old rule. Set it in the first minutes of your account, before you connect an inbox or hand the agent a real job, and everything from that point forward is covered.

Nothing about the agent gets worse when you turn it off. The connectors, the approvals, and the tasks all work the same. The switch is purely about your words: whether Meta may learn from them. Make the choice deliberately, on day one, and you will not have to think about it again.

What connectors would a Chile launch need?

A Muse launch is not just an app listing. Each market needs connector work, the integrations that let the agent act inside local services, and Meta has said that tailoring is part of what slows expansion. On the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg said the delay was partly about running Muse efficiently and partly about tailoring the product to each country, including work on connectors. Connector work is part of the delay.

Mexico shows what that tailoring looks like. Its launch named seven Mexican connectors, from cinema tickets to department stores, alongside global ones like Gmail and Calendar. The Mexico guide covers that launch. No Chilean service has been named as a Muse connector, and no Chilean connector list exists. The global connectors, Gmail, Calendar, and Instagram, would work in Chile on day one, because they are not country specific. Local ones would need to be built and announced.

That makes connectors a useful launch signal. When launch coverage for a new market starts naming local services, the launch is close. For Chile, the list is empty, which is one more piece of evidence that no launch is imminent. Watch the connector names, not the rumor dates.
