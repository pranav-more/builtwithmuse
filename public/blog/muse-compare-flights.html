How to Use Muse to Compare Flights

Give Muse your route and dates and get flight options laid out side by side: price, times, stops, and baggage. Here is how to get a comparison you can trust.

1. The short answer

Flight shopping is twelve tabs and a headache. The price jumps at checkout, the "cheap" flight lands at 2am after a six hour layover, and you are never sure the fare you saw still exists. Tell Muse where you are going, when, and what matters to you: nonstop only, morning departures, checked bag included. It searches options and lays them out in a table so you can compare price against times, stops, and baggage rules instead of juggling tabs.

The rest of this guide covers how to ask well, what to verify before you book, and where Muse has blind spots.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first.
  • Your trip basics. Route, dates, and how flexible you are. Flexible dates get you better prices, so say so when it is true. Even a day or two of flex can change the fare.
  • Your preferences, stated plainly. Cabin, baggage needs, airlines you avoid, red eye tolerance. Muse cannot read your mind, so state them. "I will not take a red eye" is a complete and useful sentence.
  • Your dealbreakers. Separate what you prefer from what you refuse. A preference bends for the right price. A dealbreaker does not. Muse can only respect the line if you draw it.
  • Your baggage reality. Do you actually check a bag, or is that "one checked bag" just aspirational? Fares that look cheap often assume you travel with nothing. Tell Muse the truth and the comparison stays honest.

3. How to ask for a comparison that works

Vague asks get vague answers. "Find me cheap flights to Singapore" gets you a list. A detailed brief gets you a decision. Here is the sequence that works.

  1. Be specific up front. Mumbai to Singapore, December 12 to 18, nonstop preferred, one checked bag, morning departure if possible. One detailed message beats five vague ones. Include the things you assume are obvious, because they are not obvious to anyone but you.
  2. Ask for a table. Say: lay out the options in a table with price, airline, departure and arrival times, stops, total duration, and baggage included. Tables make tradeoffs visible. A paragraph hides the 4am arrival. A table does not.
  3. Ask about tradeoffs. Follow with: which of these is the best value and why. Muse will point out the cheap flight with the brutal layover versus the slightly pricier nonstop that gets you in before lunch. Best value is not the lowest number. It is the lowest number for a flight you would actually want to take.
  4. Test flexibility. If your dates can move, ask: how do prices change if I shift by a day or two. Ask about flying back a day later too. Return legs move prices just as much as outbound ones, and nobody checks.
  5. Ask it to flag the traps. Say: flag anything risky in these options. Overnight layovers, connections under an hour, arriving at one airport and departing from another across town. These are the details that turn a bargain into a story you tell with regret.

Travelers: [1 adult]
Cabin: [economy]

Do this in order.
1. Find current options and lay them out in a table with price, airline, departure and arrival times, stops, total duration, and baggage included.
2. Point out the tradeoffs in plain words: which option is the best value and why.
3. Check how the price changes if I shift my dates by a day or two.
4. Flag anything risky: overnight layovers, very short connections, or arriving and departing from different airports in the same city.

Do not book anything. I will book direct with the airline myself after I verify the price on the airline site.

4. Before you book: verify

Flight prices move fast, sometimes within the hour. Treat Muse as your researcher, not the checkout counter. When you have picked a flight, open the airline site and confirm the price, the times, and the baggage rules before you pay.

Screenshot the price Muse found before you leave the conversation. When the airline page shows a different number, you want to know exactly what changed instead of arguing with your memory.

Check three things on the airline page: the total price with bags and seats, the change and cancellation policy, and the layover length on connections. If the price Muse found does not match, the airline site wins. It is the live source, and the live source is the only one that charges your card.

When the price matches, book direct with the airline. It makes changes and disruptions far easier to handle than a third party booking. The few dollars a reseller saves you evaporate the first time you need to move a flight.

5. Where Muse has blind spots

  • Not every budget carrier appears. Some low cost airlines do not distribute everywhere. If you fly them often, check their site directly too.
  • Prices are a snapshot. What Muse found ten minutes ago may already have moved. Verify at booking time, and do not mourn a fare that is gone. Ask for a fresh check instead.
  • Complex itineraries need care. Multi city trips with tight connections deserve a human double check on layover times. If a connection looks short, it probably is.
  • Sale fares have short windows. A fare that expires tonight needs a decision tonight. If Muse surfaces something time sensitive, verify and decide in the same sitting instead of sleeping on it.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muse book the flight for me?
It can help you compare and get you to the booking page with details ready. You confirm and pay yourself, which keeps you in control of the money.
Is it better than a flight search site?
It is a different tool. Search sites list flights; Muse compares them against your actual preferences and explains the tradeoffs in plain words.
Can it track prices and alert me?
Ask it to check prices again in a few days and compare. For now, treat each check as a fresh snapshot.
Does it work for international flights?
Yes. Give it the full route including connections and it compares the same way.
What about hotels?
Pair this guide with our trip planning guide: Muse plans the whole trip, flights included, then you verify and book each part.
What if the price changed between Muse and the airline site?
The live site always wins. Do not book a fare you cannot see. Ask Muse to recheck the same route and see whether a nearby date or time beats the new price before you settle.
Is the cheapest flight actually the cheapest?
Add the bags, the seats, the meals, and your time. A slightly pricier nonstop with a bag included often beats the rock bottom fare once the extras pile on. Ask Muse for the total cost of each option, not just the headline fare.
Can Muse find mistake fares?
It compares the published options it can see. If you spot a mistake fare elsewhere, paste it in and ask Muse to check whether the routing and rules make sense before you book. Verify it is bookable before you get attached.
