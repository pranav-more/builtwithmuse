Is Muse available in Austria?

Austria is waiting, like every European country, and Meta has not broken the silence with a date. No launch date has been announced for Austria, and none has been announced for any European country. No Austrian listing exists, no Austrian statement names a date, and no leak has filled the gap. The wait is European, and Austria shares it in full.

Five markets have Muse. The United States, since September 8, 2026. Canada, since September 18. Japan, since October 23. Singapore, since October 28. Mexico, since November 5, the fifth market and the first in Latin America. Austria is not among them. Meta publishes no list of future markets and no timeline, and the current list is kept in our Muse available countries guide.

Availability trackers agree on the Austrian position. Checks in October found Muse listed only in the US and Canada, and a later check found no announcement for any of the 30 EEA countries, a group that includes Austria. The store tells the same story: the Austrian App Store carries no Muse listing, and an Austrian account that tries to sign up meets the waitlist. This guide was last checked on November 13, 2026.

Why is Austria still waiting? The EU rules

The European rules explain the shape of the wait, even though Meta has named no reason for it. Two laws do most of the work: the GDPR, which has governed personal data in the EU since 2018, and the EU AI Act, which entered into force on August 1, 2024.

The AI Act’s obligations for general purpose AI models have applied since August 2, 2025, with Article 50 transparency duties following from August 2, 2026, as one analysis of Muse’s first month details. The Act does not prohibit agentic assistants. It requires transparency, documentation, and risk management that a product with access to messages, email, calendar, and files must build in before launch, not after. Meta’s record shows it takes that sequencing seriously: in 2024 it kept its multimodal model out of the EU, citing an unpredictable regulatory environment, according to a November 2026 look at the European wait.

The Meta AI precedent shows what the sequencing costs in time. Meta AI launched in the US in 2023 and reached Europe on March 20, 2025, about 18 months later, after Ireland’s regulator asked Meta to delay training on European users’ public posts, as TechCrunch reported and an availability tracker followed. Austria was in the European rollout, in German, from day one. The precedent is not a timetable for Muse, and this guide does not treat it as one. It is evidence that European AI launches take longer, and that the delay is usually about data.

Austria adds no national law on top of the EU rules that names Muse or blocks it. The Austrian wait is the EU wait, and it ends with a European announcement. What Austria does add is something else entirely: the organization that has fought Meta hardest over AI training is based in Vienna, and that fight is the subject of the next section.

Vienna is where the privacy fight is run from

If you want to understand why Meta is careful about AI in Europe, start in Vienna. noyb, the European Center for Digital Rights, is based in Vienna and was founded by Max Schrems in 2017, as TechBullion’s profile notes. It is the organization that has forced Meta to pause, revise, and defend its AI training plans in public, twice, and it is still filing.

The first round was June 2024. On June 6, noyb filed complaints with 11 European data protection authorities, including Austria’s, over Meta’s plan to use personal data to train AI models without asking for consent, as DataGuidance reported. The complaints asked for urgent action before the policy change took effect on June 26. On June 14, 2024, Meta paused the training plan across Europe after Ireland’s Data Protection Commission intervened, according to a summary of the sequence. Training resumed in 2025 under a revised plan with an objection process, approved by Ireland’s regulator in May 2025, according to an availability tracker.

The second round is happening now. On October 27, 2026, noyb filed five GDPR complaints against Scale AI, over surveillance of workers on its Outlier platform, with regulators in France, Italy, the Netherlands, and Austria, two of the five going to Austria, as the Sowetan reported. Meta owns 49 percent of Scale AI, bought in June 2025. The complaints are about worker data, not about Muse, and this guide does not claim otherwise. They show that the organization watching Meta’s data practices is active, Austrian, and filing in the same weeks Muse is expanding.

The same day, October 27, 2026, brought a proposal that would change the rules noyb fights under. EU member states backed a Digital Omnibus proposal, Article 88bis, that would treat AI training as a legitimate interest under the GDPR, making it easier to train models on Europeans’ personal data without consent, as Cybernews reported. Schrems opposed it in strong terms, and the proposal still needs the European Parliament’s position. It is a proposal, not adopted law, and it may face court challenges. Austrian users should know it exists, because it is the direction the law might move, and because the argument about it is being led from Vienna.

None of this gives Austria a Muse date. It gives Austrian users context that users in most countries do not have: the privacy argument over AI training is a local story, run from their capital, by an organization that has already changed Meta’s behavior once. The practical lesson is the same as everywhere: when you get Muse, turn off “Help improve our AI models” in Settings under Data controls before you connect anything, and know that the setting exists because the argument above was fought.

Will Muse speak German?

Meta has said nothing about German language support for Muse. No announcement, no help page, and no launch report names German for Muse. The honest position is unconfirmed, and the context, while encouraging, does not change it.

German was one of the six languages in the Meta AI European launch on March 20, 2025, alongside English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, and Italian, as TechCrunch reported at the time. Austria got Meta AI in German from the first day of the European rollout, and Germany, the largest German speaking market, got it the same day. German is also one of the languages Meta supports most deeply across its apps. If any language beyond English is a safe bet for a European Muse launch, German is the obvious candidate. A safe bet is still a bet.

The complication is that Germany and Austria share a language but not a launch. Germany is also waiting, with no date, and our Germany guide covers its position. A German language Muse would serve both countries at once, which argues for German being ready when Europe launches. It does not argue for Austria launching before Germany, or Germany before Austria. The language is shared. The launch decision is per country, or per European wave, and Meta has announced neither.

Austrian German deserves a mention. Everyday Austrian vocabulary differs from standard German in words, greetings, and food names, and a Muse trained mostly on the German used in Germany may sound slightly foreign in Vienna. That is a quality question for launch day, not a barrier, and the test is the same as everywhere: write in the German you actually use, check the answers, check the actions. Do not assume a German launch means an Austrian German launch. Check it.

WhatsApp in Austria: the default

WhatsApp is Austria’s default messaging app, and the margin is wide. Sensor Tower’s Q4 2025 figures put WhatsApp at about 6.7 million weekly active users in Austria, in a country of about 9 million people, while Messenger managed about 965,000, in Sensor Tower’s Austria data. WhatsApp also tops the free Communication chart on Google Play in Austria, in AppBrain’s November 12, 2026 ranking.

That dominance shapes how Muse would reach Austria. The Mexico launch included WhatsApp as a surface, alongside iOS, Android, and the web, and our WhatsApp guide covers what Muse does there. In Austria, where WhatsApp is where daily life is coordinated, the WhatsApp surface would likely be the first place most users meet the agent, ahead of the standalone app. None of that is confirmed for Austria, but the market shape points that way.

The Meta AI assistant already inside Austrian WhatsApp accounts is a different product from Muse. Meta AI answers questions. Muse takes actions across apps, as our Muse vs Meta AI guide explains. Austrian users who have chatted with Meta AI in WhatsApp have experienced the assistant, not the agent, and the gap between the two is the whole pitch for Muse: not a better answer, but a finished task.

WhatsApp’s dominance also means the language question plays out inside WhatsApp first. A Muse that arrives in WhatsApp in German would reach nearly every Austrian smartphone owner through the app they already open most. A Muse that arrives in English only would still reach them, but through an app they use in German. The language of the WhatsApp surface, when it comes, will tell you how seriously Meta took the Austrian launch.

Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Austria

The voice assistant reached Austria on glasses long before any agent reached Austria on a phone. Meta AI voice on Ray-Ban Meta glasses came to Austria on April 23, 2025, in a European expansion to seven countries that also took in Sweden, Finland, Norway, Denmark, Belgium, and Germany, as Meta’s announcement records. Austrian owners of the glasses have therefore had Meta’s voice assistant for well over a year, in a product sold in Austria, while Muse itself has no Austrian presence at all.

The glasses show what Meta’s voice assistant can do, and they show it in German, in Austria, today. What they do not show is Muse. Muse is a standalone agent, not a voice layer, and the Muse voice avatar that rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, is US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers. The distinction, covered in our Muse vs Meta AI guide, is easy to blur when the same company puts an assistant on your face and an agent on your phone. Keep them separate.

The expansion still tells you something useful: Meta ships AI hardware features to Austria on the same day as the largest EU markets. Austria is not a late market for Meta’s glasses, and there is no reason to expect it to be a late market for a European Muse wave either. That is a reasonable inference from one data point, and it is labeled as one here. It is not a date, and it is not a promise.

If you own Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Austria, the voice assistant is your nearest preview of Meta’s AI. Use it, judge it, and notice where it stops. The stopping points, the tasks that need access to your calendar, your email, and your files, are the tasks Muse is built to finish.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Austria?

Search for a way in from Austria and the VPN tutorial is what you find. Routing traffic through a launch country is a real technique, and it is the wrong tool for this product, because Muse looks past traffic at the account itself. This guide recommends no tutorial and promises that none of them works.

Muse checks your App Store region, your account country, your payment method, and the card used for age verification. Austrian cards and an Austrian store region identify Austria even when traffic is routed through a launch country, and the usual result is the waitlist screen. Our App Store guide walks through the store checks.

The account risk is stated as a caution, as it is in every guide in this series, because that is what the evidence supports. Meta may suspend or close an account that shows a location mismatch, according to user reports and guides. No Meta policy has been published that confirms a ban for VPN use, and no source has proven it safe. If you try a workaround, use an account that holds nothing important.

Austria adds a particular irony to the workaround question. The country whose privacy organization has fought hardest to make Meta respect European data rules is also a country where users are tempted to bypass Meta’s own geographic rules. This guide takes no moral position on that. It takes a practical one: a workaround account is configured for another country, in another market, with another country’s connectors, and it gives you a worse version of the product, at risk, sooner, maybe. The Austrian launch, when it comes, gives you the real version, in German, with Austrian services, on a safe account. Wait for it.

What will Muse cost in Austria?

Ask what Muse costs in Austria, and the only truthful answer is that nobody outside Meta knows, because Meta has announced nothing. There is no euro price for Austria, no price in any currency for the Austrian market, and no hint in any launch statement. The reported prices belong to the United States: use without payment inside a weekly allowance, $20 a month for Power, and $100 a month for Maximum. Paying does not unlock extra features. It unlocks extra capacity, so all three tiers run the same agent, and the difference is how much of it you can use in a week.

Austria’s price will be a euro price, and it will probably match the euro price in Germany and every other eurozone country. That is an inference about how Meta prices, labeled as one here, not a fact. No euro figure has been announced for any country, and converting $20 does not produce one. The reported India pricing, Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month, reported by The Hindu on November 9, 2026, and unconfirmed by Meta, shows Meta sets local prices market by market.

The free tier exists, and its exact size is undocumented. That combination is worth stating plainly. The 100 million tokens a week figure that appears in coverage of the free tier originated in Mark Zuckerberg’s launch statement, and Meta’s help pages have not turned it into a documented limit. Instead, the help pages describe subscriptions as being in limited testing and not available in all locations, as one availability guide notes. Treat the weekly figure as a launch claim that may be confirmed, revised, or replaced when Austria launches, and treat any site that states it as a fixed Austrian allowance as guessing. The launch announcement is where the real figure will appear, if Meta chooses to publish one.

Austrian users should expect a free tier, a euro price announced with the launch, and nothing before then. The price is the easiest question in this guide, and the answer is that nobody outside Meta knows it yet.

What will launch day look like in Austria?

The likeliest shape of an Austrian launch is a wave, not a solo date. Meta AI reached 41 countries in one wave in March 2025, and a European Muse launch would plausibly arrive the same way, with Austria inside it alongside the German speaking markets. Plausibly is the key word: no source has placed Austria in any wave, and the only announcement worth trusting is one that names Austria or lists it among the launch countries.

Keep a card in your hand when you open the app for the first time. Muse is for users 18 and older, and the age check is the first thing it asks for, satisfied by a payment card or by linking Instagram or Facebook. Paying by card triggers a $1 temporary authorization, refunded in 5 to 7 business days, and the check has been unforgiving since October, with prepaid and gift cards failing often, as our age verification guide shows. After the check, the order is setting then code: turn off “Help improve our AI models” in Settings under Data controls, confirming “Turn off” when asked, connect nothing yet, and redeem an invite code within 48 hours of creating the account to collect the 1 billion token bonus on both sides.

Then test the German. Write in the German you actually use, including Austrian words and phrasing, and check whether Muse understands, answers, and acts correctly. Test one connection and one small job before connecting more. Check whether the connectors you need work with Austrian services, because connector availability is announced per market.

Do not judge the Austrian launch by the American feature list. Voice stays US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers, and outbound calling stays with paid US subscribers, as our calling guide explains. Spaces is different: launched November 6, 2026, it should be available everywhere Muse is available, as our Spaces guide explains. An Austrian launch with a working agent, German support, WhatsApp as a surface, and the core connectors would be a strong start, and the rest can follow in the months after.
