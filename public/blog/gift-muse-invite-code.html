How to Gift a Muse Invite Code to a Friend

The best part of getting into Muse is bringing your friends with you. Gifting a code takes a minute, and when your friend redeems it, you both get the token bonus.

1. Get your own referral code

Before you can gift a code, you need one. Once you are inside Muse and signed in, you get a referral code of your own. It is yours to share, and every person who redeems it earns a token bonus for both of you: 1 billion Muse tokens for them and 1 billion for you, according to Muse's current invite copy. If you have not found yours yet, the walkthrough in how to find and share your Muse referral code shows where to look.

Save your code somewhere you can reach in seconds, like your phone's notes app. Gifting usually happens in the moment, when a friend asks how you got in or mentions wanting to try it. If you have to dig through menus while they wait, the moment passes. Keep the code as text, not as a screenshot, so you can paste it cleanly wherever you share it.

One thing to know up front: your code identifies you as the referrer. That is how the bonus finds its way back to you. Send the exact code. A single wrong character sends your friend to a dead end and sends your bonus nowhere.

2. Send it to your friend

There is no ceremony to it. Text the code, drop it in a chat, read it out over a call, whatever matches how you two normally share things. What matters is not the channel. It is what travels with the code.

Always send three things together: the code itself as plain text, the instruction to redeem it in Settings within 48 hours of joining, and a link to the full walkthrough at how to redeem a Muse invite code. The second item is the one people forget, so say it twice. Most failed gifts are not bad codes. They are good codes that sat unread past the window.

Timing matters too. Send the code when your friend can act on it now, not when they are heading into a busy week. A code sent on a relaxed evening gets redeemed. A code sent before a work trip gets buried. If you read the code out loud, spell the tricky characters. If you send it in a chat, paste it as its own message so nothing else gets copied with it. Extra words stuck to the code are a classic source of typos on the receiving end.

3. What your friend needs to do

Your friend's side takes a few minutes. Install the Muse app from the official app store, sign in, then redeem your code in Settings within 48 hours of joining, according to Muse's current invite copy. That is the whole process. There is nothing for you to do on their phone. Your part ended when you sent the code well.

The order matters. They must join and sign in first, then redeem. A code entered before sign in has no account to attach to, so if your friend is the type to install now and figure it out later, tell them plainly: the figuring out later part is what kills the bonus. The redeem takes a minute. Everything else can wait.

Set expectations about the bonus too. When they redeem, both of you receive 1 billion Muse tokens each. Tell them that up front. It turns a favor into a shared win, and people act faster when they know something is waiting on the other side. If your friend hits any snag, point them to the redeem walkthrough rather than troubleshooting blind. The common fixes are all covered there.

4. You both get the bonus

Here is the part worth repeating: when your friend redeems your code, both of you receive 1 billion Muse tokens each, according to Muse's current invite copy. Not one side, both sides. Gifting a code is never a sacrifice. It is the fastest way to grow your own balance too, because every redeem pays you again.

Think about what that means at scale. One friend is a nice bonus. Five friends who all redeem is five bonuses landing in your account while you did nothing beyond sending a few texts. The bonuses stack, and tokens never expire, so there is no penalty for collecting them faster than you can spend them. There is also no downside to the friend. They lose nothing by using your code instead of joining without one. It is pure upside on both ends, which is exactly why the reminder is worth sending.

If you want to understand what the tokens actually do, Muse tokens explained covers it in full. The short version: they are the fuel for everything in the app, and a billion of them is an enormous head start for anyone just joining.

5. Do not stop at one friend

One gifted code is a favor. A shared code is a system. Add your code to the community pool on this site's homepage so strangers can grab it, and mention it wherever people ask how to get into Muse. Every redeem earns the bonus again, for you and for them, and the bonuses stack up while you do nothing at all.

Share where sharing is welcome. A reply under someone asking how to get a code is welcome. Dropping your code uninvited into unrelated conversations is spam, and it trains people to ignore you. The community pool exists precisely so your code can sit where seekers already look, without you chasing anyone.

Keep a simple habit: whenever someone in your circle mentions Muse, offer the code before they ask twice. Early access products spread through exactly these small handoffs. You were someone's handoff once. Be someone else's.

6. A message you can copy and send

Most people overthink the note that goes with the code. Keep it short and include everything your friend needs. Here is a template. Replace the bracketed parts and send it as is.

Template: Here is my Muse invite code: [YOUR CODE]. Install the Muse app, sign in, then enter the code in Settings within 48 hours of joining. Do it right away because the window is short. When you redeem it, we both get 1 billion Muse tokens. Full steps here: [link to the redeem guide].

Why this works: it gives the code, the deadline, the reward, and the guide in one screen of text. Your friend never has to ask a follow up question to get started, and the urgency is stated as a fact, not a nag. Save this template in your notes app next to your code so the next gift takes ten seconds.

7. Mistakes that cost people the bonus

Sending only a screenshot. Screenshots of codes get cropped, compressed, and misread. Always send the code as text your friend can copy. A screenshot as a backup is fine. A screenshot as the only copy is asking for a typo.

Forgetting the 48 hour note. The code without the deadline is a gift with a countdown nobody mentioned. Every code you send should carry the 48 hour instruction attached. No exceptions.

Sending it and going silent. If your friend has not redeemed by the next day, one reminder is not nagging. It is the difference between a bonus and a missed window. Send the reminder, then let it go.

Gifting to someone who cannot join yet. If Muse has not launched in your friend's country, the app may not be in their store, and the code cannot help them yet. Meta has not published a full list of launch countries, so if they cannot find the app, point them to the waitlist instead of troubleshooting a code that was never the problem.

8. How to follow up without being annoying

One reminder is helpful. Three is pressure. Here is the rhythm that works: send the code, then check in once the next day with a single short message. Something like: hey, did you get a chance to redeem that code? The window is 48 hours so it is worth doing today. That is it. If they do it, great. If they do not, the ball is in their court.

Do not resend a fresh code every few days hoping a new one resets anything. The 48 hour window is tied to when they join, not to how recently you sent the code. A new code does not buy a new window. What earns the bonus is redeeming promptly after joining, with any valid code.

9. Sharing publicly versus sharing privately

Private sharing is for people you know: friends, family, coworkers. It converts well because the trust is already there, and your one reminder carries weight. Public sharing is for everyone else: the community pool, replies to people asking how to get in, posts in communities where invite codes are welcome.

Both pay the same bonus. The difference is effort. Private sharing needs your attention per person. Public sharing needs your code in the right places once, and then it works while you sleep. Do both. Gift to your circle first, then park your code in the pool for strangers.

One rule covers both: never charge for a code. Codes are meant to be free, and the token bonus is already your reward for sharing. Selling codes burns trust with the very people you want in your circle. Keep it free. More on why in can you buy a Muse invite code.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find my referral code?
It lives inside Muse once you are signed in. The sharing guide linked in this article walks through finding it.
What does my friend need besides the code?
Just the app and a few minutes: install Muse, sign in, and redeem the code in Settings within 48 hours of joining.
What if my friend misses the 48 hour window?
Do not risk finding out. The window is short, so the rule is simple: redeem the moment you join.
Is there a limit on how many people I can invite?
There is no limit we know of on sharing your code. Each redeem earns the token bonus for both sides.
Do I really get tokens for every friend who joins?
Yes. According to Muse's current invite copy, each redeem gives 1 billion Muse tokens to the referrer and 1 billion to the new user.
Can my friend use my code if we live in different countries?
Meta has not published the full regional rules for invite codes. The practical test is simple: if your friend can find the Muse app in their store and join, redeeming follows the same steps. If the app is not available where they live, have them join the waitlist and try the code at launch.
What if two friends redeem my code?
You get the bonus twice, once per redeem. Every redeem pays 1 billion tokens to the new joiner and 1 billion to you, so there is no reason to ration your code among friends.
Can I gift a code to someone who already uses Muse?
Invite codes are for people joining Muse. If your friend is already in and has redeemed before, a second code is unlikely to do anything for either of you. Save your sharing energy for people who have not joined yet.
Can I charge money for my invite code?
No. Codes are meant to be free, and the token bonus is already your reward for sharing. Selling codes burns trust with the very people you want in your circle. Keep it free.
