Can Muse call your customers?

Short answer: no. Muse calls businesses on your behalf, not your customers, and it refuses personal numbers. The short answer

No. Muse does not call your customers, and it does not call your leads. The phone calling feature Meta added to Muse in September 2026 points the other way: Muse calls businesses on your behalf, to book things, check things, and sit on hold so you do not have to. It is a consumer feature, built for the person receiving a service, not a sales or support tool for the business providing it. Testers report that it refuses personal numbers, which rules out your customer list by design.

That answer matters because the question is everywhere. Since the feature appeared, one of the most common business questions about Muse has been some version of the title of this page. The honest answer is no, and this guide explains what the feature does instead, what real users have managed with it, the two capability questions nobody has documented yet, the week some of the calls were secretly made by people, and what businesses that need their phones handled actually use.

What the calling feature actually is

Meta added outbound phone calling to Muse on September 17, 2026, describing it through staff posts as the most requested feature since launch. There has been no formal launch announcement and no help article from Meta documenting it. The public record is staff posts, reporting by outlets such as TechCrunch and Reuters, and a growing pile of user reports. That lack of documentation matters, and it comes up again below.

What the feature does, based on that record: Muse places calls to businesses in the United States, on your instructions. The jobs users give it are the errands of everyday life. Restaurant and appointment bookings. Stock checks at stores. Quotes from contractors. Customer service lines, including the long hold queues nobody wants to sit in. Bill negotiation. It works in the same spirit as the rest of Muse: you describe the outcome, the agent does the legwork, and anything sensitive comes back to you for approval.

Access is gated. Calling is rolling out in stages behind a waitlist, and users report that you can simply ask Muse to put you on the waiting list for it. Weeks after the feature appeared, a large share of the public discussion was still people asking how others got in. It is also an opt in feature with clear limits: calls go to US businesses only, and testers consistently report that Muse refuses to dial personal numbers, including numbers you provide from your own contacts.

When Muse calls, it does not pretend to be you. The caller ID shows META, and Muse introduces itself at the start of the call as an AI calling on your behalf, and says it is taking notes. Some users have tried to make it sound less formal. The businesses on the other end know an agent is calling.

What real users report

The richest source of evidence so far is a discussion in r/MuseAgent, the Reddit community for Muse users, where one member asked whether anyone had actually used Muse to call a company customer service line on their behalf. The answers were specific, and mixed in the way real product reports usually are.

  • One user had Muse request a replacement EZ Pass transponder on their behalf, and the task completed, with Muse prompting them to sign in partway through.
  • Another had Muse call a doctor's office as a new patient and book an appointment. It took a couple of tries before it worked.
  • A third reported that a call to Amazon customer service went okay.

Review roundups of the launch period tell a similar story at greater length. Fast Company, summarising launch reviews, credited Muse with handling dental insurance hold times, requesting refunds, and cancelling duplicate subscriptions, all errands that run through phone calls and hold music. The pattern across every source is the same: simple, well defined calls usually land; anything unusual may need a second attempt; and the feature saves the most time exactly where a human loses the most, in the queue.

Two honest caveats belong here. First, half the conversation around calling is still people trying to get access at all, which tells you how staged the rollout is. Second, even users who have connected their email and calendar describe themselves as still figuring out what Muse is for. A feature can be real, useful, and badly explained at the same time, and calling today is all three.

The phone menu question nobody has answered

In late September 2026, a user in r/MetaAI asked a short question that gets to the heart of whether this feature can handle real business phone systems: does Meta Muse support keypad tones on its calls? Keypad tones, known technically as DTMF, are what your phone sends when a recorded voice says press 1 for sales or press 2 for support. A caller that cannot send them is stuck at the first menu of most large companies.

As of October 12, 2026, there is no official answer. Meta has published no specification for the calling feature at all: no help article, no list of supported call behaviours, nothing that says how Muse navigates a phone tree. What we can say from user reports is narrower. The completed calls described above, to a toll operator and to Amazon customer service, are calls that normally involve menus, so some form of menu handling clearly happens in practice. Whether that is keypad tones, spoken replies to menu prompts, or a mix, nobody outside Meta has documented. Treat simple menus as workable based on reports, and treat deep enterprise phone trees as unproven until Meta documents the feature.

The related question matters even more to the people asking it. Another r/MuseAgent user asked whether Muse can get through phone menus and transfer the call back to you when it reaches a real person, calling that probably the optimal use case for the whole feature. It is an obviously good idea: the agent waits, you talk. As of this writing, transfer back to the owner is not documented as a supported feature. Some workflow write ups describe Muse patching you in when a human is needed, but Meta documents nothing, so plan on Muse handling the entire call, start to finish, or make the call yourself when the conversation is one you need to join live.

The week some of the calls were made by people

On September 22, 2026, Reuters reported that Meta had quietly used trained human contractors to place some Muse calls when the AI kept failing at them, without telling users. Internal figures cited in the reporting put human success rates at 95 to 98 percent, above what the AI managed on its own. In one case a contractor made a racist remark during a bill negotiation call. A Meta vice president called the test a miss, and Meta rolled it back.

Why does a business reader need this story? Two reasons. The first is reliability: the calling feature is a young beta, and Meta itself reached for humans when the agent was not ready. Expect the occasional failed or fumbled call, and do not put a call that truly matters on it without a fallback. The second is transparency. Muse announces itself on every call, which is right. Meta did not announce the humans, which was not. When you delegate a conversation to an agent, knowing who, or what, is actually speaking is the minimum you are owed, and it is reasonable to hold this feature to that standard as it matures.

Will the business even take the call?

A call only helps if it connects. Reporting by CNET describes carriers learning to identify Muse AI calls and blocking some of them from reaching human agents. The caller ID reading META means the business, and its phone provider, can see an agent is calling before anyone picks up. Some will welcome it. Some phone systems are being tuned to keep agents out.

This sits inside a wider pattern of platforms pushing back on agent traffic. Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on its platform on September 20, 2026, calling it an unauthorised agent, and reservation service Resy has locked out agent users. None of this kills the calling feature, but a call that never reaches a human is a real failure mode, and it is one more reason to treat calling as a convenience for errands rather than infrastructure your business depends on.

The inbound gap, and the connector trying to fill it

Everything above is outbound. Muse has no phone number of its own, and it cannot receive calls. If a customer rings your business hoping to book, ask, or buy, nothing answers. For a business owner, that is the actual gap, and Meta does not currently fill it.

Third parties are trying. A developer has built a connector that gives Muse a phone number for inbound and outbound calling to the United States and Canada, sold as a Muse Receptionist product. In late September 2026 the developer reported the connector was pending review to become an official connector in the Muse marketplace. That status is worth stating precisely: it is a third party product, described by its own developer, in Meta's review queue at the time of reporting, not a Meta feature.

The route it uses is real, though. Since September 19, 2026, anyone can build a custom connector by handing Muse API documentation or an MCP server, and Muse builds and runs its own integration. Meta does not review custom connectors, so a phone setup you build or buy runs on your own judgement. What to use instead, if the phone is your business

Businesses asking whether Muse can call their customers usually want one of two different jobs done. The first is answering the phone: a receptionist that never misses a call, takes bookings, and answers the same questions all day. The second is calling a list: following up leads, confirming appointments, chasing quotes. Muse does neither job for a business today, because Muse works for the customer on the other end of the call, not for the business being called.

The category that does those jobs is the voice agent platforms. ElevenLabs, Retell, Vapi, and Bland sell AI agents that answer your number as your business, follow your scripts, and connect to your booking and customer systems, priced per minute of talk time. Reported pricing for ElevenLabs conversational AI runs at roughly 8 to 10 cents a minute. These are purpose built business phone tools, and they are the right comparison class when what you need is your phone answered or your leads called.

The confusion is understandable, because Meta itself sells the mirror image. Meta Business Agent, a separate product that works on WhatsApp and Messenger, answers customers on behalf of a business. If your customers live on WhatsApp, that is the Meta product aimed at you. Muse is the agent your customers may soon use to call you, which is its own reason to understand it: the clearer your pricing, hours, and booking rules are in the places Muse reads, the better it can deal with you.

The verdict

For owners wearing their consumer hat, Muse calling is genuinely useful. Hold queues, bookings, and stock checks are hours handed back, delivered by a beta that sometimes needs a second try and sometimes cannot reach a human at all. Get access through the waitlist, use it for errands with a fallback, and it earns its place.

For businesses that want their leads called or their phone answered, Muse is not that tool, not yet. It refuses personal numbers, it has no inbound side of its own, and its two most wanted capabilities, documented phone menu handling and transfer back to the owner, are undocumented as of October 2026. Two signals will tell you when that changes: Meta publishing real documentation for calling, starting with a help article, and receptionist class connectors clearing marketplace review. Frequently asked questions

Can Muse call my customers or leads?
No. The calling feature places calls to businesses on your behalf, and testers report that it refuses personal numbers. Calling your customers or your leads is not what it does, and there is no setting that turns it into a sales or support dialer.
How do I get the Muse calling feature?
It is rolling out in stages behind a waitlist in the United States. Users report that you can ask Muse to put you on the waiting list for calling, and the feature appears in your account when your turn comes. As of October 2026 many users are still waiting.
Can Muse get through phone menus?
It is not documented. Meta has published no specification for calling, including nothing about keypad tones, as of October 12, 2026. Users report completed calls to businesses whose lines normally involve menus, so some menu handling happens in practice. Simple menus look workable in reports; deep enterprise phone trees are unproven.
Can Muse transfer the call back to me when a human answers?
That is the most requested pattern in user discussions, and Meta has not documented it as a supported feature. Plan on Muse handling the whole call, or make the call yourself when the conversation is one you need to join live.
Why does the caller ID say META?
Because Muse identifies itself. The caller ID shows META, and Muse introduces itself as an AI calling on your behalf and says it is taking notes. Businesses know an agent is calling, and reporting by CNET describes carriers learning to flag or block some of these calls.
What should my business use to answer its own phone?
A voice agent platform such as ElevenLabs, Retell, or Vapi. Those products answer your number as your business, follow your scripts, and take bookings. Muse works for the customer on the other side of the call, which is the opposite job.
