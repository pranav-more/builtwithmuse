Muse for insurance

Your car insurance renewal is priced on the assumption that you will not check it. Muse can do the checking: read your current policy, shop for the same coverage at a lower price, and handle the tedious forms. This page shows the workflow exactly as people are running it, the prompt to copy, the results they reported, and the case where Muse got beaten by a single phone call.

The renewal nobody checks

The email lands a month before your policy ends. The price has gone up again, the letter says nothing useful about why, and checking whether you are overpaying means an afternoon of coverage tables, comparison sites that all want your phone number, and agents who call back at dinner. So most people do what the price assumes they will do. They pay it and move on.

Look at what the job actually is, though. Read a long document and hold its details. Compare it line by line against other documents written in the same dense language. Fill in the same facts on form after form. Sit through the legwork nobody enjoys. That is a tedious afternoon for a person, and it is exactly the shape of work people are now handing to Muse, Meta's personal AI agent, with results worth reading before your next renewal arrives.

What people actually did

One Muse user posted the cleanest version of this on X. He uploaded his auto insurance policy to Muse and asked for a better rate with identical coverage. In about five minutes, he reported, Muse found a policy saving him $3,500 a year, bought it, and cancelled the old one. The prompt he used was not clever. It was one plain sentence:

Find me a better priced auto insurance than my current policy (attached). I'd like to keep the coverage the same.

He is not the only data point. Vincent Ethier reported switching insurers with Muse's help and saving $1,157, moving from Progressive to Allstate, again by holding the coverage steady and letting the agent shop. Two different people, two four figure savings, and in both cases the saving came from the comparison nobody had time to run, not from cutting coverage.

The same document handling shows up on the paperwork side. When the Wall Street Journal tested Muse, it assembled a Global Entry application from the reviewer's calendar and inbox, including five years of international travel history that would take an evening to reconstruct by hand, and completed a lengthy form with details pulled from the inbox. Insurance is documents and forms all the way down. That is the whole job, and it is the part Muse is demonstrably good at.

The workflow, step by step

Step 1: Get your current policy as a document

Download your policy from your insurer's site or app. The page that matters most is the declarations page: it lists your coverage limits, your deductibles, and the premium you pay. If you can only find the renewal letter, that works too, since it carries the new price and the coverage summary.

Step 2: Upload it and ask for the same coverage, cheaper

Attach the policy in Muse and use the prompt the first user proved works, tightened in one place: it tells Muse to prove the coverage matches instead of just claiming it does.

Find me a better priced auto insurance than my current policy (attached). Keep every coverage limit and deductible exactly the same. Show me a table comparing my current policy with each option you find, line by line, and flag any place where the coverage differs. Do not buy anything and do not cancel anything. Bring me the options and I will decide.

Step 3: Read the comparison table line by line

A cheaper policy that quietly drops a coverage line is not a saving, it is a gap you will meet at the worst possible moment. Check the limits, the deductibles, and the exclusions against your current policy. If a line differs, the price difference is explained, and not in your favour.

Step 4: Check the winning quote yourself

Before anything is bought, take the best option to the provider's own site, or call them, and confirm the price is real and current. Prices move, and as the next section shows, some of the best prices are only ever offered to a human being on the phone.

Step 5: Switch, then set the watch

Buy the new policy, cancel the old one, and confirm both in writing. Then make the renewal boring forever by asking Muse to watch the date:

My car insurance renews on [your date]. Six weeks before that date, remind me to run this comparison again, and when I do, check whether my current policy is still the best price for exactly the same coverage.

Where it falls short

The Wall Street Journal's test is the section to read twice. Its reviewer asked Muse to find cheaper auto insurance. Muse submitted her information for quotes and reported that her current rate was as low as it could get. Then a provider called her, and the human conversation produced a better deal, worth $420 a year, that Muse had reported as unavailable. If she had accepted the agent's answer, she would have missed it.

Three limits follow from that. First, Muse can only compare offers it can reach, and some pricing lives behind phone calls and retention desks. Second, a confident answer is not the same as a checked answer, so verify the winner yourself before you buy, every time. Third, buying and cancelling are the two steps that are painful to undo, which is why the workflow above keeps them in your hands even though at least one user let Muse do both.

One caution about the document itself. A policy carries your name, address, vehicle, and claims history. Before you upload it, turn off the data sharing setting under Settings, then Data controls, so your chats are not used to improve the data model. Frequently asked questions

Can Muse buy the new policy and cancel the old one for me?
In the strongest report we found, yes. The user said Muse found the cheaper policy, bought it, and cancelled the old one in the same session, in about five minutes. We still recommend keeping the purchase and the cancellation as your own clicks. Let Muse do the reading, the shopping, and the comparison, then decide yourself. Those two steps are the hard ones to undo.
What if Muse tells me my current price is already the best?
Treat that answer as a starting point, not a verdict. When the Wall Street Journal tested Muse on car insurance, it reported that the reviewer's current rate was as low as it could get. A provider then called her and offered a better deal worth $420 a year that Muse had missed. Some prices are only offered to a human on the phone, and no agent can compare an offer it cannot see.
Does this work for home insurance or health insurance?
The published reports so far are about car insurance. The method is a document comparison, and it applies to any policy you can upload: the same coverage, checked line by line, at a lower price. Expect the results to vary more where pricing depends on inspections, medical details, or local rules that are not visible online.
Is it safe to upload my insurance policy to Muse?
A policy document carries your name, address, vehicle, and claims history, so treat it like any sensitive document. Before you upload, turn off the data sharing setting in Settings, then Data controls, so your chats are not used to improve the data model. Our guide to whether Muse is safe walks through the permissions and settings worth checking.
