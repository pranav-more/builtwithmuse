How I Find Startup Jobs on X Before They Hit Job Boards

Job boards are where open roles go to collect five hundred applicants. The real window is the week a startup raises money and the founder posts that they are hiring. Muse helps me catch that week.

Here is my playbook for finding work as a full stack developer. I do not start on job boards. I start on X, in the week a startup announces funding, when the founder is excited, hiring, and actually reading replies.

Muse watches for those moments. It scans recent posts for founders announcing a fundraise and mentioning open roles, then hands me a short list: the company, what they do, the founder, the post, and one genuine thing I can congratulate them on. Then I do the human part myself.

The short version: catch the hiring wave the week it starts, not three months later on a job board.

How it works

  1. Tell Muse your profile in one line. Full stack developer, what you build, what you are looking for.
  2. It scans X for the last seven days for founders announcing a fundraise and mentioning they are hiring.
  3. You get a short list. Each one: the company, what they do, the founder's name, a link to the post, and one specific thing worth congratulating them on.
  4. You reply with the genuine congrats, then send a short DM. Who you are, what you build, one line on why their company, ask about the role. You write it and you send it. That part is yours.

The exact prompt to try

"Find posts on X from the last seven days where startup founders announced a fundraise and mentioned they are hiring engineers. I am a full stack developer. For each one, give me the company, what they do, the founder's name, a link to the post, and one genuine thing I can congratulate them on."

The congrats has to be specific A generic congrats gets ignored. Referencing something specific from their post or their work gets a reply. The specific detail is the whole game.

Where you will hit approval gates

Reality check: Muse drafts, you send. Every reply and every DM goes out under your name, written or at least rewritten by you. Founders can smell a bot from a mile away, and one robotic message burns the bridge for good. Never automate outreach.

Why it beats the old way

  • Timing: you arrive in the hiring week, not three months later
  • Warm: a real conversation beats applicant number 487
  • Specific: your congrats references their actual work, not a template
  • Short: the whole workflow is one scan and a few messages, not forty applications

FAQ

Is this not just spam with extra steps?
It is spam if the message is generic. The rule is simple: if you cannot name one specific thing you genuinely liked about their work, do not message them.
Does Muse apply to jobs for me?
No, and you would not want it to. It finds the moment. You have the conversation.
I am not a developer. Does this still work?
Yes. Designers, marketers, ops, anyone. Change the role in the prompt and the same wave applies.
