Muse is not available in France, and Meta has announced no date for France or for Europe. France has no Muse launch, no App Store listing, and no date from Meta. The five countries that do are the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico.

Is Muse available in France?

No, and no other EU country has it either. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in exactly five countries: the US, where it launched on September 8, 2026, Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. France is not on that list. Meta has published no country list and no timeline for any further country, and it has announced no date for France.

The French App Store tells you the same thing without words: the Muse app is not in it. Storefront checks have confirmed the absence since launch. A check on September 10, 2026 queried Apple's lookup endpoint for the Muse listing and found it in the US store and in none of fifteen other storefronts, including France. A later check on October 7 found the app only in the US and Canada stores, still absent in France, Germany, Ireland, and the other stores checked. A country by country review updated October 31, 2026 lists the whole European Economic Area, including France, with no launch announced.

French tech press has reported the absence directly. In September 2026, Sirius Digital reported that Muse was limited to the US and Canada and that Meta had given no date for Europe, a finding the French SME site AI First repeated in its own review. The French site AI Sisters was blunter, writing that Muse is not available in France and advising businesses not to deploy it. Two months later, none of that coverage has been overtaken by events: France still has no date. See the full list in our Muse available countries guide.

Try the website and you get the same answer in a different form. The muse.ai pages load from France without trouble. Sign in, and a waitlist screen appears saying Muse is not available in your country or region yet. Every EU reader sees that screen, and it means exactly what it says: the block is tied to the country, not to your browser, your device, or your account settings. Nothing you click on the site lifts it. Only a French launch does, and Meta has given no sign of when that comes.

Why is Europe waiting? The EU rules behind the delay

Meta has not said why Europe waits. It has not named a rule, a regulator, or a date. But Europe's rulebook for AI is the most detailed in the world, and it is the backdrop to every Meta decision about the region. Reading the delay honestly means understanding that backdrop without inventing a reason Meta has not given.

Start with the dates, because they shape everything else. The EU AI Act's core obligations for general purpose AI models have applied since August 2, 2025, according to the European Commission's framework pages. Models already on the EU market before that date have until August 2, 2027 to fall in line. The transparency duties in Article 50 apply from August 2, 2026, requiring chatbot and voice assistant providers to make sure people know they are interacting with AI, no later than the first interaction. A later milestone requires machine readable marking of generative systems, with a grace period until December 2, 2026 for systems already on the market. A European review of Muse's first month lays out this timeline and notes the key point: the AI Act does not prohibit agentic assistants. It sets conditions for them.

The GDPR is the older law in the picture, and for an agent it may be the more demanding one. It has governed personal data in the EU since 2018, and for an agent that logs into email, reads messages, and pays on your behalf, its rules decide what may be collected, how long it may be kept, and what it may be trained on. The evidence that regulation delays launches is real: a study by the Centre for the Governance of AI, published in June 2026 and reported in October, compared US and European launch dates for 375 models from Meta, Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic released between June 2018 and May 2026. About one in nine reached the EU late or not at all, and Meta delayed around a quarter of its releases in the EU, the highest rate of the four companies. The researchers attributed most delays mainly to regulation, with data protection rules the most common obstacle, and found no strong evidence that the AI Act itself had caused delays so far.

Meta's own behavior fits the same pattern. In July 2024, Meta said it would release its multimodal Llama model outside the EU but not inside it, citing the unpredictable nature of the European regulatory environment. That decision came a month after the company paused plans to train its models on EU users' public posts. None of this proves the AI Act or the GDPR is the reason Muse has no French date. Meta has named no reason. But the pattern is consistent: Meta moves slowly in Europe, and it says so itself when it does.

The Meta AI precedent: 18 months from the US to Europe

The closest precedent for Muse in France is Meta AI, Meta's older assistant. It shows how long a Meta AI product can take to reach Europe, and how limited it can be when it arrives.

Meta AI launched in the US in 2023. Its European debut stalled when Ireland's Data Protection Commission raised concerns about Meta's plan to train AI models on public Facebook and Instagram posts from EU users. In June 2024, Meta paused AI training on EU user data. The rollout that followed took almost a year of intensive engagement with European regulators, in the words of a Meta spokesperson quoted by The Verge. Meta AI finally reached 41 European countries in March 2025, about 18 months after its US launch, and it arrived in a limited form: a text only assistant, not trained on first party data from EU users, with image generation and multimodal abilities removed. Business Today reported the launch on March 21, 2025, including the 41 country count and the text only limitation.

That history does not set a date for Muse in France. Eighteen months from September 2026 would be early 2028, and this guide does not predict that, because Meta has given no signal that Muse follows the Meta AI timetable. The precedent matters for a narrower reason: it shows that Meta will delay a European launch for a year or more over data questions, and will ship a reduced product when it finally arrives. French readers hoping for a fast Muse launch should weigh that history against their hopes.

There is one more lesson in the precedent. When Meta AI arrived in Europe, it arrived without training on EU user data, and European users could opt out of data collection. Data controls were part of the launch story, not an afterthought. If Muse follows the same path, expect its French launch to come with prominent data controls and a clear statement about training. And expect the same advice to apply on day one: turn off the "Help improve our AI models" setting, described later in this guide, before you connect anything.

Will Muse speak French?

French is the everyday language of France, in government, in schools, in business, and at home. Any French launch that only spoke English would be a partial product. Meta has said nothing about French language support for Muse, and no announcement covers French for any country. But the launch pattern so far suggests language follows country, not the other way round.

Japan launched with Japanese support on October 23, 2026, and Mexico launched in Spanish on November 5, with local connectors and a Spanish language experience. Meta localizes per country. A French launch would almost certainly speak French, because Meta already operates in French across Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, and its French language Connect posts show the company communicates with French readers directly. Those posts even mentioned Mexico, which tells you Meta's French speaking audience is on its radar. None of that is a French Muse announcement, and this guide does not present it as one.

The practical question for French readers is different: if you use Muse in English today through a workaround, will it understand French requests? Muse runs on the Muse Spark model family, and large models generally handle French well, but Meta has published no statement about Muse's French ability, and no French user has been able to test it officially. Do not assume French works perfectly until French users can actually try it. And do not assume a French launch means French connectors on day one: the connectors for French banks, French retailers, and French services would need their own deals, just as Japan's launch needed Japanese integrations.

French coverage of Muse is already detailed, even without access. AI First reviewed it for small businesses from the outside, and AI Sisters analyzed it for business readers in French. That coverage is worth reading for what Muse does. But it is not a sign that a launch is near. The sign to watch is a Meta announcement in French, naming France.

WhatsApp in France, and why it matters for Muse

WhatsApp is France's most used messaging app, and by a clear margin. According to the telecom regulator Arcep's 2026 digital barometer, based on a survey from June 2025, 87 percent of people in France who use messaging apps use WhatsApp, and 76 percent use Messenger. That makes WhatsApp the natural place for French readers to ask about Muse, and the natural place for confusion about which Meta assistant is which.

WhatsApp does not offer a separate route in. The assistant inside WhatsApp is available only in countries where Muse itself has launched, because WhatsApp is a launch surface, not a separate product with its own availability list. Mexico's launch on November 5 shows the pattern: WhatsApp arrived alongside iOS, Android, and the web, as part of the launch. In France, the assistant inside WhatsApp is Meta AI, the older assistant that lives in WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger. It answers questions in chat. Booking, paying, and acting inside your other apps is Muse's job, and Muse has not launched in France in any form.

Meta itself still has to explain the difference. The official @meta.ai account has clarified on social media that Meta AI is the assistant built into Threads, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp, while Muse is the standalone personal agent app. The two share a parent company and a chat interface, so the mix up is understandable. If an AI assistant answers you in WhatsApp in France today, it is Meta AI. WhatsApp is a likely part of a French launch, given its dominance here and the Mexico pattern, but Meta has confirmed nothing about French WhatsApp onboarding.

One more France specific note: WhatsApp's European service is provided by WhatsApp Ireland Limited, an Irish company owned by Meta in the US. That corporate detail is why Irish regulation matters so much for French WhatsApp users, and it is part of the same European regulatory picture that shapes the Muse delay. Should French businesses plan around Muse?

Not yet. French business coverage of Muse is consistent on this point, and it is worth hearing clearly. AI First, reviewing Muse for small and medium businesses, concluded that it is not worth it today for three reasons: it is not available in Europe, it is designed for an individual rather than a team, and it does not connect to business tools like a CRM, an ERP, or a document repository. AI Sisters went further, writing that Muse may never reach Europe, and advising readers not to deploy Muse in their company because it is neither available in France nor designed for professional use.

The product facts support that caution. Muse is a consumer product. There is no enterprise tier, no admin controls, no team policies, and no org level visibility. Muse for Small Business, launched September 29, 2026, connects tools like Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Canva, Slack, and Klaviyo, and Meta says nothing publishes, sends, or spends without approval. But that is a consumer account with business connectors, not a business product with controls. A French company that answers to the GDPR and to its own customers' data questions cannot put client data into an agent with no data processing agreement and no European data residency.

There is also a timing argument. Even if Muse launched in France tomorrow, a business would need French connectors, French language support, and a compliance story before it could use the agent for real work. None of those exist today. The sensible move for a French business is to follow the product, learn what it does from the coverage and from the workflows on this site, and wait for an enterprise tier and a French launch before planning anything around it.

For individuals, the picture is simpler: Muse is a personal tool, and when it reaches France it will be worth trying on personal jobs first. For businesses, the answer stays no until Meta ships both a French launch and a business tier. Do not build a business workflow on a workaround account. The account caution in the next section applies double to work use.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from France?

French language tutorials describe the same workaround as tutorials everywhere else: a VPN, a fresh email address, and a new account. They circulate widely, and many claim the method works. This guide does not promise that it works, and it does not recommend it. Here is the honest picture.

A VPN changes one thing: your IP address. Muse checks more than that. The gate sits at the account layer, and your App Store region, payment method, device settings, and account history all carry location signals a VPN does not touch. That is why users in unlaunched countries report sign ins that revealed where they really were, VPN or not, and waitlist screens that appeared even with a US server connected. Results vary by account and by day, and Meta offers no supported route for using Muse from France.

Then there is what happens to the account. Meta may review an account used from an unlaunched country, notice the mismatch, and suspend or close it. That outcome is reported as a caution in coverage of VPN use, not as a verified Meta enforcement policy, and Meta has not published a rule that says VPN signups are banned. The risk is still enough to change how you treat a workaround account: nothing important goes in it. No real email address, no payment method that matters to you, no work files. An account that disappears takes everything in it with it.

One action comes before everything else if you get in: switch off data sharing. Open the app, go to Settings, tap Data controls, and turn the "Help improve our AI models" switch off, then confirm with "Turn off". That switch decides whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off stops your chats feeding future model training. Your location stays visible, and a workaround account stays exposed. Do it anyway, on every account you open.

Rules differ by country, so check what applies where you live. This guide covers the technical picture only. Our fuller guide to using Meta Muse outside the US covers the same ground with device specific detail.

What will Muse cost in France?

France has no price for Muse, because France has no launch. That sounds obvious, but prices circulate anyway: a euro figure in a post or a video should not be trusted, because no euro figure appears in any source checked for this guide. What is reported is global pricing: free with a weekly usage limit, Power for $20 a month, Maximum for $100 a month. Paying buys more usage, not more features: the free tier runs the same agent on a smaller weekly allowance.

The tiers come with weekly limits that Meta has not published. Reporting puts the free tier at 100 million tokens a week, a figure that traces to Zuckerberg's launch statement rather than to Meta's help pages. Power is reported at 500 million a week and Maximum at 3 billion a week. Those figures are consistent across sources, but Meta's help pages do not publish them, so treat them as reported, not confirmed. The full tier breakdown is in our guide to whether Muse is free.

India is the only datapoint on local pricing, and it is unconfirmed. The Hindu's November 9, 2026 report put India pricing at Rs 1,799 a month for Power and Rs 8,999 a month for Maximum. Neither figure is confirmed by Meta, and India has not launched. The report shows Meta is thinking in local currencies for future markets, but it says nothing about France. A French launch could price in euros or in dollars. Meta has said nothing, and this guide will not guess.

The age check is the one cost that is confirmed. Muse requires users to be 18 or older, and signing up involves an age check that can ask for a payment card. The check places a $1 hold that is returned in 5 to 7 business days: a hold rather than a payment. Free tier users face the same check, as reported. French cards should be fine for the check when it arrives, but the check is only reachable after launch.

What will launch day look like in France?

France has no launch date, so this section borrows from the five countries that do. It describes their launch days, applied to France.

The App Store listing appears first, in France, with the Google Play listing following the same day. Downloading is free. Signing up needs two things: a Meta account and an age check, since Muse is for adults 18 and over. If you already have a Meta account from Facebook or Instagram, link it, but use one sign in method throughout: some users have found empty accounts appearing when the sign in method differs from the first one.

The 48 hour clock starts the moment you create your account. Redeem a code in Settings within 48 hours, and both sides get 1 billion tokens. One code per account, and codes are case sensitive. No cap figure is confirmed: September sources put the per code cap at 20 uses, November creators say 30, and Meta publishes no figure, so this guide states no cap as fact. If you joined the waitlist, your code arrives on launch day. If you did not, grab one from the community pool on the homepage, and redeem it the same day you create your account.

Muse becomes useful when it can act inside your apps. It connects to services like Gmail, Google Calendar, and Instagram, plus dozens more, and then works inside them. A French launch would bring an unknown mix of connectors, but Japan and Mexico both launched with local ones alongside the global set. Turn off the "Help improve our AI models" setting before you connect anything at all. Begin with a single app and a single small job, and verify the result before going further.

Not everything arrives at once. The US shows the staging clearly: two months passed before Voice reached all US users on November 5, Spaces launched November 6, and Muse Mail arrived November 10 for paid subscribers in the US and Canada. A French launch would bring the core agent first, with newer features following on their own schedule. The French language question adds its own uncertainty: whether French arrives on day one or later is unknown.
