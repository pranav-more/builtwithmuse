Is Muse available in Cambodia?

No. Muse launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. Cambodia is not among them. Meta has published no country list, no timeline, and no statement naming Cambodia.

Cambodia is also quiet in the secondary signals. Malaysia received a direct reply from Meta's official account on Threads in November, saying "Not yet." Vietnam has a wave of tutorial videos promising workarounds. Cambodia has neither: no official reply, no press report naming it, no tutorial wave, and no community thread describing a Cambodian signup attempt. That silence is itself the finding. There is nothing to report about Cambodia beyond the absence, and this guide will not invent any.

The country tables that track Muse put Cambodia in the unannounced group, with every other market outside the five. A table checked October 31, 2026, showed only four confirmed countries at that date, and Cambodia was not one of them. The table is open for anyone to check, and its Cambodia position has not moved since.

Each confirmed launch, with its date and source, is recorded on the Muse available countries page. Cambodia is not on it, and that page is the fastest way to confirm that nothing has changed.

What happens when you try from Cambodia?

The defining experience of Muse in Cambodia is a single screen: the block message. Sign in on muse.ai and Muse answers, "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet," with a Join the waitlist button underneath. Everything before that screen works. The marketing pages load, the demos play, the pricing shows. The product itself stops at the account step, and that is by design, not by error.

The App Store agrees with the website. Search the Cambodian storefront for Muse and you will find no app published by Meta Platforms, Inc. In September 2026, an early check of sixteen storefronts found the listing only in the US, and no Cambodia specific check since has found it. If your search comes up empty, that is the rollout telling you the same thing the website did. The guide to Muse not showing in your App Store walks through the checks that confirm it.

What connects the two is the account check. Muse decides availability from the country on your Meta account, not from the device in your hand or the network you are on, so the website can load while the product stays shut. An independent check of the block sets out that distinction in detail.

That one check covers all of it: the iPhone app, the Android app, the web, the Mac app, and the Muse chat inside WhatsApp. No surface has its own rule, so no surface offers a way around the others. A launch, when it comes, will open them together.

How does Facebook shape Cambodia's internet?

Cambodia is one of the most Facebook first countries in the world. A report from the Ministry of Information, published in May 2026, found more than 15.6 million Facebook accounts in Cambodia, about 86 percent of the population, and described social media as dominating the country's information landscape. The Khmer Times reported the findings. DataReportal's Digital 2026 figures for Cambodia count 12.0 million internet users, 67.3 percent of the population, and put Facebook's advertising reach at 14.0 million accounts, more than the adult population, because accounts outnumber people. Those figures are gathered in a Cambodia digital marketing overview.

That dominance has a practical shape. Facebook is where Cambodians get news, where sellers run Facebook Live sales, and where small businesses reach customers. Khmer is the default language for mass market digital content, and Facebook's video and live formats carry much of it. A Cambodian reader is far more likely to meet a Meta product inside Facebook than anywhere else.

For Muse, the implication is mixed. The account base is ready: Cambodians already have Meta accounts, and the account layer Muse depends on is familiar. But the product is not here, and the surface where Cambodians spend their time, Facebook itself, has no Muse. The agent lives in its own app, on the web, and inside WhatsApp, and none of those is where Cambodia's attention already is.

Which messenger do Cambodians actually use?

Muse's chat surface is WhatsApp. Cambodia's messenger is Telegram. A 2023 report found Telegram had become the country's most popular messaging app, and a Khmer Times report put Telegram at more than 10 million users in Cambodia. The same report that counted Facebook's reach counted Telegram's. Messenger, meanwhile, was losing ground: DataReportal figures gathered in the same overview show Messenger's advertising reach falling by 250,000 accounts in a year.

That mismatch matters. The guide to using Muse in WhatsApp describes a chat you message like a contact, after connecting your account once. In Cambodia, that habit would need building from scratch, because WhatsApp is not where Cambodians message each other. Meta has announced no Telegram version of Muse, and no connector for Telegram. For now, the realistic expectation is the standalone app and the web, if and when Cambodia opens.

The broader lesson is that a launch is not just a switch. The rollout pattern so far is local: Japan launched with Japanese services, Singapore with local connectors, Mexico in Spanish with local retailers connected from the start. A Cambodia launch on the same pattern would need to meet Cambodians where they are, in Khmer, on Facebook, with Telegram habits in mind. None of that has been announced.

Does Muse speak Khmer?

Khmer is the language of Cambodia's mass market. It is the default language for the Facebook Live sellers, the everyday users, and the content most Cambodians actually read, while English mostly serves business and tourism audiences. An agent that only speaks English would reach a narrow slice of the country, which is why the Khmer question matters more here than in many markets.

The answer is unknown. Meta has published no list of the languages the Muse agent supports, and nothing in any Meta source checked for this guide mentions Khmer. The launch material is in English, and there is no language table to consult. Treat Khmer support as unproven.

The one launch that shows localization is Mexico, where Muse arrived in Spanish on November 5, 2026. That shows Meta builds launches for local languages. It shows nothing about Khmer: no Khmer testing, no Khmer interface, and no Khmer connectors have been reported anywhere.

Do not read Meta AI's language support as Muse's. Meta AI, the assistant inside Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and WhatsApp, is a different product from Muse, the standalone agent, and what one supports says nothing about the other. The Muse vs Meta AI explainer sets out the difference.

Can a VPN get you Muse in Cambodia?

Tutorials that promise Muse in Cambodia usually have the same shape: change your address, and the product opens. The shape is wrong. Muse does not decide from your address. It decides from the country on your Meta account, and a changed address does not change the account.

That is the whole mechanism. A VPN alters the address the internet sees, and nothing else that matters here: not the account country, not the region on your App Store account. The account check runs at sign in, after the page has loaded, which is why a page that loads is not a product that opens.

What happens after a VPN signup, according to reports from other unlaunched countries, is usually the waitlist screen, or an account that stops working later. Some reports warn that signing up through a VPN may break Muse's terms and that Meta can limit or close accounts. Those warnings are user beliefs and cautionary reporting, not verified Meta enforcement, and this guide states them as caution, not fact.

Nothing here is a method, and nothing here is a promise. The practical advice is negative: do not pay for a "Cambodia Muse method," and do not buy an account from anyone. Purchased accounts have been reported as disconnected without warning in other markets. The guide to buying codes explains why paying for access is a bad deal in every market.

What privacy setting should you turn off first?

The first setting to change is the data switch, and the time to change it is before your first chat. In the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off "Help improve our AI models." Confirm "Turn off" when the dialog asks. From then on, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models.

The switch covers your Muse chats only. It does not cover your Facebook posts or your Messenger messages, which follow their own rules. The default is on, unless you switch it off, according to multiple reports on Muse's defaults, and it governs chats from the moment you set it. Every chat before that falls under the old rule.

Treat the approval as the second control. Before Muse publishes, sends, or spends anything, it asks, and that question is the control that matters most day to day. One switch decides what Meta does with your words. The approval decides what the agent does in the world. Set the switch first, then hold the approval habit, before you connect an inbox, a calendar, or a Facebook Page.

What will Muse cost in Cambodia?

There is no Cambodia price to report. A price belongs to a launch, and there is no launch, no local currency, no local billing, and no announced figure of any kind. Anything presented as a Cambodia price today is invented, and the reported India pricing in rupees, published by The Hindu on November 9, 2026, does not change that: Meta has not confirmed it, and it says nothing about Cambodia.

The pricing in circulation is American: a free tier with a weekly usage limit, then Power at $20 a month and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy usage capacity, not features. Meta has published no number for the free tier's weekly limit; the up to 100 million tokens a week figure is from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement. The tiers are set out in full in the guide to whether Muse is free. Do not read those dollars as a Cambodia price.

Watch the verification step, not just the price. Verification is reported to accept either a payment card or a linked Instagram or Facebook account; the card route carries a $1 temporary authorization, refunded in 5 to 7 business days. Meta has not confirmed the card requirement in the sources checked, and a reader with no card may find that step is the first real barrier. Knowing the step exists before launch day is the preparation; discovering it during signup is the failure mode. The age verification problems guide covers the failure modes and fixes.

Where does Cambodia sit in the regional rollout?

Singapore launched on October 28, 2026, the first market in Southeast Asia. Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand, and Cambodia are all still waiting. Geography does not predict the order: Singapore's launch tells you nothing about when its neighbors open, and no Meta source has described a regional sequence.

Infrastructure is not the constraint anyone names. Cambodia switched on 5G on January 1, 2026, in Phnom Penh and 14 provinces, with three carriers offering it. The constraint Meta itself names is different: on the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg described Muse as compute intensive and said per country tailoring, including connectors for local services, takes time. That account of the call is set out in an analysis of the global rollout. It is context for why expansion is slow everywhere, not a statement about Cambodia.

The wait has no date, and no honest guide can give it one. What a reader can do is the unglamorous preparation: a clean account, a held code, the privacy setting understood, and a habit of checking the App Store and the newsroom instead of tutorial promises. When the position changes, it will change there first, and this guide will follow the primary sources.
