Is Muse available in the Netherlands?

No. The Netherlands is not on the Muse launch list. As of November 13, 2026, exactly five countries have Muse: the US, where it launched on September 8, 2026, Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. Neither is any other EU country. Meta has published no country list and no timeline for any further country, and it has announced no date for the Netherlands.

A country by country review updated October 31, 2026 lists the whole European Economic Area, including the Netherlands, with no launch announced, and the Dutch App Store agrees: the Muse app does not appear in it. Independent storefront checks have found the same result since launch. A check on September 10, 2026 queried Apple's lookup endpoint for the Muse listing and found it in the US store and in none of fifteen other storefronts, including the Netherlands. One availability wiki, checked in October 2026, put it directly: app store listings in Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain, Italy, and Ireland show nothing. See the full list in our Muse available countries guide.

From the Netherlands, the website gives the same result as the store. Muse.ai loads normally, then stops you at sign in: a waitlist screen saying Muse is not available in your country or region yet. The screen is the same across the EU. It is a country block, not a bug, and it does not depend on your browser or device. Only a Dutch launch removes it. Meta has given no sign of when that comes, and no Meta page names the Netherlands.

Meta's whole official position fits in one sentence: it is working on more countries, takes care with each one, and has no timing to share. Treat any date you see for the Netherlands in a social post or a video as a guess, not a fact.

The Dutch advantage: English is not the problem

The Netherlands tops the world in English proficiency, and that fact changes the launch question here. The 2025 edition of the EF English Proficiency Index, published in November 2025, ranks the Netherlands first out of 123 countries and regions, with a score of 624 and a rating of Very High English Proficiency, based on tests from 2.2 million adults. The Netherlands has held the top spot in every edition of the index for years. English is the language of Dutch higher education, Dutch tech, and Dutch business, and most Dutch adults read and write English comfortably.

What does that mean for Muse? It means the Netherlands is the one country in this wave where language is almost certainly not part of the wait. An English only Muse would work for most Dutch users on day one. Voice mode, which lets you talk to Muse and hear it answer, would work in English for Dutch users who speak English fluently, which is most of them. The Japanese and Mexican launches needed full localization. A Dutch launch needs far less, because the audience is already there in English.

What remains is the country gate, in four separate pieces of work: the regulatory work for the Netherlands specifically, the local connectors, the account layer, and the App Store listing. At the Japan launch, Meta's chief executive said Muse is compute intensive to run, and that each country needs adaptation and connector work. The Netherlands is in the same position, with no start date given. The wait is not about language. Dutch readers should watch for the country announcement, not a language one.

One language note anyway. Dutch is the official language of the Netherlands, and Meta has said nothing about Dutch language support for Muse. An eventual Dutch launch could include Dutch, or it could launch in English first. Meta has said nothing either way, and this guide does not predict it. The safe expectation is English first, matching how most global AI products reach the Netherlands, with Dutch unconfirmed. If you are comfortable in English, and most Dutch users are, that is not a blocker.

WhatsApp is the Netherlands' main chat app

WhatsApp is the main chat app in the Netherlands, by a wide margin. One estimate from January 2025 put WhatsApp at about 13.3 million users in the Netherlands, or 73.5 percent of the population, making it the most used messaging app in the country, well ahead of Facebook Messenger at about 6.8 million. Another source claims a higher figure, around 79 percent of the population. The figures differ by source and method, and this guide uses the attributed estimate. They agree on the point: WhatsApp is where the Netherlands chats, in family groups, work groups, school groups, and neighborhood groups.

The answer inside WhatsApp is the same as the answer in the App Store. Muse is available in WhatsApp only where Muse has launched, because the country rule travels with the product into every surface. When Mexico launched on November 5, WhatsApp came with it, alongside iOS, Android, and the web. That is how launches work: WhatsApp is part of the package, not a back door. In the Netherlands, the assistant in WhatsApp is Meta AI, the older assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger. It chats and answers questions. It does not log into your accounts or act on your behalf. That is Muse's job, and Muse has not launched in the Netherlands in any form.

The confusion has a simple cause: one company, one chat interface, two products. Meta's official @meta.ai account has spelled out the difference, explaining that Meta AI is the assistant built into Threads, Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp, while Muse is the standalone personal agent app. An AI assistant in your WhatsApp in the Netherlands today is Meta AI. When Muse launches here, WhatsApp is a likely part of it, given how dominant WhatsApp is and given the Mexico pattern. But Meta has confirmed nothing about Dutch WhatsApp onboarding.

For now, WhatsApp stays Meta AI's home in the Netherlands. Do not expect Muse to appear inside it ahead of a Dutch launch, and do not mistake the assistant already there for the agent. Launch day may make WhatsApp onboarding the easiest way in, since the app and the account are already on your phone. The EU rules behind the wait

Start from what is known, which is very little. Meta has announced no European date, named no rule as the cause, and pointed to no regulator. The EU rulebook below is the context Meta operates in, not a confirmed explanation. It matters because it sets the conditions any launch must meet, and those conditions take time to satisfy.

The EU AI Act's transparency duties are the part that touches an agent most directly. From August 2, 2026, the duties in Article 50 apply: providers must make sure people know they are interacting with AI, no later than the first interaction. The Act's core obligations for general purpose AI models have applied since August 2, 2025, with models already on the EU market before that date given until August 2, 2027 to fall in line. Machine readable marking of generative systems follows, with a grace period until December 2, 2026 for systems already on the market. The AI Act does not prohibit agentic assistants. It sets conditions for them, and Meta has to meet those conditions before it can offer Muse in the Netherlands.

The evidence that data rules delay launches comes from a study by the Centre for the Governance of AI, published in June 2026. It compared US and European launch dates for 375 models and found about one in nine reached the EU late or not at all, with Meta delaying around a quarter of its releases in the EU, the highest rate of the four companies studied. Data protection rules were the most common regulatory obstacle named. That fits the GDPR's role: it has governed personal data in the EU since 2018, and for an agent that reads email, checks calendars, and pays on your behalf, its rules decide what may be collected, how long it may be kept, and what it may be trained on.

The Meta AI timeline shows how long the engagement can take. Meta AI launched in the US in 2023. Its European debut stalled in June 2024, when Ireland's Data Protection Commission raised concerns about training on EU users' public posts and Meta paused AI training on EU user data. Almost a year of intensive engagement with regulators followed, and Meta AI reached 41 European countries in March 2025, about 18 months after its US launch, as a text only assistant not trained on EU user data. That history does not set a date for Muse in the Netherlands. It shows that Meta will delay a European launch for a year or more over data questions.

The Netherlands has its own regulator in the picture too. The Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens enforces the GDPR in the Netherlands, though under the GDPR's one stop shop mechanism the lead authority for Meta's EU operations is Ireland's Data Protection Commission, since Meta's European headquarters is in Dublin. The Dutch authority can still raise concerns, and it can act on complaints from Dutch users. Meta has not said any regulator is the reason for the Dutch delay, and this guide does not claim one is. The point is narrower: a Dutch launch has to clear EU level rules, and that work takes time Meta has not quantified.

Dutch startups and builders are watching

The Netherlands has a strong startup and builder scene, and Muse's absence is felt there in a specific way. Dutch founders, freelancers, and small teams read about Muse's agent capabilities in English, in the same feeds as their US counterparts, and they can see exactly what they are missing. The product reads as built for them: an agent that handles email, calendar, travel, and shopping, in English, on a phone. The wait is not a language wait. It is a country wait, and it is visible.

That visibility cuts both ways. Dutch builders are also the audience most likely to try workarounds, and the most likely to hit the account layer gate and the location leaks described later in this guide. If you are building a product and thinking about Muse as a channel, do not plan around a Dutch launch date. Plan around the five launched countries, and treat the Netherlands as a future market with no date. A launch announcement is the only signal that changes that.

For Dutch businesses, the connector question matters too. Muse's value comes from the services it connects to: email, calendar, shopping, travel, and local services. A Dutch launch would need Dutch connectors alongside the global ones, and those take deals and integration work. Japan's launch needed Japanese integrations. Mexico's needed Mexican ones. The Netherlands would need its own, and Meta has announced none of them. If you run a Dutch service and wonder whether Muse will connect to it, there is no answer yet, and no public connector program to join.

The practical advice for Dutch builders is the same as for Dutch users: join the waitlist, learn the product from the guides on this site, and check the Dutch App Store. When launch day comes, the English fluency advantage means Dutch users will be up and running fast. The preparation you do now is what makes that speed possible.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from the Netherlands?

Every unlaunched country has the same workaround tutorials, and the Netherlands is no exception: a VPN, a fresh email address, and a new account. Many of them claim success. This guide does not promise the method works, and it does not recommend it. Dutch readers should read the honest picture below before trying anything, because the English app makes the temptation stronger here than in most countries.

Four signals give you away, and a VPN changes none of them. The App Store region, the payment method, the device, and the account history all carry location information, and Muse checks location at the account layer, where all four are visible. A VPN changes only your IP address. That is why users in unlaunched countries report accounts showing their real country, VPN connected or not, and waitlist screens appearing even with a US server connected. The method works for some accounts and fails for others, with no pattern anyone can rely on. Meta publishes no supported path from the Netherlands.

Keep a workaround account bare. No everyday email, no payment method you depend on, no work documents. The reason is the account risk: Meta may review an account used from an unlaunched country, notice the location mismatch, and flag or terminate it. Reports describe that as a caution, not as a verified enforcement policy, and Meta has published no rule that says VPN signups are banned. The risk is real enough that losing the account means losing everything in it.

If Muse opens for you from the Netherlands, the first thing to do is switch off data sharing. Go to Settings, open Data controls, turn off "Help improve our AI models", and tap "Turn off" to confirm. The switch decides whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off keeps your chats out of Meta's training data, and nothing more. Location checks still see you, and it does not shield the account. Do it anyway, no matter which account or country.

The safe path costs nothing and risks nothing. Join the waitlist, get a code on launch day, and open a real account. A workaround account is borrowed, and borrowed accounts can be taken away. The wait is frustrating, but it is not worth an account that disappears.

What will Muse cost in the Netherlands?

A Dutch price does not exist yet, because a Dutch launch does not exist yet. No euro figure appears in any source checked for this guide. Treat a euro price in a post or a video as invented. The reported pricing is global: free at a weekly limit, $20 a month for Power, $100 a month for Maximum. Money buys usage room, not extra features, and the free tier is the same agent with a smaller weekly allowance.

The weekly limits are the reported part of the pricing. Reporting puts the free tier at 100 million tokens a week, Power at 500 million, and Maximum at 3 billion. The free tier number traces to Zuckerberg's launch statement. Meta's help pages publish no limits at all, so all three figures stay reported, not confirmed, despite appearing consistently across sources. Our guide to whether Muse is free covers the tiers in detail.

The only local pricing ever reported is India's. A November 9, 2026 report in The Hindu gave India prices of Rs 1,799 a month for Power and Rs 8,999 a month for Maximum. Both figures are unconfirmed, and India is still waiting. The report shows Meta is thinking in local currencies for future markets. It says nothing about the Netherlands. A Dutch launch could price in euros or in dollars. Meta has said nothing, and this guide will not guess.

The confirmed cost is not a subscription but a check. Muse is an adults only product: 18 and over. Signing up involves an age check that can ask for a payment card, placing a $1 temporary hold, refunded in 5 to 7 business days. That is a hold, not a purchase, and even the free tier involves the check, as reported. Dutch cards should be accepted for the check, but the check is only reachable after launch.
