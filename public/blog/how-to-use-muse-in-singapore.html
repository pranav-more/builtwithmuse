How to Use Muse in Singapore

1. Is Muse available in Singapore?

Yes. Muse launched in Singapore on October 28, 2026. Singapore is the fourth country to get Muse, after the United States on September 8, 2026, Canada on September 18, 2026, and Japan on October 23, 2026. It is free to use on iPhone, Android, and muse.ai, with a weekly usage allowance that Meta says covers most users. Paid plans run from S$22 to S$139 for more tokens and credits. Support for AI glasses is planned but not live yet.

The launch matters because Singapore is the first Southeast Asian country to get Muse, and the first launch where shopping and travel are the story from day one. Meta did not just switch on the app. It connected Muse to Shopee, Lazada, Grab, ShopBack, Singapore Airlines, Agoda, Klook, and Trip.com, plus Gmail, Google Calendar, and Instagram. Those connectors are rolling out progressively, so a service that is missing today may appear in the coming weeks. This page is the full guide: how to get Muse, what it costs, how to set up shopping and travel, what it gets wrong, and the privacy setting to change first.

We track the status every day. The full list of Muse available countries records every launch as it happens, and this page now shows Singapore as launched.

2. How do I get Muse in Singapore?

There is no workaround to do now. Download the Muse app from the App Store on your iPhone or from Google Play on your Android phone, or open muse.ai in a browser on your computer. Sign up with your usual account and you are in. The region check that used to stop signups from Singapore is gone, because Singapore is now a launched country.

Muse is free to start. The free plan comes with a weekly usage allowance that Meta says covers most users, so try it for a week before you think about paying. If you run out of allowance, paid plans run from S$22 to S$139 and buy more tokens and credits. Start free, see how much you actually use, and only pay if the allowance runs short.

When you sign up, connect the services you already use. Gmail and Google Calendar let Muse read your bookings and your schedule, and Instagram connects your saved posts and messages. The Singapore shopping and travel services connect the same way, in Settings, under Connectors. Connect one service at a time, and check what each one can do before you give it more access. The next sections walk through shopping and travel one by one.

If you have an invite code from the community pool, redeem it in Settings within 48 hours of joining. Codes still exist, and the pool is how members share them. Read how referral codes work and where to get one if you want the detail.

3. What can Muse do in Singapore?

Muse is a personal agent that acts for you inside the apps you already use. In Singapore that means the everyday services, not a demo. Nicole Tan, Managing Director of Meta Singapore, put it this way: "Muse is your personal agent, built to help make everyday life easier. We are connecting Muse with some of the most iconic brands in Singapore and the region, so you can get things done with the services you already use and love."

The launch connectors fall into three groups. Shopping: Shopee and Lazada for price comparison and product recommendations, Grab for everyday essentials and meal ingredients, and ShopBack for hotel, flight, and retail comparison with cashback. Travel: Klook for experiences, Agoda and Trip.com for hotels and flights, and Singapore Airlines for flights. Everyday: Gmail, Google Calendar, and Instagram, which give Muse your bookings, your schedule, and your saved posts to work with.

The point is not that Muse can open an app. The point is that it can chain them together. It can look at your calendar, see a free weekend, find a flight, compare hotels, and book an experience, all in one conversation. That is the difference between a chatbot that answers questions and an agent that gets things done. The next two sections show the shopping setup and the travel setup in detail, because that is where the launch is most useful and where the mistakes are most expensive.

4. How do I set up shopping with Muse?

Connect Shopee and Lazada first. Once they are connected, ask Muse to compare prices instead of opening two apps yourself. Tell it what you want in plain language, for example a universal travel adapter under SG$25, and it will compare listings and recommend one. Check the recommendation yourself before you buy, because the price it shows is only as good as the listing it found.

Grab is the everyday one. Connect Grab, then ask Muse to gather meal ingredients or everyday essentials. Muse puts together a basket and shows you the total and the delivery time before anything is ordered. Nothing is ordered without your approval. Muse asks first, and you say yes or no. That approval step is the whole safety net for shopping, and it is worth keeping on.

ShopBack is the comparison layer. Connect it and Muse can compare hotel, flight, and retail prices with cashback included, so the cheapest option on paper is not always the cheapest once cashback is counted. Ask Muse to show the cashback next to the price, so you can see the real total.

Three habits make shopping with Muse work well. First, connect one service at a time and test it with a small request before you trust it with a big one. Second, always read the basket, the total, and the delivery time before you approve. Third, remember the connectors are rolling out progressively. If a service you want is missing from Settings, check again in the coming weeks instead of assuming it will never arrive.

5. How do I set up travel with Muse?

Connect Gmail and Google Calendar before you connect the travel services. Muse uses your calendar to see when you are free and your email to see the bookings you already have. That is what lets it plan a trip around your real schedule instead of guessing. Then connect Klook, Agoda, Trip.com, and Singapore Airlines.

Klook is the experiences service. Ask Muse to find things to do at your destination and it can book experiences in the conversation, without sending you to another app. Agoda and Trip.com cover hotels and flights, so ask Muse to compare both before you pick. Singapore Airlines covers flights, and Muse can check its options alongside the others.

A good first trip to try is a short one. Ask Muse to plan a weekend away: tell it the dates, the budget, and what you like doing. It will check your calendar, compare flights and hotels, and suggest experiences from Klook. Read every step before you approve it. Check the flight times, the hotel location, and the total price yourself. Muse does the searching and the comparing. You do the deciding.

Travel is also where Muse is most impressive and most risky at the same time. It can watch the weather at your destination and flag travel requirements from your calendar, which is genuinely useful. But a wrong booking costs real money, so never approve a booking you have not read. The next section covers what Muse gets wrong, and it is worth reading before your first trip.

6. What does Muse get wrong in Singapore?

Muse can make mistakes, and the Singapore launch testing shows it plainly. In testing by The Straits Times, Muse monitored the weather and flagged travel requirements from the calendar, which worked well. Then it failed to cancel a restaurant booking after accessing a Chope account that did not exist, and it recommended a restaurant that had closed. Those are not small errors. One is an action taken on an account that was never real, and the other is a recommendation that wastes an evening.

Meta warns during registration that Muse can make mistakes and asks users to check its work. Take that warning seriously. Check prices before you approve a purchase. Check that a restaurant is still open before you go. Check that a booking was actually cancelled before you assume it was. Muse is good at searching and comparing, and it is not good enough yet to trust without checking.

The safest way to use Muse is to keep it on a short leash for anything that costs money. Let it search, compare, and draft. Keep the final yes for yourself. If Muse says it did something, ask it to show you the confirmation. If it cannot show you, treat it as not done. That one habit covers most of the ways Muse goes wrong.

7. How do I keep control of what Muse does?

Muse seeks approval before purchases or emails. That is the default, and it is the control that matters most. When Muse wants to order something or send something, it asks first and waits for your answer. Do not turn that off. If a service ever acts without asking, check its settings before you use it again.

Permissions can be restricted to read only. That means Muse can look at a service but cannot act in it. Use read only for any service you want Muse to check but not touch. For example, let Muse read your calendar to plan around it, but keep the power to book flights in your own hands until you trust it.

Passwords and payment methods are not visible to Muse. It cannot see them, and it cannot use them directly. That is a real limit, and it is also a protection. You still enter payment details yourself when a purchase is approved, so a mistaken order cannot complete without you.

The pattern to remember is simple. Muse proposes, you approve. Muse searches, you decide. Muse drafts, you send. As long as you hold the final step, Muse stays a helper instead of becoming a risk.

8. Which privacy setting should I turn off?

Do this before anything else. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off Help improve our AI models. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models.

One thing that has changed with the launch: there is no longer a location mismatch to worry about. Before October 28, 2026, using Muse from Singapore meant using it from a country where it had not launched, and an account could get flagged if Meta reviewed it. That risk is gone now that Singapore is launched. The privacy setting still matters, because it is about your chats, not your location.

9. Do I still need an invite code?

Invite codes still exist, and the community pool still shares them. If you have a code from the pool, redeem it in Settings within 48 hours of joining. The pool is also how members in countries where Muse has not launched yet get in when their turn comes, so codes keep moving even now that Singapore is live.

If you are in Singapore, the code is a bonus, not a barrier. You can sign up directly and start using Muse today. The code is worth having if you want to pass one on to a friend, or if you want to join the pool yourself. Read how referral codes work and where to get one for the full detail.

Learn what Muse actually does while you are at it. Read what Meta Muse is and what it can do, and how Muse tokens work. Your first day goes to using it, not figuring it out.

10. What about the VPN and second Apple ID workarounds?

Before October 28, 2026, the only way into Muse from Singapore was a workaround. On a laptop, the VPN method meant getting a free invite code, connecting to a US server, creating your account, and redeeming the code. On an iPhone, the second Apple ID method meant unlocking the US App Store, then using a US VPN to make the app work. Those methods were real, and they were the only option while Singapore was not launched.

They are history now. Do not use them to get Muse in Singapore. Sign up directly from Singapore, with your usual account, in the Singapore App Store or on muse.ai. The workarounds added risk and extra steps for no benefit, and they are no longer the way in. The old guides stay online as a record of how people got in before launch, and they still help readers in countries where Muse has not launched yet. For Singapore, they are done.

If you used a workaround before launch, you do not need to undo anything special. Your account is yours, and Singapore is now a launched country. Go to Settings and check that your connectors and permissions are set the way you want them, then use Muse normally.

The waitlist is now for other countries, not Singapore.
