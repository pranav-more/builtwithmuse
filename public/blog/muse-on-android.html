Does Meta Muse Work on Android?

Short answer: yes. Muse is on the Google Play Store for Android. The longer answer is that installing the app is only step one, because you still need an invite code to get inside.

1. Yes, it is on the Play Store

Meta Muse is available on Android through the Google Play Store. You install it like any other app: open the store, search for Muse, and download it. There is nothing unusual about the install itself.

One thing worth knowing: the Android app and the invite system are two separate things. The Play Store gives you the software. The invite code gives you the access. Plenty of people install the app, open it, and stall at the code screen because they expected the download to be the whole process. It is step one of two.

Once it is installed, open it and sign in to reach the screen where a code can be redeemed. The app guides you from there, and the redeem step itself takes less than a minute.

2. Install it the safe way

Search results on the Play Store can be crowded, and popular apps attract copycats. Before you tap install, check that the developer listed is Meta. If the name looks slightly off, or the app asks for strange permissions at install time, back out and search again. The real listing is the one published by Meta itself.

Do not sideload a Muse APK from a random site, no matter what the page promises. Sideloaded builds are a classic way to distribute malware, and they buy you nothing here: even a perfect copy of the app still needs an invite code to get you inside. The Play Store is the only download source you should trust.

Keep the app updated once it is installed. Invite only apps change fast during a rollout, and an outdated build can throw strange errors at the redeem screen that look like a bad code but are really just an old app. If a redeem fails, updating first is cheaper than hunting for a second code.

3. You still need an invite code

Installing the app is only step one. Muse is invite only, so the app alone does not get you inside. You need a code from someone who is already a user, either a friend or a stranger sharing through the community pool. Without a code, the app is just an icon on your home screen.

Codes are free by design. When your code is redeemed, both you and the person who shared it receive 1 billion Muse tokens each. Nobody in this chain pays anything, which is also why you should never buy a code from anyone. A seller is charging for something the system gives away.

The fastest legitimate source is the community pool on this site, where members share working codes. The full walkthrough is in how to get a free Muse invite code.

4. The one sitting setup

Do the whole thing in one sitting: install, sign in, claim a code, redeem it in Settings. This is not just tidiness. Codes must be redeemed within 48 hours of joining, and the clock is easy to lose track of once the code is sitting in a chat thread. One sitting means no loose ends.

An order that works:

  1. Install Muse from the Play Store and open it.
  2. Sign in with the account you want to keep. Decide now, because switching accounts later is its own headache.
  3. Claim a code from the community pool. Copy it carefully, character by character. Most "invalid code" errors are typos.
  4. Open Settings in the app and redeem the code immediately.
  5. Confirm it worked by checking your token balance. Our guide on checking your token balance shows where to look.

Do this on a stable connection. A redeem that drops halfway can leave you unsure whether it went through, and guessing wrong means either a wasted code or a needless support hunt.

5. Redeem in Settings within 48 hours

Once you have a code, do not sit on it. Redeem it in Settings within 48 hours of joining, according to Muse's current invite copy. When the redeem goes through, both you and the person who shared the code receive 1 billion Muse tokens each. The window is short on purpose, so redeem the moment you are set up.

The most common failure here is not technical, it is human. People claim a code "for later", the chat scrolls on, and by the time they look for it the window has closed. Treat a claimed code like a boarding pass: useless once the gate closes, so use it now.

If a redeem fails, rule out the simple causes first. Check for typos in the code, confirm you are signed in to the right account, and make sure the app is updated. If it still fails, the code may already be used. Claim a fresh one from the pool rather than retrying the same string.

6. Android gotchas worth knowing

Android is not one thing. It is thousands of device and account combinations, and a few of them trip people up.

  • Multiple Google accounts. If your phone has more than one Google account, the Play Store may install Muse under one while you sign in to the app with another. That is fine, but keep track of which account holds your Muse access so you do not lock yourself out later.
  • Work profiles. Apps installed in a work profile live in a separate container. Install Muse in your personal profile unless you have a reason not to, so your access is not tied to a device policy you do not control.
  • Play Store country versus where you are. The store you see is set by your Play country, not your location. If Muse is missing from your store, check which country your Play account is set to before assuming anything about availability. Google limits how often you can change it, so do not switch casually.
  • Battery savers and data saver. Aggressive battery optimization can delay notifications, including the waitlist email alert you are waiting for. If you are expecting a code by email, keep notifications on for your mail app.

None of these are Muse specific. They are the usual Android friction points, and knowing them in advance saves a support thread.

7. What if it is not in your country's Play Store

Muse has not launched everywhere yet, so the app may not appear in your country's Play Store. This is a rollout decision by Meta, not a problem with your phone, and reinstalling the Play Store will not fix it.

If that is your situation, the country guides have you covered: how to use Muse if it has not launched in your country and how to use Muse in India. They cover the current state of access and the settings worth knowing about.

And if you would rather just wait, join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day. Waiting is a legitimate strategy when the alternative is fighting region restrictions.

8. Your first hour inside Muse

Once the redeem goes through, you are in, and both sides have their tokens. Tokens never expire, so there is no rush to spend them. Start by checking your balance so you know where you stand. Our token balance guide shows exactly where to look.

Then put the app to real work. Give it something you actually need done and see what comes back. The point of the first hour is calibration: learning what it is good at, where it needs clearer instructions, and how much context helps. Vague prompts get vague results, so bring a real task, not a test sentence.

Your own invite code becomes shareable now too, and every redeem earns both sides the token bonus, so sharing it is genuinely win win.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Muse Android app free to download?
Downloading the app costs nothing. What grants access is the invite code, which is also free through the community pool.
Do I need an invite code on Android?
Yes. Muse is invite only on every platform, so the app alone does not get you inside.
How long do I have to redeem my code?
Redeem it in Settings within 48 hours of joining, according to Muse's current invite copy.
What if Muse is not in my country's Play Store?
Muse has not launched everywhere yet. Check the country guides linked in this article, or join the waitlist for a code on launch day.
Can I sideload the Muse APK from another site?
Do not. Sideloaded builds are a common malware vector, and they get you nothing extra because you still need an invite code. The Play Store is the only source to trust.
Can I use one Muse account on two phones?
Meta has not published details on using one account across multiple devices. Try signing in with the same account on the second phone, but confirm everything works before relying on it.
Will my tokens move with me to a new phone?
Tokens are linked to your account rather than the phone itself, so in principle they follow you when you sign in on the new device. Meta has not published the exact mechanics, so confirm your balance after switching.
The Play Store says the app is not compatible with my device. What now?
Update Android and the Play Store itself first, then check your store country. Some devices are simply not supported during the rollout, and Meta has not published a compatibility list. If it stays unavailable, the waitlist is your fallback.
