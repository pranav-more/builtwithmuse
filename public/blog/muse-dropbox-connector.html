How to Use the Dropbox Connector in Muse

Connect Dropbox to Muse and your files stop being a place you dig through and start being material Muse can work with: find the latest version, build a shared link, or run a client onboarding from the folders you already have. Here is how to connect it and the settings to check first.

1. The short answer

As of October 25, 2026, Dropbox is a live connector in Muse. Dropbox says so itself, on its own help page: once connected, Muse can find and retrieve Dropbox files, create files and folders, move, copy, organize, or delete items, create and inspect shared links, and start and manage file requests. The connector was available from September 28, 2026, in English in the United States and Canada, across all Dropbox accounts and Muse plans, and Dropbox named it again in the October 7 iPad app release notes, which listed Dropbox among the business connectors. It was still listed in the October 15 in app check, which counted 54 connectors in the web app. There is no October change to how it works. One limit to know up front: Dropbox says the connector is designed for individual use and is not intended for enterprise or team deployments, so this guide is written for a person and their own files, not for an admin rolling it out to a company.

Here is the honest version. The Dropbox connector matters because your files are where your work already lives. Proposals, contracts, brand material, client folders, invoices, photos, recordings. Before this connector, Muse could talk about your work in the abstract. With it, Muse can open the actual folder and work from the actual files. The value is not that Muse can find a file. Dropbox search can do that. The value is that Muse can reason across a whole folder: which version is the latest, what is missing from this client folder, what did we agree in that contract, and what should go in the welcome pack for a new client. The rest of this guide covers how to connect it, the per action settings to check first, and the uses that are actually worth your time.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • One Dropbox account. The connector works with the Dropbox account you connect, and only that account. Pick the one where your work files actually live.
  • Your Dropbox sign in. You connect with your Dropbox account, the same way you sign in to Dropbox itself.
  • Approvals set to ask me. Before you connect anything, set approvals so nothing is created, moved, deleted, or shared without your tap. This is the single most important step on this page, because this connector can change your files, not just read them.
  • Folders worth connecting. The connector is only as good as what is in your Dropbox. A folder with clear names beats a pile of files called final, final2, and final FINAL. Tidy the names before you connect, and everything after gets easier.

3. How to connect it, step by step

The connection itself takes a few minutes. The setup around it matters more, because this connector can write and delete, so run the steps in order and do not skip the permissions step at the end.

  1. Open Muse and go to Settings. The connectors list lives there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  2. Find Dropbox in the connectors list and open it. If Dropbox is not listed, your account has not received it yet. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Check again in a few days.
  3. Click Connect and review the connection details. Read what Muse is asking for before you accept it. If a scope surprises you, stop and check what it covers before continuing.
  4. Sign in to Dropbox and authorize Muse. Use the Dropbox account where your work files live. Remember this is the one account the connector will use, so sign in with the right one the first time.
  5. Set the per action permissions. After you connect, open the Dropbox connector settings in Muse. You can change the setting for each action: reading or downloading files, creating or deleting content, managing file requests, and sharing content. Set anything that changes files to ask you first. You can also reset to defaults if you ever want to start over.
  6. Set approvals to ask me. In Muse settings, set approvals so that creating, moving, deleting, and sharing always wait for you. This is what stops Muse from ever reorganizing a folder you have not reviewed.
  7. Test with a read only request. Ask Muse to find a file you know is there and summarize it. Read the summary against the original. If it matches, the connection is good. Do not ask it to move or delete anything on the first day.
  8. Use it read only for a week. Finding, reading, and summarizing only. Once you trust what it reads and how it describes your files, start approving one small change at a time.

You are my file assistant. Use my Dropbox connector only.
Rules: you never create, move, delete, or share anything without my explicit approval. You find, read, and summarize. I approve every change.
My folders: [name your main folders, e.g. Clients, Finance, Brand]
Every time I ask about a file, do this in order.

2. Summarize what is inside it in a few short lines.
3. If there are several versions, tell me which one is the latest and what changed between the two newest.
4. If something is missing that should be there, say so plainly. Do not guess.
5. End by asking me what to do next. Do not act until I say so.

4. What it actually does

The connector earns its place with five uses. Everything else is a variation on one of them.

Find the file, and the right version of it

Ask Muse to find the contract for a named client, the latest brand guidelines, or the invoice from last month. This beats searching Dropbox yourself when you remember what a file is about but not what it is called, because Muse reads for meaning. The version question is where it earns its keep: ask which copy is the latest, what changed between the two newest versions, and whether the one a client was sent matches the one on file. Check the answer against the folder before you quote a number or a date from it to anyone, because a confident answer about the wrong version is worse than no answer at all. Our Gmail connector guide covers the other half of this habit, finding the message that sent the file in the first place.

Brief yourself from a folder

Point Muse at a project folder and ask for the state of it: what is in there, what is missing, and what needs to happen next. This is the use Dropbox itself describes, and it is the one that saves the most time on a Monday morning. A good folder briefing tells you where the project stands without you opening ten files. Use it before a client call, before a handover, and when you return to a project after a month away. The briefing is only as honest as the folder, so if the folder is a mess the briefing will say so, and that is useful information too. Fix the folder, then ask again.

Run a client onboarding from your materials

This is the standout use Dropbox describes for small businesses. When a new client signs, your checklists, welcome materials, and project templates are already sitting in Dropbox. Ask Muse to onboard the client using those materials, and it can pull the documents together, create a dedicated folder for the client, set up a file request for the information that is still missing, and draft a welcome note in your voice. The point is not that any one of those steps is hard. The point is that doing all of them, in order, without dropping one, is where onboarding usually breaks. Muse holds the sequence. You approve each step. The file request deserves a special mention: instead of chasing a client over email for a logo or a form, Muse can create a request that lets the client upload straight into the right folder, and then tell you when it arrives.

Build shared links without opening Dropbox

Ask Muse to create a shared link for a file or a folder and tell you what the link covers. This is a small use that adds up, because sharing is the step people usually do in a rush, and a rushed share is how the wrong file goes to the wrong person. Slow it down: ask Muse what is in the folder first, then approve the link. And remember that connecting Muse does not change your existing Dropbox sharing settings. What was shared stays shared, what was private stays private, and Muse works inside the sharing you already set. If a folder should never be shared, say so in your prompt rules and keep the approval setting on ask for sharing.

Turn stored material into new work

If your Dropbox holds long form material, brand guidelines, past campaign plans, a recorded walkthrough, you can ask Muse to build from it instead of starting blank. A week of social captions from your brand voice, a video description from your campaign plan, a one page summary of a long document for a client who will not read the long version. The material you already made is the raw stock, and Muse is the junior who reads it all before drafting. The drafts still need your read, because Muse can only remix what you stored, and if the stored material is out of date the draft will be too. Keep the source files current and the output stays honest.

5. What it cannot do

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector.

  • It cannot show you file previews inside Muse. Dropbox says so plainly: at launch, Muse does not include file previews or an embedded Dropbox interface. You get answers about your files, not a view of them. Open the file in Dropbox when the layout, the formatting, or the exact wording matters.
  • It cannot change your Dropbox sharing settings by connecting. Connecting Muse leaves your sharing exactly as it was. That is good news for safety and a limit for convenience: if a folder was never shared, Muse does not share it for you without the approval you set.
  • It cannot see files outside the account you connected. Files in another Dropbox account, another cloud drive, or a local folder are invisible to it. It reasons from what you connected, nothing more. Our connectors list shows the other file tools Muse can reach if your files live in more than one place.
  • It cannot run a team deployment. Dropbox designed this connector for individual use. If you are an admin looking to roll it out across a company, this is not the tool for that, and the per action settings are set per person, not per team.
  • It cannot guarantee it found the right file. A summary can quote the wrong version, and a search can return the draft instead of the final. Check anything that touches money, legal terms, or a client commitment against the original file before you act on it.

6. What to watch for

As of October 25, 2026, Dropbox is a settled connector: live since September 28, named again in the October 7 iPad app release notes, and still listed in the October 15 in app check. There is no October update to how it works, so the habits around it still matter. The most important habit is the approval habit. This connector can create, move, and delete, which makes it more powerful than the read only connectors and more dangerous if approvals are loose. Keep every action that changes files on ask, and keep it that way even after you trust it. Trust is not a reason to remove the safety net. It is the reason the safety net can stay quiet in the background.

Be careful with sensitive files. Contracts, financial records, identity documents, and anything with client data deserve a human read before any change goes out, even a small one. A move is a small change with big consequences if the file lands in the wrong folder and someone acts on the old location. Set a rule for yourself: Muse finds and drafts, you read, you approve. The moment you start approving without reading is the moment the connector stops being an assistant and starts being a risk.

Check your data controls. In Muse settings, open Data controls and look at the switch for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to read your files. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. And remember that connecting Dropbox means Meta's terms and privacy policies apply to how Muse accesses and uses your content, while Dropbox keeps your source files safe in your account. Two companies, two sets of rules, and the file stays yours in Dropbox either way.

Finally, keep your folders tidy, because the connector rewards tidiness more than any other connector on this site. Clear folder names, one latest version, and an archive folder for the old ones. A tidy Dropbox turns Muse from a search box into a colleague who knows where everything is. A messy Dropbox turns it into a colleague who keeps bringing you the wrong draft. The ten minutes you spend renaming files pays for itself the first time Muse finds the right one without being asked twice.

Frequently asked questions

What can Muse do with my Dropbox files?
Muse can find and retrieve files, create files and folders, move, copy, organize, or delete items, create and inspect shared links, and start and manage file requests. Anything that changes your files asks for your approval first when your approvals are set to ask me.
Which Dropbox account does Muse see?
Only the one you connect. Muse works with the content available through that Dropbox account and the access you authorize. Files in another account stay invisible to it, and you can manage or disconnect the integration at any time.
Can Muse change my Dropbox sharing settings?
No. Connecting Muse does not change your existing Dropbox sharing settings. What was shared stays shared and what was private stays private. Muse works inside the sharing you already set, and sharing actions ask for approval first.
Can I use the Dropbox connector outside the United States, Canada, and Japan?
Not yet. Muse itself has launched in the United States, Canada, and Japan only, and Dropbox says the connector is available in English in the United States and Canada. Join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day in your country.
Can I preview Dropbox files inside Muse?
Not at launch. Dropbox says Muse does not include file previews or an embedded Dropbox interface. You get answers about your files, so open the file in Dropbox when the layout or the exact wording matters.
What happens if I disconnect Dropbox?
Muse loses access to those files. Your files stay in Dropbox, and nothing Muse planned is carried out after you disconnect. You can reconnect the same account or a different one later.
