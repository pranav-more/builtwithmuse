Is Muse available in Argentina?

As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US, where it launched on September 8, 2026; Canada, on September 18; Japan, on October 23; Singapore, on October 28; and Mexico, on November 5. Argentina is not among them. A Spanish language availability guide updated in early November states the position plainly: Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, and Chile have no date. The guide records no launch for the rest of Latin America beyond Mexico.

The absence is easy to confirm yourself. Search your App Store or Google Play store for Muse from Meta and you will not find the listing, because the listing is only published in the five launched markets. The same applies on the web: muse.ai loads from anywhere, and the block appears when you try to sign in. That pattern is the normal one for an unlaunched country, not a fault on your phone.

Meta has published no country list and no timeline for what comes next, and its help pages say only that Muse is not available in all locations yet. An availability tracker checked against Meta's own announcements lists every other country as no launch announced. Until Meta announces Argentina, every date you see attached to an Argentina launch is a guess. This guide will not invent one, and it will show you how to spot the launch when it actually happens.

What do the App Store checks show?

The most useful check is a storefront sweep, because it tests what your phone actually sees. On November 7, 2026, a Spanish language guide checked 32 App Stores and found Muse present in the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and, for the first time, Mexico, and absent in the other 27, including Argentina. The same check on October 29 and October 31 had already shown Argentina empty.

An earlier check tells the same story from a different angle. On September 10, 2026, a check of Apple's lookup service found the Muse listing in the US store and in none of 15 other storefronts tested. The listing carries a 17+ content rating and needs iOS 18.0 or later, but the rating is beside the point in Argentina: the listing is not published there at all.

Store checks matter because they are evidence, not opinion. A launch in Argentina would show up first as a listing in the Argentine store, the way Mexico's listing appeared on November 5 with the name Muse de Meta and a publication date of that day. Checking your own store is free, takes seconds, and beats any article claiming a date.

What does the Mexico launch mean for Argentina?

Mexico launched on November 5, 2026, as the fifth market and the first in Latin America, in Spanish, for users 18 and over, on iOS, Android, and the web. The Mexico guide covers that launch in full. For Argentine readers it is the most important launch since the US one, because it proves two things at once: Meta can run Muse in Spanish, and Latin America is on the rollout map.

What it does not prove is timing. Mexico's launch was announced the same day it happened, with no advance date, and Meta has said nothing about which country follows. The Spanish language guide tracking availability puts it bluntly: for the rest of Latin America, Mexico changes nothing yet. Argentina, Colombia, and Chile have no date, and no order of arrival has been announced.

There is also a practical reason launches stay narrow. On the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg said Muse is compute intensive to run and that the delay was partly about running it efficiently and partly about tailoring it to each country, including work on connectors. Each new market needs its own connector work, and Mexico's seven launch connectors were Mexican businesses. An Argentina launch would need its own set, and none has been named.

Will Muse speak Spanish?

Yes, almost certainly, but with one honest caveat. The official Muse App Store listing includes Spanish among 39 languages, so Spanish speakers in the US already use Muse in Spanish. That listing is the strongest language evidence in circulation. And Mexico launched in Spanish, which means Meta has already shipped a Spanish language Muse experience. The language is not the blocker for Argentina.

The caveat is that Meta has published no language list for Muse itself, so nobody outside Meta can confirm exactly which languages the agent supports on day one in a new market. Spanish is the safest expectation in this whole guide: it is the launch language of the one Latin American market, it is in the store listing, and it is the language Meta AI has spoken in Argentina since July 2024.

That last point deserves emphasis, because it separates the two products. Meta AI, the assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, arrived in Argentina in July 2024 as part of a Spanish language expansion covering Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Mexico, Peru, and Cameroon. The expansion added Spanish support across Meta's apps. Muse is a different product with a different rollout, and its Spanish support comes from its own launch, not from Meta AI's. But the precedent shows Meta does not treat Spanish as an afterthought.

What about Muse inside WhatsApp?

Muse inside WhatsApp follows the same country list as everything else. If Muse has not launched in Argentina, the Muse chat inside WhatsApp has not either, because the gate is your Meta account, not the app you open. Mexico's launch coverage said Muse would be usable from WhatsApp, without giving a date for that part. Argentina has neither the app nor the WhatsApp route.

What does work inside WhatsApp in Argentina is Meta AI. It has been there since July 2024, answering questions in Spanish inside the chat app. It is not Muse: it does not connect to your email and calendar to complete tasks, it does not ask for approvals before acting, and it has no invite codes. The confusion between the two is the single most common misunderstanding in Argentina, and the guide comparing Muse and Meta AI sets out the differences.

When Muse does reach WhatsApp in Argentina, the sign will be unmistakable: a Muse entry point inside the app that asks you to connect a Muse account, the way it works in launched markets. Until then, any WhatsApp contact or number claiming to be Muse in Argentina is not Meta's product. Do not hand it your details.

Can a VPN unlock Muse in Argentina?

Treat every tutorial that promises this as unverified. The reason is structural: the gate sits at the account layer, not the web layer. Muse's marketing pages load from anywhere, and the check happens when you sign in, against your Meta account. A VPN changes the address you appear to browse from. It does not change the country on your Meta account.

That is why reports of VPN results are mixed, and why this guide promises no method works. Some people report getting through with a US account and a VPN; others report the same block, or an account that stops working later. One guide that tested the question warns that Meta checks more than your IP address, including your payment card country and your app store region, and that a mismatch can lead to suspension. Treat that as caution, not as a verified fact about how Meta enforces its rules: Meta publishes no enforcement procedure.

The risk to weigh is your Meta account itself, which holds your Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp history. Workarounds that misrepresent your region sit against Meta's terms, and losing the account would cost far more than early access is worth. The honest position is simple: waiting is the only route this guide can stand behind, and the wait is for Meta's announcement, not for a better tutorial.

What would Muse cost in Argentina?

There is no Argentina price, no peso price, and no announcement of either. The only pricing in circulation is the reported US pricing: free with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy weekly usage capacity, not features: the agent, the connectors, and the approvals are the same on every tier.

The free tier deserves a precise note. The figure of 100 million tokens a week comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, not from Meta's help pages, so treat it as reported, not as a published limit. Meta's help pages describe subscriptions as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, which is another way of saying the pricing you see depends on where your account lives.

Do not convert $20 at the day rate and call it the Argentina price. Meta has announced no Argentina pricing, no peso pricing, and no date for either. The checkout screen inside your own account, on launch day, is the only price that will bind you. Until then, budget in dollars and expect the US figures to be the starting point, not a promise.

Which privacy setting should you change first?

Before your first real task, change one setting. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off "Help improve our AI models". Toggle the switch off and confirm "Turn off" when the dialog asks. From that point, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models. It takes seconds, and it is the one choice that governs what happens to your words.

Why does this guide put a privacy switch before any feature? Because the setting governs chats from the start of the account, and every chat before you change it falls under the old rule. The guides for launched markets say the same: set it first, then connect your inbox and calendar. It changes nothing about what the agent can do for you. It only changes what Meta may do with your chats.

Pair it with the approval habit. Muse asks before it sends, books, or spends anything, and reading each approval is how you stay in charge of an agent that acts in the world. The data switch and the approval habit together are the whole safety setup: one governs your words, the other governs its actions.
