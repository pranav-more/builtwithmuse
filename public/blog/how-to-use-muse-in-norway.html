How to Use Muse in Norway

Muse is not available in Norway yet, and Meta has announced no date. Add Datatilsynet’s 2023 precedent, the Norwegian language question, and the glasses that already carry Meta AI, and Norway has the most interesting waiting position in Europe. This guide sets it out.

Is Muse available in Norway?

No. Muse is not available in Norway, and Meta has announced no launch date for Norway. Norway is grouped with the rest of Europe in every availability check: waiting, with no date, and no statement that names the country.

The launch list has five names. The US on September 8, 2026. Canada on September 18. Japan on October 23. Singapore on October 28. Mexico, the fifth market and the first in Latin America, on November 5. Norway is not among them, and neither is any other European country. Meta publishes no country list and no timeline for further launches. The full list is in our Muse available countries guide.

The Norwegian App Store has no Muse listing, and a Norwegian account meets the waitlist screen. One tracker groups Norway with Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Switzerland under a single line, no date yet, as BreakOrbit’s availability page shows. Another found no announcement for any of the 30 EEA countries. This guide was last checked on November 13, 2026.

Norway is in the EEA, not the EU. What does that change?

Norway’s position is the most misunderstood in this whole series of guides, so it is worth getting precise. Norway is not a member of the European Union. It is a member of the European Economic Area, the EEA, together with Iceland and Liechtenstein. That distinction changes some things about Muse’s launch, and changes nothing about others.

What it changes is how EU law reaches Norway. EU regulations do not apply in Norway automatically. They are incorporated into the EEA Agreement by a decision of the EEA Joint Committee, and then implemented in Norwegian law by the Norwegian parliament. The EU AI Act is marked as EEA relevant and is expected to follow that path, but as of August 2026 the exact Norwegian timeline had not been finalised, according to an operators guide to Norwegian AI regulation. Norway has moved on its own track in the meantime: Nkom became the national coordinating AI supervisor on March 21, 2025, and KI Norge, a national AI arena inside Digdir, launched on June 12, 2026.

What it does not change is the practical position for a product sold into Norway. The AI Act applies to AI systems placed on the EU or EEA market, and to AI whose output is used there, regardless of where the provider is established, under Article 2. A company selling an AI agent to Norwegian users has to meet the Act’s requirements whether or not Norway’s own implementing law has caught up, as a guide to the AI Act in Norway explains. The GDPR reached Norway the same way, through the EEA Agreement and then the Norwegian Personal Data Act, which entered into force in July 2018. The pattern is incorporation, not exemption.

For Muse, the EEA distinction is therefore not a shortcut. Norway does not get Muse sooner because it is outside the EU, and it does not get Muse later because its implementing law is pending. Meta groups Norway with the EEA, the trackers group Norway with the EEA, and the launch, when it comes, will almost certainly be a European launch that includes Norway, not a Norway only launch. The EEA question is interesting law. It is not a reason to expect different timing.

Datatilsynet: the regulator that set the European precedent

Norway’s data protection authority, Datatilsynet, has already shaped how Meta operates across Europe, and the story is the strongest evidence that Norway’s regulator matters far beyond Norway’s borders.

On July 14, 2023, Datatilsynet ordered Meta to stop behavioral advertising on Facebook and Instagram in Norway unless it obtained consent, with fines of 1 million kroner a day for failing to comply, as MediaPost reported. The order took effect on August 4, 2023, and the daily fines began to accrue while Meta argued that its Irish regulator was already handling the issue.

Datatilsynet then asked the European Data Protection Board to extend the ban, and on October 27, 2023, the Board adopted an urgent binding decision imposing the ban across the entire European Economic Area, all 30 countries, as Reuters reported. A regulator in a country outside the EU had set a rule that bound the whole EEA. Meta moved to a consent based approach and a subscription model in response. That is the precedent to remember when someone says Norway’s position does not matter to Meta. It has mattered before, decisively.

The scrutiny has not stopped. In late October 2026, the Oslo District Court temporarily blocked Meta’s new commercial terms after ten Norwegian media companies, including NRK, TV 2, and Schibsted, sued, arguing the terms would let Meta use their content without a way to refuse, according to PPC Land’s report. The order is interim relief, not a final judgment, and its scope is unclear from the published material. It is included here for one reason: Norway’s courts and regulators are actively testing Meta’s terms right now, in the same months Muse is expanding elsewhere. That climate does not predict a Muse date. It explains why Meta would be careful about a Norwegian launch.

Will Muse speak Norwegian?

Meta has said nothing about Norwegian language support for Muse. No announcement names Norwegian, and no launch coverage discusses it. Norwegian speakers should treat every claim about Norwegian support as unconfirmed.

The language history is thinner for Norwegian than for most European languages. Norwegian was not one of the six languages in the Meta AI European launch on March 20, 2025, as TechCrunch reported at the time. Norwegian does not appear on WhatsApp’s current list of device languages for Meta AI access either, a list that includes Danish, Dutch, and Polish. WhatsApp says you can chat with Meta AI in any language, and the list governs device language access in some countries rather than chat language, so the absence is not proof that Meta AI cannot work in Norwegian. But it is a thin signal, and it is the only signal there is.

Norway adds a wrinkle that no other country in this series has: two written standards, Bokmål and Nynorsk, both official, plus Sami languages with their own status. Meta has said nothing about which written standard a Norwegian Muse would use, or whether it would handle both. That is a detail for launch day, not a reason to expect a delay, but it is a reminder that Norwegian support is not one question. It is several.

The launches so far show Meta localizes per country, Japanese for Japan and Spanish for Mexico, which argues for Norwegian support if and when Norway launches. The counterweight is that Norway is a small market by Meta’s standards, and small markets rarely get first day language work. The test, as ever, is launch day itself: write in Norwegian, check the answers, check the actions.

Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Norway: the closest thing to Muse today

The closest thing to a Muse presence in Norway today is not an app. It is a pair of glasses. Meta AI voice on Ray-Ban Meta glasses expanded to Norway on April 23, 2025, as part of a seven country European expansion that also covered Germany, Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Sweden, and Finland, according to Meta’s own announcement.

That matters for two reasons. First, it proves Meta can and does ship AI features to Norway on the same timetable as EU member states, EEA status notwithstanding. The glasses expansion treated Norway as part of Europe, not as an exception. Second, it gives Norwegian users a way to experience Meta’s voice assistant today, on their face, in a product that is actually sold in Norway.

It matters just as much to state what the glasses are not. Meta AI on glasses is a voice assistant that answers questions and identifies what the camera sees. Muse is a standalone agent that takes actions across apps. They share a company and a name family, and they are different products, as our Muse vs Meta AI guide explains. Voice features from Muse itself, including the Muse voice avatar that rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, are US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers. The glasses do not bring Muse to Norway. They show that Norway is not forgotten in Meta’s European hardware plans, which is a softer signal, and worth exactly that much.

If you own Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Norway, the voice assistant on them is the nearest preview of how Meta’s AI sounds and responds. Use it to learn what voice AI does well and badly. Do not use it as evidence that Muse is coming soon. The two products have separate launches, separate announcements, and, so far, separate maps.

WhatsApp and Messenger in Norway

Norway’s messaging habits are split, and the split is closer than the headlines suggest. Two surveys point in different directions, and the truth is that both of Meta’s apps are big in Norway.

Sinch, citing a Global GWI survey, names Messenger as Norway’s most popular messaging app, in Sinch’s country by country table. Sensor Tower’s Q4 2025 data shows WhatsApp with about 1.7 million weekly active users in Norway and Messenger above 1 million, in Sensor Tower’s Norway data. WhatsApp also tops the free Communication chart on Google Play in Norway, in AppBrain’s November 13, 2026 ranking.

For Muse, the split means the door is uncertain. Muse works inside WhatsApp in launched markets, covered in our WhatsApp guide. Its Messenger surface is unconfirmed. A Norwegian user who lives in Messenger and a Norwegian user who lives in WhatsApp should both check the standalone app first on launch day, because the standalone app is the product and the messaging surfaces follow Meta’s per country choices.

Meta AI reached Norway in the March 20, 2025 European rollout, as one of the 14 countries outside the EU included alongside the 27 EU members, as TechCrunch reported. Norwegian users therefore already have Meta’s assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger. Muse would be a separate arrival, and its absence is not a sign that Meta has skipped Norway. It is a sign that the agent product has not started its European expansion at all.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Norway?

A VPN will not reliably get you Muse from Norway, and the reasons are specific to how Muse checks location, not to Norway. The tutorials describe routing traffic through a launch country. Muse checks more than traffic.

Your App Store region, your account country, your payment method, and the card used for age verification all carry location signals, and they leak past a VPN. A Norwegian card and a Norwegian store region say Norway even when the IP address says the United States. The usual result is the waitlist screen, or an account that works briefly and then stops. Our App Store guide explains the store checks.

The account risk deserves the same careful wording used in every guide in this series. Meta may suspend or close an account that shows a location mismatch. That is a caution reported by users and guides, not a verified Meta policy. No source has published a Meta rule that says VPN use leads to a ban, and no source has proven it safe. If you try anyway, use an account that holds nothing important.

Norway adds one more reason to wait. The EEA position means a Norwegian launch, when it comes, will likely arrive with Norwegian specific terms, a Norwegian App Store listing, and possibly Norwegian language support. A workaround account gives you none of those, configured for another country, at risk, in another language. The real launch is the better product. The VPN is a worse version of it, sooner, maybe, briefly.

What will Muse cost in Norway?

Pricing is the emptiest question in this guide. Meta has said nothing about what Muse would cost in Norway, in kroner or otherwise, and no Norwegian figure exists in any announcement, help page, or launch report. Readers sometimes quote the United States figures as if they were global, and they are the only figures available: a free tier bounded by weekly use, Power at $20 each month, and Maximum at $100 each month. The distinction between the tiers is capacity. A paying user gets a bigger allowance, not a different product, and the free tier runs the same agent within a smaller weekly limit.

Norway’s currency question is sharper than most. Norway is outside the EU and outside the euro, so a Norwegian price would be a krone price, set for the Norwegian market, and it would need to account for Norwegian VAT, which Meta has not addressed publicly for Muse. Do not convert $20 into kroner and call it the price. The conversion tells you the exchange rate, not Meta’s pricing decision.

India shows how Meta prices when it prices locally. The Hindu reported on November 9, 2026 that Power would cost Rs 1,799 a month in India and Maximum Rs 8,999 a month, and Meta has not confirmed either figure. If Norway follows the same pattern, its price will be a krone figure set for the Norwegian market, not a dollar figure converted at the exchange rate. The free allowance deserves equal caution. The figure of 100 million tokens a week comes from Mark Zuckerberg’s launch statement, not from Meta’s help pages, and those pages describe subscriptions as in limited testing and not available in all locations, as one availability guide notes.

The short answer: expect a free tier, expect a krone price that does not exist yet, and expect the price to be announced with the launch, not before it. If the cost matters to your decision, the launch announcement is the document to wait for.

What will launch day look like in Norway?

A Norwegian launch would most likely arrive as part of a European launch, not alone. The pattern from Meta AI in 2025, 41 countries at once, suggests that when Europe gets Muse, it gets it in a wave. Norway, as an EEA member, would plausibly be in that wave, but plausibly is not certainly, and no source has said so. Watch for Norway to be named.

Norway changes nothing about the first hour, and the first hour has a fixed order. The age check comes before anything else, because Muse is 18 and older, and it is proved with a payment card or a linked Instagram or Facebook account. With a card, expect a $1 temporary authorization that is refunded in 5 to 7 business days, and expect strictness: our age verification guide records the card declines and prepaid failures users have reported since October. Once the account exists, the setting comes next, and the code after it. Open Settings, then Data controls, turn off “Help improve our AI models”, confirming “Turn off” when asked, and redeem an invite code within 48 hours of creating the account for the 1 billion token bonus on both sides.

Then start small and check the language. Write in Norwegian, or in the language you actually use, and see whether Muse answers well and acts correctly. Test one connection, one app, one job. Check whether the connectors you need work with Norwegian services, because connector availability varies by country and is announced per market, as it was for Mexico.

Do not expect the full US feature set. Voice is US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers. Outbound calling, generally available November 12, 2026, is for paid US subscribers only, as our calling guide explains. Muse Mail, launched November 10, 2026, is for paid subscribers in the US and Canada, as our Muse Mail guide covers. Spaces, launched November 6, 2026, should be available everywhere Muse is available. A Norwegian launch day with a working agent, a Norwegian listing, and a growing set of connections is a good day, even with the newest features still to come.
