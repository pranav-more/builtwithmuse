Is Muse available in Peru?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US (September 8, 2026), Canada (September 18), Japan (October 23), Singapore (October 28), and Mexico (November 5). Peru is not among them, and Meta has announced no launch date for Peru. The country list shows the same five, and nothing else.

The Peruvian store tells the same story. A storefront sweep of 32 App Stores on November 7, 2026 found Muse present in the five launched markets and absent in the other 27, including Peru. The app is not listed in the Peruvian store, and the web route ends at sign in with the not available message.

Meta publishes no country list and no expansion timeline. Its help pages say only that Muse is not available in all locations yet. Until Meta announces Peru, there is no date, no window, and no queue to join, and any page claiming otherwise is guessing.

Why is there no Peru specific news?

Here is an unusual fact about Peru: no Peru specific statement about Muse exists at all. Searches in Spanish and English turn up regional tables and Latin America roundups, but no Peruvian outlet has reported a Peru date, a Peru statement from Meta, or a Peru specific launch hint. Peru appears in availability tables only as part of the rest of Latin America, with no announcement. The tracker lists Brazil and the rest of Latin America as no launch announced.

That silence is itself information. It means no local launch is being teased, no local press has been briefed, and no regional announcement names Peru. The Spanish language availability guide tracking the rollout names Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, and Chile as having no date, and Peru sits in the same position by the same logic: unlaunched, unannounced, unnamed. For the rest of Latin America, Mexico changes nothing yet.

Silence also creates room for invented dates. Pages that attach a month or a season to a Peru launch are filling the gap with guesses, because there is nothing real to fill it with. The test is simple: if Meta has not announced it, it is not a date. Peru has no announcement, so Peru has no date.

How do Muse launches actually arrive?

The five launches share a pattern: each was announced the day it happened. Japan arrived on October 23 through Meta's Japanese newsroom. Singapore followed on October 28. Mexico appeared on November 5, with its App Store listing published the same day. None had an advance date, a countdown, or a pre registration window.

A second pattern matters for Peru: regional announcements come first, and global pages catch up later. Meta's older muse.ai business page still said the US and Canada after Japan had launched. New countries arrive through regional announcements first. So the first sign of a Peru launch will most likely be a regional announcement or a store listing, not an update to a global page.

The pace is set by compute and by local work. On the October 21, 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg said Muse is compute intensive to run and that expansion was partly about efficiency and partly about tailoring the product to each country, including connectors. Peru will need its own announcement, its own store listing, and its own connector work. None of the three exists yet.

What does the Meta AI precedent say?

Peru's best precedent is not Muse at all. It is Meta AI, which reached Peru in July 2024 as part of a seven country expansion that added Spanish support across WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, and Facebook. Peru was in that first Spanish wave, alongside Cameroon, Ecuador, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, and Argentina.

That history answers the language question and nothing else. It shows Meta treats Peru as part of its Spanish speaking rollout map, and it shows Spanish support arriving with the product rather than after it. It does not predict Muse's timing: Muse is a separate product with a separate rollout, a separate account check, and a separate cost structure.

The language evidence for Muse itself is separate and strong. The App Store listing includes Spanish among 39 languages, Spanish speakers in the US use Muse in Spanish today, and Mexico launched in Spanish. Meta has published no language list for Muse, so the exact day one set in Peru is unconfirmed, but Spanish is the safest expectation in this guide.

Will Muse work inside WhatsApp in Peru?

Not yet, and the reason is the country list, not the app. Muse inside WhatsApp is the same product behind the same account check, so it is closed in Peru exactly as the app is. Mexico's launch coverage said WhatsApp use would follow, without a date, which tells you the WhatsApp surface can arrive after the app even in a launched market. Peru has neither route today.

What Peruvian readers already have in WhatsApp is Meta AI, present since July 2024. It answers questions in Spanish inside the chat app. It is not Muse, and the difference is the whole point: Muse connects to your accounts and completes tasks, asking for approval before anything important. The comparison guide draws the line between the two.

Until Meta announces Peru, any WhatsApp account claiming to offer Muse in Peru is unofficial. The genuine route will come through Meta's own announcements, your own app store, and a Muse account you create yourself. Does a VPN work from Peru?

No verified method exists, and the block is built to resist the obvious workaround. The check happens at sign in, against your Meta account, not against the web page you loaded. Your account country, your store region, and your payment card country are compared, and a VPN changes none of them. It changes only the network address you appear from.

One country guide reviewed the question in November 2026 and concluded the workaround is both against the terms and technically fragile: the app is simply not listed on storefronts outside supported countries, and a VPN cannot fix that. Reports from users are mixed, which is what you would expect when the outcome depends on several signals no tutorial controls.

The caution here is about proportion. A Meta account holds years of Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp history, and an enforcement action puts all of it at risk for the sake of early access to one app. This guide recommends the boring route: wait for Meta's announcement, and spend the wait preparing the account you will use on the day.

What would Muse cost in Peru?

There is no Peru price and no announcement of one. The only figures in circulation are the reported US ones: free with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy weekly usage capacity, not features, so the free and paid experiences differ in how much you can use, not in what the agent can do.

Attribution matters for the free tier. The 100 million tokens a week figure comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, not from Meta's help pages, so it is reported rather than published. Meta's own subscriptions wording says Muse is in limited testing and is not available in all locations yet, which leaves room for the Peru offer to differ when it eventually appears.

Do not translate $20 into soles and call it the Peru price. No sol price has been announced, no Peru specific pricing exists, and currency conversion is not how Meta sets local prices. The figure on the checkout screen inside your own account, on launch day, is the only one that will apply to you.

The first setting to change

The most important tap on launch day is not a feature but a refusal. In the app, open Settings, then Data controls, and switch off "Help improve our AI models", confirming "Turn off" in the dialog that follows. That single choice stops Meta using your chats to improve its models, and it governs everything you say afterwards.

Set it first because it is retroactive in the wrong direction: it covers chats from the moment you change it, and nothing before. The first session with a new agent is exactly when people share the most, because they are testing what it can do with real material. Make the switch the first thing you do, and the testing is covered.

The switch changes nothing about capability. Connectors still connect, approvals still arrive, tasks still complete. The only thing that changes is whether Meta may learn from your words. Decide it deliberately, once, and move on to the interesting parts.

What does the waitlist message actually mean?

Visitors from Peru who open muse.ai and sign in see one of two messages: not yet available in your country, or added to the waitlist. Both mean the same thing: no access. The difference is only in wording, and neither message comes with a date, a position in a queue, or a promise. Other regions see the not available message or land on the waitlist.

The waitlist wording misleads because it suggests a line you are standing in. No public waitlist or country by country roadmap has been announced, and early checks found no official waitlist page for the rest of the world. No public waitlist or roadmap could be found. Joining registers interest. It does not reserve a place, and it does not change the account check that decides access.

The practical consequence is that the waitlist message is not a step toward access. It is the end of the road for now, and the road reopens only when Meta announces Peru. Do not pay anyone who claims to move you up the waitlist, and do not treat the message as a sign that a launch is near. The sign will be an announcement, not a message on a sign in page.
