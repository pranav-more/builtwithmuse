Does Muse have voice chat?

Voice chat is the feature where you talk to Muse and it talks back, live, inside the app. It is rolling out in stages, the first users reported it on October 15, 2026, and it is not the phone calling feature. Here is what it is, who has it, how to check, and what to do while you wait.

The short answer

Muse voice chat is the feature that lets you talk to Muse out loud and hear it talk back, in a live two way conversation inside the app. It is not the same as Muse phone calling, which is Muse dialing a business for you. Meta showed voice chat at the Muse reveal. As of October 15, 2026, it is rolling out in stages and most users do not have it yet: the first ordinary users reported it arriving on October 15 itself, while many paying subscribers say they have waited weeks with nothing. There is no switch to turn on and no waitlist for voice chat that Meta has documented. This page tracks what is actually known: what voice chat is, how it differs from phone calls, who has it, how to check your own account, and what to do while you wait.

The confusion is not a small one. Two of the most active Muse discussions of the October 13 to 15 window, in the largest user community the product has, were this exact question, asked in almost the same words. People are not failing to read an announcement. There has been no announcement to read, and into that silence two different features with similar names have wandered and become tangled. Untangling them is most of what this page does.

What is Muse voice chat?

At the reveal, Meta presented voice as the natural way to use a personal agent: you speak, Muse answers in voice, and the conversation keeps moving without typing. The point is work that happens while your hands and eyes are busy: driving, cooking, walking, settling a child. A voice conversation also carries the back and forth of a real chat, with follow up questions and corrections mid sentence, rather than one recorded message at a time.

Two nearby features are not voice chat, and naming them clears up half the confusion. Voice notes are recorded clips you send into a chat; Muse listens and replies, usually in text. Dictation is your phone keyboard turning speech into text before Muse ever sees it. Voice chat is the third thing: a live conversation in which Muse itself speaks. When users say voice mode, this is the feature they mean. It is also the feature people picture when they say talking to Muse, which is why its absence is felt more keenly than its presence would be remarkable.

One more piece of context. The models behind Muse are changing at the same time. Meta has said its newest model family, Muse Wonder, will soon power experiences in the Muse app, rolling out first to subscribers. Meta has not said that voice chat is tied to that model rollout, and the two should not be read as the same event. Our Muse Wonder guide tracks the model side of the story, including what subscribers are seeing first.

Is voice chat the same as Muse calling businesses?

No, and this is the mix up that fills the discussion forums. The two features share a microphone and nothing else. One is a way of talking to your own agent. The other is your agent talking to strangers on the phone network.

Voice chat

You talk to Muse. Muse talks back to you. Nobody else is on the line, no phone number is dialed, and the conversation never leaves your Muse app. It is a way of speaking to your own agent, hands free.

Phone calling

Muse talks to a business, on the phone network, for you. You are not part of the conversation. Muse dials a restaurant, a salon, or a support line, handles the call, and reports back with a summary and a transcript.

The status of the two features is different as well. Phone calling has been live since September 17, 2026 for users in the United States who asked for it, rolling out in stages behind a request list. Voice chat was shown earlier, at the reveal, and is only now reaching its first users outside Meta. If a post says Muse called someone, that is the phone feature. If a post says someone talked with Muse, that is voice chat. Our guide to Muse phone calling covers the calling feature in full: what it does, what real users report, and what it refuses to do.

Who has voice chat today?

The honest answer is a small and growing minority, and the evidence is user reports rather than a Meta announcement. On October 15, 2026, in a discussion in r/MuseAgent asking whether anyone had received the voice chat feature yet, two users said it had arrived for them that day. Others in the same discussion said it had not arrived for them. The waiting side of the story is just as clear: on October 13, 2026, another user asked the same question with visible frustration, noting that more than two weeks after the big reveal, even paid users did not have it.

One report in that October 13 discussion sits between the two states. A user said they had received voice responses to voice clips simply by asking Muse to reply in voice. That is not the full live conversation, but it suggests parts of the voice system are already reachable on some accounts. Treat it as a report, not a promise: other users who try the same thing may get text back.

One more remark from the October 13 thread, recorded here as atmosphere rather than evidence: a commenter who presented as close to the team said the feature was coming soon for them and teased more to come. Commenters are not announcements. Nothing public describes how Meta chooses who receives voice chat first. There is no documented waitlist, no sign up form, and no setting that requests it, which makes voice chat the opposite of the phone feature beside it, where users report you can ask Muse to put you on the waiting list. Anyone selling early access to voice chat is selling something Meta has not announced.

What do people want it for?

The discussions are just as clear about the demand as they are about the wait. The first use is the car. Drivers want to talk to Muse the way they talk to a passenger: ask a question, hear the answer, follow up, all without looking at a screen. The October 13 thread about a parent who chats with Muse while driving is full of this, along with a wish that the app would notice motion and restrain itself until the car stops.

The second use is the busy hands hour: cooking, carrying, fixing, walking between meetings. Typing is the tax on every one of those moments, and voice removes it. The third is simpler, and maybe the largest: many people think better out loud. They want to talk an idea through with Muse the way they would with a colleague, hearing answers at speaking pace instead of reading them at screen pace. None of these uses needs Muse to dial anyone. All of them are waiting on the same staged rollout.

How do you check whether you have it?

There is no announcement when a staged feature lands on your account, so checking is a two minute job.

  1. Update the Muse app to the newest version in your app store. Staged features ride on app versions, and an old build can hide a feature your account already has.
  2. Open a chat with Muse and look for a voice control near the message box: a control that starts a conversation, rather than the recorder that sends a clip.
  3. Send a voice clip and ask Muse to answer in voice. Some users report spoken replies to clips already work on their accounts, and it is the fastest test of how far voice has reached on yours.
  4. If none of that is there, your account does not have it yet. Nothing is broken, and nothing needs fixing on your side.

Then check again after each app update. Every staged Muse feature so far, from calling to the newest models, has arrived quietly between one update and the next, and the users who notice first are the ones who look.

What can you do while you wait?

The wait does not have to be wasted, because the habits that make voice chat useful can start today. Send voice notes instead of typing long messages; Muse handles spoken requests well, and you build the habit of talking to it. Use the dictation on your keyboard for long prompts when you are walking. Ask Muse to keep answers short and structured, which matters even more once the answer will be spoken aloud.

You can also prepare the ground the way you would for text. The memory, connectors, and approvals you set up now carry into voice later, and an agent that already knows your calendar and your rules is far more useful spoken to than a stranger is. Setting that groundwork up is covered in our Muse setup guide.

One distinction is worth keeping straight while you experiment. The voice capability that exists today, for the users who have it, is the phone feature: Muse speaking to businesses on calls. If your real need is Muse's voice doing errands, that is the feature to request, and it sits in a different queue from voice chat entirely. If your need is hearing Muse answer you, clips and dictation are the bridge, and the live conversation is the destination.

One use case comes up again and again in the discussions: driving. A user posting on October 13, 2026 described a parent who chats with Muse while driving, and wished the app would notice motion and limit itself. Until voice chat and proper driving safeguards arrive, dictation plus short answers is the safer pattern, and anything that needs reading can wait for a stop.

If your interest in voice is really about your business phones, that is a different product category entirely. Voice agents that answer a business line, take bookings, and follow your scripts exist today, from companies built for exactly that job. What about the do it yourself voice gadgets?

Both voice discussions include the same hobbyist workaround: building a small voice gadget with Meta's open source SDK and a tiny development board, the M5StickS3, so a physical device on your desk can talk to Muse. It is a maker project, not a product. It needs building, it will not behave like the finished feature, and Meta does not support it as a route to voice chat. We mention it because it shows the depth of the demand, and because some readers will enjoy it. For everyone else, the app update is the whole story.

It is also worth saying what the gadget is not. It does not unlock voice chat on your account, and it does not make you part of the rollout. It is a separate machine talking to Muse through tools Meta published for builders, which is precisely why it works today while the feature in the app does not.

A note on accessibility

For some users this wait is not about convenience. In the October 13 discussion, a blind user reported accessibility bugs in the current app and said an early access request for voice, sent through Muse itself, went nowhere. Voice is the primary interface for users who cannot type comfortably, and in a staged rollout they are reached last in practice. Meta has published no accessibility timeline for voice chat. If voice access is essential for you, that gap is worth knowing before you build daily habits on Muse, and it is the strongest argument in the whole discussion for Meta to publish a real schedule. Until Meta does, treat every date attached to voice chat, on any site, as a guess.

Frequently asked questions

Is Muse voice chat available?
For a small number of users, yes, as of October 15, 2026, when the first ordinary users reported it arriving on their accounts. Most users, including many paying subscribers, do not have it yet. Meta has announced no date for the full rollout.
When is Muse voice chat coming to my account?
Nobody outside Meta knows, and Meta has published no schedule and no order for the rollout. The pattern of other Muse features suggests it arrives quietly alongside app updates over the coming weeks. Checking after each update is the only reliable signal.
How do I get Muse voice chat?
There is nothing to switch on and no waitlist that Meta has documented. Update the Muse app, open a chat, and look for a voice control that starts a live conversation rather than recording a clip. If it is not there, your account has not received it yet.
Is Muse voice chat the same as Muse calling businesses?
No. Voice chat is you talking to Muse inside the app. Phone calling is Muse dialing a business on the phone network and handling the call without you. The two features were announced on different tracks and are rolling out separately.
Can Muse answer me in voice today?
Some users report that asking Muse to reply in voice to a voice clip already works on their accounts. That is a spoken reply to a recording, not the full live conversation. It is still the quickest test of what your own account can do today.
Is there any way to talk to Muse by voice while I wait?
Yes, the partial routes: send voice notes, dictate long messages with your keyboard, and ask for short answers you can scan fast. Hobbyists also report building voice gadgets with Meta's open source SDK, but that is a maker project, not an official path.
