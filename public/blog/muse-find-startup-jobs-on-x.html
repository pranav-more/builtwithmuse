How to Use Muse to Find Startup Jobs on X

Founders post open roles on X before they ever reach a job board. Muse can watch those posts for you and surface the ones that fit.

1. The short answer

Startup hiring happens in public on X. Founders post "we are hiring" threads weeks before a role appears on a job board. Tell Muse the role you want, the skills you have, and where you can work, and it will search X posts, filter the noise, and return a shortlist of real openings with links.

The rest of this guide covers the exact method, how to vet a lead before you apply, and how to turn a post into an introduction.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access to use it.
  • A clear picture of your target role. Title, skills, remote or location, stage of startup you like. The sharper this is, the better the shortlist.
  • Thirty minutes. The first search is a setup session. After that, checking in takes minutes.

This guide goes deeper than our startup jobs on X use case, which covers the everyday routine version.

3. The method, step by step

  1. Describe your target. Tell Muse: I am a product designer with four years of experience, looking for early stage fintech startups, remote, worldwide. Founders use specific words, so add the phrases they post with: hiring, looking for, join our team, open role.
  2. Let Muse search. Ask it to find recent X posts matching your criteria from the last seven days. It scans posts, not just profiles, which is where the fresh roles live.
  3. Filter hard. Tell Muse to drop anything without a clear role, anything older than two weeks, and anything outside your skills. Ten good leads beat fifty vague ones.
  4. Rank by fit. Ask Muse to rank the shortlist by how well each role matches your background, with one line on why each made the cut.
  5. Repeat weekly. Startup hiring moves fast. A weekly check keeps you early without living on the timeline.

4. Vet the lead before you apply

A post is a lead, not a job offer. Before you invest time, ask Muse to check the basics: is the poster actually on the team, does the company page look real, is there a website with the role listed.

Apply through the official channel the founder points to, usually a site or an email. That keeps your application in their real pipeline instead of lost in DMs.

5. Turn a post into an introduction

The best applications to a founder post are short and specific. Ask Muse to draft a reply or DM that references the actual post, names one relevant thing you have done, and asks about next steps. One message, human tone, no flattery.

Then send it yourself. Founders can smell automation. Muse writes the draft, you bring the judgment, and your name goes on it.

6. What to watch out for

  • Posts go stale fast. A hiring post from a month ago is usually filled. Prioritize this week.
  • Verify the role is real. If there is no company site or the poster has no connection to the company, skip it.
  • Never pay to apply. A real startup never charges applicants. If money comes up, walk away.

Frequently asked questions

Why X instead of a job board?
Founders post roles on X first, often weeks before a listing exists anywhere else. You are competing with dozens of applicants instead of thousands.
Can Muse apply for me?
It can draft everything, but you should send applications yourself. Hiring is a trust interaction and your name is on it.
How often should I check?
Weekly is the sweet spot. Daily burns you out, monthly misses the window.
What if I am not technical?
Founder posts cover design, marketing, ops, and support roles too. Describe your skills and Muse filters for them.
Does this work outside the US?
Startup hiring on X is global, and remote roles are the most commonly posted. Set your location filter to match.
