How to Use Muse in China

No, Muse is not available in mainland China. Muse has launched in only five countries, Meta's Geographic Use Policy names China as a restricted territory for its AI services, and Meta's apps do not operate on mainland networks. This guide states the position plainly, separates mainland China from Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan, and gives no workaround.

Muse is not available in mainland China, and no launch is announced. Three barriers stand in the way: the rollout has not reached China, Meta's Geographic Use Policy names the People's Republic of China, including Hong Kong and Macau, as a restricted territory, and Meta's services are blocked on mainland networks. None of the three has a date for lifting.

Is Muse available in China?

No. Muse launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5. China is not among them. Meta has published no country list, no timeline, and no statement naming a China launch.

China is different from other unlaunched countries, and the difference is worth stating plainly. In most unlaunched markets, the only barrier is the rollout: Meta has not opened the product there yet. In mainland China, there is a second barrier on top of the first. Meta's written policy names China as a restricted territory for its AI services, and Meta's services are blocked on mainland networks. A launch in mainland China would need both barriers to move, and neither has moved.

For the confirmed list of launched markets, see the Muse available countries page. For the position in the places most often confused with mainland China, see the Hong Kong guide and the Taiwan guide.

What are the three barriers?

Barrier one is the rollout. Muse is live in five countries. Meta has announced no date for any further country, and no Meta source names China as a coming market. That alone would be enough to answer the question: no launch, no date, no timeline.

Barrier two is written policy. Meta's Geographic Use Policy, published for developers on the Meta developer site and last updated November 9, 2026, lists Restricted Territories that may not access Meta's services. The list includes the "People's Republic of China (including Hong Kong SAR and Macau SAR)." The policy states that services may be accessed only in jurisdictions where Meta has enabled access. The policy page is open for anyone to read.

Keep the distinction explicit. The Geographic Use Policy governs the Meta Model API, the developer services, not the consumer Muse app directly. It is not a consumer launch statement, and this guide does not present it as one. But it is the only written Meta document that names China as restricted, and it shows Meta's written position on the territory. The consumer app has not launched in China either, and the policy and the rollout point the same way.

In practice there is a third barrier, covered in the next section: the network block. Even the policy and the rollout aside, Meta's services do not load on mainland networks. A reader in mainland China faces all three at once.

Are Meta's services blocked in mainland China?

Facebook has been blocked in mainland China since July 2009. That date is recorded in guides to internet access in China, which note that Facebook is fully blocked on the mainland and that its sister platforms face the same treatment. WhatsApp has been blocked since 2017, and Apple removed WhatsApp from its App Store in China in 2024, at the request of the Chinese government. The WhatsApp block and the App Store removal are documented in that report.

Meta's own filings say the same in formal language. Meta's annual report states that user access to Facebook "has been or is currently restricted in whole or in part in China, Iran, and North Korea." That statement is quoted in a guide to Facebook in China. And in 2026, Meta's chief global affairs officer, Joel Kaplan, said Meta had decided not to offer its services in China. That statement was reported from a public discussion.

The block covers the whole Meta estate: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger are all unavailable on mainland networks without special measures. A guide to what is blocked in China puts it directly: Meta's apps are gone entirely from the mainland. Muse runs on Meta's infrastructure, and Meta does not operate services in China. That is the position, and no tutorial changes it.

How is mainland China different from Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan?

The network block applies to mainland China only. It does not apply to Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan, where Meta's services work normally. Readers in those places face the rollout barrier only: Muse has not launched there either, but the network is not in the way. If you are reading this from Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan, your position is the ordinary unlaunched market position, not the mainland position.

Hong Kong deserves a specific note, because the policy names it. The Geographic Use Policy lists the People's Republic of China "including Hong Kong SAR and Macau SAR" as a restricted territory. That is the developer policy, and it sits alongside the fact that Hong Kong's networks are open and Meta's apps work there. The Hong Kong guide covers the distinction in full, and it is the right guide for a Hong Kong reader.

Taiwan is in a similar position: open networks, no Muse launch, no date. The Taiwan guide covers it. The three guides together cover the whole picture: mainland China faces the policy and the network block on top of the rollout, while Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan face the rollout alone.

What happens when you try from mainland China?

On a local mainland connection, the experience is different from the experience in other unlaunched countries. In most unlaunched markets, muse.ai loads and the block message appears at sign in. From a mainland connection, Meta's pages may not load at all. That is the network block, not the account gate, and it looks different: a page that never loads, rather than a page that loads and says no.

The two failures are worth telling apart. "Page will not load" is the network. "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet" is the account gate, and it appears only once the page has loaded and you have signed in. A mainland reader who sees the first failure is stopped before the second failure can even appear.

The storefront picture matches. Search the China App Store for Muse and you will find no listing published by Meta Platforms, Inc. The China storefront is a separate store with its own rules, the same store where Apple removed WhatsApp in 2024, and a Muse listing has never appeared in it. The guide to Muse not showing in your App Store walks through the checks that confirm a listing is genuinely absent.

Does Muse speak Chinese?

Chinese language tutorial content about Muse is easy to find: guides, videos, and signup walkthroughs, mostly aimed at readers outside the mainland. Their existence proves that Chinese speaking users want Muse. It proves nothing about whether the agent handles Chinese, and nothing about a mainland launch. Demand and support are different things, and only one of them is visible.

Support itself is unknown. Meta has published no list of the languages the Muse agent supports, and no Meta source checked for this guide mentions Simplified Chinese or Traditional Chinese. The launch material is in English, and there is no language table to consult.

Mexico is the only launch that shows localization in action: Muse arrived there in Spanish on November 5, 2026, with local services connected from the start. That proves Meta localizes launches. It proves nothing about Chinese, and no source has reported Chinese testing, a Chinese interface, or Chinese connectors.

Can a VPN get you Muse in China?

This section gives no workaround, no method, and no recommendation. It states the facts that bear on the question, and stops there.

First, the legal fact: China allows only government approved VPN services, and consumer VPN apps face blocking and removal. Apple removed VPN apps from its App Store in China, and VPN apps are widely reported as unreliable or fully blocked on mainland networks. The position is that a VPN is not a reliable tool for reaching Meta's services from the mainland, and the tools that are allowed are not the tools tutorials sell.

Second, the product fact: Muse's gate sits at the account layer, not the connection. A changed address does not change the country on your Meta account or the App Store region tied to your Apple ID or Google account. Reports from other unlaunched countries describe the same wall after a VPN signup: the waitlist screen, or an account that stops working later.

Third, the caution: some reports warn that using a VPN to sign up may break Muse's terms and that Meta can limit or close accounts. Those warnings are user beliefs and cautionary reporting, not verified Meta enforcement, and this guide states them as caution, not fact.

Anyone selling a China Muse method is selling a promise this guide will not repeat. Do not pay for one, and do not buy an account: purchased accounts have been reported as disconnected without warning in other markets, and a bought account is one you do not control.

What privacy setting matters if you ever get access?

Read this section as conditional. If you get access to Muse one day, from a place where it is available, the first thing to do is the data switch. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and switch off "Help improve our AI models," confirming "Turn off" when asked. From that moment, Meta may not use your chats to improve its models.

Reports on Muse's defaults say the switch starts on and stays on until the user changes it. It applies to chats from the moment it is set, so anything said before that falls under the earlier rule. Setting it first is the only way to cover everything, and it takes seconds.

The approval is the other control worth building early. Muse checks with you before it publishes, sends, or spends anything, and reading each approval before granting it is the habit that matters most day to day. The switch is about your words. The approval is about the agent's actions.

What will Muse cost in China?

A price needs a checkout, and there is no checkout. The reported US tiers, free with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month, describe a product on sale in five countries. They describe nothing about China, where there is no launch, no billing, and no price. The guide to whether Muse is free sets out the tiers in full, for readers in markets where the product exists.

Converting the US figures into yuan produces arithmetic, not a price. The reported India pricing in rupees, published by The Hindu on November 9, 2026, is unconfirmed by Meta and irrelevant to a market with no launch. There is nothing to convert, because there is nothing to buy.

Age verification is also not a live question here. Muse is for adults 18 and over, and verification is reported to require a payment card or a linked Instagram or Facebook account. But verification is a step inside a signup flow that does not open in mainland China. The age verification problems guide covers it for readers in launched markets.

What would have to change for a launch?

Three things would have to change, in order. First, the written policy naming China as a restricted territory would have to change. Second, Meta would have to announce a launch that includes China. Third, an App Store listing would have to appear in the China storefront. None of the three has happened, and no signal suggests any of them is near.

Watch the policy page, Meta's newsroom, and the store. Those are the three places a real change would show up first. A tutorial video promising access today is not a signal. A date on a random blog is not a signal. A change in the policy page's Restricted Territories list would be a signal, and it has not happened.

The honest close is this: nothing suggests a mainland launch is near, and this guide will not soften that. The position has been stable for years on the network side, and the policy side was reaffirmed in November 2026. If the position changes, this guide will be updated from primary sources, and the update will say what changed and where it was published.

What should you do today?

Be realistic about what today can hold. From a mainland connection, the practical step is watching, not signing up. The network block and the rollout gate both stand in the way, and no step in this guide removes either. Do not pay for a method, a code that promises access, or an account.

If you are outside the mainland, in a place where Muse is live or where the ordinary waitlist path works, the normal preparation applies. A code is not required to use Muse: muse.ai/join is an open entry point that needs no code, though it carries no token bonus. But a code redeemed within 48 hours of account creation gives both sides 1 billion Muse tokens, one code per account, and the code is case sensitive. Get a free muse referral code from the community pool, and hold it ready for use if and when you are eligible.

A code does not clear geography, and it does not clear a network block. Hold one only if you expect to be eligible somewhere Muse is available. Otherwise, the useful step is the boring one: watch the policy page, the newsroom, and the store, and check back when something real changes.
