Is Muse available in Israel?

As of November 13, 2026, no. Muse is live in five countries: the US, since September 8, 2026; Canada, since September 18; Japan, since October 23; Singapore, since October 28; and Mexico, since November 5. Israel is not among them. The full country list shows the five launches and the dates, and nothing beyond them.

Israel specific coverage agrees. An October 22, 2026 guide checked against Meta's official announcements states plainly that Muse is not yet available in Israel, and that Meta has not announced a date for expansion. An availability table updated October 31, 2026 carries the row UAE, Saudi Arabia, Israel: no launch announced. That table's country count predates the Mexico launch, so use it for the Israel row, not for the total.

Meta has also published no list of additional countries and no date for expansion anywhere, which means there is nothing to watch except Meta's own announcements and the App Store listing. There is no official waitlist, no signup queue, and no country list you can check, according to an October 2026 availability review. If a site claims an Israel date, it is inventing one.

Does Muse speak Hebrew?

The honest answer is that nobody outside Meta knows. Meta has not published a language list for Muse, and one guide that checked Meta announcements writes that it does not know whether Muse will work well in Hebrew and will not write something it has not seen, in its October 22, 2026 Israel guide. That restraint is the right standard, and this guide follows it.

What exists is a listing. The Muse US App Store listing shows English plus 38 other languages, and Hebrew is on that list, alongside Arabic, Greek, and Turkish, according to a September 2026 Israel guide. The same guide calls this groundwork, not confirmation: a language on a store listing is not proof the agent handles Hebrew well in practice, and Meta has confirmed neither an Israeli launch nor Hebrew quality.

Hebrew is also a right to left language with its own script, which makes the quality question sharper than a translation checkbox. An agent that reads your mail, drafts messages, and books things in Hebrew needs to handle the language in context, not just display it. Until Muse launches in Israel and Israelis test it, Hebrew support is a listing plus a hope, and this guide will not upgrade it further.

What do Israelis already have?

Meta AI, the simpler assistant, arrived in Israel with Hebrew support at the end of June 2026, inside WhatsApp on phone and desktop, and on the meta.ai website, according to the September 2026 guide. Israeli press covered the launch on June 30, 2026: Meta AI available in Hebrew for the first time, through WhatsApp, desktop, and mobile, offering content generation, image analysis, and reasoning tasks, in Ynet coverage and a second report the same day.

That assistant is useful, and it is not Muse. Muse and Meta AI are different products: Meta AI chats and answers, while Muse connects apps and acts, booking, buying, calling, and remembering across tasks. Israelis using Meta AI in Hebrew today are using the chat half of Meta's plan, and the acting half has no Israel date. How Muse works inside WhatsApp explains what the acting half looks like when it arrives in the same app.

The timing also tells you something about Meta's order. Meta AI reached Israel in June 2026, relatively late compared with other major players, as one July 2026 analysis puts it, with the product in Hebrew landing in the middle of 2026, in that analysis. A company that took until mid 2026 to bring its chat assistant to Israel in Hebrew is unlikely to rush its agent there without a Hebrew plan, and no such plan is published.

Why is there no date for Israel?

Meta Connect on September 23, 2026, added features but named no new countries and no dates, according to the September Israel guide. That set the pattern: every Muse announcement since has added capability, not geography, except the three launches that actually happened, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. Israel has appeared in no announcement, no keynote slide reported in coverage, and no Meta statement checked for this guide.

The closest thing to a reason comes from the October 21, 2026 earnings call, where Zuckerberg described Muse as compute intensive to run and said the delay was partly about running it efficiently and partly about tailoring it to each country, including work on connectors, according to coverage of the call. A November 2026 report adds that Meta pointed to the cost of running the agent as the reason for the slow rollout, in that report, which also notes that a clinic in Tel Aviv cannot use Muse yet. Cost plus tailoring is the stated shape of the delay, and Israel is inside it, not excepted from it.

None of this is a prediction. Compute constraints ease, connectors get built, and a country can move from no date to live in a single announcement, as Mexico did on November 5. The correct posture for Israel is readiness without expectation: assume no date until Meta says otherwise, and have the account ready for the day it does.

What does Israel's new privacy law mean for Muse?

Israel overhauled its privacy law just before Muse existed. Amendment 13 to the Protection of Privacy Law, approved by the Knesset on August 5, 2024, came into force on August 14, 2025, introducing broader definitions of personal data, mandatory Data Protection Officers for various entities, and substantially increased penalties and enforcement powers, according to Linklaters data protection guide, last updated August 2026. The framework is broadly aligned with GDPR principles, though key differences remain in practice.

The enforcement teeth are real. The Privacy Protection Authority gained broader authority including financial penalties capped at 5 percent of annual turnover for larger organizations, according to a Legal 500 briefing, and courts can award compensation without proof of damage, according to a summary of the amendment. An agent that reads mail, sees locations, and spends money touches exactly the categories this law now guards more strictly.

This guide does not claim the law is why Muse is absent from Israel. Meta has cited no Israeli legal reason, and the rollout pattern is global, not Israel specific. The law matters differently: when Muse does arrive, it arrives into a regime where consent must be explicit and documented, disclosure duties are tighter, and the regulator can audit. That is context for your settings choices on day one, not a launch explanation.

Can a VPN unlock Muse in Israel?

No verified method exists. VPN and US account workarounds are unverified and may break Meta terms, according to the September Israel guide, which calls them a gamble with your Meta account. That is the bluntest correct summary available, and this guide adopts it: a gamble, not a method.

The technical evidence points the same way. A September 2026 technical check searched 15 storefronts besides the US and found the Muse iPhone listing in none of them, concluding the gate sits at the account layer, not the web layer. A VPN changes where your traffic appears to come from; it leaves your App Store country, your account region, and the region Meta has on file untouched.

The caution wording matters, so here it is plainly: no source confirms Meta bans accounts for VPN use, and no source confirms it is safe. This guide states the risk as caution, never as verified fact. If your Meta account holds your WhatsApp chats, your Instagram, and your Facebook identity, the downside of a wrong guess is larger than the upside of early access to an app that will launch in Israel on Meta's schedule, not yours.

Which privacy setting should you change first?

Amendment 13 sets the standard, and the toggle is where you apply it. Israel's amended law expects consent to be explicit and documented, and the matching control in Muse sits in Settings, under Data controls: Help improve our AI models, switched off, with "Turn off" tapped to confirm. Make that check on the day you create your account, before your first long chat, because the switch decides whether your Muse interactions help improve Meta's AI models.

The toggle is your control, and Amendment 13 is the backstop. The law tightened disclosure duties, so collecting data requires clear disclosure of the purpose of processing, the controller identity, and your rights, according to the amendment summary linked above. That does not replace the toggle: the law governs what Meta must tell you, and the toggle governs what you allow. Check both, starting with the toggle.

After the switch, treat connectors as separate decisions. Each app you connect widens what Muse can see, so add them one at a time, starting with the apps your first tasks need, and leave the rest disconnected until a task asks for them. Amendment 13 gives you disclosure and inspection rights as a backstop, but Help improve our AI models is the switch you hold, and off is what keeps your early chats out of model training.
