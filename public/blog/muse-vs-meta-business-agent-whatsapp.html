Muse vs Meta Business Agent

One agent works for you inside an app. The other faces your customers on WhatsApp and Messenger. They have different jobs, different bills, and different controls, and the coverage keeps swapping their names. Here is the clean separation.

The short answer

Meta now sells two agents with confusingly similar names, and they stand on opposite sides of your counter. Muse works for you, the owner, inside the Muse app: it reads your tools, prepares your work, and waits for your approval. Meta Business Agent works for your business but faces your customers, answering them on WhatsApp and Messenger. One is your staff. The other is your shopfront. Coverage of the two mixes them up constantly, business owners mix them up in public threads every week, and the pricing, the control, and even who pays are different for each. This guide separates them cleanly.

Meet Muse, the owner's agent

Muse is the personal agent Meta launched on September 8, 2026, on phones, on the web, and inside WhatsApp as a contact you talk to. Its customer is the individual holding the account. For a business owner, Muse is back office leverage: connect your Page, your Instagram professional account, your ad account, your books and your store, and Muse works across them under your eye. It analyses your Page performance, drafts next week's campaign, audits your email automations, flags the expenses that look wrong, and prepares the invoice follow ups. The governing rule of the whole product is that nothing publishes, sends, or spends without your approval, with a separate agent, Sentinel, gating outbound actions.

Muse for Small Business, announced on September 29, 2026, is not a second product. It is this same Muse, with business skills and fifteen named business connectors switched on inside the same app and the same settings screen. You pay for Muse the way any user does: a free tier with a weekly limit, Power at $20 a month, or Maximum at $100 a month, with the paid tiers buying usage volume. Our complete setup guide walks the whole offer.

The limit that defines Muse is whose side it is on. It is your agent, loyal to the account holder. Your customers cannot talk to it, and it does not answer them. When a customer messages your business at midnight, Muse is not the one who replies.

Owners blurred this line before Meta packaged it. By the week of the Small Business launch, one third of Muse users had already connected a business account, an Instagram business profile or a Facebook Page, according to Vishal Shah, Meta's vice president of AI products, speaking to The Wall Street Journal. Demand pulled the business offer into existence. It also means a great many owners met business Muse first and assume the customer facing agent must be a feature hiding somewhere in their app. It is not. It is a different product, and the next section is its portrait.

Meet Meta Business Agent, the customers' agent

Meta Business Agent is the other product, launched earlier, on June 3, 2026. It lives on WhatsApp and Messenger, inside your business's own messaging presence, and its job is to deal with the people writing to you. A customer asks about price, timing, delivery, or availability; the Business Agent answers as your business, in the conversation, without you touching your phone. Where Muse is priced to the person, the Business Agent is priced to the business, in tokens, according to the comparison coverage that has tried to untangle the two: the business pays for the work the agent does with its customers.

The easiest way to hold the difference is by who is talking. With Muse, you talk to the agent. With Meta Business Agent, your customers talk to the agent. Almost every muddled article, and most of the muddled comment threads, come from coverage that watched Meta demonstrate the second and described it using the name of the first.

It is also worth knowing what the Business Agent is not. It does not read your QuickBooks, plan your week, or audit your subscriptions. It has one desk, the customer conversation, and it sits at that desk around the clock.

Who pays, who controls, what each can touch

Who each serves. Muse serves the account holder, one person, across that person's life and business. Meta Business Agent serves the business as an institution, and through it, every customer who writes in.

Who pays. Muse is paid by the owner on the consumer pricing tiers, free, Power, or Maximum, and the business features ride on those same plans. Meta Business Agent is paid by the business for its customer facing work, priced in tokens per the comparison coverage. Two products, two bills, two budgets.

Who controls it. Muse waits for you. Its approval gates mean a draft is a draft until you say go, and its audit trail shows what it did. The Business Agent is built to act in the moment with customers, inside the rules and content the business gives it, because a customer conversation cannot wait for an owner to wake up. The India version of the product, covered below, documents the safety valve: the owner can take a conversation over from the agent.

What each can touch. Muse touches your tools: Page, Instagram, ad account, store, books, inbox, calendar, the fifteen business connectors and the dozens more in the full list our connectors list tracks. The Business Agent touches your customer conversations on WhatsApp and Messenger. Their territories meet at your business, and barely overlap.

The India picture

For our readers in India, the distinction is not academic, because only one side of it is open to you today. Muse the app has launched in the United States and Canada only, and our countries hub tracks its rollout. The customer facing side is a different story. Meta has pushed Business AI on WhatsApp specifically for Indian small businesses: an agent on your WhatsApp business presence that answers customer questions, recommends products, captures leads, and books appointments, working in native languages, with the owner able to step into a conversation. WhatsApp is where Indian commerce already talks, and Meta aimed the customer facing agent straight at it.

So an Indian shop owner reading launch coverage should translate carefully. The dazzling owner agent in the American reviews is not yet available to you. The agent your customers can meet on WhatsApp is the one built for your market, and for a business whose sales happen in chat, it is arguably the one that matters more anyway.

Which one does your business need?

Most businesses, once they see the two clearly, want both, for different shifts. The customer facing agent earns first wherever customers arrive by message: it answers at midnight, captures the lead that would have cooled by morning, and books the appointment while you work. The owner agent earns where the owner's own hours go: the inbox triage, the books review, the campaign draft, the follow ups. One fills the front of the shop. The other keeps the back room in order.

Sequence them by pain. If unanswered messages are costing you sales, start at the shopfront. If your own desk is where the hours vanish, start with Muse when it reaches you, and prepare for it now with the hygiene our risk checklist lays out: clean accounts, approvals understood, and a clear line around money. What no business needs is the confusion itself, paying for one agent while expecting the behaviour of the other.

Geography sequences them for many readers whether they like it or not. Under every launch post outside the launch countries, the comments are dominated by availability: owners in Pakistan noting Muse is not there, Brazilian owners asking when it arrives. If you are outside the United States and Canada, your first agent will almost certainly be the customer facing one, because it is the one your market can already touch. Plan for Muse the owner agent the way you plan for a product launch you care about: read the setup material, get your accounts in order, and join the waitlist so the day it opens in your country is a working day, not a reading day.

Can you plug in your own AI instead?

Builders ask a sharper version of this question, and it is worth answering at the level the public record supports. In r/MetaAI, a developer building software on the official Messenger platform asked whether he may use his own AI with Meta Business Messaging, or whether he has to use Meta's own AI. Nearby, a software company in Saudi Arabia was looking for a WhatsApp Business Platform partner, a BSP, to build a WhatsApp first product for real estate businesses. Both threads point at the same landscape: Meta offers its own agent, and a platform with partner companies also exists around WhatsApp and Messenger business messaging.

What we cannot honestly give you is a settled yes or no for a specific product idea. The answer turns on Meta's current Business Messaging policies and on how your product uses the platform, and the public threads we found debate it rather than settle it. Treat Meta's own agent as the promoted path, the partner route as a real but policy governed one, and read the current policy documents before you build on either. Anyone who quotes you a simpler answer is guessing.

Frequently asked questions

Are Muse and Meta Business Agent the same product?
No. They are two separate products that coverage and comment threads constantly mix up. Muse, launched September 8, 2026, is the owner's personal agent inside the Muse app. Meta Business Agent, launched June 3, 2026, works for a business facing its customers on WhatsApp and Messenger. Different users, different jobs, different bills.
Which one answers my customers on WhatsApp?
The customer facing one: Meta Business Agent, and in India the Business AI on WhatsApp that Meta has aimed at small businesses there. Muse never talks to your customers. It works for you, inside your account, on your own tools.
Who pays for each agent?
You pay for Muse yourself, on the consumer tiers: free with a weekly limit, Power at $20 a month, or Maximum at $100 a month, and the Small Business features ride on those plans. The Business Agent is priced to the business for its customer facing work, in tokens, according to the comparison coverage of the two products.
I run a business in India. Which of these can I use today?
The customer facing side. Meta has pushed Business AI on WhatsApp for Indian small businesses, answering questions, recommending products, capturing leads, and booking appointments in native languages. Muse the app has launched only in the United States and Canada so far, so the owner agent is the one you are still waiting for.
Can I use my own AI with Meta Business Messaging instead?
Builders are asking exactly this in public, and the honest answer is layered. Meta promotes its own Business Agent, and a partner ecosystem of WhatsApp Business Platform providers also exists. Whether a specific product may plug in its own AI turns on Meta's current Business Messaging policies. The public threads debate it without settling it, so read the current policy before you build.
Can a business run both agents?
Yes, and most will eventually. They do different shifts: the Business Agent minds the shopfront, answering customers on WhatsApp and Messenger, while Muse works the back room, your inbox, books, Page, and campaigns, under your approval. There is no shared setup between them; you are adopting two products that meet at your business.
