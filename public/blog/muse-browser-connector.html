How to Use the Browser Connector in Muse

Browser is Muse built in web browsing, not a third party service: research, price checks, and bookings on the open web, with nothing to sign up for. Here is what it does, where it struggles, and the approval rule that keeps it safe.

1. The short answer

As of October 25, 2026, Browser is a live connector in Muse, and it is the only connector on this page that is not a third party service. It is Muse's own built in web browsing: one of the 54 connectors counted in the October 15 in app check. There is nothing to sign up for and no account to link. Muse opens pages, reads them, fills in forms, and carries out multi step tasks on the open web, from checking a price to booking a hotel. Every purchase still waits for your approval, and some sites block it or work badly, so the Browser connector is the most powerful connector Muse has and the least predictable one.

Here is the honest version. The Browser connector is what makes Muse an agent instead of a chatbot with a fixed list of apps. The fixed connectors cover 54 services. The browser covers everything else: the shop with no connector, the hotel site with no integration, the form that only exists as a web page. The rest of this guide covers what to ask it to do, the setup that keeps it safe, the uses that are actually worth your time, and the limits to know before you lean on it. Our complete connectors list shows where Browser sits among the 54 live connectors in the web app.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • Nothing to connect. Browser is built in. There is no sign in, no token, and no third party account. If it is listed in your connectors, it is ready.
  • Approvals set to ask me. This is the single most important step on this page. The browser can reach a checkout, so anything that spends money or submits a form must wait for your tap.
  • A payment method, only if you want purchases. Hotel bookings made through the browser pay with a one time card from Stripe Link. Set that up before you ask Muse to book anything.
  • Patience for the first week. Some sites work smoothly and some fight back. Expect to watch the first few tasks closely and learn which of your sites behave.

3. How to use it, step by step

There is no connection step, so the method is about how you ask. A vague request gets a vague browse. A request with the site, the goal, and the limit gets work you can trust.

  1. Check that Browser is listed. Open Muse Settings, then Connections, and confirm Browser is there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  2. Set approvals to ask me. Do this before your first browse. Purchases, form submissions, and anything that creates an account must wait for you. This is what stops Muse from buying something you only asked it to look at.
  3. Name the site and the goal in one sentence. Say which site to use and what done looks like. Check the price of this item on this site is a task. Find me a good deal somewhere is a wish, and wishes get browsed badly.
  4. Give it the details up front. Dates, sizes, budgets, and the exact item name. Every detail you leave out is a detail Muse guesses at, and a guessed date on a hotel booking is a real problem.
  5. Watch the first task end to end. Read what Muse did, which pages it opened, and what it is asking you to approve. If it went to the wrong site or the wrong page, say so now. The correction teaches the next request.
  6. Approve one step at a time. Approve the search, then approve the choice, then approve the purchase. Three small approvals beat one big approval you did not read.
  7. Use it read only for a week. Research, price checks, and comparisons only. Once you trust what it reads and how it reports, start approving the actions that follow.

4. What it actually does

The connector earns its place with five uses. Everything else is a variation on one of them.

Research the live web

Ask Muse to check a price, read a page, compare two products, or find the answer to a question that changed since its training. This is the use that answers the question every chatbot gets wrong: what does it cost right now, and what does the page actually say. Name the site you want checked. A request with a site gets a read you can verify, because you can open the same page and see the same words. A request without one gets a browse you cannot check, and a price you cannot trust.

Compare before you buy

Ask Muse to compare the same item across the sites you name: price, shipping, delivery time, and return policy, in one short list. This is the use that saves the money you would have spent on the first result. The comparison works best when you name the exact item and the exact sites, because then Muse is reading pages instead of guessing which product you meant. Read the top three yourself before you approve anything. A comparison you did not read is a purchase you did not make, it is a purchase Muse made for you.

Book hotels where no connector exists

Flights run through the Duffel connector, but hotels run through the browser. Muse drives a browser through consumer travel sites such as Expedia and Hotels.com, and pays with a one time card from Stripe Link. The approval step is the whole point: Muse stops before payment and shows you the exact dates, the exact total, and the cancellation terms. Read them. A hotel booked on the wrong dates is a real problem, and the fix is a cancellation, not a correction. Our Duffel connector guide covers the flight half of the same trip, including the approval rule and the wrong name caution.

Fill in forms and finish multi step tasks

Ask Muse to fill in a form, complete a signup, or carry a task across several pages: find the form, fill it, check the details, and stop for your approval before it submits. This is the use that saves the twenty minutes of clicking you never wanted to do. It works best on simple forms with clear fields. Long forms with uploads, identity checks, or payment steps need you watching, because those are the steps where a wrong entry costs real time to undo.

Track what changes

Ask Muse to check a page again later: has the price dropped, is the item back in stock, did the event page change. This is the quiet use that compounds, because a price you check once is a guess and a price you check every week is a pattern. Name the page, name the number you care about, and name how often to check. A weekly check on three pages you actually buy from beats a daily check on thirty pages you never will.

Read the page behind the headline

Ask Muse to open a link and tell you what it actually says, not what the headline promised. This is the use for the article a friend sent, the terms page you never read, and the product page with the detail buried in paragraph nine. Muse reads the whole page and reports the part that matters, with the link so you can check it. Treat the summary as a pointer, not a verdict: if the detail touches money, a contract, or a deadline, open the page and read that paragraph yourself.

5. What it cannot do

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector.

  • It cannot buy without your approval when approvals are set right. That is a feature, not a bug. If you turn approvals off, you lose the safety net, so keep them on for anything that spends money.
  • It cannot get past every site. Some sites block automated browsing, show a bot check, or hide content behind a sign in. October 2026 spot tests reported Muse struggling on some large hotel sites, including Hilton, Marriott, and Hyatt. A site that blocks the browser is not a site Muse can use that day.
  • It cannot log in for you everywhere. We could not verify exactly which logins Muse can complete through the browser and which ones it cannot. Expect it to struggle on sites with extra verification steps, and keep your passwords to yourself.
  • It cannot book flights through the browser. Flights run through the Duffel connector, not the browser. If you ask for a flight, Muse should use Duffel, and the hotel half of the trip is the part the browser handles.
  • It cannot guarantee it read the page right. A price can move between the read and the purchase, and a page can show different content to different visitors. Check the final total on the checkout page before you approve it.

6. What to watch for

As of October 25, 2026, Browser is a settled connector: one of the 54 in the October 15 in app check, and unchanged by the October 7 iPad app launch and the October 23 Japan launch. There is no October update to how it works, so the habits around it still matter. Never approve a purchase you did not read. The approval screen is the last place a wrong date, a wrong quantity, or a wrong total can be caught, and it is the only place that matters. A purchase approved unread is a purchase you made, even if Muse clicked the button.

Expect some sites to fail. The browser is a guest on the open web, and some hosts do not want guests. A bot check, a blank page, or a checkout that loops is a site telling you no, not a task to force. Try the site yourself, or try a different site, and move on. The connector that works on nine sites out of ten is still worth having, as long as you know which nine.

Be careful with personal data in forms. An address, a phone number, or a payment detail typed into a form is data that leaves your account. Only ask Muse to fill in forms on sites you trust, and read the filled form before you approve the submit. The moment you stop reading the form is the moment a typo becomes your address on someone else's database.

Finally, verify the final number, not the first number. Prices change, fees appear at checkout, and a deal that looked good on the search page can look ordinary on the payment page. The rule is simple: the number on the approval screen is the number you are paying. If it does not match the number Muse quoted, stop and ask why before you tap anything.

What is the Browser connector?
It is Muse's own built in web browsing. It is not a third party service, so there is nothing to sign up for and no account to link. Muse opens pages, reads them, fills in forms, and carries out tasks on the open web.
Can Muse buy things through the browser?
Yes, with your approval. Muse can shop and book through the browser, including hotels on consumer travel sites, and pays with a one time card from Stripe Link. Nothing is bought without your approval when your approvals are set to ask me.
Why would I use the browser instead of a connector?
Because the fixed connectors cover 54 services and the browser covers everything else. The shop with no connector, the hotel site with no integration, and the form that only exists as a web page are all browser jobs.
