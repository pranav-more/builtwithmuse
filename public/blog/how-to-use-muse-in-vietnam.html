Is Muse available in Vietnam?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is available in exactly five countries. It launched in the US on September 8, 2026, reached Canada on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico on November 5, 2026, its fifth market and its first in Latin America. Vietnam is not on that list, and Meta has announced no Vietnamese launch date.

Vietnamese press has confirmed the position directly. A Vietnam.vn article published October 13, 2026, headlined that Meta launched the world's smartest AI but that it is not yet available in Vietnam, reported that the Muse registration page is only available in certain markets, and that Vietnamese users accessing it will see an advance registration message and cannot use it at this time. The availability trackers agree: one tracker checked on October 31, 2026, lists the Philippines, Indonesia, and Vietnam together as no launch announced, and another tracker lists Vietnam as Not yet with a check date of September 27, 2026.

Meta itself publishes no country list and no expansion timeline. Its Help Center describes Muse subscriptions as in limited testing and not available in all locations yet, and it requires users to be located in a country where Muse operates. Vietnam has received no official statement of its own: no reply from a Meta account, no mention in a newsroom post, no local announcement. The silence is the status, and the Vietnamese press confirmation is the strongest evidence of it.

The message Vietnamese users actually see

Open muse.ai from Vietnam and the site loads normally. The marketing page, the feature descriptions, and the newsroom links all work. The block appears when you try to sign in. Vietnamese users see an advance registration message: the registration page is open only in certain markets, and Vietnam is not one of them. The Vietnam.vn report describes exactly this: Vietnamese users accessing the registration page see the advance registration message and cannot use the product at this time.

The general message Meta shows in unlaunched markets is a close cousin: Muse is not available in your country or region yet. We will let you know when it is. In an October 8 test from outside the launch countries, the sign in page showed a Join the waitlist for Muse prompt with that message. Whether you see the advance registration wording or the waitlist wording, the meaning is identical: your account is outside the countries where Muse is live, and there is no action on the page that changes that.

The App Store tells the same story. Search the Vietnamese App Store for Muse from Meta and you will not find it. The listing exists in the US store and in each launch country as Meta added them, and it has never appeared in Vietnam. No source has reported a Vietnamese Google Play listing either. The gate sits at the account layer, not the web layer, which is why the site looks open while the product stays closed.

The Vietnamese tutorial wave: what the videos show

Vietnam has the most active workaround tutorial scene of any unlaunched country in this wave, and it is worth describing accurately, because Vietnamese searchers will find these videos before they find this guide. Vietnamese YouTube tutorials promise to set up Muse in about 90 seconds, from any country, and they walk through the same sequence: change your Facebook settings, open muse.ai in Chrome, sign up with the email tied to your Facebook account, verify your age, and enter an invite code for 1 billion tokens.

One widely viewed tutorial, a Vietnamese setup guide published about 11 days ago, tells viewers to skip its first step if they are in the US, Canada, Japan, or Singapore, and warns viewers elsewhere that bypassing the restriction may violate Meta's Terms of Service. A second tutorial, published about 7 days ago, adds a preparation step the first video lacks: open Facebook settings, change the region format to United States (English), and change the account language to English (US), with a warning that skipping this step will place you on a waitlist. Both videos then follow the same path: muse.ai, sign up with a Facebook tied email, age verification matching the date of birth on the Facebook account, and an invite code entered in Settings.

Describe these videos accurately and the picture is clear. They are workarounds, built on changing account region signals and using a browser, and their own authors warn that the methods may violate Meta's terms and may stop working. The second video's region change step is particularly telling: it exists because the check is at the account level, and the tutorial is trying to move the account's signals, not just the connection. This guide does not recommend these methods, does not promise they work, and does not reproduce their steps as instructions. It describes them so that a Vietnamese reader who finds them understands what they are: unofficial, unendorsed, and risky.

The waitlist screenshots in these videos are also worth understanding. When a tutorial shows a waitlist screen, it is showing the same message described above: Muse is not available in your country or region yet. The tutorials treat the waitlist as something to get around. Meta treats it as the product telling you the truth. The waitlist is not a queue with a number. There is no public waitlist page, no sign up queue, and no published mechanism for notification beyond Meta saying it will let you know when your country is supported.

Why videos, and why now

The Vietnamese tutorials pitch Muse in a specific way: as a video creation tool. The setup guides describe Muse as currently the easiest free AI tool for video creation, and one instructs viewers, after receiving the token bonus, to chat with Muse and ask it to generate two or three videos, warning that skipping that step means video generation will not work. That framing is the tutorials' own, and it explains why the demand in Vietnam looks different from the demand elsewhere: Vietnamese creators are searching for a free video tool, and Muse is being presented as one.

Treat the video claims with care. Meta's announcements describe Muse as a personal agent that carries out tasks: email, travel, forms, shopping, and connected app work. The video generation claims in the tutorials come from the tutorial authors, not from Meta's launch materials, and this guide has not verified them against any primary source. What is verified is the demand itself: Vietnamese creators want AI video help, they are finding Muse tutorials that promise it, and the tutorials are recent, specific, and widely shared.

The lesson for a Vietnamese reader is to separate the product from the pitch. Muse, when it launches in Vietnam, will be a personal agent with a token system, connectors, and a weekly usage limit. Whether it generates videos the way the tutorials describe is unconfirmed, and the token bonus the tutorials promise depends on redeeming a code within 48 hours of account creation, which requires an account in a launch country. The pitch is ahead of the product in Vietnam, and the product has not arrived yet.

Can a VPN get you Muse in Vietnam?

No method is promised to work, and every method carries risk. The region check happens at the account level when you sign in. A VPN changes the IP address a site sees. It does not change the country attached to your Meta account, your App Store region, or your payment card. Guides that have studied the signup report that Muse looks at account region and App Store country too, so a VPN often gets people to the sign in screen and stops there. The Vietnamese tutorials implicitly confirm this: their region change step exists precisely because a VPN alone does not move the account signals.

Meta's own account has warned about this directly. Replying to a user in Malaysia on Threads, Meta's official @meta.ai account wrote that you might get past the signup with a US VPN, but you would be bypassing the geo restriction, risking your account. That reply was written for Malaysia, and it applies to Vietnam exactly as it applies everywhere else: the risk is to the account, and the account is the same Meta account that holds your Facebook and Instagram.

Treat that risk as a caution, not a verified certainty. Meta has said bypassing the restriction risks your account, and it has not published a list of accounts it has acted against. What is certain is narrower: no method is promised to work, the tutorial authors themselves warn their methods may stop working at any time, and the App Store listing appears only in launch countries. Waiting for the official launch is the only route that keeps your account fully safe.

The privacy setting to turn off first

When you do get into Muse, do this before anything else. Open the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls, and turn off the Help improve our AI models setting. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models. Turning it off keeps your conversations out of training data, and it is the single most important privacy step for any new Muse user, in Vietnam or anywhere else.

The setting matters more for Muse than for a chatbot, because Muse sees more of your life. Connect Gmail and it reads your email. Connect a calendar and it knows your schedule. Connect shopping and it knows what you buy. For Vietnamese creators, who may connect Facebook and Instagram accounts tied to their audiences, the exposure is larger still. The data sharing switch is your own control over whether those conversations feed future models, and it takes seconds to switch off. Do it on day one, before your first real task, not after a month of chats.

One caution, stated plainly. If you reach Muse from Vietnam before it launches here, and Meta ever reviews your account and sees the mismatch between your location and your account, the account can get flagged or banned. That is a caution, not a verified fact about enforcement. Turning the data sharing setting off does not hide your location. It only keeps your chats out of training data.

What will Muse cost in Vietnam?

Meta has announced no Vietnamese price for Muse, because Muse has not launched in Vietnam. The prices that exist are the US ones, reported consistently across launch coverage: Free at $0 with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Paid plans buy usage capacity, not features, which means the free tier gets the same agent with a smaller weekly allowance.

The free tier's size comes from Mark Zuckerberg's launch statement, which put the free allowance at 100 million tokens a week. That figure is attributed to Zuckerberg, not to Meta's help pages, and Meta's help pages describe the free tier only as a weekly usage limit. Whether a Vietnamese launch would price in dong, and at what level, is unconfirmed. The only local pricing reported anywhere is India, where The Hindu reported Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month on November 9, 2026, and that report is unconfirmed by Meta. Vietnam has no equivalent report.

Do not pay anyone who offers to sell you Muse access for Vietnam. Meta's own account has described offers selling early access as not official Meta offers, and the Vietnamese tutorials themselves are free. The real product, when it arrives, will be free to start, from Meta, through the App Store, Google Play, or muse.ai.

Vietnam and the regional rollout

Vietnam sits inside the fastest moving region in the Muse rollout. Singapore launched October 28, 2026, with eight local connector partners including Shopee, Grab, and Lazada, all of which operate in Vietnam too. Japan launched October 23. The Asia Pacific expansion is real, and it is moving market by market, with local connectors built per country. That pattern is the best news Vietnam has: Meta is clearly building for this region, not ignoring it.

But the pattern also shows why Vietnam waits. Each launch is its own announcement, its own App Store listing, and its own connector set, and Meta has named no Vietnamese partner, no Vietnamese connector, and no Vietnamese date. The Philippines and Indonesia are in the same position, grouped with Vietnam in the trackers as no launch announced. No source names an order for the next Southeast Asian market, and guessing the order would be speculation.

What a Vietnamese launch would need is clear from the launches so far: an App Store and Google Play listing in Vietnam, a newsroom announcement, and local connectors for the apps Vietnamese users already live in. Until Meta names Vietnam, the status is what the Vietnamese press reported in October: not yet available, advance registration message, cannot use it at this time. This guide will be updated the day that changes.
