Is Muse available in Denmark?

No. Muse is not available in Denmark, and Meta has announced no launch date for Denmark or for any European country. Denmark’s position is the same as every other EU member state: waiting, with no statement, no listing, and no timeline.

Muse launched in the US on September 8, 2026. Canada followed on September 18, Japan on October 23, Singapore on October 28, and Mexico, the fifth market and the first in Latin America, on November 5. That is the whole list. Meta publishes no country list for what comes next and no timeline. Our Muse available countries guide tracks the list as it changes.

You can verify the absence yourself in a minute. Search the Danish App Store and there is no Muse listing to install, and an account registered in Denmark that attempts signup reaches the waitlist screen. The trackers match what the store shows: checks in October found Muse listed only in the US and Canada at that time, and a later check found no announcement for any of the 30 EEA countries. This guide was last checked on November 13, 2026.

Why is Denmark still waiting? The EU rules and a Danish warning

Denmark’s wait is the European wait, and the European wait has a regulatory shape that a Danish company described more bluntly than most. In October 2026, usenti, a Danish company that builds AI for businesses, published a comparison that noted Muse had no published EU data processing agreement and no EU data residency option announced, as usenti’s comparison states. For a Danish team deciding whether Muse is safe to use for work, that gap is the whole question in miniature.

The legal backdrop is the same as for every EU country. The GDPR has applied since 2018. The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, with general purpose AI model obligations applying since August 2, 2025, and Article 50 transparency duties from August 2, 2026, as one analysis of Muse’s first month sets out. The Act does not ban agentic assistants. It sets duties that a product with deep access to personal data has to meet, and Meta has a history of delaying European launches while it works through those duties, including keeping its multimodal model out of the EU in 2024 over what it called an unpredictable regulatory environment, according to a November 2026 look at the European wait.

Denmark’s own regulator is Datatilsynet, the Danish Data Protection Agency, which is the lead supervisory authority for companies established in Denmark, including usenti, as the same comparison notes. Denmark was not among the 11 countries whose authority received a noyb complaint in June 2024 over Meta’s AI training plan, as DataGuidance reported, but the complaint’s effect was European: Meta paused the plan across the EU on June 14, 2024, after Ireland’s regulator intervened. Danish users were covered by a decision made in Dublin, which is how EU privacy enforcement works for Meta.

Meta has named no reason for Muse’s absence from Denmark, and the usenti observation is not a reason either. It is a symptom: the paperwork a European business launch needs does not exist in public yet. Until it does, Danish teams have a concrete question to ask Meta, and Danish consumers have a concrete reason to wait.

Will Muse speak Danish?

Meta has said nothing about Danish language support for Muse. No announcement, no help page, no launch report names Danish. That is the full extent of what is known, and the rest of this section is context.

The March 20, 2025 European launch of Meta AI covered six languages, and Danish was missing from the list. English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, and Italian were the six, as TechCrunch reported at the time, which meant Danish speakers waited longer and used Meta AI in another language first. That history belongs to Meta AI, not to Muse, but it establishes a pattern worth noting: Danish has not been among the first languages Meta’s AI products speak in Europe.

The position has improved since. Danish now appears on WhatsApp’s list of device languages for Meta AI access, which covers the languages on which access is available in some countries. WhatsApp says you can chat with Meta AI in any language, so the list is about device language gating, not about which languages the assistant understands. Danish being on the list means Danish devices are covered. It does not mean Muse will speak Danish.

Danish is a small language market, and small language markets depend on Meta doing the work rather than assuming it has been done. Muse’s per country localization, Japanese for Japan and Spanish for Mexico, argues that a Danish launch would include Danish. The argument is reasonable. It is not evidence. Launch day is the test: write in Danish, check the answers, check the actions.

WhatsApp and Messenger in Denmark: a split market

Denmark is a split messaging market, and the split is unusually even. Unlike Portugal or Austria, where WhatsApp clearly dominates, Denmark has two big apps running close together, and the surveys disagree about which one leads.

Sensor Tower’s Q4 2025 figures put WhatsApp at 1.68 to 1.79 million weekly active users in Denmark, and Messenger at 1.35 to 1.42 million, in Sensor Tower’s Denmark data. Sinch, citing a Global GWI survey, names Messenger as Denmark’s most popular messaging app, in Sinch’s country by country table. WhatsApp tops the free Communication chart on Google Play in Denmark, in AppBrain’s November 12, 2026 ranking. Three sources, three angles, one conclusion: both apps matter in Denmark, and neither can be ignored.

For Muse, that means the entry point is genuinely uncertain. Muse works inside WhatsApp in launched markets, as our WhatsApp guide explains. Its Messenger surface is unconfirmed. Danish users split their time between the two apps, so a launch that arrives in only one of them would reach only part of the country. The standalone app is the reliable door: it is the product, and it does not depend on which messaging app you prefer.

Meta AI reached Denmark in the March 20, 2025 European rollout, inside WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger, as TechCrunch reported. Danish users already have the assistant. What they do not have is the agent. The distinction, covered in our Muse vs Meta AI guide, is the difference between getting an answer and getting a task done.

Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Denmark

Danish glasses owners have lived with Meta’s voice assistant for more than a year and a half, and that history starts on April 23, 2025. That was the day Meta AI voice on Ray-Ban Meta glasses expanded to seven European countries, Denmark among them, together with Sweden, Finland, Germany, Austria, Belgium, and Norway, according to Meta’s announcement. The assistant on the glasses answers questions and sees through the camera, and Danish users have had it on their faces since that spring.

The glasses are a useful preview and a limited one. They show what Meta’s voice assistant sounds like, how it answers, and what it sees through the camera. They do not show what Muse does, because Muse is a different product: a standalone agent that takes actions across apps, not a voice layer on glasses. The Muse voice avatar, which rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, is US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers.

The expansion is still a meaningful signal for Denmark. Meta included Denmark in a European AI hardware rollout on the same day as the largest EU markets, which shows Denmark is not an afterthought in Meta’s European plans. It does not show that Muse is coming soon. Hardware rollouts and agent launches run on separate tracks, with separate announcements. Treat the glasses as evidence that Denmark is on Meta’s map, not as evidence of a date.

If you wear Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Denmark, use the voice assistant to form your own view of Meta’s AI: what it gets right, where it hedges, how it handles Danish. That experience will make you a sharper judge of Muse when it arrives. It will not make Muse arrive sooner.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Denmark?

The VPN tutorials that promise Muse from Denmark describe a technique that works for video streaming and fails for an agent that knows your account. This guide recommends none of them and promises none of them works.

Muse’s location checks go beyond the IP address. Your App Store region, your account country, your payment method, and the card used for age verification all identify Denmark, and they do so even when traffic is routed through another country. The usual outcome is the waitlist screen, the same screen Danish users get without a VPN. Our App Store guide details the store region checks.

The account risk is stated here the same way as in every guide in this series, because the evidence is the same. Meta may suspend or close an account that shows a location mismatch. That is a caution reported by users and guides, not a verified Meta policy. It is not proven safe, and it is not proven to trigger a ban. If you test a workaround, use an account with nothing in it that you need.

A VPN also cannot fix the Danish gaps that matter. It does not create an EU data processing agreement, it does not add Danish language support, and it does not put Muse in the Danish App Store. The usenti observation from October 2026, no published EU data agreement and no EU residency option, describes a product that is not ready for European business use on paper, and a VPN does not change the paper. Wait for the launch that comes with the documents.

What will Muse cost in Denmark?

A search for the Danish price of Muse turns up nothing Danish. Meta has announced no krone price, and it has announced no price at all for the Danish market, in any currency. The figures that do exist come from the United States, and they are reported figures rather than a published price list: free access with a weekly limit on use, Power for a reported $20 monthly, and Maximum for a reported $100 monthly. Features stay the same across those tiers. Money buys capacity, so the paid plans let you use the same product more, and the free tier lets you use it less.

Denmark uses the krone, not the euro. A Danish price would be a krone price, set for Denmark, and converting the US dollar figures will not predict it. Local pricing is a decision Meta makes per market, and Denmark’s decision has not been made in public.

Do not treat the free allowance as a settled fact. The weekly figure in circulation, 100 million tokens, was given by Mark Zuckerberg in his launch statement, and Meta’s help pages do not document it as a limit. What the help pages do say, as one availability guide notes, is that subscriptions are in limited testing and are not available in all locations. India points the same way: The Hindu reported Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month on November 9, 2026, unconfirmed by Meta, which suggests pricing is decided market by market. Denmark’s free allowance and Danish price, if they differ from the launch claims, will be stated at launch, and that statement is the one to trust.

For Danish users, the practical answer is the same as everywhere in Europe: expect a free tier, expect a local price announced with the launch, and do not pay anyone who claims to know the price early. Nobody outside Meta knows it, because Meta has not set it in public.

What will launch day look like in Denmark?

Launch day in Denmark will probably look like launch day everywhere else in Europe: a listing, an announcement, and a lifting of the waitlist. The announcement to watch for is one that names Denmark. A European announcement that lists countries and includes Denmark counts. A rumor does not.

Sort out the card before launch day, because the first gate is an age check and it does not wait for you to find a card that works. Muse admits users 18 and older only, and the check runs against a payment card or against a linked Instagram or Facebook account. The card route holds $1 temporarily, refunded in 5 to 7 business days, and it has been strict since October, with prepaid cards failing often, as our age verification guide documents. A Danish bank card is the sensible choice for a Danish launch. Keep a second option ready, because a failed check on launch day wastes the hour when everyone else is getting set up.

Setting, code, first connection, in that order. Turn off “Help improve our AI models” in Settings under Data controls. Redeem an invite code within 48 hours of creating the account. Connect one app and try one small job, then check the result. Danish users planning work use should also check whether an EU data processing agreement has appeared by launch day, given the gap usenti noted in October, as usenti’s comparison describes. If it has not, that is a decision to make with eyes open, not a reason to panic.

A Danish launch will be a beginning, not the finished product. The voice avatar that reached all US users on November 5, 2026 stays US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers, and connectors open up country by country, so the Danish connector list will grow after launch rather than arriving complete. Spaces is the exception to expect: launched November 6, 2026, it should be present wherever Muse is present, as our Spaces guide explains. Judge launch day by whether the agent works in Danish and handles a real task, not by whether every feature from the United States has crossed the Atlantic with it.
