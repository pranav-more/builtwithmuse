Is Muse available in Portugal?

No. Muse is not available in Portugal, and Meta has announced no launch date for Portugal or for any other European country. The position has not changed since Muse launched in the US on September 8, 2026, and every availability tracker checked for this guide still lists Portugal as waiting.

Muse is live in five countries: the US (September 8, 2026), Canada (September 18, 2026), Japan (October 23, 2026), Singapore (October 28, 2026), and Mexico (November 5, 2026). Mexico is the most recent launch, the fifth market, and the first in Latin America. No European country is on the list, and Meta publishes no country list and no timeline for what comes next. You can see the full picture in our Muse available countries guide.

What you see in Portugal matches that status. The Muse app has no listing in the Portuguese App Store, and signing up from a Portuguese account leads to the waitlist screen rather than a working account. Availability trackers that checked the App Store in October found Muse listed only in the US and Canada at that time, and a later check found no announcement for any of the 30 EEA countries. This guide was last checked on November 13, 2026.

Why is Portugal still waiting? The EU rules behind the delay

Meta has named no reason for the European wait, and this guide will not invent one. What exists is a backdrop, and Portugal sits inside it: the EU rulebook that already changed how Meta launches AI products in Europe.

The General Data Protection Regulation, GDPR, has applied across the EU since 2018, and it governs how personal data can be used to train AI models. The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024, with obligations for general purpose AI models applying since August 2, 2025, and transparency duties under Article 50 applying from August 2, 2026. The AI Act does not ban agentic assistants, but it adds duties around transparency, risk, and documentation that a product like Muse has to meet before it can be offered in the EU, as one analysis of Muse’s first month sets out in detail.

Meta has a record of holding products back from Europe over data questions. In 2024 it kept its multimodal AI model out of the EU, citing an unpredictable regulatory environment, according to Euronews reporting covered in a November 2026 look at the European wait. The same caution shaped Meta AI, which reached Europe about 18 months after the US. None of this proves the AI Act is the reason Muse is absent from Portugal. It explains why a launch that needs deep access to messages, calendars, email, and files would take longer in Europe than in the five markets where Muse is already live.

Portugal adds no extra national barrier on top of the EU rules. There is no Portuguese law that names Muse, and no Portuguese regulator has published a statement about it. Portugal waits because Europe waits, and the wait ends when Meta announces a European launch, not when any Portuguese rule changes.

The Meta AI precedent: what 18 months tells Portugal

The closest precedent for Muse in Portugal is Meta AI, and it is worth understanding precisely, because it is often quoted loosely. Meta AI, the assistant built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, and Messenger, launched in the US in 2023. It reached Europe on March 20, 2025, as a text only chat assistant in 41 European countries and 21 overseas territories, in six languages: English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, and Italian, as TechCrunch reported at the time.

The delay had a specific cause. Ireland’s Data Protection Commission, Meta’s lead privacy regulator in Europe, asked Meta in June 2024 to delay training its models on European users’ public posts, and Meta paused the European rollout. The revised plan, with an easier objection process, was approved in May 2025, and training on public posts by adults began, according to an availability tracker that followed the whole sequence. The gap from the US launch to the European launch was about 18 months.

That history is useful context, not a timetable. Meta AI and Muse are different products: Meta AI answers questions inside Meta’s apps, while Muse is a standalone agent that takes actions across apps on your behalf, as our Muse vs Meta AI guide explains. Meta has given no signal that Muse follows the Meta AI schedule, and the 18 month gap should not be read as a prediction that Muse arrives in Portugal in early 2028. The precedent tells you that European launches take longer and that data questions are usually the reason. It tells you nothing about a date.

One part of the precedent does matter for Portugal specifically. Portuguese was one of the six launch languages, which means Portugal got Meta AI in its own language from the first day of the European rollout. That is not true of every European country, and it is the strongest language signal Portugal has. Whether it carries over to Muse is unconfirmed, and the next section takes that up directly.

Will Muse speak Portuguese?

Meta has said nothing about Portuguese language support for Muse. No announcement, no help page, and no launch coverage names Portuguese for Muse. That is the honest position, and everything else in this section is context around it.

The context is encouraging but not conclusive. Portuguese was one of the six languages in the Meta AI European launch, and Meta has supported Portuguese across its apps for years. Muse’s own launches show that Meta localizes per country: Japan launched with Japanese, and Mexico launched in Spanish. A product that takes actions in messaging apps and email has to work in the language its users write in, so a Portuguese launch without Portuguese support would make little sense. But none of that is an announcement, and none of it promises anything.

There is also a complication that rarely gets mentioned. Portuguese has two large variants, European Portuguese and Brazilian Portuguese, and they differ in spelling, grammar, and everyday vocabulary. Brazil, the largest Portuguese speaking market in the world, is also still waiting for Muse, with no announcement, according to the same availability tracker. Meta has not said whether a Portuguese launch would cover both variants, one of them, or neither at first. Do not assume that a Brazil launch automatically means a Portugal launch, or the reverse.

Until Portuguese users can try Muse, treat every claim about Portuguese support as unconfirmed. The test is simple: when Muse launches in Portugal, open it, write in Portuguese, and see whether it answers well and takes actions correctly. No tutorial or review from another country can answer that question for you.

WhatsApp in Portugal, and why it matters for Muse

If Muse reaches Portugal the way it reached Mexico, most Portuguese users will meet it first inside WhatsApp. That is not a guess about Meta’s plans. It is a statement about Portugal: WhatsApp is the country’s default way to message, more so than in almost any other European country.

Portugal has the highest WhatsApp penetration rate in Europe, according to Statista figures from October 2025. Around 8 million people in Portugal use WhatsApp actively, about 78 percent of a population of roughly 10.3 million, in figures compiled from Statista data. WhatsApp Business is also deeply used by Portuguese shops and services, which means the app is where daily life and daily business already happen.

Muse works inside WhatsApp in the markets where it is live. The Mexico launch on November 5, 2026, included WhatsApp as one of the surfaces, alongside iOS, Android, and the web. Our guide to using Muse in WhatsApp covers what works there and what does not. None of that is confirmed for Portugal yet, but the pattern is clear: where WhatsApp is the main messaging app, it is the main door into Muse.

Do not confuse the Meta AI button already in your WhatsApp with Muse. Meta AI is the assistant that answers questions in a chat. Muse is a separate agent that can read your messages, check your calendar, and take actions across apps. The blue circle in WhatsApp today is Meta AI, not Muse, and its presence tells you nothing about when Muse arrives.

What Portugal’s privacy rules mean for Muse

Portugal’s privacy rules for Muse are the EU rules, applied through Portuguese law. The GDPR applies directly, and Portugal’s own data protection law, Law no. 58/2019 of August 8, sets out how it works nationally, as the CMS expert guide to Portuguese data protection law details. The regulator is the Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados, the CNPD, which handles complaints from people in Portugal.

Portugal was not one of the 11 countries whose data protection authority received a complaint from the privacy group noyb in June 2024 over Meta’s plan to train AI models on users’ personal data. The complaints went to Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Greece, Italy, Ireland, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, and Spain, as DataGuidance reported. Portugal’s absence from that list does not mean Portuguese users were unaffected: the Irish Data Protection Commission, as Meta’s lead supervisor in the EU, handled the case, and Meta paused the training plan across Europe on June 14, 2024.

The practical point for a future Muse user in Portugal is the data sharing setting. When you get access, open Settings, then Data controls, and turn off “Help improve our AI models”, confirming “Turn off” when asked. Do that before you connect anything else. The setting controls whether your interactions help train Meta’s models, and turning it off first means your early use is not feeding training while you learn what the product does.

That setting is not the whole privacy story. Muse asks for access to messages, email, calendar, and files to do its work, and each connection is a separate choice. Connect one app at a time, check what Muse does with it, and only then connect the next. A careful start matters more than a fast one, and the setting above is the first step, not the only one.

Should you use a VPN to reach Muse from Portugal?

Search for a way to use Muse from Portugal and you will find VPN tutorials. This guide promises none of them works, and recommends none of them. What follows is the honest picture of why workarounds are unreliable, so you can judge the tutorials for yourself.

Muse checks more than your IP address. Your App Store region, your account’s country, your payment method, and the card used for age verification all signal where you are, and those signals often leak past a VPN. The result most people report is the waitlist screen, the same screen you get without a VPN, or an account that works briefly and then stops. Our guide to App Store problems explains the store region checks in detail.

There is also an account risk to weigh. Meta may suspend or close an account if it sees a location mismatch, and that risk is reported as a caution by users and guides, not stated as a verified Meta policy. This guide states it that way deliberately: no source has published a Meta rule that says VPN use leads to a ban, and no source has proven it safe either. If you try a workaround despite the caution, use an account that holds nothing important: no main email, no payment method you need, no messages you cannot lose.

A VPN also solves none of the Portugal specific questions. It does not add Portuguese language support, it does not put Muse in the Portuguese App Store, and it does not change which connectors work with Portuguese services. The wait is for a real launch, and a VPN is not a shortcut to one.

What will Muse cost in Portugal?

There is no Portuguese price to quote, because Meta has published none. No euro figure, no dollar figure for Portugal, and no statement about what a Portuguese subscription would cost. The only numbers in public are the reported figures from the United States: a free tier with a weekly usage limit, Power at $20 a month, and Maximum at $100 a month. Those tiers differ in how much you can use, not in what the product does, so paying buys a larger allowance rather than extra features.

Meta’s own help pages are the reason to handle the free allowance with care. They describe subscriptions as being in limited testing and not available in all locations, as one availability guide notes, drawing on Meta’s help centre. The number usually attached to the free tier, 100 million tokens a week, does not come from those help pages. It comes from Mark Zuckerberg’s launch statement, which makes it a claim made at launch rather than a limit documented for users. Treat it as a rough sense of scale, and expect the real allowance to be stated, or restated, when Portugal launches.

Do not convert the US prices into euros and treat the result as the Portuguese price. Local pricing, if it comes, will be set by Meta for the Portuguese market, and it may differ from a straight currency conversion. The reported India pricing, Power at Rs 1,799 a month and Maximum at Rs 8,999 a month, reported by The Hindu on November 9, 2026, and unconfirmed by Meta, shows that Meta does set local prices rather than converting dollars. Portugal would get its own figure, if and when Meta announces one.

The practical answer for Portugal: expect a free tier when Muse launches, expect paid plans to cost something in euros that Meta has not announced, and do not pay anyone who claims to sell early access. The price question answers itself on launch day.

What will launch day look like in Portugal?

Launch day in Portugal, when it comes, will likely follow the pattern of the five launches so far. The App Store listing appears, the waitlist lifts for Portuguese accounts, and the announcement names the country. Watch for a Meta announcement that names Portugal specifically. A post that says Europe, or a rumor from a single outlet, is not a launch.

The first hour matters more than the first week. Age verification comes first: Muse is for users 18 and older, and verification uses a payment card, with a $1 temporary authorization refunded in 5 to 7 business days, or linking Instagram or Facebook, as our age verification guide explains. Have a card ready, and expect the check to be strict: card declines have been stricter since October, and prepaid cards often fail.

Then the setting, then the code. Turn off “Help improve our AI models” in Settings under Data controls before you connect anything. Redeem an invite code within 48 hours of creating your account for the 1 billion token bonus on both sides. Start with one app and one small job, a calendar check or a single message search, and confirm the result before you connect more.

Do not expect everything on day one. Voice rolled out to all US users on November 5, 2026, and is US only to start, as our Muse voice guide covers. Connectors roll out gradually per country, as they did in Mexico. Spaces, launched November 6, 2026, is available everywhere Muse is available, so it should be there from the start. The realistic launch day is a working agent, in Portuguese or in English, with a growing set of connections, not the full US feature set at once.
