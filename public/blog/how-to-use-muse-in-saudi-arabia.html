Is Muse available in Saudi Arabia?

No. As of November 13, 2026, Muse is live in five countries: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico. Saudi Arabia is not on that list, and no Arab country is. Meta has published no Saudi Arabia launch date, no Gulf timeline, and no country list that names Saudi Arabia as coming next. The full list of countries where Muse is live tracks every launch, and the Middle East has not appeared on it.

The Gulf specific check is dated October 2, 2026. On that date, Adly checked Meta's pages and reported the short answer in one line: Muse is not available in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Egypt, or any other Arab country, and Meta had not published a rollout date for other countries. The same check noted that Muse for Small Business, the business skills Meta added on September 29, 2026, followed the same map: the US and Canada only. You can read it in the Adly availability check for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Egypt.

Later trackers extend the same finding. An independent check of Apple's App Store in 22 countries on October 8, 2026 found "Muse from Meta" listed in the US and Canada stores only, with the UAE and Saudi Arabia among the stores where it did not appear. A tracker updated on October 31, 2026 grouped the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Israel under one line: no launch announced. Three more countries launched between the first check and this guide. None was in the region.

Muse is not Meta AI, and the difference matters most here

Saudi Arabia is the country where the Muse and Meta AI confusion does the most damage, because Meta AI is genuinely present and genuinely Arabic. Meta AI, the assistant inside WhatsApp, Instagram, and Messenger, has been available in several Arab countries, with Arabic support, since February 2025. A reader in Riyadh who chats with Meta AI in Arabic every day could reasonably assume Muse is already here. It is not.

The February 2025 rollout is worth understanding, because it is the source of the confusion. Meta brought Meta AI to the Middle East and North Africa in a rollout across ten countries, and Arabic support arrived with it. That was the chat assistant, not the agent. Muse is a separate product with its own app, its own account, and its own country gate, and it has not followed Meta AI into the region. Adly states the distinction in its first paragraph, and it is the distinction this whole guide rests on: same company, same chat box in WhatsApp, different product, different map.

The practical test is simple. If you can tag an assistant in WhatsApp and get an answer in Arabic, you are using Meta AI. Muse would do jobs: book, buy, connect, file, chase. Nothing in your WhatsApp today does that under Meta's name. When Muse launches in Saudi Arabia, the announcement will name the agent, not the assistant, and the app listing will appear under Meta's name in the Saudi store. Until then, Arabic Meta AI is the product you have, and it is not a path into Muse.

Does Muse support Arabic?

The listing says yes, the agent is unproven, and the two facts must be kept apart. The App Store listing for Muse already names Arabic among its supported languages, next to Urdu, Hindi, Bengali, Turkish, and dozens more. That listing detail is noted in the guide to Muse in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan, published on September 27, 2026, which calls it a hopeful sign while stressing that it is not a launch date.

A listing is about the app page, not a promise about conversation. Meta has published no list of the languages the Muse agent itself supports when it talks to you. The listing covers the languages the store page is available in, and most of the countries whose languages appear on it still cannot sign in. Arabic on the listing tells you Meta prepared the store page for Arabic readers. It tells you nothing about when the agent opens in Riyadh.

What Arabic support would look like inside the agent is also untested from Saudi Arabia, because no one here can run it. The reasonable expectation is that an agent launching in an Arabic speaking market would handle Arabic, given the listing and given that Meta AI already does. Reasonable is not confirmed. This guide will update with a real test when a launch makes one possible, and it will not grade Arabic quality from a store page.

That shared status does not mean a shared launch. Each country so far has launched on its own announcement, and nothing Meta has published suggests the Gulf would arrive as a bloc. A UAE launch, if it came, would not automatically open Saudi Arabia, and a Saudi launch would not automatically open the UAE. Watch each country on its own evidence. The UAE appears in Meta's hardware plans before Saudi Arabia does. What happens when you try from Saudi Arabia?

The site loads and the account stops you. Open muse.ai from Riyadh or Jeddah and the marketing pages read normally. Sign in, and the region message appears: "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet." The open entry point at muse.ai/join, which needs no code in launched countries, shows the same region message from Saudi Arabia. The gate is at the account step, not the web step.

On a phone, the store tells the story first. The Saudi App Store and Google Play do not list Meta's agent, so a search returns namesakes: other apps using the name, and the Chalhoub loyalty app described above. The seller line is the test. Meta's listing reads "Muse from Meta" with Meta Platforms, Inc. as the seller. A different seller means a different product, whatever the icon looks like.

WhatsApp changes nothing. Muse runs inside WhatsApp in launched countries, but the country rule covers the Muse chat inside WhatsApp exactly as it covers the standalone app. Opening WhatsApp in Saudi Arabia does not route around the gate, because the gate reads the account, not the app. Meta AI inside WhatsApp keeps working, because it is a different product on a different map.

What the agent actually does

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent: software that carries out jobs instead of only answering questions. You describe the outcome, and the agent works across your connected apps to reach it, booking travel, drafting mail, moving calendar entries, tracking orders, and paying at checkout. Before anything consequential, sending, publishing, spending, it asks you. Approvals are the control that makes delegation safe.

The work happens in Meta's cloud, inside the agent's own secure virtual machine, with a system called Sentinel watching over what goes to connected services. That design is part of why each country takes tailoring: local connectors, local services, and the compute cost of running the agent all have to be worked out per market. If you are new to the product, the full explainer on what Muse is covers the agent, the tokens, and the invite codes in one place.

Cost is simple where Muse is live, and unpriced where it is not. The free tier carries a weekly usage limit. Power costs $20 a month and Maximum costs $100 a month, and both buy larger weekly allowances, not new features. No Saudi price exists, and the dollar figures on Meta's help page name no currency, so a Saudi launch price cannot be inferred from them. The token mechanics are covered in the guide to Muse tokens.

Why no date for Saudi Arabia, or for the region

Meta has published no rollout date for any country beyond the five that are live, and the Middle East has not been named as next in any statement, post, or page this guide could find. The launches that did happen arrived as individual announcements: the US, Canada, Japan, Singapore, and Mexico, each on its own day, each through its own channel. No forward list exists to place Saudi Arabia on.

The Meta AI history explains why readers expect a date, and why expecting one is a mistake. Meta AI reached the region in February 2025, in a rollout across ten countries with Arabic support. That was a fast, broad, regional move. Muse has moved the opposite way: slowly, one country at a time, with no regional wave. The assistant's pattern is not the agent's pattern, and using one to predict the other has produced nothing but wrong dates.

The general reason for the pace came from Mark Zuckerberg on Meta's earnings call on October 21, 2026. Muse is compute intensive to run, he said, and expansion depends on running it more efficiently and on adapting it to each country, connectors included. That is a company level explanation for a slow rollout, not a Saudi Arabia explanation, and it carries no queue. It tells you why launches are spaced out. It does not tell you where the next one lands.

A month attached to Saudi Arabia on any unofficial page is invention, not information. Only a Meta page can date a Meta launch. This guide will update the day one does.

Workarounds, and why Gulf rules make this section longer

The mechanics come first, because they settle most of the question. The gate reads the account, not the wire. Your App Store region is set in your Apple ID or Google account. The country on your Meta account is a setting plus history. A VPN changes the IP address you appear from, and nothing else on that list. From Saudi Arabia, a VPN leaves a Saudi account Saudi, and the region message stays.

The legal picture is where Gulf readers need extra care, and where this guide stays narrow. Rules on VPN use differ by country across the Gulf, and this guide makes no claim about Saudi law. What can be cited is reporting about a neighbor: the tryagentsonic guide notes that in the UAE, misusing a VPN can carry heavy fines. That is a statement about the UAE, from a September 2026 guide, and it is included here only as a reason to check your own country's rules rather than assume they match another's. Saudi rules are separate, and readers should check them from a Saudi source before considering any workaround.

Beyond the legal question sits the account question. Under the terms, you must be in a country where Muse is available. An account that reaches Muse with its signals pointing in different directions is exposed under those terms, and flagging or a ban is possible. This guide found no published case involving a Saudi account, so the point stays a caution. The risk is one directional: the workaround either holds forever, or the account and everything in it goes.

Before you connect anything: the training switch

One switch governs whether your chats become training data, and it is worth setting before the agent hears anything from you. In the Muse app, go to Settings, then Data controls. Find "Help improve our AI models" and toggle it off, then confirm "Turn off" when the dialog asks. Your chats, in Arabic or English, stay out of training data from that point on.

Order matters. The switch governs chats from the start of the account, so setting it after a week of use leaves a week of chats under the old rule. Set it first, then connect the inbox, then hand the agent its first job. The sequence takes under a minute and it is the most useful minute in the setup.

The switch changes nothing else. It does not affect availability, it does not move the country on your account, and it does not limit what the agent can do. It is a single choice about your words, and it belongs at the top of the setup list because everything the agent hears after it is covered by it.

What a Saudi launch would look like

A Saudi launch should present as three facts arriving together: the country's name in a Meta announcement, the app in the Saudi stores under Meta's name, and sign in opening for Saudi accounts on muse.ai. The five launches so far each arrived that way. A lone fact, an announcement without a listing, or a listing without sign in, is not a launch yet.

There is a check you can run without waiting for press. Take the US listing address for "Muse from Meta" and replace the us part with sa. Apple's lookup pages are public, so the result is immediate: a missing page means no launch yet, and a loaded listing means the launch has probably begun. Treat the listing as the leading signal and sign in as the trailing one, since the account check runs on its own schedule.

Watch the UAE as context, not as a trigger. If the UAE launches first, that tells you Meta is moving in the region, and it tells you nothing about the Saudi date. Each country opens on its own announcement, and the gap between neighbors could be days or months. The waitlist on this page is how you hear about the Saudi opening the day it happens, without watching another country's news for a signal that may never come.

Advance notice is not part of the pattern. No launch date has been announced ahead of time for any country, and the announcement has landed with the opening, or days before it. Plan around surprise, not around a calendar.
