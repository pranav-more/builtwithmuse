Is the Facebook connector live in Muse?

As of October 25, 2026, Facebook is a live connector in Muse, and for most people it needs no setup at all. Meta's Help Center says Facebook connects automatically if your Facebook account is in the same Accounts Center as your Meta account, so there is nothing to install and nothing to sign in to a second time. Once it is connected, Muse can read your Facebook Page and your posts, draft new posts for your approval, and tell you what is getting attention. That is the short answer: it is live, it is automatic for most accounts, and its best use is turning your Page into something you can ask questions about instead of something you scroll through.

Facebook was named as a Muse connector on launch day, September 8, 2026, when Alexandr Wang listed the connectors only available to Muse, including Facebook with Marketplace, Instagram with DMs, Threads, and Messenger. Facebook Page support then came with the business connector set announced September 29, 2026, which covered Facebook Pages and Instagram professional accounts for small business use. Note the dating here: the facts above come from the September launch record and the September 29 business set, and the October checks counted 54 connectors in the web app without announcing a Facebook change. If your account shows something different, trust what your own Settings show, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.

Here is the honest version. The Facebook connector is not a Facebook app inside Muse. It is a bridge between Muse and the Facebook account you already have. Muse reads what the connector is allowed to read, reasons about it, and answers in plain words. The value is not that Muse can open Facebook. The value is that you can ask it a question that would take you an hour of scrolling to answer yourself, like which of your posts actually worked this month, or what your Page said about a topic last year. The rest of this guide covers how to check the connection, what it can actually do, what it cannot do, and the safety rules to set before you let it near anything you publish.

What do you need before you connect Facebook?

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched. Japan joined on October 23, 2026, as the first market outside North America.
  • A Meta account with Facebook in the same Accounts Center. This is the whole connection for most people. If your Facebook account is already in the same Accounts Center as your Meta account, Meta connects it automatically and there is nothing else to set up.
  • A Facebook Page, if you want Page work. Personal profile reading and Page reading are different jobs. Page support arrived with the September 29, 2026 business connector set, so check that your Page is the one linked to the account Muse sees.
  • Approvals set to ask me. Before you ask Muse to do anything with Facebook, set approvals so nothing is published without your tap. This is the single most important step on this page.
  • The Instagram connector, optional but recommended. Facebook and Instagram are the two halves of most small business posting. Our Instagram connector guide covers that half, and our complete connectors list shows all 54 live connectors in the web app so you can see what else your account has.

How do you connect Facebook to Muse?

The connection itself takes a few minutes, and for most people there is no connection step at all, because Meta connects Facebook automatically. What takes time is checking that the right account is connected and that your approvals are set right. Run the steps in order and do not skip the approvals step at the end.

  1. Check Accounts Center first. Open Accounts Center in your Meta account settings and confirm your Facebook account is listed there alongside your Meta account. If it is not there, add it. This is the step that decides whether Muse sees your Facebook at all, and it is also the step most people skip.
  2. Open Muse and go to Settings, then Connectors. The connectors list lives there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  3. Find Facebook in the connectors list and open it. If it shows as connected, you are done with setup. If it is not listed, your account has not received it yet, or your Facebook account is not in the same Accounts Center. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Fix the Accounts Center link first, then check again in a few days.
  4. Confirm which Page Muse sees. If you run a Facebook Page, ask Muse which Page it can see before you ask it to do anything with the Page. A personal profile and a Page are different things, and asking first stops Muse from drafting a business post in your personal voice, or the other way round.
  5. Set approvals to ask me. In Muse settings, set approvals so that publishing anything always waits for you. Meta says that by default Muse will not take important actions without your approval, and many connectors can be set so Muse can only read data and cannot take actions at all. Use that read only setting for your first week if you want to be careful.
  6. Test it with a small ask. Ask Muse to summarize your last five Page posts and tell you which one got the most reactions. If the answer matches what you see on the Page, the connection is working and you can trust it with bigger asks. If it does not match, stop and check the account link before you go further.
Copy this prompt
Look at my Facebook Page posts from the last 30 days. Tell me which 3 posts got the most reactions and comments, what those 3 posts have in common, and draft one new post in the same style for this week. Do not post anything. Show me the draft first.

What can Muse actually do with Facebook?

The connector earns its place with five uses. Everything else is a variation on one of them.

Read your Page and your posts

Ask Muse what your Page posted this week, what it posted about a topic last month, or what your most recent posts actually said. This is the everyday use, and it is the one to build a habit around. Instead of opening Facebook and scrolling back through your own timeline, you ask a question and get an answer. It is also the safest use, because reading costs you nothing and risks nothing. If you run a Page for a business, start here: ask Muse to list your last ten Page posts with their dates, then read the list against the Page itself to confirm it is seeing the right Page.

Draft posts in your voice

Tell Muse how you sound once, then ask it to draft posts for the week. The drafts arrive for your approval, and the approval step is the whole point: you read, you edit, you publish. The drafts get better over a few weeks because Muse learns from the edits you make and the posts that actually worked. The failure mode to avoid is approving a draft you did not read. A draft you did not read is a post you did not write, and your audience will notice the difference before you do. Keep the approval step on for every post, even the small ones, because the small ones are the ones that slip through.

Tell you what is getting attention

Ask Muse which of your posts got the most reactions and comments, and what those posts have in common. This is where the connector turns from a reader into an analyst. A good answer does not just name the top post. It tells you the pattern behind it: the topic, the length, the time of day, the kind of image, the question that got people talking. Use that pattern to plan next week, not to copy last week. The posts that worked are a clue about what your audience wants more of, not a template to repeat until it stops working. Run this check once a week, on the same day, and keep a short note of what you learn each time.

Search your posts, groups, events, and saved items

The connector listing describes Facebook access as covering friends, profiles, posts, groups, events, stories, and saved items. That makes Muse useful as a search tool for your own Facebook history. Ask it to find the post where you announced a price change, the event you created last spring, or the article you saved and never read. This beats scrolling when you remember the meaning of a post but not the words in it, because Muse reads for meaning. It is still worth checking the result against the original post before you quote a date or a number from it to anyone, because a summary can get a detail wrong while getting the gist right.

Marketplace, with a caution

Facebook with Marketplace was named as a Muse connector on launch day, September 8, 2026. What we have not verified is what Muse can actually do inside Marketplace: whether it can search listings, track a listing you posted, or only read Marketplace content the way it reads posts. Say this plainly, because it matters: do not plan a Marketplace workflow around Muse until you have tested it yourself with a small ask and seen what comes back. Ask it to find one of your own listings first. If it can, you have a tool. If it cannot, you have your answer, and you keep doing Marketplace by hand.

What can the Facebook connector not do?

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector.

  • It cannot publish without your approval when approvals are set right. That is a feature, not a bug. If you turn approvals off, you lose the safety net, so keep them on for anything that leaves your account.
  • It cannot see Facebook accounts outside your Accounts Center. A second profile, a Page owned by a different account, and a group you never joined are all invisible to it. It reasons from what you connected, nothing more.
  • It cannot verify Page numbers on its own authority. We have not verified that Muse can pull Page insights numbers, so check any number it quotes, reach or reactions or comments, against the Page itself before you report it or act on it. A wrong number acted on fast is worse than a slow read you verified.
  • It cannot manage your ads. Ads live in Meta Business Manager, which is a separate entry in the connectors list, and we have not verified what Muse can do inside it. Do not assume the Facebook connector covers ad work.
  • It cannot guarantee it read everything right. A summary can miss a post, and a draft can misread your tone. Check anything that touches money, prices, or an unhappy customer against the original post before you act on it.
  • It cannot fix a quiet Page. If your Page has not posted in months, Muse will summarize the quiet. The connector makes a posting habit easier, but it does not create the habit for you.

What should you watch for?

As of October 25, 2026, Facebook is a settled connector: live since launch day, September 8, still in the live directory, with Page support from the September 29 business set. There is no October update to how it works, so the habits around it still matter. Check the summary against your Page for the first week, especially any number or date it quotes. A wrong date acted on fast is worse than a slow read you verified.

Be careful with the difference between your profile and your Page. The most common Facebook mistake in Muse is asking it to draft a business post and getting a draft in your personal voice, or asking about your personal posts and getting Page results. Name the Page in your prompt every time until you are sure Muse knows which one you mean. A prompt that says "my Page" is a guess. A prompt that names the Page is an instruction.

Be careful with sensitive posts. Anything about money, prices, legal terms, or an unhappy customer deserves a human read before any reply goes out, even a drafted one. Set a rule for yourself: Muse drafts, you read, you publish. The moment you start approving without reading is the moment the connector stops being an assistant and starts being a risk.

Check your data controls. In Muse settings, open Data controls and look at the switch for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to read your Facebook. That switch controls whether Meta may use your chats to train future models.

Finally, remember that Facebook is only one of the Meta connectors. If the question is about your DMs, that is Instagram Messages or Messenger, not Facebook. If it is about short text posts, that is Threads. Our Threads connector guide covers that side, and the complete connectors list shows where each Meta app starts and stops. Picking the right connector is half the job, and picking the wrong one is the reason most first tries feel broken.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Facebook connector live in Muse?
Yes. As of October 25, 2026, Facebook is a live connector in Muse. It was named on launch day, September 8, 2026, and Meta's Help Center says it connects automatically through Accounts Center.
Do I need to set up the Facebook connector?
Usually not. If your Facebook account is in the same Accounts Center as your Meta account, Meta connects it automatically. Check Settings, then Connectors, to confirm it shows as connected.
Can Muse post to my Facebook Page?
Muse can draft posts for your Page and publish them when you approve. Nothing is published without your approval when your approvals are set to ask me, so every post waits for your tap.
Can Muse see my Facebook Marketplace listings?
Marketplace was named as part of the Facebook connector on launch day, but we have not verified what Muse can actually do inside Marketplace. Test it with one of your own listings before you plan any workflow around it.
Can I use the Facebook connector outside the United States, Canada, and Japan?
Not yet. Muse itself has launched in the United States, Canada, and Japan only. Join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day in your country.
What happens if I disconnect Facebook?
Muse loses access to that account and its Page. Your posts stay on Facebook, and nothing Muse drafted is published after you disconnect. You can reconnect the same account later through Accounts Center.
