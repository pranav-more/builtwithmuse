Yes, companies use Muse, but not the way the headlines suggest. Ten big retailers are announced, not live. The businesses actually running on Muse are small ones. And the brands measuring orders from AI agents report hundreds, not millions.

What does it mean for a company to use Muse?

Ask who uses Muse and you get three different answers, because three different groups use it in three different ways. The first group is the big retail brands Meta named at its Connect event in September 2026. The second is small businesses, the plumbers, salons and grocery stores that run daily work through it. The third is millions of everyday shoppers, who are the largest group by far and the reason shopping is the most common thing Muse does. Keep the three groups apart and the story gets honest fast. Mix them up and every headline about Muse overstates it.

The other distinction that matters is announced against live. Meta announced ten big retailers on September 23, 2026. Announced means Meta said the connector is coming. Live means a user can switch it on today and check out. As of the trackers checked on November 14 and 15, 2026, all ten are still listed as announced, not live. That gap is the most important fact in this guide, and it is the reason the headline names are not the story. The story is what is actually running.

So here is the short answer, before the detail. No large US company has publicly announced it uses Muse as an enterprise customer. The named retailers are mostly a promise so far. The businesses actually running on Muse are small. And the big brands that have measured orders from AI agents report numbers in the hundreds. That sounds like a letdown. It is actually the useful truth, because it tells you where the real usage is: small businesses and shoppers, not big brand press releases. The rest of this guide names names, with sources, so you can check every claim.

Which big brands did Meta name as Muse partners?

At Meta Connect on September 23, 2026, Meta said it was adding ten retailers to Muse: Walmart, Best Buy, American Eagle Outfitters, DICK'S Sporting Goods, Fanatics, Gap, Michael Kors, Sephora, Ulta and Wayfair, plus Shop Pay and PayPal for payments, with Expedia coming soon for travel and Instacart named for groceries. The wording in Meta's own recap was 'we're adding', which is an announcement, not a switch being flipped. Two connector trackers checked on September 26 listed all ten as announced rather than live, and noted that none of the ten had said anything about Muse in their own newsrooms. The tracker page is at the TruCommerce partner table.

Two months later, that status has barely moved. A connectors list updated on November 14, 2026 still lists Walmart, Best Buy and the rest as announced in September 2026, with a note to check your own Muse settings to see what is live for you. A second library checked on November 15, 2026 lists Walmart and Best Buy the same way, announced, with availability varying by account and region. The one concrete move since September belongs to Walmart itself: on October 21, 2026 Walmart launched its own Agent Gateway, a system that lets AI agents shop with spending limits without seeing card details. Whether Muse uses that gateway today is not confirmed in any source checked for this guide. Walmart is the announced retailer with the most real agent infrastructure, and the most likely to work well if the connection is live. The November lists are at the AgentMsgr connectors list and the Cask and Hound library.

What is actually live today is a shorter list. Every eligible Shopify store is reachable through Shopify Catalog, with direct checkout on by default and no fees for selling through it. Ticketmaster is live as a reviewed connector, with purchases completing inside Ticketmaster, for shoppers in the US and Canada. Link by Stripe is live as the payment rail, and Plaid is live for finances. The first named retailer to actually go live is not American at all: Loblaw, the Canadian grocer, connected its catalogue on November 18, 2026, and it is covered in the Loblaw shopping guide. And the biggest name in retail is the exception: Amazon blocked Muse on September 20, 2026, saying agent access violated its terms, and no source gives a date for its return. That block is covered in the Amazon guide.

What are small businesses in the US actually doing with Muse?

The business story Meta is actually telling is not about big brands. It is about small ones. On September 29, 2026 Meta launched Muse for Small Business, a set of business skills and connectors inside the regular Muse app, for the US and Canada only. It is free with limits, and the paid plans are Power at $20 a month and Maximum at $100 a month. Fifteen third party connectors are named: Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe and Zoom, plus Facebook Pages, Instagram professional analytics and Meta ad accounts. The setup is covered in the small business setup guide. What follows is what owners do with it once it is set up.

Meta shared the actual requests in a press release in mid October 2026, and the most common request is ad performance review. The verbatim prompts owners send most often are 'Analyze my Meta ads. What are my highest performing ads and why?' and 'What are top recommendations to improve my Meta ads performance?' The second most common use is campaign setup from a creative brief: 'Taking into consideration the attached creative brief, how do you suggest I set up a Meta ads campaign?' The third is chaining connectors into one funnel: Meta Ads plus Shopify plus Canva plus HighLevel, where Muse reads the catalogue, picks the angle, briefs the creative and builds the ads. Meta's own rule, repeated everywhere, is that nothing publishes, sends or spends without the owner's approval. The writeup of Meta's release is at Social Media Today.

The named examples are small and specific, which is the point. Tom Mulholland, 66, runs Mulholland Grocery in Malvern, Iowa, and uses Muse to create ads and track orders. Krys Benyamein, a realtor and columnist, had Muse analyze 1,133 of his Instagram posts. Meta tested the concept with 35 business owners at its Los Angeles campus in early September 2026 before the wider announcement. None of these is a big brand case study, and that is the honest shape of business use in 2026: a grocery store in Iowa, a realtor, and a few dozen owners in a pilot, not a Fortune 500 rollout. Treat the Mulholland and Benyamein cases as reported, not independently verified: they come from Meta's pilot coverage, not from interviews by this site.

What do the numbers say, and what do brands report?

Meta's own numbers are large. On its third quarter earnings call on October 21, 2026, Mark Zuckerberg said more than 10 million people in the US and Canada set up Muse in about six weeks, and about 25 percent use it on a given day. Shopping is the most common use case: over 25 percent of weekly active users shop through it, with an average purchase over $200, travel included. Those are Meta's figures, from the earnings call, and the coverage is at Marketing Brew. They describe people setting up and shopping, not companies adopting. That distinction is where the brand story gets thin.

WIRED contacted roughly 50 companies and published the answers on October 31, 2026, and the numbers are small. DoorDash said it had taken 'several hundred' orders from third party agents including Muse, on an earnings call on October 29, 2026, against about 3 million deliveries a day. Hilton called agent bookings insignificant. T-Mobile said nothing of significance. eBay said very, very small. Ally Financial said quite low. Life Time Group reported zero cancellations from agents. Chipotle said agentic orders were up a single digit percentage, and that agents stumbled on loyalty discounts and after hours ordering, so Chipotle is fixing its site code. The WIRED piece is paywalled, so the figures here are cited through the readable mirror at Technology Tangle.

Two more answers from the same reporting round show the direction of travel. Insurify blocked Muse, then reversed course and launched an AI Agent Gateway on October 28, 2026. Amazon blocked Muse and now signals it may work with outside agents. Bank of America warned its customers against sharing credentials with AI assistants. And Zuckerberg plans to charge businesses a fee on Muse transactions, predicting it will eventually exceed advertising revenue. Read together, the picture is consistent: big brands are preparing for agents, measuring tiny volumes today, and deciding the rules as they go. Nobody is reporting a flood of Muse orders. The honest headline for a brand in November 2026 is that the traffic is real, the orders are rare, and the infrastructure is being built now.

What do people actually use Muse for?

If companies are the small story, people are the big one. A catalog of more than 1,000 sourced Muse builds, counted in late September 2026, shows money errands as the most common use, about 20 percent of the sorted cases. The pattern is bills, subscriptions and refunds. Users report AT and T fiber cut from $80 to $40 a month plus three months free, Spectrum from $75 to $40, Verizon from $516 to $386 a month, and $300 off a wifi bill in under ten minutes. Subscription audits find forgotten plans, including one at $100 a month. Refunds and claims include a $1,000 United fare credit after a price drop, a $250 Delta delay credit in about five minutes, and over $4,000 in medical bill corrections from questioning itemized bills. Insurance switches reported by five users saved a median of about $1,356 a year. The catalog is at shipwithmuse, and the figures are as reported by users, not audited.

Travel is the second most common use. One Europe trip involved 146 cash and award flight searches across 21 cities and seven dates. A Croatia to Italy car ferry saved $2,200 in rental drop off fees. A hotel was found inside Heathrow terminal during an outage. Two orchestra seats were bought for $117 on the same night. The connectors doing this work are Duffel, covering more than 500 airlines, Ticketmaster and Fever. The money challenge cases Meta promoted tell the same story from the other side: a startup founder who found car insurance $3,500 cheaper a year, a product manager who saved $1,250 at a car dealership, and a forgotten $200 Verizon gift card found. Those cases are covered in full in the money challenge guide, and they are self reported, not audited.

The rest is household and business admin. One parent has Muse watch four kids sports schedules across four messaging apps and send one daily summary. Another did about 95 percent of a US passport renewal through the agent. A dying water heater was replaced for a $1,450 install against a $2,300 quote. A plumbing company runs its whole morning, job board, customer texts and office brief, from one 6 a.m. message. A salon ran its back office through Muse for a week. One user found $1,742 a week in leaking ad spend by having Muse reconcile Meta, pixel and analytics data. And people build small apps with it: a workout tracker in about ten minutes, a photo based calorie tracker, a kids bank app with daily interest. What does not work yet is worth one honest line: captchas stall bookings, verification codes stop calls, and some retailers block the agent outright. The catalog records those failures too.

Does Muse call a business customers for it?

Short answer: no, and the direction matters. Muse calls businesses on the user's behalf. It does not call a business customers or leads. Outbound calling went generally available on November 12, 2026 for paid subscribers in the US only, on Power at $20 a month or Maximum at $100 a month, calling US businesses. Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang announced it the same day, and his example call types were insurance, cable, the DMV and dinner reservations. The calling guide is at can Muse call businesses for you.

The concrete example of calling in action comes from a Globe and Mail reporter, Joe Castaldo, who on October 30, 2026 had his Muse agent phone US music stores to ask about stock and price. The agent did not identify itself as AI, but it did disclose that the call was being recorded. That is a user sending an agent to a business, not a business sending an agent to its customers. Testers report that Muse refuses personal numbers, so there is no version of this feature where a company points Muse at a lead list today. Businesses are the recipients of Muse calls, and the question for them is how to handle an agent on the line, not how to deploy one.

There is one piece of history worth knowing. Reuters reported in September 2026 that Meta tested human contractors placing some Muse calls, then rolled that back. The launched feature is the agent itself on the line, identifying itself as calling on the user's behalf and saying the call is being transcribed. For a business owner, the practical read is simple: your customers' agents may already be calling you, and the call will sound like a person asking a normal question. Train whoever answers the phone to treat an agent caller the way they would treat any caller, and to say clearly what they can and cannot do by phone.

How can a brand get on Muse?

There are three ways in, and they are not equal. The first is a reviewed connector: submit it at muse.ai/platform, and Meta reviews it for functional, security and legal requirements, with end to end testing. Approved connectors appear in the directory. Meta says more than 1,500 applications arrived in less than a week after launch, so the queue is long and no timeline is published. The second way is Shopify: any eligible store is reachable through Shopify Catalog, with direct checkout on by default, no fees for selling through it, and the merchant remaining merchant of record. No application, no review, no sign in required from the shopper. The third way is the open web: Muse browses with a full browser in its Secure VM, and pays either through Link, which issues a single use virtual card scoped to the approved purchase, or with credentials the shopper saved. Link works at more than 1 million businesses that accept it. A fourth route is the open protocol: Personal Agent Protocol (Poppy), which Muse looks for first on a site.

The open web route is the one most brands already have, whether they know it or not. A brand on WooCommerce, Magento or a headless stack is already reachable by Muse. Whether Muse can finish the purchase is decided by the brand's own pages: a variant an agent can resolve, a price that holds until approval, a checkout that takes a card with no account, and bot defenses that do not treat the Secure VM as an attacker. That is ordinary checkout hygiene, and it is the same work on every agent surface. The merchant sees an ordinary guest, card not present order, with no Muse attribution: the traffic files as direct. The merchant can also refuse, as Amazon did on September 20, 2026.

The honest advice for a brand in November 2026 is to start with the second and third routes, not the first. Make sure your store is eligible for Shopify Catalog if you run Shopify, and make your checkout finishable by an agent paying with a one time card if you do not. Apply for the reviewed connector in parallel, but do not plan around it, because the queue is long and the timeline is unpublished. And watch the numbers that matter: not the press release count, but whether agent orders appear in your own orders at all. As of late September 2026, no direct to consumer brand had publicly reported receiving a Muse order, and the WIRED reporting in late October put the largest reported number in the hundreds. Build for the future, measure the present.

How do you check what is live for you?

The connector list inside your own Muse account is the authority. Open Muse, go to settings, then connectors, and read what is actually there. A partner name that appears in the news but not in your list has not reached your account yet, and that is normal: connectors roll out by account and region, over days and weeks, not all at once. If a name is missing today, check again in a few weeks before concluding it is not coming. The same applies to features: Muse Voice, for example, reached the US on November 5, 2026 and Canada on November 18, and the other four launch countries have no date yet.

The same check applies to the shopping promises. If Muse offers to shop at a retailer, ask it to show you the basket and the total before you approve anything, and read the store name on the basket as well as the price. If it cannot shop somewhere you expected, the likely reason is that the connector has not reached you, not that you did something wrong. And if you are new to Muse, redeem a code within 48 hours of creating your account: both people get 1 billion Muse tokens, one code per account, case sensitive.
