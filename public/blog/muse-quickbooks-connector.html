How to Use the QuickBooks Connector in Muse

Connect QuickBooks to Muse and your books stop being a report you avoid and start being answers in plain words: who owes you, what you spent, and what is left. Here is how to connect it, what it can read, and the money safety rules to set first.

1. The short answer

As of October 25, 2026, QuickBooks is a live connector in Muse. Connect it and you can ask Muse about your books in plain words: who owes you money, what you spent this month, and whether the cash coming in covers the cash going out. That is the short answer, and it is worth a lot to a small business owner who only opens the accounting app when tax time arrives.

QuickBooks arrived on September 29, 2026, as one of the 15 business connectors in the Muse for Small Business launch, and it was named again in the October 7, 2026 iPad app launch and expanded connector list. Across Muse as a whole, an in app check on October 15 counted 54 connectors live in the web app, with about 58 to 60 live in total including phone only features, and 18 announced but not yet live. Our full connectors list keeps the dated picture for every connector, so check there before you plan a workflow around any single one.

Here is the honest version. QuickBooks is where the money truth lives, and most owners avoid it because it speaks in reports. Muse turns those reports into answers. You ask a question the way you would ask a bookkeeper, and Muse reads your QuickBooks data and answers in plain words. The catch is the writing side. Meta has not published a list of exactly which QuickBooks actions Muse can take, so nobody outside Meta can say for certain whether Muse can create an invoice, mark one paid, or move an expense into the right category. The reading side is the safe bet. The writing side is something to test with one small action and your approvals set to ask me, and this guide marks that line clearly wherever it matters.

2. What you need

  • Muse access. You need an account with access first, in the United States, Canada, or Japan, where Muse has launched.
  • A QuickBooks account with your books in it. The connector reads the company you connect, so sign in with the company whose money you actually want to ask about.
  • Your QuickBooks sign in. You connect with your QuickBooks account, the same way you sign in to QuickBooks itself.
  • Approvals set to ask me. Before you connect anything that touches money, set approvals so nothing is created, sent, or changed without your tap. This is the single most important step on this page.
  • A rough picture of your money. Who your biggest customers are, what you spend the most on, and how you charge. You do not need exact numbers. A rough picture lets Muse give useful answers on day one instead of generic ones.
  • A second connector if money arrives somewhere else. If customers pay through Stripe or buy through Shopify, connect that too. Our small business finances guide shows how the money connectors work together.

3. How to connect it, step by step

The connection itself takes a few minutes. The setup around it matters more, because this connector touches money. Run the steps in order and do not skip the approvals step at the end.

  1. Open Muse and go to Settings. The connectors list lives there. What is printed there is what your account has, because rollout is staged and two accounts can see different lists on the same day.
  2. Find QuickBooks in the connectors list and open it. If QuickBooks is not listed, your account has not received it yet. There is nothing to install and nothing you broke. Check again in a few days.
  3. Sign in with QuickBooks. Use the account that holds the company you want Muse to read. If you run more than one company in QuickBooks, pick the main one first and check what Muse can see before you assume it sees the rest.
  4. Grant the access Muse asks for. Muse needs permission to read your books and, if you allow it, to act on them. Read the permission screen before you accept it. If a scope surprises you, stop and check what it covers before continuing.
  5. Set approvals to ask me. In Muse settings, set approvals so that anything that creates, sends, or changes a record always waits for you. This is what stops Muse from ever acting on your money without your say.
  6. Test with a read only request. Ask Muse how much you are owed right now and who owes the most. Read the answer against QuickBooks. If the numbers match, the connection is good. Do not ask it to change anything on the first day.
  7. Use it read only for a week. Questions, summaries, and drafts only. Once you trust what it reads, test one small write action if you need it, and watch exactly what it does before you trust a second one.

4. What it actually does

The connector earns its place with six uses. The first three are reads, and they are the ones to build a habit around. The last three mix reading with drafting, and every draft waits for your approval before anything happens.

Who owes you, and how late

This is the use that pays for the habit. Ask Muse who owes you money, how much each customer owes, and how many days past due each invoice is. The answer arrives as a plain list, not a report you have to decode. The habit is to run it every Monday and chase the top three. Most small businesses do not have a sales problem. They have a collection problem, and the money is sitting in invoices that nobody chased. A list you actually read beats a report you never open, and the Monday list is the one that turns forgotten invoices into money in the bank. If a customer always pays late, ask Muse to show you their payment history. A pattern you can see is a conversation you can have, and the conversation starts with facts instead of feelings.

What you spent, and where it went

Ask Muse what you spent last week or last month, grouped by category, and which categories grew the most. This is the use that catches the quiet leaks: the subscription you forgot, the supplier whose prices crept up, the category that doubled while nobody was watching. The question to ask is not just what did I spend, but what changed. A flat total tells you nothing. A category that jumped by half tells you where to look. Run it monthly and keep the answers, because three months of answers show a trend that one month never will. When a category surprises you, ask Muse to list the individual expenses inside it. The line items are where the story lives, and the story is usually one vendor, one subscription, or one habit.

The cash picture in plain words

Ask Muse for the cash picture: money in, money out, and what is left, for this month and for the next few weeks. This is the question owners ask their accountant and then wait a week for the answer. With the connector, the answer takes a minute, and you can ask it again whenever a big bill lands. The follow up question matters more than the first one: ask what is due to come in and what is due to go out before the end of the month, so you see the gap before it arrives instead of after. If the picture is tight, ask which invoices, if paid this week, would fix it. That turns a worry into a short list of calls to make. Cash flow is the reason most small businesses fail while still being profitable on paper, and a plain word answer you check weekly is the cheapest insurance against it.

Invoice follow ups, drafted for approval

Once you know who is late, ask Muse to draft the follow up messages in your voice. Give it the tone once: firm but friendly, or short and direct, and tell it never to threaten a customer you want to keep. The drafts arrive for your approval, and the approval step is the whole point. You read, you edit, you send. The drafts get better over a few weeks because Muse learns from the edits you make and the messages you actually send. The failure mode to avoid is approving a draft you did not read. A follow up you did not read is a message you did not write, and a wrong amount in a chase email is worse than a late one. Check the amount in QuickBooks before you send, every single time.

The month end close, without the dread

Month end is the chore owners put off until it becomes a pile. Ask Muse to walk through the month with you: income by category, spending by category, invoices still open, and anything that looks uncategorized or duplicated. This is not a formal close, and it does not replace your accountant. It is a tidy up pass that keeps the pile small. The trick is to run it in the first week of the new month, while the last month is still fresh in your head. Anything you cannot place now will be twice as hard to place in six months. If something looks wrong, ask Muse to show you the underlying entries. A summary you trust is built on line items you checked, and the checking takes minutes when the month is fresh.

Tax time prep, started early

Tax time is where a year of small messes becomes one big bill from your accountant. Ask Muse to pull your income and expenses by category for the year so far, and to flag anything that looks uncategorized or personal. That list is the prep work, and doing it in October beats doing it in March. Be clear about the limit here. Muse can pull the lists and spot the mess. It cannot file your taxes, and it cannot replace your accountant. Take the lists to your accountant and let a human make the calls that carry legal weight. The value is that you arrive with clean lists instead of a shoebox, and clean lists cost less in accountant hours than a mess does.

5. What it cannot do

The limits are honest and worth knowing before you lean on the connector.

  • It cannot be relied on to write to your books until you have seen it do so. Creating invoices, marking them paid, and moving expenses between categories are write actions, and Meta has not published which of them Muse can take in QuickBooks. Test one small action with approvals on ask me and watch what it does before you trust a second one.
  • It cannot see books you never connected. Other company files, other accounting tools, and the spreadsheet you keep on the side are invisible to it. It reasons from the company you connected, nothing more.
  • It cannot fix a messy chart of accounts. If your categories are a tangle, Muse will summarize the tangle. Clean up the categories that carry most of your money first, and the answers get dramatically better.
  • It cannot file taxes or replace your accountant. It can pull lists and spot problems. The filing, the judgment calls, and the legal responsibility still belong to a human you pay for that job.
  • It cannot guarantee every number it quotes. A summary can miss an invoice, and a total can be read from the wrong date range. Check anything that touches money, a customer, or a tax decision against QuickBooks itself before you act on it.
  • It cannot see cash that never entered QuickBooks. Cash payments, money that went through a personal account, and income you never recorded are invisible to it. The answers are only as complete as the books you keep.

6. What to watch for

As of October 25, 2026, QuickBooks is a settled connector: live since September 29, named again on October 7, and still on the list in the October 15 in app check. There is no October update to how it works, so the habits around it matter more than the news. Check the numbers against QuickBooks for the first week, especially any total or date it quotes. A wrong total acted on fast is worse than a slow read you verified.

Be careful with money approvals. Keep anything that creates, sends, or changes a record on ask me until you trust the loop, and keep it there even after you do. The failure mode you want is a draft waiting for you, not a change already made. If Muse ever asks to do something with money that you did not ask for, say no and check what it was trying to do. A connector that touches money deserves more caution than one that reads your email, not less.

Elsewhere on the list, PayPal was announced on September 22 and its October live status is unverified, so do not plan a workflow around it yet. Amazon has no connector and none is expected. If a tool you need is not on the list at all, ask Muse to build a custom connector: any service with a public API can be connected that way, and Meta does not review custom connectors. Our connectors list keeps the dated status for all of these, so check there before you build on any single claim.

Finally, check your data controls. In Muse settings, open Data controls and look at the switch for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to read your books. Financial data deserves that decision made deliberately, not discovered later.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muse create invoices in QuickBooks?
That is the honest gap in this guide. Meta has not published which write actions Muse can take in QuickBooks, so ask Muse to draft an invoice first and check whether it can create one, with approvals set to ask me. Do not rely on it until you have seen it work once.
Does the QuickBooks connector work with my accountant?
Yes, and that is the right way to use it. Your accountant still works in QuickBooks directly, while Muse reads the same books and answers your questions between visits. Take the lists Muse pulls to your accountant and let a human make the calls that carry legal weight.
How many QuickBooks companies can I connect?
Meta has not said whether the connector takes one company or more. Connect your main company first and check what Muse can see before you assume it sees the rest.
Can I use the QuickBooks connector outside the United States, Canada, and Japan?
Not yet. Muse itself has launched in the United States, Canada, and Japan only. Join the waitlist and we will send you a fresh code on launch day in your country.
What happens if I disconnect QuickBooks?
Muse loses access to your books. Your records stay in QuickBooks, and nothing Muse drafted is sent or changed after you disconnect. You can reconnect the same company or a different one later.
Is my financial data used to train Muse?
There is a Data controls switch in Muse settings for helping improve the models. If you do not want your chats used for training, turn it off before you start asking Muse to read your books.
