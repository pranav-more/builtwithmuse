How to Use Muse for Sales Research

Give Muse your ideal customer and get back researched prospects: who they are, why now, and who to contact. Here is the research workflow that works.

1. The short answer

Sales research is reading: funding news, hiring pages, tech stacks, leadership changes. It is also the part of sales nobody wants to do, which is why most prospect lists are thin and most outreach is generic. Muse does the reading at machine speed. Describe your ideal customer once, and it builds prospect lists, researches each account, and drafts openers based on real facts instead of templates.

The rest of this guide covers defining your target, the research workflow, and the verification habit that keeps you credible.

2. What you need

Muse access. You need an account with access first.

Your ideal customer profile. Industry, company size, geography, and the problem you solve. Five sharp lines beat a vague paragraph. Example: I sell inventory software to independent retailers with five to fifty stores in India. They lose stock to poor tracking and they run on spreadsheets. That one paragraph tells Muse who fits and who does not.

Your product in one paragraph. What it does and who it helps, so Muse can judge fit. Write it the way you would explain it to a friend, not the way your website does. Muse matches prospects against plain language far better than marketing copy.

Your red lines. Which companies are off limits: existing customers, competitors, anyone who already said no. Give Muse the do not contact list upfront so it never builds a list you cannot use.

3. The research workflow

Define the target. Tell Muse: I sell inventory software to independent retailers with five to fifty stores in India. Find me fifty companies that fit. You get a working list in minutes instead of a week of tab hopping.

Add buying signals. Ask: which of these recently raised funding, opened new locations, or are hiring for operations roles. Signals turn a list into a priority order. A retailer opening its tenth store has a stock tracking problem right now. A retailer that did nothing new this year can wait.

Research the top ten. For each priority account, ask for a brief: what the company does, recent news, likely pain points for my product, and the right person to contact with their role. This is the part that used to take an afternoon per account. Read the brief before you write a single word of outreach.

Draft openers from facts. Ask Muse to draft a first line for each prospect based on something real: their new store opening, the funding round, the job post. Real beats clever. "Saw you opened your third store in Pune" starts conversations. "I noticed you" starts nothing.

Copy this prompt

Copy paste into Muse
Do sales research for me. Here is my business:
What I sell: [inventory software]
Who it helps: [independent retailers with five to fifty stores]
Region: [India]
The problem I solve: [they lose stock to poor tracking and run on spreadsheets]
My goal: [find fifty companies that fit, then research the ten most promising]
Off limits: [existing customers, competitors, anyone on my do not contact list]

Do this in order.
1. Build a list of companies that match my ideal customer. Include company name, what they do, and rough size.
2. For each company, look for buying signals: recent funding, new locations, hiring for operations roles. Rank the list with the strongest signals first.
3. For the top ten, write a short brief: what the company does, recent news with dates, likely pain points my product solves, and the job title of the right person to contact.
4. Draft one opening line per company based on a real fact from the brief. No templates, no flattery, no "I noticed you".

Rules: put the date on every fact. If you cannot verify something, say so instead of guessing. Research only. Do not contact anyone.

4. From research to outreach

Use the brief, not the template. A message that references their actual expansion plan gets read. A message that says "I noticed you" gets deleted. If you can swap the company name for any other company and the message still works, it is a template. Rewrite it.

Keep every draft in your voice. Muse gives you the facts and a starting draft; you edit until it sounds like you wrote it. Read it aloud. If a line makes you cringe, your prospect will cringe harder. Shorten it, use plain words, keep your rhythm.

Then send it yourself and track replies in your personal CRM setup. Research is Muse's job. Relationships are yours. Log who replied, who said no, and who asked for a call, so next week's research builds on this week's instead of starting over.

5. The verification habit

Verify every fact before it goes out. Funding amounts, names, job titles. One wrong fact kills your credibility with that account. If your opener congratulates them on a round they did not raise, you are done with that prospect. Check the fact, then send.

Check recency. News from a year ago is not a buying signal. Ask Muse for the date on everything. A store opening last month is a reason to write. A store opening last year is trivia.

Respect opt outs. If someone says no, log it and move on. Muse can track your do not contact list so you never double tap. Persistence is following up with the interested. Pestering the uninterested burns your name in the market.

Frequently asked questions

Can Muse find contact details?
It can often find public business contact info. Private personal details are off limits, and buying scraped lists is a bad idea for deliverability and trust.
How many prospects can it research?
Work in batches. Ten deep briefs beat a hundred shallow ones, and your outreach quality is what converts.
Does this replace a sales database?
It replaces the manual research part. You still need your own system of record for pipeline and follow ups.
What about enterprise accounts?
Same workflow, deeper briefs. For big accounts, ask Muse to map the buying committee: economic buyer, champion, and blockers.
Can it write the whole email?
It can draft it. You should always edit. Your reputation rides on every message with your name on it.
How do I know the research is current?
Ask Muse to date every fact it gives you. Treat anything older than a few months as background, not as a reason to write.
What if Muse cannot find the right person to contact?
Job titles vary by company. Ask for the role that owns the problem, like head of operations, instead of a name. Then confirm the actual person on LinkedIn before you write.
Is this just spam with extra steps?
No. Research is not outreach. You still write and send every message yourself, you reference real facts, and you respect every opt out. That is the opposite of spray and pray.
