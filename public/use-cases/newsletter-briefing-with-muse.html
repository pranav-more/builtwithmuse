I Stopped Reading Newsletters. Muse Reads Them for Me.

Newsletters pile into my Gmail every week. I read almost none of them. So I put Muse on the job: every morning it reads everything and tells me what actually mattered.

I have a newsletter problem. Every time someone recommends a good one, I subscribe. Then it joins the pile. At last count I had a pile of them hitting my Gmail every week, and I was reading maybe two of them. The rest just sat there making me feel guilty.

So I gave the job to Muse. Every morning it goes through the last 24 hours of newsletters in my inbox and hands me a short brief: the few things actually worth my time, and why. Everything else gets skipped without me ever seeing it.

The short version: Muse reads the whole pile so I can read three paragraphs.

How it works

  1. Newsletters pile into Gmail all week. I changed nothing. Subscribe freely, zero guilt.
  2. Every morning at 8, Muse scans the last 24 hours of newsletters in my inbox.
  3. It applies one ruthless filter. Only three kinds of things make the cut: a named framework, a case study with real numbers, or a genuinely uncommon insight.
  4. I get a short brief. Each item gets a one paragraph summary, why it matters, and a link to the full piece. On slow news days it just says nothing stood out today, and I move on with my morning.

The exact prompt to try

"Go through the newsletters in my Gmail from the last 24 hours. Pull out only what is worth my time: a named framework, a case study with real numbers, or a genuinely uncommon insight. For each one, give me a one paragraph summary, why it matters, and the link. If nothing clears the bar, just say so."

The filter is the whole trick Most people ask AI to summarize everything, which just gives you shorter versions of things you did not need to read. The filter decides what deserves your attention in the first place.

Where you will hit approval gates

Reality check: Muse needs access to your Gmail to do this, and you decide exactly what it can read. It only scans the newsletters, and every link in the brief opens the original piece so you can verify anything that sounds off.

Why it beats the old way

  • No more skimming a pile of newsletters over coffee
  • The filter is stricter than my willpower ever was
  • Slow news days cost me zero minutes
  • I actually read the good stuff now instead of saving it for later

FAQ

Does Muse read my whole inbox?
No. You connect Gmail and point it at the newsletters. It scans what you tell it to scan, nothing more.
What if nothing is worth reading?
Then it says so. Nothing stood out today is a valid result, and honestly one of the best parts. No filler just to look busy.
Can I change the filter?
Yes, that is the first thing to customize. My bar is frameworks, real numbers, and uncommon insights. Yours might be deals, job posts, or research papers. Tell Muse your version.
